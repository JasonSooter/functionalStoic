## Trip Ideas

### ⭐ London canals — Regent's Canal boat trip + London Canal Museum

**Marked top priority** from your own note on the Sketchplanations [[Bookshelf/Highlights & Notes/Articles/Canal Locks|Canal Locks]] piece: _"When going to England, this is a top priority!"_ ([highlight](https://readwise.io/open/873688227))

**London Canal Museum** — 12–13 New Wharf Road, King's Cross, London N1. About a five-minute walk from King's Cross St Pancras.

- The building is a **Victorian ice warehouse, built 1862–63 for Carlo Gatti**, to store ice imported from Norway. Two **ice wells** sit beneath it, partly excavated; one is viewable directly from the public area of the museum, the other via CCTV.
- The wells were filled with soil and debris around 1956 and rediscovered in the 1980s.
- The museum owns **Long Tom**, a 12-seater trip boat.
- It sits **on the Regent's Canal, with Battlebridge Basin directly behind it.**
- ⚠️ **Opening days, hours and admission prices are not recorded here.** The museum's own site could not be fetched on this run. Check [canalmuseum.org.uk](https://www.canalmuseum.org.uk/) before going, and check whether a 2FOR1 rail offer applies.

> [!important] Planning detail that will otherwise bite you
> The famous tourist waterbus runs **Little Venice ↔ Camden Lock**, and that stretch **does not reach the museum.** Battlebridge Basin is at King's Cross, east of Camden — so "the canal boat trip" and "the Canal Museum" are two separate outings unless you book something that runs the eastern section or through the Islington Tunnel. Worth sorting out before you commit a day to it.

**Boat operators to check** (Little Venice ↔ Camden unless stated): London Waterbus Company, Walker's Quay, and **Jason's Trip** — which you will enjoy on nominative grounds alone. ⚠️ Schedules and fares not verified this run; all three run seasonally and Camden gets extremely busy at weekends.

**Why this is a good family day with three kids aged 10–13:** the locks are a working mechanism you can watch operate, which is the Sketchplanations point; the ice-well story is the kind of concrete industrial history that survives a teenager's scepticism (Norwegian ice, shipped to London, sold by a Swiss-Italian café owner); and the towpath is walkable and cyclable, which makes it a [[Cycling]]-compatible route rather than a queue.

**Adjacent, if you want a full day of it:** Camden Lock itself, the Islington Tunnel portals, and the towpath walk between them. See also [[Travel/United States/New York/Erie Canal Bike Trip Notes|Erie Canal Bike Trip Notes]] — you have done the American version of this, and the contrast in scale and preservation is the interesting part.

### London Transport Museum Depot (Acton)

_Added 2026-09-26 by Claude (`source/ai/claude`), from a Readwise task (your note: "Add to London note as a place to go when visiting")._

> [!quote] Dave Walker, Diagram Club #087, "Before You Encounter a Milk Churn" (July 2026)
> We went to the London Transport Museum Depot. To explain: It's not the actual main London Transport Museum, which is in Covent Garden, but it's the additional overflow and storage part of the museum, out to the west of London. ([View Highlight](https://readwise.io/open/1051276772))

- [ ] **Visit the London Transport Museum Depot**
- **Not a walk-in museum:** it opens **only for Open Days and booked guided tours**. Where: 2 Museum Way, 118–120 Gunnersbury Lane, Acton W3 9BQ, **2 minutes from Acton Town** (Piccadilly/District) ([ltmuseum.co.uk](https://www.ltmuseum.co.uk/)).
- **Coming dates** (as listed 26 Sep 2026): the last **Open Days** of 2026 are **25–27 Sep 2026** (i.e. now); then tours: _Depot Discovery_ (Fri/Sat, 9 Oct 2026 – 20 Mar 2027), _Design Icons_ (Sat, 10 Oct – 13 Mar), _Art and Poster Store_ (Sat, 24 Oct – 20 Mar), **Depot Family Tours** (Wed, 28 Oct 2026 – 31 Mar 2027). ⚠️ Ticket prices not checked; 2027 Open Days not yet listed.
- **What's there**, per Walker: signage, buses and trains, geographic (not Harry Beck) maps, and a few bikes, _"a bit of a sideshow"_: a TfL hire bike, two Condor road bikes, some historic machines. Pairs with the main museum in Covent Garden.
