## Ditching Tech: Finland Returns to Books and Sweden Says No to Screens for Toddlers — Helen Russell (2024)
Source: [[Ditching Tech - Finland Returns to Books and Sweden Says No to Screens for Toddlers]] · [Original](https://mshelenrussell.substack.com/p/ditching-tech-finland-returns-to) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5f0rsyhccg0pdf4f7fpftw0)

> Education has always been a priority and there was even a 19th-century decree that a couple couldn’t marry in the Lutheran church before both passed a reading test.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Russell reports Finland moving away from digital learning tools back to books after years of digitising classrooms, citing University of Helsinki research on retention and a 19th-century rule that couples had to pass a reading test to marry in church.
- **Why it matters:** Finland is the lead case in her argument about screens in childhood.

## What’s So Special About Finland? — Uri Friedman (2016)
Source: [Original](https://www.theatlantic.com/international/archive/2016/07/nordic-american-dream-partanen/489032/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jx70heg1x0p7e7788d7dff)

> “What Finland and its neighbors do is actually walk the walk of opportunity that America now only talks,” Partanen writes. “It’s a fact: A citizen of Finland, Norway, or Denmark is today much more likely to rise above his or her parents’ socioeconomic status than is a citizen of the United States.”

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The interview with Anu Partanen argues that Finland and its Nordic neighbours deliver the American Dream's freedom and opportunity better than the US, via paid parental leave, subsidised child care, public health care and free tuition. It also names drawbacks, such as Finland's long parental leave hurting women's careers.
- **Why it matters:** Finland is the article's central subject and comparison case, though treated as a whole country rather than any specific site.

## How the Finnish survive without small talk — Laura Studarus (2018)
Source: [Original](https://www.bbc.com/travel/story/20181016-how-the-finnish-survive-without-small-talk) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k7bvvp6xkzebw2ybndhant)

> Finnish people often forgo the conversational niceties that are hard-baked into other cultures, and typically don’t see the need to meet foreign colleagues, tourists and friends in the middle.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The article examines Finnish culture's aversion to small talk, with the saying 'Silence is gold, talking is silver', minimal interaction with baristas and strangers, and silence among friends as comfortable. Experts offer hypotheses involving language, distance between cities, and respect for personal space.
- **Why it matters:** The whole piece is about Finland's national character, so the country itself is the subject.

## How to Homeschool Like the Finnish — Weird, Unsocialized Homeschoolers (2019)
Source: [Original](https://www.weirdunsocializedhomeschoolers.com/how-to-homeschool-like-the-finnish/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k8y55wr3cr680awg4wnqxt)

> In Finland, students don’t start school until age 7, and they still are given many breaks and time outdoors.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The piece holds up the Finnish school system as a model: minimal testing, teachers who stay with students for years, school starting at 7, cozy classrooms where kids warm up in socks, 15-minute outdoor recesses between lessons and hours outside even in winter.
