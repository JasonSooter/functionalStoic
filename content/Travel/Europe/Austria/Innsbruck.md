# Innsbruck

Innsbruck is the capital of Tyrol on the river Inn, in Austria. It shows up in the reading as the stop where Bill Bryson first feels he is really in Europe.

## Bruno de Finetti was born here

> [!quote] Carlo Rovelli, _There Are Places in the World Where Rules Are Less Important Than Kindness_
> De Finetti was born in 1906 in Innsbruck of Italian parents.

Source: [Readwise highlight](https://readwise.io/open/1044954204) · [[Bookshelf/Highlights & Notes/Books/There Are Places in the World Where Rules Are Less Important Than Kindness|There Are Places in the World…]]

- **Who:** Bruno de Finetti (1906–1985), founder of **subjective probability**: probability as a degree of belief, not a property of the world. His line: _"Es existiert keine objektive Wahrscheinlichkeit"_ (there is no objective probability) ([Universität Innsbruck](https://www.uibk.ac.at/archive/public-relations/presse/archiv/2011/072701/genialer-mathematiker-mit-innsbrucker-wurzeln.html.de)). He described himself as having _"Italian parents, Austrian citizens"_; his father was a railway engineer working in Innsbruck ([MacTutor](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/De_Finetti/)). The family moved to Italy when he was a child, and he taught at Trieste and Rome.

## The place to find on a return visit

- [ ] **His birth house: Conradstraße 8, Saggen**, with a **memorial plaque unveiled on 26 July 2011**, sponsored by the University and the City of Innsbruck. His daughter **Fulvia de Finetti** was there; it coincided with the 7th International Symposium on Fuzzy Probabilities, run by the university's Department of Technical Mathematics ([Universität Innsbruck press release, 2011](https://www.uibk.ac.at/archive/public-relations/presse/archiv/2011/072701/genialer-mathematiker-mit-innsbrucker-wurzeln.html.de)). ⚠️ That the plaque is still up in 2026 wasn't checked.
- **Saggen** is the 19th-century villa district just northeast of the old town ⚠️ (location from general knowledge), so it's a short walk.

## Why he's worth the detour

The idea behind his work is the one in [[Concepts/Statistical Literacy|Statistical Literacy]]: a probability is a statement about **what you know**, which is why two honest people can assign different ones. It's the root of Bayesian thinking.

## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)
Source: [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5bejymvrsa36tx491hjk0p4)

> I walked through the station at Innsbruck with an almost eerie sense of familiarity

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Opening of chapter 19. Bryson hasn't been back for eighteen years, but the station seems unchanged and the buffet still serves the goulash with dumplings he ate four times in three days as a student. He takes a room at the Goldene Krone and walks the town in slanting afternoon sun, calling it "an ideal little city", then returns along Marie-Theresienstrasse.
- **Why it matters:** Here he feels, "certainly for the first time on this trip", a sense of wonder at his surroundings: "I was in Europe now." It is the emotional turn of the Austrian chapter, before the disappointments of Salzburg and Vienna.
