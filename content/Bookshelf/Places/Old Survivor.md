## How to Do Nothing — Jenny Odell (2019)
Source: [[How to Do Nothing — Reader's Companion]] · [[How to Do Nothing]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dqzpb3r4swq76nt5ycv8x3)

> it’s Oakland’s only old-growth redwood left standing, a miraculous five-hundred-year-old holdover from the time before all of the ancient redwoods were logged following the Gold Rush.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The Introduction opens in the Oakland hills with this hidden, twisted, 93-foot redwood on a steep rocky slope in Leona Park, found by a naturalist in 1969. It survived logging by seeming useless, which Odell links to Zhuangzi's useless tree and uses as her image of resistance-in-place and of witness and memorial.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's governing metaphor and the first place she asks the reader to attend to.
