## There Are Places in the World Where Rules Are Less Important Than Kindness — Carlo Rovelli (2020)

Source: [[There Are Places in the World Where Rules Are Less Important Than Kindness]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5bevfkq6e64wcbcfjkc8bpc)

> Leopardi was living in isolation in Recanati, a small provincial village in a remote part of central Italy.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The essay "Leopardi and Astronomy". Rovelli says the meaning of Leopardi's teenage _History of Astronomy_ becomes clearer from its context: the boy lived isolated in Recanati, and his father Monaldo, close to the authority of the Church, opposed Copernicus, whom the fifteen-year-old passionately believed in. "For all its erudition, the book is really an act of rebellion." Rovelli also wonders whether Leopardi's life might have turned out better had his first attempt to escape Recanati not been stopped by his father.
- **Why it matters:** The town's remoteness is the essay's explanation for why astronomy meant freedom to the young poet. Set in a cosmopolitan city, the book would read as ordinary precocity rather than revolt.
