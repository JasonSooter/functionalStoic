## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)
Source: [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5bekhjxssp97jf2vcp2cktf)

> Sofia had a dark and enormous department store called TSUM, at least as big as Selfridge’s in London, spread over five floors

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 21. Bryson remembers the store from 1973 with Katz: five floors of goods that looked made before 1938, with families crowding round a television as if in a museum. Back in 1990 he finds the departments stripped bare. He stays at the hard-currency Sheraton on Lenin Square and feels guilty eating better than nine million Bulgarians.
- **Why it matters:** TSUM is the chapter's central symbol of the shortage economy. With [[Vitosha Boulevard]], it carries his picture of a country where people now have money and nothing to buy.
