## Death in Venice — Thomas Mann (1912)
Source: [[Death in Venice]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5c4pv4knkcja4jkva6mfcvy)

> He did not find his idol at San Marco. However, while taking tea at his little round wrought-iron table on the shady side of the square, he suddenly whiffed an unusual aroma in the air, an aroma he now felt he had been inhaling for days without being conscious of it, a cloying medicinal smell redolent of squalor and sores and dubious hygiene.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** San Marco is Aschenbach's daily destination from the Lido, where he takes tea before his walks. In chapter five, at his table on the shady side of the square, he first notices the medicinal smell of disinfectant; after a British travel clerk tells him about the cholera, he paces the square's flagstones deciding whether to warn Tadzio's mother, and resolves "I shall say nothing." On Sundays he follows the Polish family into the basilica, standing at the back on the cracked mosaic floor in the "golden dusk", then trails them out through the clock-tower gateway into the Merceria.
- **Why it matters:** The square is where the hidden epidemic and Aschenbach's moral surrender meet, staged among unsuspecting tourists. The basilica adds the incense-and-sickness atmosphere of the pursuit scenes.
