## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)

Source: [[Neither Here Nor There]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ce6zw4p60ssygmftt71kzy)

> But what I really dislike about buildings like the Pompidou Center, and Paris is choking on them, is that they are just showing off. Here’s Richard Rogers saying to the world: “Look, I put all the pipes on the outside. Am I cute enough to kiss or what?”

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** With the Louvre too crowded, Bryson goes to the Pompidou Center determined to like it and fails: it is weathered, too big for its cramped neighbourhood, and badly designed inside, with nowhere to sit or meet. It becomes his case against architects who forget that cities are for people.
- **Why it matters:** The building is the target of one of the book's sharpest set pieces on architecture.
