> Jakarta is the largest city in Southeast Asia, but it’s sinking at a rate of up to 15 centimetres a year

- **Role:** subject
- **In the video:** The video explains why Jakarta, a megacity of more than 11M people on a low-lying coastal plain at the mouth of 13 rivers, is sinking up to 15 cm a year, mainly from groundwater extraction. It traces the Dutch founding of Batavia, post-1945 population growth and informal riverbank settlement, then the government's two responses: a sea wall and a new capital.
- **Why it matters:** The city is the article's whole subject: a real-world case of a capital literally sinking and being partly abandoned.
