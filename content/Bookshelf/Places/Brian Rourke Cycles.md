## It's All About the Bike: In Pursuit of Happiness on Two Wheels — Robert Penn (2010)
Source: [[It's All About the Bike]] · [[It's All About the Bike - In Pursuit of Happiness on Two Wheels]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5e332qt7g8kj2xf34rhnt7z)

> The ‘oven’, a heated panel at the back of the paint shop, ensured each coat was dry in minutes.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Penn returns to Brian Rourke's premises after two seasons to see his frame painted. In Jason Rourke's paintshop he spends an hour matching tins of blue and orange, Jason vetoes purple, and the frame gets several coats of orange and metallic blue, each dried in minutes by a heated 'oven' panel. The finished frame is then assembled by the shop mechanics.
- **Why it matters:** The paintshop is where the colour of the bespoke bike is chosen and applied, the emotional high point of the frame chapter, and it sits in the same Stoke-on-Trent premises as the fitting and welding.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Place documented by the text; only the point comes from OSM. Text names Stoke-on-Trent and the Rourke shop (ch.1 'Diamond Soul'); en Wikipedia has no article on the business; OSM maps Brian Rourke Cycles, Bursley Road, Smallthorne, ST6 3HF: https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/255392418
