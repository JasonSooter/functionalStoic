## Breakneck — Dan Wang (2025)

Source: [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dcnf1rdkg3p46naavcpkx1)

> Chongqing is my favorite Chinese city to visit because it has the country’s, and perhaps the world’s, most dramatic urban setting.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** The cycling trip ends in Chongqing, built on steep hillsides between the Yangtze and Jialing rivers: stacked buildings, a metro line running through an apartment block, wartime air-raid tunnels turned into hotpot restaurants. Returning in 2022, the author finds the Jialing nearly dry in a drought.
- **Why it matters:** The end of the bike trip and a vivid case of urban engineering and of climate vulnerability.

## Notes on China — Dwarkesh Patel (2024)

Source: [Original](https://www.dwarkeshpatel.com/p/notes-on-china) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5edfq1h93ara7x8mt7gys7h)

> Chongqing is by far the coolest city I've ever visited. It's this insane cyberpunk multi-level metropolis of over 20 million people.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Chongqing is praised as the best city he has visited; he got a cheap two-floor hotel room overlooking two rivers and the skyline, and from atop its tallest building compares the endless skyscrapers to the Hubble Deep Field. A mall there is also where high schoolers ask for selfies.
- **Why it matters:** Chongqing is the emotional high point of the trip and the piece's main image of urban scale. It also recurs in the Youngsters section.
