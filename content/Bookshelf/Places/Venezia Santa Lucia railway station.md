## Death in Venice — Thomas Mann (1912)

Source: [[Death in Venice]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5c4pwcr9t53w6kwf09yh5nq)

> He entered the station racked by indecision.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Having decided the sirocco and the canal stench are harming him, Aschenbach rides up the Grand Canal to leave Venice, grieving. At the station he learns that his trunk has been sent to Como by mistake, feels a reckless joy, cancels his ticket and is back on the Lido within the hour.
- **Why it matters:** The plot pivots here: chance overrides his decision to leave, and everything after follows from it.
- **Real vs. fictional:** ⚠️ The text says only "the station"; Santa Lucia, Venice's only rail terminus at the time, is inferred.
