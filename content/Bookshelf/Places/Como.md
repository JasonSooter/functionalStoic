## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)

Source: [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5bejpy67j8xv6n2mzyjsmkn)

> Como was a lovely little city, clean and perfect, in a cupped hand of Alpine mountains at the southern end of the narrow, thirty-mile-long lake of the same name.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 16, after Milan. Bryson takes a room at the Hotel Plinius, has two coffees on the Piazza Roma overlooking the lake and walks the poplar-lined promenade. He compares the Basilica of San Fidele, begun in 914, with the larger domed cathedral, "more splendid than any provincial church I had seen since Aachen", and spends an hour inside.
- **Why it matters:** A restful interlude between [[Milan]], which he found charmless, and the Swiss chapter. It is his last warm Italian scene before he crosses into Switzerland.
