## Another Brooklyn — Jacqueline Woodson (2016)

Source: [[Another Brooklyn]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dcmt3e23f4bk7vhrh7jycx)

> Because Bushwick had once been a forest and we had been called ghetto girls even though we were beautiful and our arms were locked together and our T-shirts blared our names and zodiac signs.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** August, her father and brother move from SweetGrove, Tennessee, to a top-floor apartment in Bushwick, which the book dedicates itself to. From the window and then the streets she watches the neighbourhood of the 1970s: the bodega, the handball court, the park where DJs run cords from the street lights, the looting on Broadway the summer the lights went out, the fires in the wood-frame houses, and the friendship with Sylvia, Angela and Gigi.
- **Why it matters:** The neighbourhood is the novel's world; its dangers and its freedoms shape the girls' friendship and August's growing up.
