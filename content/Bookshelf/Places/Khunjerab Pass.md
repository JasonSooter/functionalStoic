## It's All About the Bike — Robert Penn (2010)
Source: [[It's All About the Bike]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5c3nndrk1m3wmm30ekn2f1v)

> At midday, I reached the Pass — a short, flat section of tarmac edged by snow. I was exalted. It was a pivotal moment: the highest point of my three-year round-the-world ride.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Chapter 3 ("All Geared Up") opens with Penn recalling his second crossing, a seven-day climb from Gilgit on a cold September day. At the top he meets Leslie, a paraplegic Scottish woman riding a hand-cranked recumbent tandem, and that meeting leads into the history of hand-driven velocipedes and the drivetrain.
- **Why it matters:** It is a recollection rather than part of the present-day build, but it frames the chapter. The pass links Penn's own journey to the argument about hand power versus leg power.
