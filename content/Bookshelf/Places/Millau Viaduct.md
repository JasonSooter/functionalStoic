## A Love Letter to Cable-Stayed Bridges — Practical Engineering (2025)

Source: [Original](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YSQhtlyfPtU) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ebsc93xa07yhstwn52jxcj)

> Compare that to the sleek multi-span wonder of the French Millau (MEE-oh) Viaduct with eight spans, six of which are longer than a thousand feet or three hundred meters.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the video:** Presented as the multi-span ideal: concrete only in the deck and eight pylons, no anchorages, with the world's tallest pylon taller than the Eiffel Tower; revisited later as a single-cable-plane example.
- **Why it matters:** It is the narrator's showcase for why cable-stayed multi-span designs beat suspension bridges.

## An Engineer’s Love Letter to Cable-Stayed Bridges — Practical Engineering (2025)

Source: [Original](https://practical.engineering/blog/2025/2/14/an-engineers-love-letter-to-cable-stayed-bridges) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ebsdkm871g3vfw6dyp63sk)

> Compare that to the sleek multi-span wonder of the French Millau(MEE-oh) Viaduct with eight spans, six of which are longer than a thousand feet or three hundred meters.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Presented as the showcase of multi-span cable-stayed design: eight spans, eight pylons, no anchorages, curving above the French countryside. It is also called the tallest bridge in the world, its tallest pylon taller than the Eiffel tower. It returns later as the example of single cable planes.
- **Why it matters:** Gives the argument its most vivid proof case for why cable-stayed beats suspension on multi-span crossings; swapping in another bridge would work but Millau is the emotional centrepiece.
