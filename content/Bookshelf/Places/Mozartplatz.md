## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)

Source: [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5bek07fp5295jh9qy960d7y)

> The main square in Salzburg, the Mozartplatz, was quite astonishingly ugly for a city that prides itself on beauty.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 19. He describes "a big expanse of asphalt, as charming as a supermarket parking lot", a begrimed statue of the composer and half-broken benches crowded with noisy Italian teenagers. "It was awful, awful." He had remembered Salzburg as beautiful from his student trip with Katz.
- **Why it matters:** The square is the centre of his disappointment with a remembered city. He later crosses to the modern right bank and likes it better: see also [[Getreidegasse]].
