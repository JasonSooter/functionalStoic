# Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II

The 1860s glass-roofed shopping arcade beside the cathedral square in [[Milan]], Italy.

## At a glance

## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)
Source: [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5bejkhy2tgemb5tdth6d237)

> The Galleria is an imposing shopping arcade, four stories high, built in the grandiose style of the 1860s, and still probably the most handsome shopping mall in the world,

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 16. Bryson lists "the city's three glories: La Scala, the cathedral, and the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II". After the dim, tourist-free cathedral he crosses to the Galleria, then strolls the expensive shopping streets nearby, which leave him cold.
- **Why it matters:** His verdict on Milan is a rich city without charm; the Galleria is the exception that lets him admire something before he moves on to the [[Santa Maria delle Grazie]] refectory.
