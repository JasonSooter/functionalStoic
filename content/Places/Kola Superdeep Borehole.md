# Kola Superdeep Borehole

Soviet scientific borehole on the Kola Peninsula in north-west [[Russia]], near the Finnish border. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is humanity's deepest attempt to drill into the Earth.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> They chose a spot on Russia’s Kola Peninsula, near the Finnish border, and set to work with the hope of drilling to a depth of fifteen kilometers.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The Soviets choose a spot on the Kola Peninsula and aim to drill fifteen kilometres down. After nineteen years they reach 12,262 metres, less than a third of the way through the crust. The rocks are not as predicted: the sedimentary layer is 50 percent deeper, no basalt is found, it is far hotter (180°C at 10,000 metres) and the rock is saturated with water.
- **Why it matters:** It shows how little we know of what lies beneath our feet, the theme of the chapter on Earth's interior.
