# Lake Union

Small freshwater lake in the middle of [[Seattle]], linked to Lake Washington by the Montlake Cut. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is where Joe Rantz's first freshman boat rows together for the first time, and where Joe first feels part of a crew.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86m5hym6xzy9kfxt4bbsbxh)

> To the west, silver headlights crawled slowly across the spidery steel arch of the new Aurora Bridge. To the south, the amber lights of downtown Seattle danced on the waves.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** On November 28, 1933, the day Tom Bolles names the first freshman boat with Joe in it, the chosen boys row west through the Cut and Portage Bay onto Lake Union, looking for calmer water than Lake Washington offers. At the north end they stop, breathing in unison; looking at the Aurora Bridge, the lights of downtown and the radio towers on Queen Anne Hill, Joe cries for the first time since his family left him. In the autumn of 1934 the crew later rows a shell Pocock built for California to the lake's south end and carries it on their heads down Westlake, Fourth Avenue and Columbia Street to the docks. The Rantz family's new house stands near the lake's north end.
- **Why it matters:** The lake gives the book its first glimpse of what the crew can become: a moment of shared rhythm that cracks Joe's lonely guard. Set on the rough open lake, the calm, intimate scene would not happen.
