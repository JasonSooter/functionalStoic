# United States Capitol

Seat of the US Congress, on Capitol Hill in [[Washington DC|Washington, D.C.]]. In _How to Know a Person_, young Lyndon Johnson's daily run up the hill to it is Brooks's picture of raw ambition.

## How to Know a Person — David Brooks (2023)
Source: [[How to Know a Person]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/652822/how-to-know-a-person-by-david-brooks/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8gxramvznwwns006jef8tmg)

> Johnson would leave his basement room in the shabby hotel where he was staying and walk toward the U.S. Capitol Building. After a few blocks, the building would loom on the hill before him.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Drawing on Robert Caro's account, Brooks describes Johnson as a young aide leaving a basement room in a shabby hotel and walking toward the Capitol. As it loomed on the hill he sped up and ran "up the hill and across the plaza" to his office while people gawked. Brooks follows this with the Johnson family's twice-lost ranch on the Pedernales, the source of his drive.
- **Why it matters:** The run is Brooks's image of the career-consolidation life task: "Johnson's ambition in physical form". The scene needs the Capitol on its hill, the seat of the power he was running toward.
