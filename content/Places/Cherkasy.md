# Cherkasy

City on the Dnieper in central [[Ukraine]]. In _The Son and Heir_ it is the scene of Frans Münninghoff's "disgrace at Cherkasy", when the SS interpreter lets a Russian prisoner go.

## The Son and Heir: A Memoir — Alexander Münninghoff (2020)
Source: [Original](https://www.letterenfonds.nl/en/books/heir-and-successor) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m85n9hjevpcjfqb5w9p44ebs)

> The last time I worked as an interpreter was in 1943, in Cherkasy on the Dnieper River.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In his own words, Frans recalls interpreting at SS interrogations of Soviet officers in 1943, during the retreat after Stalingrad. A Baltic acquaintance tells him that after questioning the prisoners are trucked off in groups of twenty and shot in a ditch on the edge of Cherkasy, so he pulls the next prisoner, a young man, out of the barn and lets him escape. He is seen, and the episode lands him in a high-risk storm-troop unit; later he remembers lying wounded in the melting snow at Cherkasy.
- **Why it matters:** It is the one wartime act of decency the book can attribute to Frans, and the reason he never spoke of his storm-troop months. Without the shootings at Cherkasy, the son's question of who his father really was would have no counterweight.
