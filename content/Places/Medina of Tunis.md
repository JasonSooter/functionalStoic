# Medina of Tunis

The old walled city at the heart of Tunis, in [[Tunisia]]. It is the crowded setting that shapes a Peace Corps volunteer's early fear and later sense of belonging.

## Peace Corps: The Great Adventure — Peace Corps (2002)
Source: [[Peace Corps - The Great Adventure]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL16050643W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5c3q1cd4xsvq8h33wktnwty)

> I would stay motionless, listening to mopeds whir through the crowded streets of the medina (the traditional walled city) and the constant hammering of the brass smiths outside my window.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In "YSWF: Living in an Arab World" (Lora Parisien, Tunisia 1989–91), the author is posted to Tunis and at first stays indoors to avoid harassment, listening to the medina from her room. She walks thirty minutes to the Bourguiba Institute, where she teaches English, and counts how often she is approached. After about six months Tunis feels like home, and she is later led through the medina's labyrinth to a friend's family home, where a marriage proposal is made.
- **Why it matters:** The crowded old city shapes both her early fear and her later sense of belonging. ⚠️ The text places her within earshot of the medina but doesn't say her flat is inside it.
