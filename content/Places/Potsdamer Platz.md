# Potsdamer Platz

Square in central [[Berlin]], wasteland beside the Wall until it was rebuilt with corporate towers after reunification. In David Byrne's _Bicycle Diaries_ it is the test case for whether a city centre can be made from scratch.

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7641wnfsjbfen0femxtvd50)

> In one former prewar center, Potsdamer Platz, the massive new edifices of the corporate state have gone up—Sony, Mercedes, Siemens, and others have new steel-and-glass buildings there.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Byrne describes post-Wall Berlin as planning on a massive scale, none of it organic. He calls the rebuilding "a colossal experiment" that asks whether a vibrant centre can be created from scratch, then rides on through Mitte, where boutiques are pushing out the galleries.
- **Why it matters:** Potsdamer Platz carries his central question about Berlin: whether planned renewal can produce a living city.
