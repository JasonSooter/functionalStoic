# February House

Former artists' commune at 7 Middagh Street in Brooklyn Heights, [[Brooklyn]], since demolished. In _Great Short Books_ it is where Carson McCullers, living alongside W. H. Auden, was inspired to write _The Ballad of the Sad Café_.

## Great Short Books — Kenneth C. Davis (2022)
Source: [[Great Short Books]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8f01ktebqz4qpnek0ka2ej5)

> She later moved into a Brooklyn house rented by George Davis, a prominent literary editor, and shared with the British poet W. H. Auden and his lover. Known as “February House,” it emerged as a bohemian commune whose luminary residents included composer Benjamin Britten, the children of Thomas Mann, and poet Paul Bowles.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In his biography of Carson McCullers, Davis says she moved into the house after her 1941 divorce. Salvador Dalí visited, and the house was underwritten by Gypsy Rose Lee. "It was here, after a night in a Brooklyn bar with Auden, that McCullers was inspired by a dwarf she had seen to write _The Ballad of the Sad Café_." Richard Wright and his wife also moved into the commune, Davis notes in Wright's entry.
- **Why it matters:** It is the birthplace of the novella's central figure, the hunchback Cousin Lymon, whose arrival sets the story going in [[The mill town (The Ballad of the Sad Café)|McCullers's mill town]]; a Georgia story, conceived in Brooklyn.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real. Wikipedia (February House) gives the address as 7 Middagh Street and says the house was demolished soon after George Davis left, to build the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway; the pin is on Middagh Street.
