# 1939 New York World's Fair

World's fair held in Flushing Meadows, Queens, in 1939–40. Its Futurama pavilion links two very different readings: in Tom Hanks's 'The Past Is Important to Us' Bert relives one day there, and in _Happy City_ it is the exhibit that sold America the motorway future.

## Uncommon Type — Tom Hanks (2017)
Source: [[Uncommon Type]] · [Original](https://penguin.co.uk/books/1114786/uncommon-type/9781786091338.html) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k5w40k7cn8ape055p6mkc7)

> The IRT discharged fairgoers at the Bowling Green Gate, where Bert paid seventy-five cents for admission and bought a guidebook for a dime.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In 'The Past Is Important to Us' Bert takes the IRT out to the Fair, tours the Trylon and Perisphere and the Futurama, eats pie at Borden's with Carmen and Virginia, and overstays his deadline on a bench by the Lagoon of Nations as the fountains light up.
- **Why it matters:** The Fair is the story's main stage, the place where Bert repeatedly relives a single day and finds Carmen.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83qhpj8gbm5wzymdexbtxvx)

> They would grow into the most persuasive piece of propaganda in the history of city planning, when Bel Geddes expanded the model into Futurama, a vast pavilion for the 1939 World’s Fair in New York.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Norman Bel Geddes's models of automated superhighways, first shown at a 1937 planning conference, grow into GM's Futurama, where visitors ride above a vast diorama of motorways and a pedestrian walkway lifted over a perfect car street. More than 24 million people see it.
- **Why it matters:** Montgomery treats it as the moment the idea of freedom as speed was sold to a whole nation, paving the way for the car-built city.
