# Brompton Oratory

Neoclassical Catholic church in Knightsbridge, London, near Harrods. Andrew Ritchie could see it from his kitchen window while he drew his folding bike, and he named the bike after it.

## The Brompton: Engineering for Change — Will Butler-Adams (2022)
Source: [[The Brompton - Engineering for Change]] · [Original](https://profilebooks.com/work/the-brompton/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8c38phydbwkpdtwtytjtpkt)

> Andrew really did build prototype parts in his shed, and do the drawings at his kitchen table, looking out of his window at a neoclassical church in Knightsbridge, just down the road from Harrods. The church was the Brompton Oratory, and that’s where he got the name.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The introduction tells the founding story. Andrew Ritchie invented the bike and its central hinge in 1975 while running a landscape gardening business. He built prototype parts in his shed and did the drawings at his kitchen table, looking out at the Oratory, and the bike took its name from the church.
- **Why it matters:** The Oratory has no part in making the bike. It matters because a world-famous product carries its name, and because it shows how homespun the beginning was: one inventor, a kitchen table and the view from a London flat.
