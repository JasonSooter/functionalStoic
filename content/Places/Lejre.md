# Lejre

Village west of Roskilde on Zealand, Denmark, where great royal halls of the 6th to 10th centuries have been excavated and which scholars identify with Heorot. Hrothgar's hall, Grendel's raids and the mere a few miles off are the core of _Beowulf_.

## Beowulf — Seamus Heaney (1999)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1810107W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne5wkpvrj6z792rsvkqngr)

> the hall of halls. Heorot was the name

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Hrothgar builds Heorot, 'the hall of halls', and for twelve winters Grendel raids it by night until Beowulf arrives and tears off his arm there. Grendel's mother then enters the hall and seizes Aeschere. Hrothgar leads Beowulf a few miles to a mere under a frost-stiffened wood, where Beowulf dives in, kills her in her hall under the water and brings Grendel's head back to Heorot.
- **Why it matters:** Heorot is the poem's central stage for its first two fights, the embodiment of Danish glory and of how fragile it is.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Heorot is legendary; Wikipedia notes that excavations at Lejre found three successive halls about 50 metres long (mid-6th century, about 680 and about 880), and Fred C. Robinson calls Lejre 'the actual location of Heorot'. Grendel's mere is unnamed and unlocated.
