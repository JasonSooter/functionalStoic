# Mount Unzen

Volcano on the Shimabara Peninsula in western [[Japan]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where a pyroclastic flow killed Harry Glicken and 42 others.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Eleven years later he was one of forty-three scientists and journalists fatally caught up in a lethal outpouring of superheated ash, gases, and molten rock—what is known as a pyroclastic flow—at Mount Unzen in Japan

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Harry Glicken had left his observation post at [[Mount St Helens]] the day before it erupted. Eleven years later he is one of forty-three scientists and journalists killed at Mount Unzen by a pyroclastic flow of superheated ash, gas and molten rock.
- **Why it matters:** A brief, grim coda to the St Helens story, underlining the danger of watching volcanoes up close.
