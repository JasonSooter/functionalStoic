# Reichstag building

Seat of the German parliament in Berlin's Tiergarten, built in the 1880s and restored with Norman Foster's dome. It is the setting of the final chapter of _Germany: Memories of a Nation_.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)
Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m75397rfddp3pxmh85q5aa)

> I want to end it in the Reichstag, seat of the German Parliament and site of Germany’s national deliberations.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 2 opens by the Spree beside the restored Reichstag, where rows of white crosses name people killed trying to cross from East to West Berlin. Chapter 15 has Scheidemann proclaiming the Republic from its balcony on 9 November 1918. Chapter 30 traces the building from the 1880s through the 1933 fire, its capture by Soviet troops in 1945 (their graffiti preserved), Christo and Jeanne-Claude's wrapping and Norman Foster's glass cupola, with the Holocaust Memorial and Tiergarten memorials placed deliberately nearby.
- **Why it matters:** MacGregor ends the book here because, after Foster's restoration, every one of the building's memories is 'still present and active'; it stands for a democracy that keeps its past in view.
