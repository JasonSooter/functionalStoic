# 103 Leninsky Prospekt

KGB officers' tower block on Leninsky Prospekt in south-west [[Moscow]]. In Ben Macintyre's _The Spy and the Traitor_ it is Oleg Gordievsky's flat, where he finds the first sign that the KGB suspects him.

## The Spy and the Traitor — Ben Macintyre (2018)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19749410W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7p6z9b1n3fcb2pxd5yzyg74)

> It took less than a minute to spring the locks on the front door of the flat on the eighth floor of 103 Leninsky Prospekt, a Moscow tower block occupied by KGB officers and their families.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** The book opens here on May 18, 1985: KGB Directorate K technicians pick the locks of the eighth-floor flat, hide bugs behind wallpaper and in the phone, put cameras in the light fittings and dust his clothes with radioactive powder. Hours later Gordievsky, just in from London, opens two locks and finds the third dead bolt locked. He never used it and never had the key, so he concludes the KGB has been inside.
- **Why it matters:** It is the scene that frames the whole book: from here the story goes back to explain how he came to be suspected and whether the escape plan must be triggered. Set anywhere else it would lose the point that the KGB was bugging its own officers' homes.
