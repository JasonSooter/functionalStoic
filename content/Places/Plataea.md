# Plataea

Ancient Boeotian city on the plain below Mount Kithairon, site of the decisive land battle of 479 BC. In _Gates of Fire_ it is where the Persian army is destroyed and the novel's Persian narrator is spared by speaking the names of the Spartans of Thermopylae.

## Gates of Fire — Steven Pressfield (1998)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/133742/gates-of-fire-by-steven-pressfield/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8afp4g49pvngpncc49y6gym)

> At last in the spring His Majesty’s land forces faced the Hellenes in battle upon that plain adjacent to the Greek city of Plataea, a day’s march northwest of Athens.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In the postscript, the Persian historian who took down Xeones's story describes the battle the following spring: 300,000 men of the Empire against 100,000 free Greeks, with the full Spartan army in a line of _lambdas_ "ten thousand across and eight deep", exactly as Xeones had foretold. Helots overrun the Persian palisade with orders to take no prisoners; with a blade at his throat the narrator cries out "Leonidas. Dienekes. Alexandros. Polynikes. Rooster," and the slaughter around him stops. Dekton, the Rooster, hears his account of Xeo's body sent to Phaleron and spares him, confirming that Xeo's ashes are already home in Sparta.
- **Why it matters:** It is the payoff of the whole frame: the defeat Thermopylae made possible, and the moment the dead Spartans' names save the man who recorded them. Set anywhere else, the circle from the Hot Gates back to the Spartans would not close.
