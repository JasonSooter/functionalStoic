# Campus Martius

Historic district of [[Rome]] along the Tiber. In _Happy City_ it is imperial Rome's crowded public quarter, where private glory crowded out the public good.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83dc55vr6tjfjn4zkmb8vx8)

> The Campus Martius, Rome’s public district, became a cluttered treasure box of spectacular imperial structures, almost all of them turned inward, with scant means to move between them.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Each emperor added bigger forums and monuments with no overall plan, while ordinary Romans lived on narrow streets in noisy tenements prone to collapse. The wealthy escaped to country villas.
- **Why it matters:** Rome is the counter-example to Athens: a city whose grandest spaces served the ambitions of its rulers rather than the shared life of its citizens.
