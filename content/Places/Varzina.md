# Varzina

River on the Kola Peninsula, Russia, flowing into the Barents Sea. Hugh Willoughby's ships wintered in the bay at its mouth in 1553, the grim end of England's first attempt on the Spice Islands told in _Nathaniel's Nutmeg_.

## Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Giles Milton (1999)

Source: [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Reader's Companion]] · [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1970050W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m82ef6cmdhk767sfdmvyrmm5)

> On 14 September, they again sighted land and shortly afterwards ‘sailed into a faire bay’ somewhere close to the present border between Finland and Russia.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** After a blundering course far inside the Arctic Circle, Willoughby's two ships sail into a "faire bay" in September 1553, which Milton places close to the present border between Finland and Russia. Cheered by seals, bears and deer, they mean to stay a week but decide to winter there; the ice, cold and isolation then destroy the expedition, and years later a search ship finds the two ships' rotting hulks, which had become charnel houses.
- **Why it matters:** It is England's maiden expedition to the Spice Islands and the book's opening example of how ill-prepared the early merchant adventurers were for the route they chose.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The book names no place beyond "a faire bay" near the Finnish–Russian border; Wikipedia's article on Hugh Willoughby says the ships wintered in a bay formed by the Varzina River on the Kola Peninsula coast, so the pin is the river mouth.
