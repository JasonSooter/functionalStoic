# Binondo

Old Chinese quarter and trading district of [[Manila]]. In _Bicycle Diaries_ it is where David Byrne finds karaoke on the street and markets of winding lanes and one-table stalls.

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m76rhv9gxrwwsp74mvy67q9c)

> Binondo is the area I end up biking to today. Karaoke machines are everywhere. Right on the street!

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Byrne bikes to Binondo, a "funky old city center" of winding streets and tiny stalls where even the smallest vendors have karaoke machines. Traffic slows to a crawl, so bikes and small trucks move better than cars, and the custom-painted jeepneys rule the road.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's best bike-level view of Manila street life, and it leads into Byrne's argument that crowded markets grow their own human-scale design, unlike planned malls.
