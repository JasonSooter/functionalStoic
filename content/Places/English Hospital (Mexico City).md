# English Hospital (Mexico City)

Mexico City's "English Hospital", the Hospital Inglés, now the ABC Medical Center. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is where Frida spends most of 1950 undergoing repeated spinal operations, with Diego sleeping in the next room.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)
Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m74ggmg56jbtzt6exp9j7z5f)

> During Frida’s year in the English Hospital, Rivera took a small room next to hers so that he could spend the night near his wife.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** By early 1950 Frida is so sick that she has to go into hospital in Mexico City, and she stays there a year. She goes back to the English Hospital and puts herself in the care of Dr. Juan Farill, who fuses her spine; infections and repeated operations follow. Her room is decorated with candy skulls, Matamoros candelabra, wax doves and the Russian flag, and visitors sign Stockholm Peace Congress petitions pinned to the wall. After six operations she paints four or five hours a day, working again on _My Family_. In May 1954 a diary entry has her rushed here again by ambulance, screaming, after a fall.
- **Why it matters:** It is the stage for the long medical ordeal of her last years, and the book shows her turning even this room into a political, painted world of her own.
- **Where is it?:** The book says only "the English Hospital". ⚠️ The Museo Frida Kahlo's photographs of Diego and Frida there name it the Hospital Inglés (ABC). ABC's history places its predecessor, the Sanatorio Cowdray, on Mariano Escobedo (today's Hotel Camino Real) from 1923, and the move to Avenida Observatorio in 1964, but doesn't say where the merged hospital worked in 1950. The pin uses the Cowdray site; the alternative is the old American Hospital in San Rafael.
