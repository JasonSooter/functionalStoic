# Woodward's Building

Redeveloped former department store on the edge of the Downtown Eastside, [[Vancouver]], Canada. In _Happy City_ it is Charles Montgomery's model of mixed-income housing built into one block.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84c816r0akc9nb4xc9xmgsn)

> Vancouver again provides inspiration, this time on the site of Woodward’s, an abandoned department store that marked the frontier between super-shiny Vancouverism and the grit of Canada’s poorest neighborhood.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 10. Activists, developers, the city and senior governments put a university, shops and three towers on the site: subsidized housing for families, rooms for very poor singles, and market condos in "the W". Those condos, sold under Bob Rennie's slogan "Be Bold or Move to Suburbia", sold out within a month. Architect Gregory Henriquez gave each tower its own lobby around a public atrium. Montgomery admits it sped up gentrification but says it locked in two hundred affordable homes.
- **Why it matters:** It is the chapter's example of fairness built into a single block, showing that rich and poor neighbours can share a site by design.
