# Gamla stan

Old town on a central island in [[Stockholm]], Sweden, where Bill Bryson's Neither Here Nor There first cracks his image of a rich, tidy Sweden.

## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74125W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5b443ekytxes8rrrww0wbyf)

> The area had an oddly Central European feel to it: narrow, hilly streets lined by severe, heavy buildings, the color of faded terra-cotta, sometimes with chunks of plaster missing, as if they had been struck a glancing blow by tank fire

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 12. Bryson's first walk in Stockholm takes him across the Strömbron Bridge into Gamla Stan. He finds "a kind of knocked-about charm" but none of the prosperity he expected: dirty windows, plaster missing, corners knocked off by trucks. At the end of the chapter he is back on the Strömbron, staring out over gray, rain-studded water, when he takes his decision: "I was going to Rome."
- **Why it matters:** Gamla stan is the first crack in his image of rich, tidy Sweden, a theme the Drottninggatan and [[Olof Palme assassination site|Sveavägen]] scenes develop. The bridge beside it is the book's hinge from Scandinavia to Italy.
