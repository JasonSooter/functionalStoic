# Sulukule

Old Romani neighbourhood by the land walls in Fatih, [[Istanbul]], since largely demolished for redevelopment. In David Byrne's _Bicycle Diaries_ it is the setting of a cold, raucous belly-dance night.

## At a glance

| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m76422k7w7c0w3rgvr3ys3fx)

> This gypsy neighborhood, a thousand years old, is almost all run-down houses and tea shops filled with people hanging out on the semipaved streets in the cold night air. Sadly, the whole neighborhood is threatened with demolition now, as it’s coveted by real-estate developers.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** With Turkish expatriates and a Kazakh banker, Byrne goes to a stone-cold house heated by a bucket of coals, with a four-piece band and dancers. He is moved by the music ("The sadness of the world is in this music"), and the night is interrupted by a TV host polling residents before the elections.
- **Why it matters:** He presents it as the polar opposite of the Sabancı mansion that afternoon, and its threatened demolition fits his theme of cities trading character for development.
