# Phaleron

Ancient harbour town of Athens on the Saronic Gulf, today's Palaio Faliro. In _Gates of Fire_ it is where Xeones finds his cousin Diomache in a sanctuary of the veiled Persephone, and where his body is sent at the end.

## Gates of Fire — Steven Pressfield (1998)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/133742/gates-of-fire-by-steven-pressfield/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8a5e77pm4gc9xqhn40kxtmd)

> She informed us the sanctuary was that of Demeter and Covert _Kore,_ Persephone of the Veil.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Sitting by the Skyllian spring at Thermopylae, Xeones tells Dienekes about the embassy to Athens. Carrying Arete's letter, he followed a boy guide into Phaleron port, "lit bright as noon" by torches as families fled to Troezen and Salamis. They tried the Griddle, a three-storey tenement over a slop shop and inn. Shouts from the windows sent them to a temple on "a surprisingly cheerful slope two streets above the water": a converted house behind olives that served as an asylum for wives "put by" by their husbands, and only women could enter. There he found Diomache, aged and cropped and serving as a novice, beside a bronze of the veiled Persephone. Weeks later, as the Persians burn Athens, the wounded Xeones keeps asking whether this sanctuary survived. When he dies, the Persian captain Orontes pays three Athenian toughs three gold darics to carry his body, wrapped in the scarlet cloak, to "that sanctuary known as Persephone of the Veil" and wait for the priestesses. At Plataea the Spartan Dekton confirms that a priestess of that temple brought Xeo's ashes home to Sparta.
- **Why it matters:** It closes the thread of Xeo's love for Diomache, which runs from the novel's opening. Her choice of the veiled goddess, who moves between death and life, echoes the spring at the Hot Gates where he tells the story, and his body's return to her temple gives the frame story its last act of honour.
