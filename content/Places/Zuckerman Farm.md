# Zuckerman Farm

Fictional farm in E. B. White's _Charlotte's Web_, pinned at its real-world model within [[Maine]]. Nearly all of the story takes place in and around Zuckerman's barn.

## Charlotte's Web — E. B. White (1952)
Source: [[Charlotte's Web]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL483391W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dr0ps63srz2ytrjxmm62m2)

> It was the kind of barn that children like to play in. And the whole thing was owned by Fern’s uncle, Mr. Homer L. Zuckerman.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Wilbur is sold to Fern's Uncle Homer and goes to live in the manure pile in the cellar of Zuckerman's barn, where he meets Charlotte, Templeton and the other animals. Over the summer and autumn Charlotte's webs make him famous, and people come from miles around to see him. Wilbur returns here from the Fair at the end and spends the winter and later seasons in the barn.
- **Why it matters:** Nearly all of the story happens in and around this barn. It is the home Wilbur fights to keep, and the cycle of seasons there frames the book.
- **Real vs. fictional:** fictional farm; real model

## Great Short Books — Kenneth C. Davis (2022)

Source: [[Great Short Books]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8f01wwvx6rfw9z52r6t3eqw)

> In 1938, the Whites moved to a saltwater farm in North Brooklin, Maine, they had purchased in 1933. It was there that he was inspired to write Charlotte’s Web (1952).

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Davis's summary: Wilbur, a pig rescued from slaughter by Fern, is sent to live in the nearby barn of her uncle, Homer Zuckerman, and "in the extraordinary universe of that barn" meets the sheep, a stuttering goose, other farm animals and the rat Templeton. In White's biography Davis says the Whites moved to their saltwater farm in North Brooklin in 1938, that White was inspired to write the book there, and that he died there in 1985.
- **Why it matters:** Davis links the barn's world to the real farm, which supports the pin on White's place; he does not say the barn is a portrait of White's own.
