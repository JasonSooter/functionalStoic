## Diane Ackerman on the Evolutionary and Existential Purpose of Deep Play — Maria Popova (2016)
Source: [Original](https://www.brainpickings.org/2016/08/04/diane-ackerman-deep-play/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jx88pxq5mtpe03ra6tkabz)

> I had a most unusual experience. Midway through my daily swim in the ocean, my peripheral vision was drawn to what at first appeared to be a snorkel.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** On a July research trip to Nantucket, the author swims in the ocean each day and is drawn toward a long-necked bird that lets her swim alongside it. The encounter becomes her example of a transcendent, present-moment experience that frames the Ackerman discussion of deep play.
- **Why it matters:** It is the opening scene that grounds the essay's abstract subject in a concrete place; the island itself is not otherwise discussed.

## Maria Mitchell and the Spider’s Web: A Touching Testament to Tenacity from America’s First Woman Astronomer — Maria Popova (2016)
Source: [Original](https://www.brainpickings.org/2016/08/01/maria-mitchell-diary-spider/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jx8bxbyg5e8v1n23j9ksft)

> grew up in a large Quaker family on the small island of Nantucket and fell in love with the cosmos as a young girl.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** Mitchell grew up on Nantucket in a Quaker family of ten children, working in a converted closet study. The diary entries about her instrument repairs are set in her New England winter life there.
- **Why it matters:** Nantucket is her formative home and the setting of the diaries, though the text gives few details of the island.

## Moby-Dick; or, The Whale — Herman Melville (1851)
Source: [[Exploration of Moby Dick]] · [[Moby Dick]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76s9dbxbx2qcq6kpnpbj7)

> Nantucket! Take out your map and look at it. See what a real corner of the world it occupies; how it stands there, away off shore, more lonely than the Eddystone lighthouse.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Ishmael insists on sailing in a Nantucket craft and praises the island as a mere 'elbow of sand' whose people took to the sea and now own two thirds of the globe. He and Queequeg lodge at the Try Pots inn, where Mrs. Hussey serves chowder and confiscates Queequeg's harpoon. At the wharf Ishmael signs on the Pequod with the owners Peleg and Bildad, Queequeg proves himself with his harpoon, Elijah warns them, and the ship sails on Christmas Day. Near the end, in 'The Symphony', Starbuck begs Ahab to turn back for Nantucket and his wife and child, and Ahab refuses.
- **Why it matters:** It is the home port that gives the voyage its owners, crew and creed, and it ends as the home Ahab will not return to. Nantucket's isolation sets up the whaleman's bond with the sea that the book argues for.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The Try Pots inn, run by Mrs. Hussey, is invented and is folded into this note.
