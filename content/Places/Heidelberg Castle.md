# Heidelberg Castle

Ruined castle above the Neckar in [[Heidelberg]], Baden-Württemberg. In _German: How to Speak and Write It_ a full-page photograph of the town and its castle introduces Germany's foremost university town.

## German: How to Speak and Write It — Joseph Rosenberg (1962)
Source: [[German - How to Speak and Write It]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kn8s67hvf9rgeqeway3f23jn)

> Germany’s foremost university town stands on the Neckar near its confluence with the Rhine. For nearly six hundred years it has enjoyed a world-wide reputation as a seat of learning, and it still retains many old buildings which preserve the medieval appearance of the town. The ruined castle is one of the chief monuments of Germany.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In the front matter, a plate captioned "Heidelberg and its 13th-century castle" shows the town on the Neckar near the Rhine, praised for nearly six hundred years as a seat of learning and for its preserved medieval buildings, with the ruined castle named as one of Germany's chief monuments.
- **Why it matters:** One of the book's illustrations of "characteristic aspects" of German life, it stands for German learning and history before the lessons begin. It is a picture caption, not a scene, so its weight is modest.
