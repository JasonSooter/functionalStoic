# The exiles' village (Last of the Breed)

A hidden settlement of dugouts and log huts in the Siberian forest, home to 29 exiles, where Joe Mack meets Natalya and her father in _Last of the Breed_; pinned at [[Aldan]] as a best guess.

## Last of the Breed — Louis L'Amour (1986)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL51048W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6xdshrpjhsgr6bdatea0xgs)

> The village, when they came to it, was a mere cluster of huts in the deep forest.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Following blazed trees, Joe Mack reaches a shelter at the end of an icy gorge, "an old stable that was fixed up as a place to sleep for workmen," where Natalya (Talya) is holding a pistol on Peshkov; she feeds him bear meat and coffee. Her father, Stephan Baronas, a Lithuanian professor and former guerrilla, leads him up a trail to the village of mostly log-faced dugouts, hidden by trees, whose 29 people hunt, trap and grow crops far from the huts. Nearby Joe Mack finds a cave behind aspen and larch, reached along a rock shelf, and makes it his winter base.
- **Why it matters:** The village ends his solitary flight: it gives him allies, a reason to stay through the winter, and Natalya, while its fur deal with Wulff ties it to the hunt.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Fictional and unnamed.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text gives only loose clues: a narrow windy valley with evergreens and aspen, a river nearby, flora like Manchuria's, and Baronas's remark that Wulff is "miles from here, in Aldan." No reference work names a model. Pinned at Aldan as the one named town the exiles deal with; the alternative is anywhere in the forests south of the Stanovoy Range, where Joe Mack had turned south to winter.
