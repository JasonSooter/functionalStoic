# Lake Ozark

Small resort town in the Ozarks of central Missouri. In _Stoner_ it is where William Stoner and Katherine Driscoll spend the only ten days their affair ever has together.

## Stoner — John Williams (1965)
Source: [Original](https://www.nyrb.com/products/stoner) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8b4949b45932ft3aw5h7szg)

> At different hours they took separate buses, and met at Lake Ozark, a resort village in the outlying mountains of the great Ozark range.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** While Edith takes Grace to her mother's in St. Louis for Christmas, Stoner says he will be working in Kansas City and Katherine that she is visiting relatives in the East. They take separate buses to Lake Ozark, where they are the only guests of the only lodge open all year, in a cabin overlooking a frozen lake. For ten days they walk in the snowy pines, watch a doe come down from the hills, drink coffee in the lodge office that doubles as the village store and restaurant, and talk by the fire. Katherine says, "We will have had this week," and on the last morning she wedges her wedding band in a crevice by the fireplace before they take the bus back to Columbia.
- **Why it matters:** It is the one place outside Columbia where the lovers can live openly, and the novel's single stretch of untroubled happiness for Stoner. The ring left behind marks it as a moment they know will not come again; soon after their return, the affair is exposed and ends.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The town is real; the lodge and cabin are unnamed.
