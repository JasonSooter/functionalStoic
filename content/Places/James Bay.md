# James Bay

Southern arm of Hudson Bay, between Ontario and Quebec. In _Nathaniel's Nutmeg_ Henry Hudson winters here in 1610–11 before his crew mutinies and casts him adrift.

## Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Giles Milton (1999)

Source: [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Reader's Companion]] · [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1970050W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8efcpzq2ypg0fsbxaj485ef)

> But as the first snows began to fall and the men were forced to winter in desolate James Bay, their enthusiasm plummeted and a handful of conspirators began to whisper of mutiny.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** On his last voyage Hudson passes through John Davis's "furious overfall", later Hudson Strait, into the great bay and believes he has "won the passage" to the Spice Islands. Forced to winter in James Bay, the crew turns: Hudson is "seized by two men, pinioned with a rope, and cast into the ship’s shallop" with seven supporters, "without food, drink, fire, clothing or other necessaries". With him dies the hope of a North-West Passage to the spices.
- **Why it matters:** It ends Hudson's story and the dream of a northern short-cut, leaving the spice race to be fought in the East.
