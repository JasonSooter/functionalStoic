# Midtown Atlanta

Walkable district north of downtown [[Atlanta]], built on a pre-war streetcar grid. In _Happy City_ it is the healthy half of a comparison with the cul-de-sac suburb of Mableton.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m841xq9tpnhww6krq36c0evw)

> Frank found that a white male living in Midtown, a lively district near Atlanta’s downtown, was likely to weigh ten pounds less than his identical twin living out in a place like, say, Mableton, in the cul-de-sac archipelago that surrounds Atlanta, simply because the Midtowner would be twice as likely to get enough exercise every day.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Lawrence Frank's research compares Midtown, where shops, bars and transit lie a few blocks apart on an old streetcar grid, with Mableton, a suburb of big lots, wide winding roads and distant shopping plazas. The Midtowner is twice as likely to get enough daily exercise and is likely ten pounds lighter than an identical twin out in the cul-de-sacs.
- **Why it matters:** It turns the book's argument that urban form shapes how much we move into a measurable health difference.
