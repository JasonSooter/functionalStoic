# Molesey Lock

Lock on the Thames by Hampton Court ([[England]]). In Three Men in a Boat (Jerome K. Jerome) it is the busiest, most colourful lock on the river.

## At a glance
| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Three Men in a Boat | Jerome K. Jerome | setting | On a fine Sunday the lock is a rainbow tangle of blazers, parasols and boats. |

## Three Men in a Boat — Jerome K. Jerome (1889)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1793164W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5gbydb01bd4cykaerphns7v)

> I don’t think I ever remember to have seen Moulsey lock, before, with only one boat in it.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The narrator describes Moulsey lock on a fine Sunday as a blaze of blazers, parasols and boats, the busiest lock on the river, with all of Hampton and Moulsey dressed for boating.
- **Why it matters:** A vivid picture of Victorian boating crowds.
