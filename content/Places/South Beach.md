## Walkable City — Jeff Speck (2012)
Source: [[Walkable City]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL36883695M/Walkable_City) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbjhey3n82q8br9hrgn818)

> I lived comfortably for a full decade in one of the most extreme exceptions to that rule, Miami’s South Beach, where I could easily go for a month at a time without a stroller sighting.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Speck lived for ten years in South Beach, where he saw almost no children and no adults aged thirty-five to fifty-five, yet found it a great place physically, socially and economically. Later he says his apartment was in the art deco district while his job was on the mainland in Little Havana, a twenty-minute drive, and the Walk Score for South Beach is given as 92.
- **Why it matters:** South Beach is his example of a childless, adults-only walkable district as the demographic future of cities, and his own pre-Washington car-dependent life. It is mostly an illustrative backdrop rather than a scene.
