# Rockefeller Center

Complex of office towers and plazas in Midtown [[Manhattan]]. In _Happy City_ its owners ask how to keep people off their plaza and are talked into inviting them instead.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m841xfbye5td1j52ygkm5tpw)

> Early on, Project for Public Spaces was asked by the owners of Rockefeller Center to suggest how spikes might be configured to keep people from sitting under or touching the yew trees on their plaza.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Plaza management saw people as a problem. Fred Kent, William Whyte's onetime research assistant, politely suggested benches rather than spikes; the owners retrofitted the plazas to accommodate people, the start of a transformation into one of the most visited sites in New York. The story introduces Whyte's method of "triangulation", arranging things so strangers are nudged close enough to talk.
- **Why it matters:** It shows the Whyte school repairing a hostile space by welcoming people, the American counterpart to Gehl's Copenhagen.
