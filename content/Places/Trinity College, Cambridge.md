# Trinity College, Cambridge

Isaac Newton's college at the University of [[Cambridge]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Edmond Halley's 1684 visit there sets off the writing of the Principia.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80ghjsxjvm1r5qba5vw033j)

> Quite what Halley expected to get from him when he made his unannounced visit in August 1684 we can only guess.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Halley calls on Newton at Cambridge and asks what curve a planet would follow under an inverse-square force. Newton answers at once, an ellipse, and says he has calculated it but cannot find the papers; Halley persuades him to redo the work, which becomes the Principia. The text names Trinity as Newton's college in an aside, and describes Newton's alchemy and his habit of not remembering to get up.
- **Why it matters:** It is the turning point that gets universal gravitation written down. ⚠️ The text places the meeting "at Cambridge"; that it happened in Newton's rooms at Trinity is inferred from the aside naming his college.
