# Lake Mungo

Dry lakebed in western New South Wales, [[Australia]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Jim Bowler finds ancient human remains here that upset the tidy story of human dispersal.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83337f3fefrskjg8680a0sk)

> In 1968, a geologist named Jim Bowler was poking around on a long-dried lakebed called Mungo in a parched and lonely corner of western New South Wales when something very unexpected caught his eye.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In 1968 the geologist Jim Bowler spotted human bones sticking out of the dried lakebed. Because Australia has always been an island, the Mungo people must have crossed open sea to reach it, and then found their way more than two thousand miles inland from the presumed point of entry on the north coast.
- **Why it matters:** Evidence that early people were far more mobile, and earlier, than the standard account allows.
