# Grimsby

Port town on the Humber estuary in Lincolnshire, England. In _Cod_ it and neighbouring Hull become the great steam-trawling ports.

## At a glance

| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|

## Cod: A Biography of the Fish That Changed the World — Mark Kurlansky (1997)

Source: [[Cod]] · [Original](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/1032753/cod) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kksp30xx5eker3gf89c1vz)

> These cod grounds turned the twin ports of Hull and Grimsby on the Humber River, traditional fishing towns, into major ports.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** After the Silver Pits ground near Dogger Bank was found in 1837, the twin Humber ports of Hull and Grimsby boomed; a Hull yard built the steam trawler Zodiac in 1881. Their trawlers later fished Iceland and supplied cod-liver oil, and the ports declined after Iceland's Cod War victories.
- **Why it matters:** Birthplace of steam trawling and a loser of the Icelandic limits.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real; the text treats Hull and Grimsby together, and the pin is Grimsby.
