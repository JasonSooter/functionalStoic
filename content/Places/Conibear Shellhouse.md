# Conibear Shellhouse

The University of Washington's rowing boathouse on Union Bay, built in 1949, at the [[University of Washington]] in Seattle. In _The Boys in the Boat_ the gold-medal shell Husky Clipper hangs from its dining-commons ceiling today.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m87k3rnreenb1aj66jwkbtdv)

> Today she is in Washington’s Conibear Shellhouse, a spacious open building built in 1949 and recently renovated.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In the epilogue the surviving crew lines up at the Conibear Shellhouse for photographs before their fortieth-anniversary row in 1976. On a winter day in 2008, after Joe's death, a small group gathers near it to plant a new oak, and Joe's daughter Judy places nine shovelfuls of soil at its base, one for each boy. The book leaves the Husky Clipper here: she hangs in the dining commons with Lake Washington behind a wall of glass, and each October the freshman coach points up at her and tells the story.
- **Why it matters:** It is where the boat and the legend live on, passing the crew's story to each new generation of Washington rowers.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Not the 1930s boathouse where the crew trained; see [[ASUW Shell House]].
