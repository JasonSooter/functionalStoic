# Ninth Avenue

North–south avenue on the West Side of [[Manhattan]]. In _Bicycle Diaries_ its new protected bike lane is David Byrne's proof that safe design changes how riding feels.

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m772xft69jwvb50611kt9b23)

> When I head downtown on the new Ninth Avenue bike lanes, as I do fairly often, I notice the difference. I instantly feel as if a weight has been lifted.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Byrne contrasts painted lanes that drivers swerve into with the new lanes on Ninth Avenue, Broadway and Grand Street, protected by a curb or by a lane of parked cars. On Ninth he no longer feels paranoid, his "adrenalized state" almost dissipates, and he moves faster without jockeying around double-parked cars and cabs.
- **Why it matters:** It is the epilogue's concrete case, echoing Peñalosa's test that a bike lane must be safe for an eight-year-old.
