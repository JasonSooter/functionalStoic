# South Pacific Gyre

Vast circulating current in the South Pacific, one of the most lifeless stretches of ocean. A Nautilus piece uses its seabed sediments to show microbes surviving for ages.

## What Boredom Does to You — Manoush Zomorodi (2017)
Source: [Original](http://nautil.us/issue/53/monsters/what-boredom-does-to-you) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k7dg8sx79d11zphv1mf28m)

> Among the habitats that Bradley studies is the South Pacific Gyre. The crew of the ship JOIDES Resolution drilled a long vertical core from the gyre, wearing masks to protect themselves from the hydrogen sulphide gas the core emits.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** A ship crew drills a long vertical core from the gyre's seabed sediment, wearing masks against the hydrogen sulphide it emits. Bradley uses the core to show a journey from modern to ancient environments, with sediment dating upward of 95 million years and living microbes.
- **Why it matters:** The gyre's sediments are the marine analogue to the salt crystals as habitats for long-dormant microbes.
