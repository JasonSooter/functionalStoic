# Arthur Dent's house

Arthur Dent's house on the edge of an unnamed West Country village, pinned at a best guess in Somerset. It is where _The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy_ opens, with the bulldozers, the bypass and the pub down the lane.

## The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy — Douglas Adams (1979)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2163649W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne281gx6dk7g80630j465s)

> The house stood on a slight rise just on the edge of the village. It stood on its own and looked out over a broad spread of West Country farmland.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 1 opens on the Thursday morning the council is to knock the house down for a bypass. Arthur lies in the mud of the lane in front of the bulldozers until Ford Prefect talks Mr Prosser into taking his place and drags Arthur to the Horse and Groom, the village pub, where he orders six pints because 'the world's about to end'. Arthur runs back to find the house being demolished just as the Vogon fleet arrives.
- **Why it matters:** The house's demolition for a bypass is the small-scale rehearsal for Earth's demolition for a hyperspace bypass minutes later; the gag only works because the place is so ordinary and English.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The house, the lane and the Horse and Groom pub are fictional; the village is never named in the novel.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text says only that the house 'looked out over a broad spread of West Country farmland', that Arthur moved there from London about three years earlier, and that 'the pub in the village' is the Horse and Groom. No reference work found names a real model village. Pinned at Somerset's representative point as the heart of the West Country; Dorset, Devon or Wiltshire would fit the text equally well.
