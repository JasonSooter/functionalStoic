# Reeperbahn

Nightlife street of St. Pauli in [[Hamburg]], with the side street Grosse Freiheit, which Bill Bryson finds disappointingly tame in Neither Here Nor There.

## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74125W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5b43qwdhkp75qr45fw2b1fs)

> I walked a few hundred yards inland and uphill to the Reeperbahn, that famed mile-long avenue of sin. It looked disappointingly unlusty.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 9. Bryson walks up from the docks and finds the famous street underwhelming in daylight, comparing it with Las Vegas seen "over a cup of coffee and a doughnut". The seedier attractions are on side streets like Grosse Freiheit, where he walks as far as the Kaiserkeller at number 36, "where the Beatles used to play", past live-sex-show photographs.
- **Why it matters:** It deflates Hamburg's reputation. The comedy only works because the Reeperbahn is so famous, and because the rest of the chapter goes on to find Hamburg rich, green and enviable (see [[Außenalster]]).
