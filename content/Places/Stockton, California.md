# Stockton, California

City in the Central Valley of [[California]]. In _Happy City_ it is the foreclosure capital where Charles Montgomery tours repossessed homes; in Jeff Atwood's essay it is the site of a guaranteed-income pilot.

## The Road Not Taken is Guaranteed Minimum Income — Jeff Atwood (2025)
Source: [[The Road Not Taken Is Guaranteed Minimum Income]] · [Original](https://blog.codinghorror.com/the-road-not-taken-is-guaranteed-minimum-income/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ebqcxh29p7j04jsskvdayc)

> In 2019, directly inspired by King, mayor Michael Tubbs – at age 26, one of the youngest mayors in American history – launched the $3 million Stockton Economic Empowerment Demonstration in 2019.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the article:** Stockton was the site of a guaranteed-income pilot giving 125 residents $500 a month for two years. Recipients showed better financial stability and more full-time work.
- **Why it matters:** It supplies the main American precedent for the argument. It is evidence rather than a scene.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83dcd48gcerqavjdncp2b6y)

> In 2007, more people had lost their homes in Stockton, the county seat, than anywhere else in the nation except Detroit.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 3 opens in a Stockton parking lot, where realtor Cesar Dias runs a minibus "Repo Home Tour" of foreclosed houses. The tour leaves the desiccated downtown along Interstate 5 for subdivisions of artificial lakes and golf courses, and files through bank-owned homes such as an "executive home" with a three-car garage in Spanos Park East, its lawn left brown for months.
- **Why it matters:** Stockton is the book's entry into the dispersed city, the sprawling American dream shown at the moment it proved economically and socially fragile.
