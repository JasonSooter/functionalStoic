# Hazelbrook

Village in the Blue Mountains of New South Wales, west of Sydney, in [[Australia]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is home to the Reverend Robert Evans, who hunts supernovae from his back deck.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8066thn8zhedjq3wpyy5q94)

> when I visited him and his wife, Elaine, in their picture-book bungalow on a tranquil edge of the village of Hazelbrook, out where Sydney finally ends and the boundless Australian bush begins.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 3 opens with Evans, a quiet minister in the Blue Mountains "about fifty miles west of Sydney," who has an uncanny memory for star fields. Bryson visits his bungalow and finds no observatory: the telescope, the size of a hot-water tank, lives in a storeroom off the kitchen and is carried to a small deck with a narrow view between the roof and the eucalyptus tops.
- **Why it matters:** The modest setting is the point: one amateur on a suburban deck finds dying stars that professional observatories miss, which leads Bryson into supernovae and the scale of the universe.
