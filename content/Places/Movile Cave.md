# Movile Cave

Sealed cave near Mangalia on the Black Sea coast of [[Romania]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is the unnamed Romanian cave whose blind, colourless creatures show how whole ecosystems can go unnoticed.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> In the 1980s, spelunkers entered a deep cave in Romania that had been sealed off from the outside world for a long but unknown period and found thirty-three species of insects and other small creatures

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The cave's spiders, centipedes and lice were all blind, colourless and new to science. They lived off microbes in the scum on the cave's pools, which in turn fed on hydrogen sulfide from hot springs. Bryson offers it as proof that even a far bigger survey effort would still miss whole ecosystems.
- **Why it matters:** It closes his argument about how much life remains undiscovered with a world that had been sealed off entirely.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The book doesn't name the cave. Reference works identify a sealed Romanian cave discovered in 1986 with a chemosynthetic, hydrogen-sulfide-based ecosystem as Movile Cave, near Mangalia, which matches every detail Bryson gives.
