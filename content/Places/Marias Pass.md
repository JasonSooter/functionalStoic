# Marias Pass

Low pass over the Continental Divide at the southern edge of Glacier National Park, Montana. In Ryback's memoir it marks the end of the snowbound Glacier stretch.

## The Ultimate Journey - Canada to Mexico Down the Continental Divide — Eric Ryback (1973)
Source: [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8berm3j65t92tj2hgn9g450)

> Marias Pass had been the turning point, and I felt sure now that it would be all downhill to Lincoln.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** The brothers cross Marias Pass to a clearing sky, gentle mountains and a clear, dry trail through the Lewis and Clark Forest, and burst into the forest "blissful and triumphant". That evening Eric reckons they have walked 24 miles that day and over 120 in five days. He is sure it will now be "all downhill to Lincoln", though thunderstorms hit their camp that night.
- **Why it matters:** It closes the Glacier ordeal: the turning point after which the trek becomes a matter of distance rather than survival, at least until the next snow.
