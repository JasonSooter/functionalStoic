# Taung

Town on the edge of the Kalahari in [[South Africa]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ a quarry here yields the Taung child, the first australopithecine.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8332v38g0ss3a0z7y5xq5dy)

> from a limestone quarry on the edge of the Kalahari Desert at a dusty spot called Taung.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In late 1924 Raymond Dart, head of anatomy at the University of the Witwatersrand, was sent a small, remarkably complete child's skull with an endocast from a limestone quarry at Taung. He named it Australopithecus africanus and called it amazingly human, but the authorities dismissed him, and for decades the skull, now one of anthropology's supreme treasures, sat as a paperweight on a colleague's desk.
- **Why it matters:** Bryson's lead example of how egos and orthodoxy can bury a major find in paleoanthropology.
