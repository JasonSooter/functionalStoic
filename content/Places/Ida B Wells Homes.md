# Ida B. Wells Homes

Former public housing project on [[Chicago]]'s South Side, demolished in the 2000s. In _Happy City_ its courtyards are the setting of Frances Kuo's studies showing that greenery makes neighbours friendlier and safer.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83qj4rdqkkeym49z62p3byv)

> In the mid-1990s environmental psychologists Frances Ming Kuo and William Sullivan took a stroll around the Ida B. Wells project, a low-rise social housing complex in Chicago.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Kuo and Sullivan notice that the project's bare concrete courtyards are always empty while the green ones, however ragged, are full of social life. Their studies find residents with green views report less fatigue and aggression, and buildings facing trees and grass have about half the violent crime of those facing barren courtyards.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's key evidence that nature fosters neighbourliness and safety, so dense housing must make room for it.
