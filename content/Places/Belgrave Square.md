# Belgrave Square

Belgrave Square is a large garden square in Belgravia, west London, laid out in the 1820s with grand terraced houses. In _The Importance of Being Earnest_ Jack's house there is weighed by Lady Bracknell as a suitor's qualification.

## The Importance of Being Earnest — Oscar Wilde (1895)

Source: [[The Importance of Being Earnest]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL27776452W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76a9r0szay8ms16nanvz1)

> Well, I own a house in Belgrave Square, but it is let by the year to Lady Bloxham. Of course, I can get it back whenever I like, at six months’ notice.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Asked if he has a town house, Jack says he owns one in Belgrave Square, let to Lady Bloxham. When he gives the number, 149, Lady Bracknell shakes her head: 'The unfashionable side'. In Act Three Jack gives the same number as the address of Cecily's grandfather, the late Thomas Cardew.
- **Why it matters:** A status marker in Lady Bracknell's vetting, and a joke that even the best square has a wrong side. No scene takes place there.
