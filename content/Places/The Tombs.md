# The Tombs

Popular name of New York City's jail in Lower Manhattan, first the Egyptian Revival Halls of Justice of 1838. In Melville's _Bartleby, the Scrivener_ it is where Bartleby is jailed as a vagrant and dies.

## Bartleby, the Scrivener: A Story of Wall-Street — Herman Melville (1853)

Source: [[Bartleby, the Scrivener - A Story of Wall-Street]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102732W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kfr7k3krvpvwzq8tx7p8h1)

> The same day I received the note I went to the Tombs, or to speak more properly, the Halls of Justice.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Removed from the building as a vagrant, Bartleby is taken to the Tombs, which the narrator calls "more properly, the Halls of Justice". The narrator visits and finds him in the enclosed grass-plotted yard facing the high wall, refusing to speak and refusing the dinners of the grub-man, Mr. Cutlets. On a later visit he finds Bartleby dead, curled at the foot of the wall.
- **Why it matters:** It is the scene of Bartleby's death and the story's end; its massive Egyptian walls echo the dead walls outside his office window.
