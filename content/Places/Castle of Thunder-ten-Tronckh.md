# Castle of Thunder-ten-Tronckh

An invented baronial castle that Voltaire places in Westphalia, in north-western Germany. It is where _Candide_ opens, and the home from which the young hero is kicked out.

## Candide — Voltaire (1759)
Source: [[Candide]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL100672W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m7560y99zxpnmy40dk1tsp)

> In a castle of Westphalia, belonging to the Baron of Thunder-ten-Tronckh, lived a youth, whom nature had endowed with the most gentle manners.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Candide grows up here under Pangloss, who teaches that it is the most magnificent of castles in the best of worlds. After Cunegonde kisses him behind a screen, the Baron drives him out with kicks, and he wanders to the neighbouring town of Waldberghofftrarbk-dikdorff, where Bulgarian recruiters trap him. Pangloss later reports that Bulgarian soldiers destroyed the castle and killed the family.
- **Why it matters:** It is the lost paradise against which Candide measures everywhere else: El Dorado is 'better than Westphalia', and Paris reminds him of 'the dirtiest village of Westphalia'. Voltaire's joke is that this boastful castle is in fact a provincial backwater.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The castle is invented; the text places it only in a region: "In a castle of Westphalia", and Candide calls himself "from the dirty province of Westphalia". The Baron was "one of the most powerful lords in Westphalia, for his castle had not only a gate, but windows". Wikipedia's article on _Candide_ likewise says only that the tale begins in the Baron's castle in Westphalia; I found no reference work naming a real model. Pinned at the representative point of the Westphalia region; the castle could be anywhere in it.
