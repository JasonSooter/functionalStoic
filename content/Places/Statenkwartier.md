# Statenkwartier

Well-to-do district of [[The Hague]] near the dunes and Scheveningen. In _The Son and Heir_ it is the young Alexander Münninghoff's territory: the war-scarred streets he roams alone after his parents split, and later his father's home.

## The Son and Heir: A Memoir — Alexander Münninghoff (2020)
Source: [Original](https://www.letterenfonds.nl/en/books/heir-and-successor) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m85zmfvdxh3r5ej5qbcmchng)

> So I became a boy of the streets, roaming the wilderness left behind in the giant ditches that the Nazis had plowed through the Statenkwartier at the end of the war as part of the Atlantic Wall project.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Living with his mother in a dark attic room at Mrs. Roovers's guesthouse in 1950, the six-year-old builds a rubble "headquarters" in the antitank ditches and wanders past the shops on Frederik Hendriklaan, realising there is nothing in them he or his mother could buy. His grandfather's detectives photograph him "in an antitank ditch, looking rather unkempt", and the picture is used in the custody case against Wera. After his abduction in 1951 he lives with his father and Mimousse in an upstairs flat on Boreelstraat, "one of the minor streets in the wealthy Statenkwartier", and while they go dancing he is sent down to eat alone at Chai Yen, the Chinese restaurant on the corner.
- **Why it matters:** The district frames both halves of the custody fight: the ditches stand for the freedom and neglect of life with his mother, the Boreelstraat flat for the comfortable, lonely life his grandfather arranges with his father. The same streets carry both, which is the book's point about a boy owned by the family rather than raised by it.
