# General Motors Building

Office tower on Fifth Avenue at Grand Army Plaza in Midtown [[Manhattan]], fronted by a public plaza. In _Happy City_ its plaza goes from a deliberately unwelcoming sunken pit to one of the city's livelier lunchtime spots.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m841xdr8bwzrq7v17fb9f344)

> The once-desolate General Motors Building plaza, for example, has been reinvigorated with a blend of public comforts and commercial panache.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** General Motors got seven extra stories in 1968 for building a plaza, but the architect sank it below the street and capped its ledges with railings that, as William Whyte noted, caught sitters in the small of the back. Under planning director Amanda Burden it was remade with movable chairs under honey locusts, a shallow pool and a house-size glass cube leading down to an Apple Store; on a September afternoon the author watches children, napping businessmen and lunching couples fill it.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's before-and-after proof that design can turn a misanthropic plaza into a social space by slowing people down and drawing them together.
