# Lilongwe

Capital of Malawi, in the Central Region. In _The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind_ it is first the distant city of tobacco auctions and the electricity office, and later where William Kamkwamba goes to school and finds mentors.

## The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind — William Kamkwamba and Bryan Mealer (2009)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/314792/the-boy-who-harnessed-the-wind-by-william-kamkwamba-and-bryan-mealer/9780593866894) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8dps5thk2r5407ax10ba6bx)

> His tobacco fetched a high price each year at the Auction Holdings Limited in Lilongwe, where the farmers sold their hundred-kilogram bales on the auction floor.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Uncle John's burley tobacco sells well at the Lilongwe auction floors. Under Muluzi, mass-produced tobacco floods them and prices fall, and after John's death Jeremiah spends the profits "in the bars of Lilongwe and Kasungu". To get electricity, William explains, a family must take a pickup to [[Kasungu]] and a minibus a hundred kilometres to the capital to apply at the ESCOM office in Magetsi House. After TED the city becomes his: "Back in Lilongwe, Tom and I visited the offices of Baobab Health, located on the grounds of Kamuzu Central Hospital", where Mike McKay shows him a windmill with a treadmill-motor generator, and they buy deep-cycle batteries and lamps at Solair to rewire his compound. He studies at [[African Bible College Christian Academy]] and lodges with Baobab's founder Gerry Douglas, where power cuts leave him "sitting in the dark in my success". In the afterword he runs an airport shuttle here and plans an innovation hub.
- **Why it matters:** At first Lilongwe stands for the far-off markets and bureaucracy that decide what a farm family earns and whether it gets power, the gap the windmill closes. Later it is the first city William lives in, where the people who spread his story become his teachers.
