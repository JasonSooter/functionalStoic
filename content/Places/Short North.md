# Short North

## Walkable City — Jeff Speck (2012)
Source: [[Walkable City]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL36883695M/Walkable_City) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbp744naaxwkf17h08ahw7)

> This was precisely the scenario in Columbus, Ohio, where the city’s convention center and arena were cut off from the gritty Short North neighborhood by a below-grade interstate highway, reamed through in the sixties.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** A barren bridge over the sunken interstate separated the convention center and arena from the Short North. In 2003 the city rebuilt it as a 200-foot-wide bridge with two retail pads, giving a 'modern-day Ponte Vecchio' of shops and restaurants. For $1.9 million it made the highway disappear and joined two walkable districts.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's central example of stitching two anchors together. Without the cap, the key idea of linking severed walkable districts loses its concrete proof.
