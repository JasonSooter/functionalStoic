## Evicted by Matthew Desmond review – what if the problem of poverty is that it’s profitable to other people? — Katha Pollitt (2016)

Source: [Original](http://www.theguardian.com/books/2016/apr/07/evicted-poverty-and-profit-in-the-american-city-matthew-desmond-review) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjryj6fs2dc8hnqx2hvjbc)

> In Milwaukee, one of the most segregated cities in the US, all black people suffer from housing discrimination and all white people benefit at least a little from the racial dividend

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The review describes Evicted as an ethnographic study of tenants in low-income housing in Milwaukee. It follows eight families, trailer-park residents and black neighbourhood renters, and argues the city's segregation shapes who gets worse housing and who is evicted.
- **Why it matters:** Milwaukee is the single city through which the book examines how poverty is profitable and how eviction works; the argument is built on it as a case study.

## Why Aren't Parks Open at Night? — Rachel Quednau (2016)

Source: [Original](https://www.strongtowns.org/journal/2016/7/29/why-arent-parks-open-at-night) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jx87bc0gsvjd7e3h9ngkmm)

> It was a beautiful summer evening in Milwaukee and some friends and I decided to meet up at our favorite park to toss our light-up frisbee.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** The author and friends play frisbee after 9:30pm while watching for police, since parks close at 10pm. This anecdote launches the argument that neighbourhood parks should stay open at night.
- **Why it matters:** Its exact identity is unknown but the anecdote grounds the argument.
