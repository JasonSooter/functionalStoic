# Geyer-Werke

Film-processing and editing works on Harzer Straße in Berlin-Neukölln. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is where Leni Riefenstahl edits _Triumph of the Will_ through the autumn of 1934.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86m60a5xf8naspgn6c9k65v)

> a world away at the lavish Geyer-Werke film studios on Harzer Strasse in Berlin, Leni Riefenstahl was peering, day and night, with weary but zealous eyes, through the double magnifying lenses of her small Lytax film-editing machine.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Through November 1934, while Pocock builds shells in Seattle, Riefenstahl sits in a white smock at her editing table for up to sixteen hours a day, surrounded by filmstrips hanging before backlit glass walls, cutting the four hundred thousand feet of footage shot at that September's Nuremberg Rally. The book describes the rally through her film and looks ahead to her work on the 1936 Olympics.
- **Why it matters:** The editing room shows the craft of Nazi spectacle being built at the same moment as the crew's craft, setting up the Berlin Games as both sport and propaganda.
