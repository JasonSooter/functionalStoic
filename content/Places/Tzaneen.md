# Tzaneen

Town in Limpopo, South Africa, in what was the Tsonga homeland of Gazankulu. In Born a Crime it is home to Trevor Noah's stepfather Abel's family, and the place where his mother refuses to play the obedient wife.

## Born a Crime — Trevor Noah (2016)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17824318W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6zbjr46h4jkg8bph1cewyy5)

> They lived in Tzaneen, a town in Gazankulu, what had been the Tsonga homeland under apartheid. Tzaneen has a tropical climate, hot and humid.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 18 ("My Mother's Life"). After Andrew's birth the family spend the Christmas holidays with Abel's mother and sisters. Noah contrasts the white farms nearby, which grow fruit for export to Europe, with the overfarmed black land twenty minutes away. In the strongly patriarchal household the nine-year-old Trevor is not allowed to do chores, and women must bow when greeting men. His mother refuses to fit in: she exaggerates the bowing to mock it and talks to the men as an equal.
- **Why it matters:** the visit shows what Abel expects of a wife, and that Trevor's mother will not accept it. That clash runs through the rest of their marriage.
