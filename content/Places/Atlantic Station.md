# Atlantic Station

Mixed-use district on a former steel-mill site in Midtown [[Atlanta]]. In _Happy City_ it is the proof that when Atlanta builds differently, people drive less.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m841xsgm2h7bvmz2mdp2naes)

> Proof sits on the edge of a tangled freeway interchange three miles north of the city center, where the 138-acre site of a former steel mill has been redeveloped into a dense mix of offices, apartments, retail stores, small parks, and theaters.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** On the edge of a freeway interchange three miles north of the centre, the old mill site now holds offices, apartments, shops, small parks and theatres. Even though much of it sits on a three-level parking garage, people who moved there since 2005 have cut their driving by a third, walking instead because their destinations now lie within a pleasant stroll.
- **Why it matters:** After the book's grim Atlanta cases, it shows behaviour changing quickly once the city's form does.
