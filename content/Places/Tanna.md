## Life on Air [revised & updated 2nd edition] — David Attenborough (2002)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL24547776M/Life_on_air) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5hx3m9w2jy8a704v7bbfxpa)

> Many of the cults personified the supernatural being who would ultimately bring this cargo. In Tanna, his name was John Frum.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Attenborough explains cargo cults and how the John Frum movement arose on Tanna from 1940, with its cleared airstrip, cargo sheds, repression by the colonial government and the 1950s 'army' in red singlets. The party spends several days driving round the island with Australian planter Bob Paul, meeting cult members.
- **Why it matters:** The island is the subject of the chapter's quest: the place where a modern religion of cargo and apocalypse is being lived, and the reason for the trip's detour.
