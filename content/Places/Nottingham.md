# Nottingham

City in the East Midlands of England, long home to the hub-gear maker Sturmey-Archer. In 2000 Andrew Ritchie drove to its closing factory at midnight to buy the hubs that kept Brompton alive.

## The Brompton: Engineering for Change — Will Butler-Adams (2022)
Source: [[The Brompton - Engineering for Change]] · [Original](https://profilebooks.com/work/the-brompton/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8c392c3fpg9vszppff4m2tv)

> Andrew Ritchie found himself in a car park in Nottingham with a van and a cheque book, on a late-night mission to save the company by buying slightly more than a thousand bicycle wheel hubs from the doors of a factory a few hours before it was going to shut down.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In late 2000 Sturmey-Archer, Brompton's hub supplier, had been sold to an investment firm and run out of cash. Andrew drove a van to the factory at midnight without telling anyone and bought just over a thousand hubs from the insolvency team. In the car park he met the head designer, Steve Rickels, and offered him a job; Rickels later designed Brompton's Wide Range hub.
- **Why it matters:** Without the hubs Brompton would have had to close. The episode opens the chapter on why the company makes about 70 per cent of its parts itself, and the book follows its consequences down to a single small part on the frame.
