# Mathildewick Cove

A fictional three-house bay on a western Norwegian fjord, pinned here at Fiskå in Sunnmøre as a best guess. It is the home of Trille and Lena and the setting of nearly everything in Maria Parr's _Adventures with Waffles_.

## Adventures with Waffles — Maria Parr (2005)
Source: [Original](https://penguinrandomhouse.com/books/249479/adventures-with-waffles-by-maria-parr-illustrated-by-kate-forrester/9781536203660) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m850peqcedwc94ety2ex68hw)

> Grandpa says Mathildewick Cove is a kingdom.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Trille narrates from the cove where he and Lena live: his family's big orange house with Grandpa's basement flat, Lena's small white house across the hedge they cut a hole in, and Uncle Tor's house down by the sea. Fields and a gravel track run down to a pebble shore and jetty, where a Midsummer bonfire is doused by Grandpa's muck-spreader and the children load Uncle Tor's fishing boat with farm animals. Above the main road are sledding slopes, Hillside Jon's farm, spruce forest and the family's mountain cabin, where Lena falls during the autumn sheep round-up and is rescued by helicopter. A ferry across the fjord, where Trille's dad works, links the cove to the unnamed town with its pedestrian street, doctor and school. In the last chapters Lena runs away from town and hides in the farm's hay barn, the old stables burn in a thunderstorm, and the book ends a year on with a second Midsummer bonfire, a wedding party on the cove's shore.
- **Why it matters:** The cove's smallness drives the story: Lena and Trille are each other's only neighbours, so the threat of Lena moving to town is the book's emotional engine. Its name is a running joke, with Minda's pirate-figurehead legend set against Grandpa's plainer origin in a woman called Mathilde Vik.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Fictional. The book places it only in Norway (kroner, May 17, a fjord and ferry), never by name.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Best guess. The text's clues are generic: "They would be coming across the fjord on the ferry.", mountains behind, Auntie Granny "about twelve miles" away, and a town across the fjord. Reference works call it a fictional village in western Norway, and the Store norske leksikon says Parr's books are set in a Sunnmøre fjord landscape; Parr was born and raised in Fiskå, Vanylven, on Sunnmøre. Pinned at Fiskå for that reason. Any other small fjord village in Sunnmøre is an equally possible model.
