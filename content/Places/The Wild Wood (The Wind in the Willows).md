# The Wild Wood

The dark wood across the water-meadows in _The Wind in the Willows_, pinned at Quarry Wood above Cookham Dean, the wood Grahame knew. Mole gets lost there in the snow and finds Badger's underground house, and the weasels and stoats come from it.

## The Wind in the Willows — Kenneth Grahame (1908)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL28570037W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne2jhsf401hq4wdkkpm8bg)

> and with great cheerfulness of spirit he pushed on towards the Wild Wood, which lay before him low and threatening, like a black reef in some still southern sea.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In Chapter III Mole goes alone into the Wild Wood in winter, is hounded by faces and whistling, and hides in a hollow until Rat finds him; caught in the snow, Mole trips on the door-scraper of Badger's house. Badger feeds them in his great kitchen and shows Mole the tunnels built by a vanished people, then lets them out at the wood's edge. Its stoats and weasels later seize Toad Hall, and at the end the friends walk through it to respectful greetings.
- **Why it matters:** The Wild Wood is the book's place of menace beside the safe river, and taming it marks the return of order.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The Wild Wood and Badger's house are fictional.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text places the wood on the far side of the river from Rat's bank, beyond the water-meadows. Countryfile identifies Quarry Wood above Cookham Dean, which Grahame knew from his years at The Mount, as its model; the Wikipedia article on Toad Hall names none. Pinned at Quarry Wood.
