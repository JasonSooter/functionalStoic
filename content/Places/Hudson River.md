# Hudson River

River running from the Adirondacks to New York Harbor. In _Nathaniel's Nutmeg_ Henry Hudson sails up it in 1609, hoping it leads to the Pacific and the Spice Islands, and finds a dead end near Albany.

## Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Giles Milton (1999)

Source: [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Reader's Companion]] · [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1970050W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8efckxf30w3fdaj8nz0mxmw)

> Hudson anchored somewhere in the region of Albany and sent his Dutch mate and four others upstream in the ship’s small boat. They returned at dusk bearing bad news.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** In September 1609 the Half Moon presses upstream past [[Manhattan]] "in search of the passage that, it was hoped, would lead to the warm waters of the Pacific". Near Albany the boat party finds the channel narrowing and shallowing: "this mighty river did not lead to the spices of the East." On the way back, anchored "down below the mountains", presumably the Highlands near Peekskill, Juet shoots a man who has climbed onto the rudder to steal, and the crew fires on people along the banks. Reports of Hudson's "groote noordt rivier" reach Holland; by 1620 Thomas Dermer finds Dutch traders already settled there, and in the 1620s eighteen settlers sail "up the Hudson to Fort Orange, close to the site of present-day Albany".
- **Why it matters:** The river fails as a spice route, but it gives the Dutch their claim to New Netherland, the colony the book will tie back to the Banda Islands.
