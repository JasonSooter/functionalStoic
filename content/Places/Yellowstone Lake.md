# Yellowstone Lake

Large lake at the centre of [[Yellowstone National Park]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ its 1973 tilt is the clue that the volcano beneath the park is still alive.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m815ajdst5dmgsa3jnkqmghp)

> water in Yellowstone Lake, in the heart of the park, began to run over the banks at the lake’s southern end, flooding a meadow, while at the opposite end of the lake the water mysteriously flowed away.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In 1973 water in Yellowstone Lake begins running over its southern banks while it mysteriously drains away at the other end. Geologists find a bulge lifting one end of the lake; the centre of the park turns out to be over three feet higher in 1984 than in 1924, before subsiding about eight inches in 1985.
- **Why it matters:** It turns the supervolcano from history into a present threat: the ground is still rising and falling over the magma chamber.
