# Finland Station

Railway terminal in [[Saint Petersburg]] (then Leningrad), where Lenin arrived in 1917. In _The Spy and the Traitor_ it is the first stop on Gordievsky's run for the border.

## The Spy and the Traitor — Ben Macintyre (2018)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19749410W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7xy5txxbknjhvhpaejqtt35)

> Gordievsky arrived at the Finland Station at 5:45. The almost deserted square in front was dominated by a vast statue of Lenin, commemorating the moment in 1917 when the great theorist of revolution arrived from Switzerland to take charge of the Bolsheviks.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** Off the overnight train from Moscow, Gordievsky reaches the Finland Station at 5:45, the square "dominated by a vast statue of Lenin" commemorating the revolutionary's arrival in 1917. From here he takes an early train up the coast to Zelenogorsk, then a bus toward the rendezvous south of Vyborg.
- **Why it matters:** The book makes the most of the irony: the station that symbolizes the revolution's arrival becomes the starting point of a KGB officer's flight in the opposite direction.
