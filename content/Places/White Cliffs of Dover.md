# White Cliffs of Dover

Chalk cliffs on the Kent coast of [[England]], facing France. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ they show how much carbon the oceans' tiny organisms have locked away.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> It is remarkable, when you behold an extraordinary natural feature like the White Cliffs of Dover in England, to reflect that it is made up of nothing but tiny deceased marine organisms

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Bryson notes that the White Cliffs of Dover are made of nothing but tiny dead marine organisms, foraminiferans and coccoliths, that have locked carbon into limestone. A six-inch cube of Dover chalk, he says, holds well over a thousand litres of compressed carbon dioxide.
- **Why it matters:** A familiar landmark makes the abstract carbon cycle tangible; minor, but memorable.
