# The River

The unnamed river of _The Wind in the Willows_, read as the Thames around Cookham and pinned there as an inference. Rat's home reach, the weir island of 'The Piper at the Gates of Dawn' and the ford of Otter's vigil all lie on it.

## The Wind in the Willows — Kenneth Grahame (1908)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL28570037W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne2f8cayehzx4q1z42efkt)

> Never in his life had he seen a river before—this sleek, sinuous, full-bodied animal, chasing and chuckling, gripping things with a gurgle and leaving them with a laugh, to fling itself on fresh playmates that shook themselves free, and were caught and held again.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Mole bolts from his spring-cleaning and sees a river for the first time in Chapter I; Rat ferries him across, takes him boating and picnicking, and Mole moves into Rat's riverside hole. In Chapter VII Rat and Mole row all night after the lost otter cub Portly, follow piping up a backwater to a weir island where they see Pan with the cub asleep between his hooves, and return Portly to the old ford where Otter keeps watch.
- **Why it matters:** The river bank is the book's home base and emotional centre, and the weir island is its mystical heart; the whole idyll depends on a slow lowland river with weirs, locks and a towing-path.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The river is never named; the Wild Wood, Toad Hall and the weir island are fictional.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text names no river or town. Wikipedia's article on Toad Hall says the book is set in the Thames Valley, and Countryfile places the riverbank 'almost certainly' on a Thames stretch centred on Cookham Dean, from Marlow to Pangbourne, where Grahame grew up and later lived. Pinned at Cookham; Pangbourne (Grahame's last home) is the main alternative.
