# Bridgeport, Connecticut

Coastal city in Connecticut. A subject in Bren Smith's “The Seas Will Save Us: How an Army of Ocean Farmers are Starting an Economic Revolution”: Polluted harbour city cited for new restorative ocean farms and a closing coal plant to repurpose.

## The Seas Will Save Us: How an Army of Ocean Farmers are Starting an Economic Revolution — Bren Smith (2016)

Source: [Original](https://medium.com/@bren_7821/an-army-of-ocean-farmers-on-the-frontlines-of-the-blue-green-economic-revolution-d5ae171285a3) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjr715nxdwdwc6a04j21sj)

> New farms are being built in polluted areas like Bridgeport and the Bronx River in order to soak up the nitrogen and carbon, pull out heavy metals, and re-build reefs.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Bridgeport appears as a site for new ocean farms that harvest ecosystem services in polluted water. Earlier the author also imagines repurposing a shuttered coal plant closing in Bridgeport for processing animal feed and salt.
- **Why it matters:** Used as the example of a degraded urban coast to be restored by farming; the argument is general, so the place is a minor illustration.
