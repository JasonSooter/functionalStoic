# Mocha

Red Sea port on Yemen's west coast. In _Nathaniel's Nutmeg_ Sir Henry Middleton's fleet is welcomed here in 1610, then seized; Middleton is chained in a kennel and later blockades the port.

## Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Giles Milton (1999)

Source: [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Reader's Companion]] · [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1970050W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8efd05t3x76gfey4whbvz3h)

> He with a frowning (and not his wonted disembling) countenance, asked me how I durst be so bold as to come into this their port of Mocha, so near their holy citie of Mecca.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In 1610 the giant Trades Increase sticks fast on a sandbank off the town, and the governor, the renegade Greek Rejib Aga, at first could not be more welcoming. Then the Turks turn on the English, killing men and chaining the rest; Middleton spends a day in "a dirty dogges kennell under a paire of stairs", his "pillow a stone", before the prisoners are marched inland to [[Sana'a]]. Back in Mocha he gets his guards drunk on smuggled aqua vitae, hides in an empty barrel and is rolled down to the beach and a waiting boat, then blockades the port until the remaining prisoners are freed. Nathaniel Courthope sails with this fleet.
- **Why it matters:** It is the disaster that wrecks Middleton's great 1610 voyage, the expedition Courthope is one of the few to survive.
