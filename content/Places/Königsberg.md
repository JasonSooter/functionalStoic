# Königsberg

Former capital of East Prussia on the Pregel, since 1946 the Russian city of Kaliningrad. In _Germany: Memories of a Nation_ it is the first of the 'lost capitals' of German culture.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)
Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m756qgn8te1x5m9bwbzqa9)

> The coronation that took place in the brick Gothic cathedral of Königsberg on 18 January 1701 was a ceremony designed to show that this monarchy was different.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 3, 'Lost capitals', tells of the city founded by the Teutonic Knights in 1254, home of Kant and of the amber trade, where Elector Frederick crowned himself king in the cathedral in 1701. Chapter 14 has the Prussian royal family retreating there after Jena in 1806, where Prussia's fightback and the Iron Cross began. Chapter 22 notes it as Käthe Kollwitz's birthplace, destroyed and taken by the Russians in 1945; only old manhole covers still read 'Königsberg'.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's prime example of a German cultural capital that no longer lies in Germany yet remains in German memory.
