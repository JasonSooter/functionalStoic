## Where We Go When Everything Breaks: Placemaking Lessons from Asheville’s Recovery — Project for Public Spaces (2025)
Source: [[Where We Go When Everything Breaks - Placemaking Lessons From Asheville’s Recovery]] · [Original](https://www.pps.org/article/where-we-go-when-everything-breaks-placemaking-lessons-from-ashevilles-recovery) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5e2yvz0523wdxgv2y5ank71)

> In Asheville, North Carolina, the storm exposed a simple truth: the city’s design ultimately shapes its resilience and response capabilities.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** After Hurricane Helene in September 2024 the city had no potable tap water for 60 days. Walkable areas, public markets, libraries, a community gym and cisterns became aid and distribution sites, while sprawling areas lacked a civic heart.
- **Why it matters:** The whole argument about placemaking and social infrastructure is drawn from Asheville's recovery.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84c89h1fcnwqk1h5j1jzczx)

> Asheville is, in many ways, a typical midsize American city, which is to say that its downtown was virtually abandoned in the second half of the twentieth century.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 11, "Everything Is Connected to Everything Else". Architect Joseph Minicozzi, sidetracked in the Appalachians on a 2001 motorcycle trip, settled in Asheville and ran the numbers on its abandoned downtown. A six-story 1923 building once owned by JCPenney, rescued and turned into shops, offices and condos, paid $330,000 per acre in property tax alone, against $50,800 per acre in retail and property taxes from the Walmart at the edge of town. The city changed its zoning and parking rules, and downtown became a leading source of the county's tax revenue.
- **Why it matters:** Asheville is the book's hard-money case: dense, mixed downtown buildings return far more to the public purse per acre than sprawl does.
