# Platte River

Broad, shallow river across Nebraska, flowing into the Missouri. In _How to Know a Person_, David Brooks retells Loren Eiseley's float down it as his model of "accompaniment".

## How to Know a Person — David Brooks (2023)

Source: [[How to Know a Person]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/652822/how-to-know-a-person-by-david-brooks/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8g97decnzasnc8yk3t62bc5)

> Loren Eiseley, an American naturalist, was doing some fieldwork on the Platte River, which runs through Mary Pipher’s Nebraska and then flows into the Missouri and eventually down into the Gulf of Mexico.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 4 opens with Eiseley breaking through a willow thicket during fieldwork, undressing and sitting in the river. He can't swim, and the Platte, "a mile wide and an inch deep", still has swirls, holes and quicksand; he lies back and floats, sensing the flow from glacier streams to the Gulf.
- **Why it matters:** Brooks uses the float as the image of accompaniment: moving alongside someone, patient and present, rather than steering. The Platte's shallowness and hidden dangers are what make the image work: a deep river would have meant swimming, not floating along.
