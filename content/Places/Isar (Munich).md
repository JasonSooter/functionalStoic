# Isar (Munich)

The Alpine river that runs through Munich, [[Bavaria]], Germany, and the swimming commute route in two articles about a man who swims to work.

## At a glance

| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| The man who swims to work | Daniel Loher | subject | A Munich man who swims 2 km to his office instead of sitting in traffic. |
| These Germans Who Swim To Work Are Happier Than You | Rebecca Schuman | subject | The same swim commute, held up as a model of German happiness. |
| The man who swims to work (BBC Capital) | Lemonfeed | subject | The swimmer himself on his daily float down the river to the Kulturstrand bar. |

## The man who swims to work — Daniel Loher (2026)
Source: [Original](http://www.bbc.com/capital/story/20170724-the-man-who-swims-to-work) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5bfwmgm0vmgy9dt2y9j9jmq)

> So he now packs his laptop, suit and shoes into a waterproof bag, straps it to his back and swims 2km to work along the Isar River in Munich, Germany.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** A short piece on Benjamin David, who swims along the river to work in trunks or a wetsuit, with rubber sandals against glass and the odd bicycle on the riverbed. People on the bridges sometimes laugh; he says it is faster and more relaxing than traffic.
- **Why it matters:** The river is the commute; without the Isar running through the city there is no story.

## These Germans Who Swim To Work Are Happier Than You — Rebecca Schuman (2026)
Source: [Original](https://www.theawl.com/2017/08/these-germans-who-swim-to-work-are-happier-than-you/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5bfwjxradh9j60b0f5e9fa4)

> Munich resident David Benjamin both lives and works near the city’s Isar river

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Fed up with gridlock on the road alongside, he swims the two kilometres to his office, wetsuit in cold weather and laptop in a dry bag, often floating on his back. The column pairs him with a woman in Rodgau who swims across the Main to her office because the early ferry stopped running (that crossing isn't located precisely enough to pin).
- **Why it matters:** The Isar swim is the column's lead example for its argument about German work-life balance.

## The man who swims to work (BBC Capital) — Lemonfeed (2017)
Source: [Original](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AKpgHkaW7lQ) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7h2c74k38cpken181cpdvw9)

> Ich komme jeden Tag zur Arbeit nicht mit dem Bus und nicht mit dem Auto, sondern indem ich hier unseren Fluss, die ISA, runterschwimme.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the video:** In the German-language BBC Capital film the swimmer explains that he skips the traffic on the road alongside by swimming down the river every morning, after checking its level, current and temperature (14 to 22 degrees, since the water comes from the Alps). He adds that the river was a major transport route for centuries, lost in the last hundred years, and that people watch from the bridges. He climbs out at the Kulturstrand, his summer culture beach, and orders a cappuccino at the bar in his trunks.
- **Why it matters:** The river is both his commute and his argument for reviving the waterway.
- **Real vs. fictional:** ⚠️ The transcript spells the river "ISA" and never names the city; it's the same BBC Capital story as the Daniel Loher article above, which places it on the Isar in Munich.
