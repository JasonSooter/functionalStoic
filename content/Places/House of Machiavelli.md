# House of Machiavelli

Machiavelli's country house, the Albergaccio, at Sant'Andrea in Percussina south of [[Florence]], where he lived in exile from 1513. In _The Pleasures of Reading in an Age of Distraction_ it is the study where he dressed in courtly clothes each evening to "converse" with the ancients, Jacobs's model of aspirational reading.

## The Pleasures of Reading in an Age of Distraction — Alan Jacobs (2011)
Source: [[The Pleasures of Reading in an Age of Distraction]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8enp0skgjaqqmtmaatk8rs2)

> He retired to his house in the country and rediscovered the pleasures of reading.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The "Responsiveness" section opens with Machiavelli's 1513 letter: "When evening has come, I return to my house and go into my study," where he takes off his muddy day clothes, puts on courtly garments, and for four hours forgets pain, poverty and death in the company of ancient authors. Jacobs sets the scene: earlier that year Machiavelli had been removed from office in Florence, imprisoned, tortured and exiled, and in his country house he rediscovered reading, carrying Dante, Petrarch or Ovid even on daytime walks about the estate.
- **Why it matters:** The study is the stage for Jacobs's idea of aspirational reading, entering the presence of great writers as one would a prince's court. The ritual depends on the retreat from public life to the estate; set in Florence's political turmoil it would lose its point.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real. Jacobs says only "his house in the country"; the house is the Albergaccio at Sant'Andrea in Percussina (Wikipedia: House of Machiavelli), where Machiavelli wrote that letter in exile.
