# Rives de Seine

Riverside park on the former expressway along the Seine's Right Bank in Paris. In the reading it is the road given back to people: the site of the Paris Plages summer beaches in _Happy City_, and a 2017 car-free park in Fast Company's list of bold moves.

## These 8 cities are taking bold steps to get rid of cars — Adele Peters (2019)
Source: [Original](https://www.fastcompany.com/90321627/these-8-cities-are-taking-bold-steps-to-get-rid-of-cars) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k7easv9anav0dnaz3m3g92)

> In 2017, a busy highway next to the Seine River became a car-free pedestrian pathway and park.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The riverbank is now full of walkers and cyclists. A quoted expert notes that the crowd could not be moved in individual cars.
- **Why it matters:** Section of the article devoted to this place's car-reduction effort.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84c868dyzkfhd2r59r19b9c)

> Paris Plages has seen pavement all through central Paris wrested away from cars and converted into sandboxes, plazas, and dance floors.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 11, "Everything Is Connected to Everything Else". Each summer the Pompidou Expressway along the Seine is buried under sand, palms and beer gardens for Paris Plages. Montgomery notes that this "beachification" is officially part of the plan to cut the city's vehicle emissions by 60 percent by 2020, and works because it makes driving harder. He says the beaches are being made permanent: the expressway is to be narrowed and a mile-and-a-half car-free zone put in its place.
- **Why it matters:** It is one of the chapter's proofs that the happy city and the green city are the same project: a pleasure for residents that is also a climate measure.
