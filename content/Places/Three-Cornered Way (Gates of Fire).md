# Three-Cornered Way (Gates of Fire)

A crossroads on the border between Attica and Megara, placed here near [[Eleusis]], where the Athens, Delphi and Corinth roads split. In _Gates of Fire_ it is where the orphaned Xeo and his cousin Diomache part for good.

## Gates of Fire — Steven Pressfield (1998)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/133742/gates-of-fire-by-steven-pressfield/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m89gtgbbe62rvkf21svcjqvj)

> Ten dawns later Diomache and I stood at the Three-Cornered Way, on the frontier of Attika and Megara, where the Athens road breaks off to the east, the Sacred Road to Delphi and the west and the Corinthian southwest, to the Isthmus and the Peloponnese.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** After burning the pyre of their guardian Bruxieus in the hills, the two cousins walk down for a week to the crossroads. They watch the traffic, Diomache talks of wanting a husband and a home, and they divide the dogs: Lucky goes with her, Happy stays with Xeo. "Diomache took her road and I took mine. She was fifteen. I was twelve."
- **Why it matters:** It ends Xeo's childhood and sends him alone toward Lakedaemon. His later search for Diomache in Athens starts from this parting.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text names no town. Its clues: "the frontier of Attika and Megara", with roads east to Athens, west to Delphi by the Sacred Road, and southwest to Corinth and the Isthmus. Those three roads met in the Eleusis plain, so the pin is at Eleusis. The actual Attic–Megarian border lay a little further west, toward Megara, which is the main alternative.
