# Lakewood, California

Lakewood is a planned postwar suburb in Los Angeles County, just north of Long Beach. In _Abundance_ it stands for the go-go era when California built homes at breakneck speed.

## At a glance

## Abundance — Ezra Klein & Derek Thompson (2025)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL44034521W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m77dbdmgjf3se6xe4z81xv)

> No suburban development epitomized this go-go era more than Lakewood, California, a planned community built on open farmland just north of Long Beach. Between 1950 and 1953, more than 17,000 homes went up.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 1 ("Grow") presents Lakewood as the emblem of postwar building: more than 17,000 homes went up between 1950 and 1953, and by the spring of 1954 farmland for sugar beets and lima beans had become one of California's twenty largest cities. The authors pair it with Petaluma to show how fast the politics of housing changed.
- **Why it matters:** It is half of the chapter's central contrast: "Today, California is more Petaluma than Lakewood." It works as a symbol of the abundance the state has lost, not as a scene.
