# Merrimack Street (Lowell)

## Walkable City — Jeff Speck (2012)

Source: [[Walkable City]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL36883695M/Walkable_City) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbjdmcwcwrejxrn89znqg2)

> A couple of years ago, while I was working on a plan for Lowell, Massachusetts, some old high-school friends joined us for dinner on Merrimack Street, the heart of a lovely nineteenth-century downtown.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Speck, planning Lowell's downtown, dines on Merrimack Street with friends, a toddler in a stroller and his pregnant wife. They wait at a crossing for a light, never notice the pushbutton, then jaywalk and a car rounds the corner at about forty-five miles per hour on a street widened to ease traffic.
- **Why it matters:** The near-miss is the book's opening personal anecdote of a street engineered for traffic failing pedestrians. Set elsewhere the story would lose its concrete, widened-street example; the text calls the street the heart of a lovely nineteenth-century downtown.
