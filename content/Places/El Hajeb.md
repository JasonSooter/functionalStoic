# El Hajeb

Town in the Fès-Meknès region of [[Morocco]], south of Meknes; it is the setting of Orin Hargraves's Peace Corps essay on being accepted as a neighbour.

## Peace Corps: The Great Adventure — Peace Corps (2002)
Source: [[Peace Corps - The Great Adventure]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL16050643W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5c3q033x4m52jnjkzqsksaw)

> I lived in the upstairs apartment of a beautiful colonial-period villa in the part of the town that had been built by the French.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In "Neighbors" (Orin Hargraves, Morocco 1980–82), the author returns to El Hajeb in late August after summer training in Rabat and finds it a comedown. He lives upstairs in a French-built villa and walks to the post office along tree-lined streets as a storm comes in. His downstairs neighbour shouts up in Arabic, asking him to tell Aisha to put the goats in the shed, and the casual request makes him feel accepted.
- **Why it matters:** The whole essay is set here, and its turn depends on his being the lone American in a small town. In a big city like Rabat, the moment of being an ordinary neighbour would not land.
