# Flushing Meadows–Corona Park

Flushing Meadows–Corona Park is a large park in Queens, New York City, built on former marshland and ash dumps, and site of the 1939 and 1964 World's Fairs. _Abundance_ uses the 1964 fair as an emblem of a country confident about building the future.

## Abundance — Ezra Klein & Derek Thompson (2025)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL44034521W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m75bej1bw63ktwa4kspmm1)

> The scene was Flushing, a three-mile stretch of natural marshlands in Queens.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In the conclusion the authors describe the 1964 World's Fair at Flushing, once marshland so full of trash that Fitzgerald called it "the valley of ashes". It held 140 pavilions on almost 700 acres and drew more than 50 million visitors to see Bell Labs' Picturephone and GM's Futurama II. President Johnson's opening speech celebrated American abundance and warned that progress had "two faces".
- **Why it matters:** It frames the book's closing choice between abundance and scarcity, recalling an era when Americans imagined the future and built it.
