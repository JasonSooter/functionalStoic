# Cowichan Lake

Long lake in the forests of southern Vancouver Island, [[British Columbia]]. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is where George Pocock finds the ancient western red cedar for his racing shells.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86m61p8dwngprw31sv905sz)

> He found just what he wanted in the misty woods surrounding Lake Cowichan on Vancouver Island.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Once converted to western red cedar for his hulls, Pocock scours sawmills on the Olympic Peninsula and in British Columbia for the best wood. Around Lake Cowichan he finds long, tight-grained sections of massive ancient trees, from which he mills planks sixty feet long and then shaves them into matching sheets five thirty-seconds of an inch thick, laid either side of the keel.
- **Why it matters:** The lake is the source of the material that makes Pocock's shells, and the boat the crew will row, what they are. It appears in the book's long passage on shell-building, not as a scene.
