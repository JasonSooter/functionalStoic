# Lincoln Center for the Performing Arts

Performing arts campus on [[Manhattan]]'s Upper West Side. In _Happy City_ its grass-roofed restaurant is an example of finding room for nature in a dense city.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83qjr8w056q7nkmvns28nkr)

> The architecture firm partly responsible for the High Line, Diller Scofidio + Renfro, demonstrated this again a few dozen blocks north, in their renovation of Lincoln Center for the Performing Arts, where they created a green hillside by adding a new restaurant building to the Lincoln campus.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Diller Scofidio + Renfro's renovation adds a restaurant whose sloping roof is planted with grass, which passers-by climb and Juilliard students lie on.
- **Why it matters:** A minor example, following the [[High Line]], that cities have more room for touchable nature than they assume.
