# Golden Rule Dairy and Bakery (Fremont)

Bakery in the Fremont neighbourhood of [[Seattle]] where Harry Rantz worked as a mechanic in the 1930s. In _The Boys in the Boat_ Joe waits outside it to see his father for the first time in five and a half years.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86yegfcybtr44081tawff2p)

> The next day Joe, still feeling awash with goodwill, bought a little gas, drove his old Franklin over to Fremont, and parked in front of the Golden Rule Dairy and Bakery.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** The day after his canoe outing with Joyce in April 1935, Joe parks his Franklin outside the bakery and waits until the workers come out for lunch on the lawn; his father, the tallest man there, freezes when he sees him. They talk in the car over a sandwich. When Joe asks to come and see his half siblings, Harry says, "I don't reckon so, Joe," then hints that the kids are sometimes home alone when he and Thula go off on excursions.
- **Why it matters:** It is the first step in Joe's reconciliation with his father, half a door opened; the hint about the unattended children sets up the later visits to the house on Bagley.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real; the book names only the neighbourhood, so the pin is approximate.
