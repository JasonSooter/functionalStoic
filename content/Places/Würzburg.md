# Würzburg

Episcopal city on the Main in Lower Franconia, Bavaria. In _Germany: Memories of a Nation_ it is where the sculptor Tilman Riemenschneider worked and was ruined after the Peasants' War.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)
Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m75w5xbmae049afena234c)

> In 1525, Würzburg was the site of one of its most important episodes.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Chapter 12 follows Riemenschneider to Würzburg, where he settled in 1483 and rose to mayor and councillor. In the Peasants' War of 1525 the council opened the gates to the rebels while the prince-bishop withdrew to the fortress above the city. When the revolt was crushed the bishop returned in triumph, and Riemenschneider was arrested and lost his patrons.
- **Why it matters:** Where the sculptor's career ended, and the root of the later myth of Riemenschneider the rebel that Thomas Mann and both post-war German states took up.
