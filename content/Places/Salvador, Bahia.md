# Salvador, Bahia

Port capital of Bahia on the Bay of All Saints, Brazil. Crusoe lands there and builds the sugar plantation he later leaves for the voyage that wrecks him.

## At a glance

| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Robinson Crusoe | Daniel Defoe | setting | His sugar plantation, abandoned for the slaving voyage that wrecks him. |

## Robinson Crusoe — Daniel Defoe (1719)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL45237W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne4a230j75w6j2w44cgshw)

> We had a very good voyage to the Brazils, and I arrived in the Bay de Todos los Santos, or All Saints’ Bay, in about twenty-two days after.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Crusoe lands in the Bay of All Saints, lives with a sugar planter, buys land and becomes a planter. About four years later merchants at St. Salvador propose the slaving voyage to Guinea that ends in his wreck. Decades on, from Lisbon, he learns the plantation has prospered and sells it for a fortune.
- **Why it matters:** The plantation is the comfortable 'middle station' he throws away, and the source of his final wealth.
