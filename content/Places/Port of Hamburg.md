# Port of Hamburg

Germany's largest seaport, on the lower Elbe in [[Hamburg]]. In Joseph Rosenberg's _German: How to Speak and Write It_ it is the subject of the first German reading text, "Im Hamburger Hafen", opening the course.

## German: How to Speak and Write It — Joseph Rosenberg (1962)
Source: [[German - How to Speak and Write It]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m0q78y95e96cr2h2wryxfcm7)

> Hamburg ist der grösste Handelshafen Deutschlands und einer der Haupthäfen des europäischen Kontinents. Hamburg liegt an der unteren Elbe und Alster, etwa 100 Kilometer von der Nordsee entfernt.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Just before the First Lesson, an illustrated passage titled "Im Hamburger Hafen" describes the port: Germany's largest commercial harbour and one of the main ports of the continent, on the lower Elbe and Alster about 100 km from the North Sea, reached by ships at high tide, in a city of over a million that is also an important industrial centre. The First Lesson then houses its invented Schulz family in Hamburg.
- **Why it matters:** It is the learner's first real German text, so the port is literally where the course begins; the choice presents Germany through its trade and industry. Any city could have served as a reading exercise, but Hamburg also anchors the lessons' fictional family.
