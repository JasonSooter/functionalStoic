# Voorburg

Small town on the edge of [[The Hague]], now part of Leidschendam-Voorburg. It is the Münninghoffs' home in exile from 1940 and the frame of Alexander Münninghoff's _The Son and Heir_.

## The Son and Heir: A Memoir — Alexander Münninghoff (2020)

Source: [Original](https://www.letterenfonds.nl/en/books/heir-and-successor) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m85b01sz67d5m4jsqsv0ww5q)

> I grew up in a large villa in Voorburg, a small town in the suburbs of The Hague.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The book opens here in 1948: the four-year-old author finds a black SS helmet in the attic and wears it into the smoking room, where the family is gathering for the Old Boss's daily canasta. Fleeing Riga in 1940, the family first squeezes into Uncle Wim and Aunt Marie's stately mansion, then after three months moves to a nearby villa that the Old Boss stocks with tinned food and names Briva Latvija ("Free Latvia"), painted in Gothic script over the balcony. During the war it hides a half-Jewish Resistance courier and others; in an SD raid one escapes through the backyard to a church tower on Oosteinde, while the Old Boss talks the senior agent into releasing the other. In September 1944 Wera arrives with her baby, and the stocked cellars carry the household through the Hunger Winter; on 3 March 1945 the villa becomes an aid station for victims of the [[Bezuidenhout]] bombing. On 1 June 1945 Frans comes home from the war and is coldly rejected by his father; the smoking room later hosts the Jesuit "think tank" and the Catholic ministers and lawyers who arrange his release. In 1950 the Old Boss declares the house too big and gives Wera until 1 February to leave, though he never sells it.
- **Why it matters:** The villa holds the book's central tension: a family that looked German to its Dutch neighbours, sheltered Resistance fighters and dealt with the occupiers, and kept its secrets in a box in the attic. "Briva Latvija was like a fortress that had somehow transcended the standard measurements of good and evil", and every exile in the book, the author's included, is an exile from it.
