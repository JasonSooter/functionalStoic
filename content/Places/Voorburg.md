# Voorburg

Small town on the edge of [[The Hague]], now part of Leidschendam-Voorburg. It is the Münninghoffs' home in exile from 1940 and the frame of Alexander Münninghoff's _The Son and Heir_.

## The Son and Heir: A Memoir — Alexander Münninghoff (2020)
Source: [Original](https://www.letterenfonds.nl/en/books/heir-and-successor) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m85b01sz67d5m4jsqsv0ww5q)

> I grew up in a large villa in Voorburg, a small town in the suburbs of The Hague.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The book opens here in 1948: the four-year-old author finds a black SS helmet in the attic and wears it into the smoking room, where the family is gathering for the Old Boss's daily canasta. Fleeing Riga in 1940, the family first squeezes into Uncle Wim and Aunt Marie's stately mansion, then after three months moves to a nearby villa that the Old Boss stocks with tinned food and names Briva Latvija ("Free Latvia"), painted in Gothic script over the balcony. During the war it hides a half-Jewish Resistance courier and others; in an SD raid one escapes through the backyard to a church tower on Oosteinde, while the Old Boss talks the senior agent into releasing the other.
- **Why it matters:** The villa holds the book's central tension: a family that looked German to its Dutch neighbours, sheltered Resistance fighters and dealt with the occupiers, and kept its secrets in a box in the attic.
