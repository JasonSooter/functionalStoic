# Royal Institution

Scientific society and lecture hall in Albemarle Street, Mayfair, [[London]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Rutherford lectures there in 1904 with Kelvin in the audience.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> In the spring of 1904, Rutherford traveled to London to give a lecture at the Royal Institution

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Founded by Count von Rumford in 1799, the Royal Institution is where, in spring 1904, Rutherford presents his disintegration theory of radioactivity and brings out his pitchblende. With the aging Kelvin present, "if not always fully awake," he notes that Kelvin had allowed that another heat source would upset his calculations, and radioactivity is that source. Kelvin beams but is unmoved.
- **Why it matters:** It is the scene where the Earth's age escapes Kelvin's twenty-odd million years; the august venue sharpens the generational handover.
