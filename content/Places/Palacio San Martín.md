# Palacio San Martín

Grand early-20th-century palace on Plaza San Martín in Retiro, [[Buenos Aires]], now used by the foreign ministry. In _Bicycle Diaries_ David Byrne watches a traditional tango orchestra there during the World Tango Festival.

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m76e9csjtt5ryz7w74gkvbxt)

> A group of us go to see a traditional tango group at a baroque palace, El Palacio de San Martín, as part of the World Tango Festival in progress here.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Byrne goes with a group of musicians to see a traditional tango orchestra at the palace during the World Tango Festival. He notes a Beaux-Arts balcony and a stained-glass panel of St. George killing the dragon; exhibition dancers perform before the elegantly dressed audience takes the floor, which he finds a little intimidating.
- **Why it matters:** It is the formal, high-society face of tango in the book, set against the tourist tango joints and the rehearsal he visits next.
