# Jewish Quarter (Corfu Town)

The old Jewish quarter (Evraiki) of [[Corfu Town]], a tangle of lanes by the synagogue. In _My Family and Other Animals_ it is where Gerry goes every morning for French lessons with the Belgian consul.

## My Family and Other Animals — Gerald Durrell (1956)
Source: [[My Family and Other Animals]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/289004/my-family-and-other-animals-by-gerald-durrell/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8fmhhw2m6x6zgytv0jnwbxh)

> The consul’s house was situated in the maze of narrow, smelly alley-ways that made up the Jewish quarter of the town.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** After the scorpion episode at lunch, Mother decides Gerry needs a tutor again, and Spiro drives him into town each morning for French with the Belgian consul. The quarter is a maze of cobbled lanes crowded with stalls of cloth, sweetmeats, silver and fruit, with donkeys, hens and bread-carriers. The consul lives "in the top flat of a tall, rickety building that leaned tiredly over a tiny square". During lessons he breaks off to shoot the quarter's starving, diseased cats from his window with an air rifle, weeping as he does it, out of pity for them.
- **Why it matters:** It is Gerry's first regular foothold inside the town, and one of the book's set-piece eccentrics lives here. The lessons themselves matter little; the cramped quarter, with its cats, is what he remembers. Set in an ordinary street, the consul's sad cat-shooting would lose its stage.
