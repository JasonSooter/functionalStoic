# Down House

Charles Darwin's home in [[Kent]], south-east of London. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where Darwin lives as an ailing near-hermit while he sits on his theory.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8330zxsg4er4kffhrr9rgbp)

> He became something of a hermit, seldom leaving his home in Kent, Down House. One of his first acts upon moving to the house was to erect a mirror outside his study window so that he could identify, and if necessary avoid, callers.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Back from the Beagle voyage, Darwin settles at Down House and seldom leaves it, plagued by illness and trying increasingly desperate treatments: icy plunge baths, dousings in vinegar, "electric chains". One of his first acts is to put a mirror outside his study window so he can spot and avoid callers. He dies here in April 1882.
- **Why it matters:** The quiet, sickly setting for the long delay before Darwin published; Bryson's portrait of the reluctant revolutionary is set here.
