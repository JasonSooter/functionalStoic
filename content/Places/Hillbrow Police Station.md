# Hillbrow Police Station

Police station on Clarendon Place in [[Hillbrow]], inner-city Johannesburg. In Born a Crime Trevor Noah is booked and held there after being stopped in an unregistered car from his stepfather's workshop.

## Born a Crime — Trevor Noah (2016)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17824318W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6zbjpx3nyzp1qw4m6aqe0dy)

> The Hillbrow police station looks exactly like every other police station in South Africa. They were all built by the same contractor at the height of apartheid—separate nodes in the central nervous system of a police state.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Chapter 17 ("The World Doesn't Love You"). Driving one of the junkers from Abel's workshop to buy cheap cell phones, Trevor is pulled over in Hillbrow and arrested on suspicion of driving a stolen car. He is charged and fingerprinted at the front desk and put in the station's holding cell with six other men. Refusing to call his mother, he gets a lawyer through a cop's referral, plays a hard coloured gangster in the cell, and befriends a huge, frightening-looking prisoner by speaking to him in Tsonga. From there he is driven to court.
- **Why it matters:** it is his first time on the wrong side of the system his mother warned him about, and the station's apartheid-era sameness carries Noah's point about a police state. The cell scenes also show how language lets him cross racial lines.
