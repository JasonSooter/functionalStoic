# Casa de la Mujer Josefina Ortiz de Domínguez

A public laundry for working women on Tepalcatitla Street in [[Coyoacán]], built under President Cárdenas. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is where Frida's students paint their second collective mural in 1945.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)

Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m73vwtcfty4er3zwxymc3ew0)

> The one in Coyoacán comprised several small houses: one for ironing, another for a nursery, another for dining, and the last a meeting room for public and social functions. It was here that “Los Fridos” painted their murals.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** After the owner of a hotel destroys their earlier work, the "Fridos" get a more fitting project in 1945: murals for this laundry, built for widows and unwed mothers who had washed clothes in muddy streams. At the opening there are speeches, a printed _corrido_ to sing and tacos of nopal cactus cooked by the laundresses.
- **Why it matters:** It shows Frida steering her students toward art that serves the public good, in keeping with her and Diego's politics.
