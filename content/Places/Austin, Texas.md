## The Comfort Crisis — Michael Easter (2021)

Source: [[The Comfort Crisis]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/634446/the-comfort-crisis-by-michael-easter/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5g0f4bvm03xe93xpjhxn794)

> After years of regularly chatting with the man, I needed to finally travel to his home base in Austin, Texas, to meet him in person and find out what else I could glean from him.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Easter visits Kashey at his home office, where Kashey explains tracking, 'reward hunger' and energy density; Easter recalls losing 15 pounds working with him.
- **Why it matters:** The visit grounds the book's chapter on hunger in a coach's practice.

## Bang Bang Crazy, Part Five (Update) — Jim Wright (2016)

Source: [Original](https://www.stonekettle.com/2016/01/bang-bang-crazy-part-five-update.html) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5hy722anbce68bjnwc7e7p2)

> Banners at the Austin rally proclaimed “An Armed Society is a Polite Society,’’ ‘'The Second Amendment Comes from God’’ and ‘‘Hey King O, I'm keeping my guns and my religion.’’

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Texas State Rep. Steve Toth spoke to a crowd at a Guns Across America / National Gun Appreciation Day rally in Austin, accusing President Obama of using children as human shields. The author uses the rally's banners and Toth's remarks as the springboard for his argument. Texas is then invoked as a rebuttal to the claim that an armed society is polite.
- **Why it matters:** The essay's rhetorical target is this rally; but the text never names a specific venue, so the place is only the city (and Texas generally).

## Six Cities — Shawni Danner (2016)

Source: [Original](https://shift.infinite.red/six-cities-one-job-719d02369338) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jx83h1wggfye4mm22msq60)

> Austin didn’t live up to my expectations as it was not as warm and much windier than I had anticipated.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the article:** The author spends two weeks in an AirBnB trying Austin as a potential home, working from cafes and co-work spaces. She finds it windy, not walkable, with slow buses and a ghost-town downtown.
- **Why it matters:** One of the six cities in a home-hunting tour; judged and rejected in detail.

## The Black Art of Escape — Casey Gerald (2019)

Source: [Original](https://nymag.com/intelligencer/2019/08/the-black-art-of-escape.html) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k7gddhdzzrsdds8pz5dcyh)

> That morning, in North Austin, Anthony Stephan House, 39, opened a package that had been left outside his front door.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** The serial bombings killed two black residents who attended the same church, with later blasts in East Austin and a suspect found in Round Rock. He distrusts the official no-motive explanation.
- **Why it matters:** The bombings push him from withdrawal toward his argument about escape and safety.

## Greenlights — Matthew McConaughey (2020)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21911019W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m700jz57yfnz3kxw6r3fqyh4)

> Camila, Levi, and I bought and moved into a home on the outskirts of Austin, overlooking the river.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** He studies at UT Austin and says the city "always let me be myself." Soon after Levi's birth in July 2008 a family crisis brings him back from Los Angeles; Camila and Levi follow, and they first rent a small home in his mother's retirement community, then buy a nine-acre property with a spring-fed well on the outskirts of the city, overlooking the river.
- **Why it matters:** The move back to Austin ends his Hollywood years and anchors the family life of the book's last part. Specific Austin scenes are pinned at [[University of Texas at Austin]], [[Catfish Station]], [[Top Notch (Austin)]] and [[Tarrytown (Austin)]].
