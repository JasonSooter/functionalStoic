## Zurich’s Insane Plan To End Traffic — OBF (2026)

Source: [Original](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kew0EIR5oxk) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5bez9mchz56x1h0p44p7nmz)

> At peak times, up to 17,000 people an hour converge on the square in front of the main station.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the video:** In the 1950s trams, taxis, cars and pedestrians jammed the square in front of the main station, and planners proposed burying the busiest tram lines in 21 km of tunnel to free the surface for cars. Voters rejected it in April 1962, 58,000 to 34,000. By the 1980s the old squares had become car parks; a 1996 "historic compromise" froze city-centre visitor parking at its 1990 level, so each new space must replace an old one and the old squares become car-free plazas. Perimeter control and tram priority followed.
- **Why it matters:** The whole city is the case study: its direct-democracy votes are the turning points, and the station square is where the problem began.

## Living in Switzerland ruined me for America and its lousy work culture — Chantal Panozzo (2015)

Source: [Original](https://www.vox.com/2015/7/21/8974435/switzerland-work-life-balance) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k5qgfjptmj6b61d1g3mw7f)

> The next day, exactly at noon, we rode the funicular to a restaurant where we dined al fresco above Zurich.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** After being scolded for eating a sandwich at her desk at a Zurich ad agency, the author is taken by colleague Tom on a funicular to a hillside restaurant. She feels guilty for an hour and a half away, but no one missed them, which shows her the Swiss view of lunch.
- **Why it matters:** Concrete scene that illustrates Swiss work-life balance.
