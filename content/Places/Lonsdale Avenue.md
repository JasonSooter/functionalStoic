# Lonsdale Avenue

Main street of the City of North Vancouver, across the harbour from [[Vancouver]], Canada. In _Happy City_ it is the spine along which the small city packed homes, shops and jobs to cut both driving and emissions.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84c8b8p11xv578twvww6qsh)

> It meant weaving more apartments, more town houses, more shops, and more jobs along Lonsdale Avenue, the city’s central spine.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 11. Alex Boston, who helped write the City of North Vancouver's climate plan and then moved there, takes Montgomery on his daily commute: a twenty-minute walk, pushing his young son in a stroller, then a fifteen-minute SeaBus ride to downtown Vancouver. The plan wove apartments, town houses, shops and jobs along the avenue, and Boston's father lives in a condo near the bus exchange and helps with childcare.
- **Why it matters:** The street shows the chapter's claim on a human scale: the same density that cuts a city's energy use also makes a family's daily life easier.
