# Unter den Linden

Berlin's grand central boulevard, running east from the [[Brandenburg Gate]]. In _The Boys in the Boat_ its lindens are replaced by swastika banners as the Nazis dress the city for the 1936 Olympics.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m878ty16hg1gpm1kry3hwbcm)

> Along the central promenade on Unter den Linden, Berlin’s wide central boulevard, the hundreds of linden trees that had given the street its name had been replaced with regimented colonnades of flagpoles bearing enormous forty-five-foot red banners with swastikas.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In the weeks before the Games the Nazis transform Berlin into what the book calls a vast movie set. Roma and Sinti families are marched to a camp at a sewage site in Marzahn, anti-Jewish signs and _Der Stürmer_ are taken down, and more than fourteen hundred homeless people are removed. On Unter den Linden the linden trees give way to rows of flagpoles with forty-five-foot swastika banners, and more hang from the Brandenburg Gate. On July 24, 1936, the American team rides in open-top buses through the Brandenburg Gate and down the flag-draped boulevard past cheering crowds; later the boys stroll it with their new cameras and answer "Heil Hitler!" with "Heil Roosevelt!"
- **Why it matters:** The boulevard stands for the illusion the regime stages for the world, the opposite of the honest teamwork the crew brings to Berlin.
