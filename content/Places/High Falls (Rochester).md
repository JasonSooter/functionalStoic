# High Falls (Rochester)

Waterfall on the Genesee River in the centre of [[Rochester, New York]]. David Byrne stumbles on it by bike in _Bicycle Diaries_ and uses its gorge, lined with old Kodak industry, to talk about cities that turned their backs on their rivers.

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m76413gq8ar1sec4r3zbq40j)

> In the center of Rochester there is a wonderful waterfall, a smaller but still spectacular Niagara where the Genesee River plummets into a deep gorge.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Byrne first found the falls by accident on a bike ride during an earlier performance in Rochester. He wonders why the city makes so little of them; Rudy Rucker tells him that thirty years ago industrial pollution hid them. Around the gorge he sees the almost abandoned Kodak plant and other factories that used the river for power and as a dump.
- **Why it matters:** The falls stand for what industry took from the waterfront, and set up the book's recurring theme of cities reclaiming their rivers.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The text calls them only "a wonderful waterfall"; the pin is High Falls, the Genesee falls in central Rochester.
