# Run

Small outlying island at the western edge of the [[Banda Islands]], Indonesia, once thick with nutmeg trees. It is the island at the heart of Giles Milton's _Nathaniel's Nutmeg_, the prize England and the Dutch fight over.

## Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Giles Milton (1999)

Source: [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Reader's Companion]] · [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1970050W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m82ef37fes6q4qh0nsz8vh8y)

> But one island, Run, was different. It lay more than ten miles to the west of Neira and was surrounded by dangerous and hidden reefs.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The prologue opens on 23 December 1616 as Nathaniel Courthope brings the Swan to Run on a secret mission ordered by King James I: a volcanic atoll whose harbour is ringed by a sunken reef, its islanders feared as head-hunters. Milton explains that Run lay more than ten miles west of Neira behind hidden reefs and the monsoon, out of reach of the Portuguese carracks, though it was thickly forested in nutmeg. Later, the pinnace Lancaster leaves at Bantam is driven by a storm onto Run, where the islanders, thinking the English too few to be a threat, trade nutmeg with them and let them build a bamboo and thatch warehouse on the northern coast. In early 1616 Samuel Castleton's English ships block the channel between Run and [[Ai (Banda Islands)|Ai]] against the Dutch, then withdraw; after Ai falls, "Run now stood alone – the only island that remained unoccupied by Dutch troops", and Richard Hunt flees there "in fear of his life". In October 1616 Courthope is given command of the Swan and the Defence for the voyage to Run.
- **Why it matters:** Run is the destination the whole book builds towards; the prologue says Courthope's talks with its chieftains "would change the course of history on the other side of the globe". Its remoteness is what keeps it open to the English when the other islands are not.
