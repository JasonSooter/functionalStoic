## Major New Study Finds Geography Can Affect Life Span as Much as Income — Eric Pfeiffer (2016)
Source: [Original](http://www.good.is/posts/health-tied-to-geography) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjqt722r1yfdpgeq2yq4fm)

> Meanwhile, lower-income people in places like Detroit and Tulsa, Oklahoma (the two poorest performing cities in the study) are seeing their life expectancy levels drop, in a widening gap between the rich and poor.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Paired with Detroit as the worst-performing city in the study. Chetty says to think specifically about why the poor fare badly in Tulsa and Detroit.
- **Why it matters:** Named as the local case that makes the article's point that geography matters.

## The Next Baby Boom: Affordable Urban Lifestyles for Millennials with Children — Jennifer Griffin (2016)
Source: [Original](https://www.strongtowns.org/journal/2016/6/29/the-next-baby-boom-affordable-urban-lifestyles-for-millennials-with-children) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jx6t4set92dtexhgdgc4fr)

> These “collar” neighborhoods are a 20-minute walk or a 5-minute bike ride to the downtown and are compact, walkable, mixed-use, and green.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The author, priced out of Park Slope and Columbia Heights, moved her family to Tulsa and uses its walkable pre-war neighborhoods at the edge of downtown as a case study. Homes there cost $175,000-$300,000 versus $500,000-$1.5 million in top-tier cities.
- **Why it matters:** Central to the argument that millennial families will seek affordable walkable neighborhoods in 2nd- and 3rd-tier cities.

## The Magic of Tree-Lined Streets — Sarah Kobos (2016)
Source: [Original](https://www.strongtowns.org/journal/2016/6/26/the-magic-of-tree-lined-streets-1) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jx6tqfjzzt50925bvp3bra)

> Unfortunately, here in Tulsa, our urban forest has suffered in recent decades.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The author bikes errands in 96-degree Tulsa heat, comfortable under mature neighborhood trees and miserable on a treeless asphalt arterial. She traces the city's loss of street trees to Dutch Elm Disease, storms, poor trimming and policy obstacles.
- **Why it matters:** Tulsa's heat and tree-poor streets anchor the argument that canopy trees are essential to walkability.

## Where Every Citizen's Heart Can Sing — Sarah Kobos (2016)
Source: [Original](https://www.strongtowns.org/journal/2016/8/18/where-every-citizens-heart-can-sing) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jx9j8g1hemyqew69hw1vtm)

> Tulsa, OK, like many cities, has its gorgeous neighborhoods and grand destinations.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The author uses her home city as the case study for her argument about beauty and the public realm. Tulsa has showpiece destinations, but most public investment goes to potholes, road widening and utilities, streets are designed to highway standards, and reliance on sales tax has driven 50 years of greenfield sprawl southward while the older core is neglected.
- **Why it matters:** It is the concrete city the whole argument is about, contrasting its postcard attractions with its everyday streets.
