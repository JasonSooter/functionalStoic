## The Blank Page Revolution — Steven Johnson (2025)
Source: [[The Blank Page Revolution]] · [Original](https://adjacentpossible.substack.com/p/the-blank-page-revolution) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5d20wgtx3k7ph9k0yxhq86k)

> I wrote about the astonishing flourishing of intellectual and commercial life in Baghdad during the Abbasid Caliphate, starting around the year 800 CE.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the article:** Johnson revisits his earlier account of Abbasid Baghdad, with its House of Wisdom (a mix of library, translation bureau and research lab), vast libraries and streets of booksellers, and admits he missed the platform underneath: cheap paper brought west along the Silk Road from China. The city sat at the nexus of the age's trade routes; the essay then follows paper to Italian merchants.
- **Why it matters:** Baghdad is the essay's example of what a new writing medium makes possible, six centuries before Gutenberg.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The House of Wisdom's site is lost, so the pin is the city.

## America's iconic war machine - BBC News — James Morgan (2015)
Source: [Original](https://www.bbc.com/news/magazine-33766644) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5hx1wj5asn4smy2fmqn2nyh)

> He and 56 other crew entered seven bombers - his was the Grim Reaper, with a painting of Bugs Bunny carrying a sickle on its nose - and flew 14,000 miles to Baghdad to drop a wave of cruise missiles, which obliterated Saddam Hussein's air defences.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the article:** Col Warren Ward is woken at night and flown with other crews non-stop from Barksdale toward Iraq. They launch cruise missiles at Iraqi air defences and land back after about 35 hours without touching the ground.
- **Why it matters:** The destination of the long-range mission that illustrates the B-52's global reach; the city itself is a target, described only briefly.

## Those With Multiple Tours of War Overseas Struggle at Home - The New York Times — Benedict Carey (2016)
Source: [Original](https://www.nytimes.com/2016/05/30/health/veterans-iraq-afghanistan-psychology-therapy.html) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jvq670x6atjbejp9zffb1n)

> In a 2007 raid in Baghdad, the team blew the front door off a house, leaving a screen door half-attached. The first man inside — the point man and team leader — tripped on the screen and fell down.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** Lundeby recounts a night extraction raid in Baghdad in which the lead man tripped on a screen door and he had to keep momentum going. It illustrates the split-second decision making that the article says is hard to unlearn.
- **Why it matters:** Illustrates combat skills at work; the specific house is not named, only the city.
