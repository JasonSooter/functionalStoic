# Bone Cabin Quarry

Fossil site near Como Bluff in [[Wyoming]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is the 1898 bonanza where bones were so plentiful someone built a cabin from them.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> There, hundreds and hundreds of fossil bones were to be found weathering out of the hills. They were so numerous, in fact, that someone had built a cabin out of them

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In 1898, between the deaths of Cope and Marsh, a trove greater than anything before is noticed a few miles from Marsh's hunting ground at Como Bluff. The first two seasons yield 100,000 pounds of bones and tens of thousands more follow each year; Bryson uses it to show that by 1900 paleontologists had tons of bones but no way to date them.
- **Why it matters:** It marks the end of the bone wars' scramble and the start of the dating problem that leads into the next chapters.
