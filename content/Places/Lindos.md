# Lindos

Ancient harbor town on the east coast of the island of Rhodes. In _Gates of Fire_ it is where the Spartan envoys see the Persian king's fleet up close and are shown the empire's map.

## Gates of Fire — Steven Pressfield (1998)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/133742/gates-of-fire-by-steven-pressfield/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m896j0974pz7j1qb260b249p)

> That evening Dienekes and the other envoys made their way on foot to the harbor at Lindos. The warships were drawn up upon the strand, within a perimeter manned by Egyptian marines.

- **Role:** setting, turning point
- **In the book:** Dienekes and the other envoys go to Rhodes, a Persian protectorate, to try to detach its navy, and fail. At the harbor they watch Tyrian triremes race and trade gifts with the Egyptian marine commander Ptammitechus. In his tent he unrolls a papyrus map of the empire, from Tyre to Susa, and urges them to submit. Dienekes answers that freedom is bought with steel and that the wheel "turns both ways."
- **Why it matters:** It is the Spartans' first direct sight of the enemy's scale. The scene states the novel's argument about freedom before the war reaches Greece.
