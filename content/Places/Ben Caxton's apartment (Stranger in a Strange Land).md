## Stranger in a Strange Land — Robert A. Heinlein (1961)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL59688W)

> Once outside the door to Ben's fiat Jill put her face to the hush box by the door and said emphatically, "Karthago delenda est!"

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Jill brings Smith here from the Bethesda hospital as the last place police would look. She bathes him in Ben's tub, then federal agents break in; Smith makes both men vanish and falls into a trance, and Jill smuggles him out in Ben's flight case.
- **Why it matters:** Smith's first use of his power against people happens here, turning the escape into a manhunt; the flat's anonymity in the big city is what makes it a hiding place.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The flat is fictional. No highlight: Reader could not anchor any span in this book.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Clues: Jill reaches it by cab from the Bethesda hospital in minutes, thinks of 'this swarming city', and the flat has a twenty-storey drop; the surrounding chapters keep mentioning Washington. No reference names a location. Pinned at Washington, D.C.; Bethesda or another suburb are the alternatives.
