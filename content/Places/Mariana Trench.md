# Mariana Trench

The deepest oceanic trench, in the western Pacific east of the Mariana Islands. Its 11 km depth is the whole premise of xkcd's What If? question about dropping a bowling ball into it.

## What if you dropped a bowling ball in the Mariana Trench? — xkcd's What If? (2026)
Source: [Original](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FYoHYq8VRt0) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5bre539x2yzsqyddscqtn5m)

> The Mariana Trench is 11 km deep, so the ball will take about 2 hours and 20 minutes to reach the bottom.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** A heavy bowling ball sinks at about 1.3 m/s and needs roughly two hours twenty to fall the trench's 11 km; lighter balls float, drift for years and may end up near California and in the Great Pacific Garbage Patch.
- **Why it matters:** The trench's depth is the whole premise; nowhere else gives the question its scale.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> in January 1960 Jacques Piccard and Lieutenant Don Walsh of the U.S. Navy sank slowly to the bottom of the ocean’s deepest canyon, the Mariana Trench, some 250 miles off Guam in the western Pacific

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Bryson first uses the trench as the extreme of ocean pressure, where transparent amphipods live unprotected seven miles down. In January 1960 Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh take the rebuilt bathyscaphe _Trieste_ to the bottom, nearly four hours' fall to 35,820 feet, and disturb a flatfish as they land. They stay twenty minutes; the text says no one has returned, partly because a Navy official thought it "a long way to go to find a flatfish".
- **Why it matters:** It anchors the book's point that we have explored the deep ocean far less than space.
