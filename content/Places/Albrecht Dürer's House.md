# Albrecht Dürer's House

Late-medieval house below the castle in Nuremberg where Albrecht Dürer lived and worked, now a museum. In _Germany: Memories of a Nation_ it is the base of the first artist to sell prints across Europe.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)
Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m764hyfzmck5m6jxyksq5c)

> The Dürer house in Nuremberg was opened to the public as a memorial museum in 1828.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 17, 'An artist for all Germans', explains that Dürer ran his print business for twenty years from this house, using Nuremberg's trade links to Venice, Antwerp and beyond to sell prints across Europe. The house opened as a memorial museum in 1828, and in 1871 his 400th birthday was celebrated in the city as he was made a national hero.
- **Why it matters:** It is where the first mass-market artist worked, and its early museum shows how Dürer was turned into a symbol of Germany.
