# Yale University

University in New Haven, [[Connecticut]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is the tiny world of J. Willard Gibbs, the great and almost invisible thermodynamicist.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80gjh1z2895cp5fck9j9s9x)

> Modest to the point of near invisibility, he passed virtually the whole of his life, apart from three years spent studying in Europe, within a three-block area bounded by his house and the Yale campus in New Haven, Connecticut.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Gibbs works unpaid at Yale for his first ten years and draws hardly any students, yet produces "On the Equilibrium of Heterogeneous Substances," which Bryson calls the Principia of thermodynamics. It appears in an obscure Connecticut journal, so Max Planck does not hear of him until too late.
- **Why it matters:** The cramped, provincial setting is the point: brilliant work done in obscurity that the wider world misses.
