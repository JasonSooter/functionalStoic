# Disneyland

Theme park in Anaheim, [[California]]. Cal Newport and Charles Montgomery both use it as a picture of engineered, purified pleasure, and Bill Bryson remembers a rare family splurge there.

## Dispatch from Disneyland — Study Hacks (2025)
Source: [Original](https://calnewport.com/dispatch-from-disneyland/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dqtwg1yqxfa8e1q0mbb8eq)

> A few days ago, I went to Disneyland. I had been invited to Anaheim to give a speech about my books, and my wife and I decided to use the opportunity to take our boys on an early summer visit to the supposed happiest place on earth.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Cal Newport visits Disneyland in Anaheim with his wife and sons after a speaking invitation. He describes riding Pirates of the Caribbean and noticing its seams (jerky animatronics, acoustic panels in the 'sky'), and uses the park as an analogy for the diluted, purified stimulation of phones.
- **Why it matters:** The park is the article's concrete analogy for digital life; he liked it but was done after a couple of days.

## The Goldilocks Rule: How to Stay Motivated in Life and Business — James Clear (2016)
Source: [Original](https://jamesclear.com/goldilocks-rule) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jx5q9r88nv6mtgrazyhn5s)

> In 1955, Disneyland had just opened in Anaheim, California, when a ten-year-old boy walked in and asked for a job.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** Ten-year-old Steve Martin gets a job selling guidebooks at newly opened Disneyland, then moves to the magic shop where he starts performing.
- **Why it matters:** Origin point of Martin's career in the habit-building anecdote.

## The Life and Times of the Thunderbolt Kid — Bill Bryson (2006)
Source: [[The Life and Times of the Thunderbolt Kid]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4620956W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5khv5ygqz1n2qcvnzvdxc06)

> “It’s Disneyland,” I said.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** On a surprise winter trip in December 1960 Bryson's father drives the family to California and, for once, spends freely on tickets. They have the almost empty park to themselves and ride the teacups, Dumbo, a submarine and a rocket to the moon; the father is enchanted, then steers them past the souvenir stand.
- **Why it matters:** The emotional high point of the chapter, showing a rare generous side of his father.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83dcghbnxdm58e67n5hv3kc)

> Officially branded “The Happiest Place on Earth” when it opened its gates in 1955, Disneyland was conceived as a pay-per-visit alternative to the freeways and sprawl that were just beginning to dominate Southern California.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Montgomery describes the park as a machine for pleasure: every view, ride, texture and scent is designed to tip the hedonic scale, and every visit begins and ends on Main Street U.S.A., a simulation of the perfect small town. He sets it beside Robert Nozick's "experience machine": its happiness depends on suspending disbelief.
- **Why it matters:** Disneyland is the book's foil, happiness by pleasant facade, against the authentic, engaged city life it argues for.
