# Carrol Ground

The Carrol Ground was a whaling ground in the South Atlantic south of the island of Saint Helena. In _Moby-Dick_ it is where the Pequod first sees the silvery 'spirit-spout' that lures it on.

## Moby-Dick — Herman Melville (1851)

Source: [[Exploration of Moby Dick]] · [[Moby Dick]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76z7ng7152pa66jwpyjjv)

> and the Carrol Ground, an unstaked, watery locality, southerly from St. Helena.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** In Chapter 51 the Pequod sweeps across four cruising-grounds: off the Azores, off the Cape de Verdes, on the Plate, and the Carrol Ground. On a serene moonlit night there Fedallah first sights a silvery jet far ahead of the bow; it recurs night after night and seems to lead the ship on toward the Cape of Good Hope.
- **Why it matters:** It marks the Pequod's run south and the start of the spirit-spout omen. The book gives it only a sentence of location, so its importance is the omen, not the place.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text calls it 'an unstaked, watery locality, southerly from St. Helena' and gives no position. I found no reference work that fixes the ground. Pinned at the Saint Helena coordinates from Wikipedia as the only real anchor the text gives; the actual ground lies an unknown distance south of the island.
