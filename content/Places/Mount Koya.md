# Mount Koya

Mount Koya is a mountain monastery complex in Wakayama Prefecture, Japan. It turns up in Four Thousand Weeks (Oliver Burkeman): The monastery and its Kii Mountains setting supply the ordeal that teaches the chapter's lesson about distraction and flight from discomfort.

## Four Thousand Weeks — Oliver Burkeman (2021)
Source: [[Four Thousand Weeks]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL22355777W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fcq7gtagnzeza3fzxvn08c)

> First, the abbot of the Mount Koya monastery had refused to let him in the door.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Steve (later Shinzen) Young trains here as a Shingon Buddhist monk in 1969, first refused entry by the abbot, then doing menial tasks, then undertaking a hundred-day solo retreat in an unheated hut. He douses himself with melted snow three times a day and discovers that full attention to the cold reduces suffering.
- **Why it matters:** The monastery and its Kii Mountains setting supply the ordeal that teaches the chapter's lesson about distraction and flight from discomfort. It is a strong, specific setting for the anecdote.
