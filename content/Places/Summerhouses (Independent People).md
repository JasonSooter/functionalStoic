# Summerhouses (Independent People)

Bjartur's fictional moorland croft in Halldór Laxness's _Independent People_, the farm on which he stakes his freedom. Pinned at Sænautasel on the Jökuldalsheiði heath in east Iceland as a best guess.

## Independent People — Halldór Laxness (1934)

Source: [[Independent People]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL757983W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5khckxr3wrnqa649b1c5frb)

> ON a knoll in the marshes stand the ruins of an old croft-house.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Bjartur buys the ruined croft of Winterhouses after eighteen years' work for the Bailiff of Rauthsmyri, renames it Summerhouses and spits at the cairn of the witch Gunnvor on the ridge, refusing to honour the valley's curse. On the knoll in the marshes, below a steep mountain, he builds his turf croft and brings his first wife, Rosa, who dies there in childbirth while he is out on the moors. Over the decades the croft sees his second marriage, the loss of children to cold and emigration, Asta Sollilja's ruin and, finally, the concrete house built on credit, after which the farm is sold to the mortgagee and he is evicted.
- **Why it matters:** Summerhouses is the novel's centre and its argument: a poor freehold where Bjartur is his own master and everyone around him pays for it.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Fictional.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The farm is invented. The text places it in a valley ringed by moorland, with a steep mountain to the north, marshes and a lake, the ridge with Gunnvor's cairn to the west and the Bailiff's farm beyond it, the Blue Mountains on the southern horizon, a high heath to the east with a five-hour pack-horse road down to the trading town of Fjord, and Glacier River out on the moors. A travel guide on Sænautasel, a turf farm on the Jökuldalsheiði heath, says the heath inspired the novel and that many local farm names match the book's, so the pin is Sænautasel. A reference summary instead places the valley in north-central Iceland, with Fjord perhaps modelled on Akureyri; that is the main alternative.
