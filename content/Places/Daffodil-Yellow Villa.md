# Daffodil-Yellow Villa

The Durrells' second house on [[Corfu]], a Venetian mansion at Kontokali, north of [[Corfu Town]]. It is the setting of the whole second part of _My Family and Other Animals_, from the arrival of Larry's guests to the fire that ends their stay.

## My Family and Other Animals — Gerald Durrell (1956)
Source: [[My Family and Other Animals]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/289004/my-family-and-other-animals-by-gerald-durrell/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8fa9dec3r3qzqc5g42jn12n)

> The new villa was enormous, a tall, square Venetian mansion, with faded daffodil-yellow walls, green shutters, and a fox-red roof. It stood on a hill overlooking the sea, surrounded by unkempt olive groves and silent orchards of lemon and orange trees.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** When Larry announces that friends are coming, Mother at first refuses, then moves the whole family to a much bigger house Spiro has found. It lies "on the hillside overlooking the shining sea and the dark, eroded hills of Albania", with a sunken walled garden, bee-hives under the lemon trees, a stone-flagged kitchen and about fifteen acres of garden and orchard sloping to the sea. Behind it rise the small hills that "really belonged to the tortoises", where Gerry watches the first tortoise of spring break out of the ground. The crumbling wall of the sunken garden is "a rich hunting ground"; a mother scorpion he smuggles in a match-box escapes across the lunch table, and the uproar sends him to French lessons in the town's [[Jewish Quarter (Corfu Town)|Jewish quarter]]. Larry's eccentric guests fill the veranda; every Thursday Theodore and the family climb to the attic to watch the seaplane from Athens land in the bay. From a creaking wooden jetty below the olives the family rows out for moonlit bathing with porpoises and fireflies, and Gerry sets off in his own boat, the _Bootle-Bumtrinket_, among the islets up the coast; Margo sulks on a small island in the bay and is caught by a sirocco. Gerry's birthday party overflows the drawing-room onto the veranda, Leslie shoots at a supposed burglar on the jetty, and in the woodcock winter Larry sets the villa on fire. Faced with Great-Aunt Hermione's visit, the family leaves for the smaller [[Snow-White Villa]].
- **Why it matters:** The move enlarges both the household and Gerry's territory: hills, wall, bay and boat all belong to this house. The book's three parts are named after its three villas, so this house frames the whole middle section, the longest and most crowded of the book.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The book names no village; the view of Albania fits the east coast. Sunvil's Durrell guide places the real Daffodil-Yellow Villa "in Kontokali near the popular town of Gouvia". ⚠️ The pin is Kontokali village, not the house itself. The tortoise hills, bay, jetty and islets are unnamed and are folded into this note.
