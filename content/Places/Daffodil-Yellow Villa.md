# Daffodil-Yellow Villa

The Durrells' second house on [[Corfu]], a Venetian mansion at Kontokali, north of [[Corfu Town]]. It opens the second part of _My Family and Other Animals_, after Larry invites a crowd of friends to stay.

## My Family and Other Animals — Gerald Durrell (1956)
Source: [[My Family and Other Animals]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/289004/my-family-and-other-animals-by-gerald-durrell/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8fa9dec3r3qzqc5g42jn12n)

> The new villa was enormous, a tall, square Venetian mansion, with faded daffodil-yellow walls, green shutters, and a fox-red roof. It stood on a hill overlooking the sea, surrounded by unkempt olive groves and silent orchards of lemon and orange trees.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** When Larry announces that friends are coming, Mother at first refuses, then moves the whole family to a much bigger house Spiro has found. It lies "on the hillside overlooking the shining sea and the dark, eroded hills of Albania", with a sunken walled garden, bee-hives under the lemon trees, a gardener's cottage, a vast stone-flagged kitchen, and about fifteen acres of garden and orchard sloping down to a shallow sea, which Gerry starts to explore.
- **Why it matters:** The move enlarges both the household and Gerry's territory. The book's three parts are named after its three villas, so this house frames the whole middle section.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The book names no village; the view of Albania fits the east coast. Sunvil's Durrell guide places the real Daffodil-Yellow Villa "in Kontokali near the popular town of Gouvia". ⚠️ The pin is Kontokali village, not the house itself.
