# Buddhas of Bamiyan

Colossal Buddha statues cut into the cliffs of [[Bamyan]], Afghanistan (destroyed 2001). The best-known sight of Dervla Murphy's Afghan journey in _Full Tilt_.

## Full Tilt — Dervla Murphy (1965)

Source: [[Full Tilt - Ireland to India With a Bicycle]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL9584029M) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5j6m7r6nyvs4nj7zj20mhy8)

> I woke this morning, looked through my window and almost fell out of bed with excitement. This hotel is built on a 1,000-foot cliff rising sheer from the valley floor and across the valley, distinct in the brilliant, early sunshine, I saw a 120-foot-high statue of the Lord Buddha standing, as it has stood for over 2,000 years, in a gigantic alcove in the golden sandstone mountain – both alcove and statue having been carved with extraordinary skill out of the rock.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** She examines the statues and the maze of monks' caves with the Indians, finds the valley unspoiled, and watches village children stage a bird-fight. She pleads with the Tourist Bureau not to spoil it with cafes and souvenir shops.
- **Why it matters:** The great Buddhas are the best-known sight of her Afghan journey, and she says she could spend a month there.
