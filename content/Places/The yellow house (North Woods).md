# The yellow house (North Woods)

The house in the woods of western Massachusetts whose owners, from colonial runaways to a dying scientist, _North Woods_ follows across four centuries. Pinned as a best guess in the Berkshires.

## North Woods — Daniel Mason (2023)

Source: [[North Woods]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL34028309W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kvc8mvgcfs9jrh6vcnh7n7)

> They reached the valley on the seventh day. Above them, a mountain. Deer track led through a meadow that rose and narrowed northly, crossed through the dark remnants of a recent fire.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Two runaways from the Massachusetts colony reach a valley below a mountain on the seventh day and lay the first stone of a cabin by a brook and pond. Major Osgood later plants his apple orchard there and his twin daughters live out their lives in the yellow house; it passes to the painter Teale, becomes the Farnsworths' Catamount Lodge with its séance, shelters Robert and Lillian, and is finally sold as Catamount Acres to Morris Lakeman and to Nora, before it burns. The nearby market town is Oakfield, with Shadd's Falls and Corbury Junction close by.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's single anchoring place; every chapter returns to it, and its changing woods, orchards and blights are the real story.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The house and Oakfield are fictional.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text puts the land "between the Hudson and the Connecticut" under the Massachusetts General Court, north and west of Springfield, a long hemlock road from Oakfield, bordering a state forest; NPR's review calls it "a yellow house deep in the woods of western Massachusetts". Bettsbridge and Corbury Junction borrow town names from _Ethan Frome_. No source names a real site, so the pin is the Berkshires representative point. Alternatives: the Pioneer Valley hill towns, the Vermont border country.
