# Danforth Avenue

Danforth Avenue is a street running through the east end of Toronto. It turns up in Four Thousand Weeks (Oliver Burkeman): The street anchors the chapter's central shift from regretting limited time to seeing choices as something one gets to make; Burkeman returns to it ('borrowed time').

## Four Thousand Weeks — Oliver Burkeman (2021)

Source: [[Four Thousand Weeks]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL22355777W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fcpvhehthbs4661vgrjfh1)

> “I was early,” he recalled, “so I spent some time in a nearby park, then checked out the shops and restaurants on Danforth Avenue. I stopped in front of a church to tie my shoe.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** David Cain attends an event in Toronto's Greektown district in summer 2018, killing time in a park and walking Danforth Avenue, stopping in front of a church to tie his shoe. Two weeks later a man shot fourteen people on the same stretch of street. Watching eyewitness videos filmed in front of that church and at the corner where he loitered, Cain realises being alive is happenstance.
- **Why it matters:** The street anchors the chapter's central shift from regretting limited time to seeing choices as something one gets to make; Burkeman returns to it ('borrowed time'). The argument needs a real, specific place where an ordinary evening later became a mass shooting.
