# Maidenhead

Thames town in [[Berkshire]], fashionable with Victorian boaters. In Three Men in a Boat the narrator dismisses it as 'too snobby to be pleasant' and passes through quickly.

## Three Men in a Boat — Jerome K. Jerome (1889)
Source: [[Exploration of Three Men in a Boat]] · [[Three Men in a Boat]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1793164W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5gkybkcb0weg7z1bx6q33st)

> Maidenhead itself is too snobby to be pleasant. It is the haunt of the river swell and his overdressed female companion.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** The narrator sketches Maidenhead as the haunt of the river swell and his overdressed companion, full of steam-launches and fashionable hotels, and the party passes through without stopping.
- **Why it matters:** A quick satirical sketch of Thames society; the town's character is the joke.
