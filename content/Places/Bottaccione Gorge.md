# Bottaccione Gorge

A limestone gorge just outside Gubbio in [[Umbria]], Italy. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Walter Alvarez finds the clay layer there that leads to the impact theory of the dinosaurs' end.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80tyc4vv70te1jv8dczhkw1)

> Walter Alvarez was doing fieldwork in a comely defile known as the Bottaccione Gorge, near the Umbrian hill town of Gubbio, when he grew curious about a thin band of reddish clay that divided two ancient layers of limestone—one from the Cretaceous period, the other from the Tertiary.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Walter Alvarez, doing fieldwork in the gorge near Gubbio, grows curious about a thin band of reddish clay between two ancient layers of limestone, one Cretaceous, one Tertiary: the KT boundary. Its thinness suggests something abrupt happened, and he turns to his father, the Nobel physicist Luis Alvarez, which leads to the iridium tests.
- **Why it matters:** This is where the asteroid theory of the dinosaurs' extinction starts.
