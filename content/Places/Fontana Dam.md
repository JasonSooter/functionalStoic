# Fontana Dam

TVA concrete gravity dam on the Little Tennessee River in [[North Carolina]], USA; it is the main case study in Practical Engineering's article on sawing a dam in half to relieve swelling concrete.

## Sawing a Dam in Half (on Purpose) — Practical Engineering (2025)
Source: [Original](https://practical.engineering/blog/2025/12/16/sawing-a-dam-in-half-on-purpose) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ce3hvxapmc6bpxpak8f35j)

> This is Fontana Dam on the Little Tennessee River in North Carolina. At 150 meters (or nearly 500 feet) in height, it’s the tallest dam east of the Mississippi River.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Finished in 1944, Fontana developed cracks found in 1972, and its concrete turned out to be permanently swelling from alkali-silica reaction. In 1976 TVA cut a relief slot through the dam with diamond wire behind a cofferdam, without draining the reservoir; the slot closes over time and has to be recut.
- **Why it matters:** It is the main case study for the article's counter-intuitive fix.
