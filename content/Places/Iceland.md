## The Comfort Crisis — Michael Easter (2021)
Source: [[The Comfort Crisis]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/634446/the-comfort-crisis-by-michael-easter/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5g8w9mzhympf4absgjrb0r2)

> It was a patch of volcanic rock about the size of Kentucky that was covered in jagged rock, ice, and moss.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Easter recounts the Viking settlement of 874 and the harsh climate: nine-month winters, 213 days of rain or snow a year and 40 mph winds. Kari Stefansson argues that centuries of hardship selected for tougher, longer-lived Icelanders.
- **Why it matters:** The island's harshness is the argument itself, the book's closing case for discomfort.

## Panama Papers Prove World Leaders Are Funneling Money Into Tax Shelters — Bridey Heing (2016)
Source: [Original](http://www.good.is/posts/panama-papers) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjs12r2a0w7p1xeshkrymh)

> In Iceland, massive demonstrations aimed at forcing Prime Minister Sigmundur Gunnlaugsson to resign have drawn thousands into the streets.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** After the leak, Iceland sees massive demonstrations that draw thousands into the streets. The Papers showed that Gunnlaugsson used an offshore firm to hide a financial interest in the country's collapsed banks. News broke midday Tuesday that he will resign, making him the first casualty of the leaks.
- **Why it matters:** Iceland is where the leak first produces a concrete political result, a prime minister giving way to street protest. The text names no particular square or building.

## What the Icelandic Know — Pixel Magazine (2016)
Source: [Original](https://medium.com/the-coffeelicious/what-the-icelandic-know-9f34c9d827cf) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jvmfkgn33hkmkxt1hpgwtj)

> Women experience sexism in Iceland as they do in other countries but perhaps it’s not as prevalent.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** An interview with photographer Gabrielle Motola about her portrait project on gender equality in Iceland, a country that tops global equality rankings. She argues sexism persists there despite structural supports like socialised child care, quotas and paternity leave.
- **Why it matters:** Iceland is the whole subject of the interview, but treated as a country-level case study rather than through specific locations.
