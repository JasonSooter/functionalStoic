# Tivoli Gardens

The amusement park beside Copenhagen's Town Hall Square (Rådhuspladsen); see [[Copenhagen]]. In Neither Here Nor There Bryson finds it shut, and the scene by the town hall on his walk back anchors his view of the Danes.

## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74125W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5b4406bn4hfz8rvjbtkqq50)

> I walked across to Tivoli, even though I could see from a distance that it was shut and darkened, as if under dust sheets.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Chapter 10. At the end of Strøget, Bryson admires the neon signs on the roofs of the Town Hall Square and crosses to Tivoli, which won't open for a couple of weeks. Walking back across the square he finds a small crowd by the town hall, where two police officers are gently helping a drugged teenage boy.
- **Why it matters:** The closed park matters less than where it puts him: the police scene beside it is the centre of his argument that Danes are humane and easygoing.
- **Not pinned separately:** Strøget, the pedestrian street he walks from Kongens Nytorv to Rådhuspladsen (he finds it "a tiny bit seedier" every visit).
