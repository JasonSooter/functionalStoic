## Range: Why Generalists Triumph in a Specialized World — David Epstein (2019)
Source: [[Range]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/550188/range-by-david-epstein/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jkzqvf22fes96kqbsc48nb)

> Jentleson also pointed me to Paradise Garden, ninety miles northwest of Atlanta, the painting- and sculpture-filled property of the late minister Howard Finster, the Frances Hesselbein of modern art.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The author visits the property of minister Howard Finster, who began making tens of thousands of artworks at fifty-nine after seeing a face in a paint splotch. On entering he is greeted by a giant self-portrait on a cinderblock wall carrying Finster's message about trying things.
- **Why it matters:** It is the physical home of the late-starter, self-taught art example; the self-portrait's text states the chapter's test-and-learn idea. It would lose its concrete scene if set elsewhere.
