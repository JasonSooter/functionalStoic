# Washington Athletic Club

Private athletic club on Union Street in downtown [[Seattle]]. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it hosts the April 1935 luncheon where the city fêtes the crews who swept California, and Joe Rantz fights back tears on its stage.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86yeexp3ptwfk8t7rvxefm4)

> The procession made its way to the Washington Athletic Club on Union Street.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** After the rain-soaked parade up Second Avenue and Pike Street on April 18, 1935, the three Washington crews are ushered into a smoky room of hundreds of Seattle's leading citizens, each paying seventy-five cents for the luncheon, with Royal Brougham as master of ceremonies and the event broadcast live. Ulbrickson promises, "we'll win at Poughkeepsie and then it will be on to Berlin and the Olympics." Called up on stage, Joe looks out at the chandeliers, white linen and silver, and is overcome. In July 1936, after Penn's Henry Penn Burke tells Washington it must pay its own way to Berlin, the fundraising drive sets up its headquarters at the club; volunteers sell fifty-cent tags, towns such as Sequim send money, and the $5,000 is raised in about two days.
- **Why it matters:** For a boy who had been abandoned and poor, the room full of the city's well-off applauding him is a first public acceptance; the book uses it to mark how far rowing has carried him.
