# Olympic Village (Vancouver)

Neighbourhood on the southeast shore of False Creek, [[Vancouver]], Canada, built as the athletes' village for the 2010 Winter Olympics. In _Happy City_ it is Charles Montgomery's showcase of green urbanism, heated partly by its own residents' wastewater.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84c8d6yr4ksd2tvqz3xfhzy)

> In its role as host city for the 2010 Olympic Winter Games, Vancouver built a village for athletes on the southeast shore of False Creek, a former industrial waterway on the edge of its downtown.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 11. Strolling through about three years after the Games, Montgomery finds a busy bakery-café, children climbing on the giant fiberglass starlings in the plaza, and paddlers launching dragon boats. The neighbourhood utility pipes wastewater to a pumping station under a nearby bridge, where its heat is recovered and sent back as radiant heat and hot water; the plant's flues are lit as public art. He reports that residents cut household emissions by three-quarters.
- **Why it matters:** The Village closes the chapter: the social energy of a dense, walkable neighbourhood is literally also its heating energy.
