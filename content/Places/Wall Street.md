# Wall Street

Street in Lower Manhattan and shorthand for US finance. Peter Singer's "hopeful pragmatist" argument considers earning to give with a Wall Street job.

## The Hopeful Pragmatist — Jed Oelbaum (2016)

Source: [Original](https://www.good.is/people/issue-37-the-hopeful-pragmatist) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jx5gnjbcd56kfadm9xc97j)

> An organization called 80,000 Hours has recommended that some people get a high-paying job on Wall Street—you’re now a high earner and therefore have a lot of money you can donate to the most effective charities.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Singer argues that taking a high-paying finance job to donate heavily may do more good than traditional charity work, and that people with a conscience inside Wall Street firms can influence decisions such as financing a coal mine.
- **Why it matters:** Wall Street is used as shorthand for the finance industry; the argument is about the sector rather than the street.

## Bartleby, the Scrivener: A Story of Wall-Street — Herman Melville (1853)

Source: [[Bartleby, the Scrivener - A Story of Wall-Street]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102732W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kfr6btpxc7n24gwfrqmtay)

> My chambers were up stairs at No.—Wall-street. At one end they looked upon the white wall of the interior of a spacious sky-light shaft, penetrating the building from top to bottom.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The narrator's law chambers, at a Wall Street number he leaves blank, look out on a light-shaft wall at one end and a black brick wall ten feet from the window at the other. Here he employs Turkey, Nippers and Ginger Nut and hires Bartleby, who copies behind a screen facing a wall and then "would prefer not to". On a Sunday the narrator finds that Bartleby lives in the office, and when the narrator moves out, Bartleby stays on in the building, haunting its stairs and landing.
- **Why it matters:** The office is the story's main stage, and the subtitle names the street; its walled-in views mirror Bartleby's "dead-wall reveries", and the deserted Sunday street sharpens his isolation.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Wall Street is real; the building and its number are left blank.

## Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind — Yuval Noah Harari (2014)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17075811W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76zfj1ft22zy0cpdhg5v4)

> The remains of the wall built by WIC to defend its colony against Indians and British are today paved over by the world’s most famous street – Wall Street.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The Dutch West India Company founded New Amsterdam at the mouth of the Hudson to control the river's trade. Threatened by Indians and the British, it built a wall; the British took the town in 1664 and renamed it New York, and the wall's remains lie under Wall Street.
- **Why it matters:** A neat physical link between a Dutch joint-stock empire and modern finance.
