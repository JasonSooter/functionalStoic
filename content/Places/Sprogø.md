# Sprogø

Small island in the Great Belt, Denmark, once the site of an institution for women. It appears in the reading as the history that shocks Helen Russell in The Year of Living Danishly.

## At a glance

## The Year of Living Danishly — Helen Russell (2015)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20000553W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5f0s50kxfsrsxdhawxtz3pa)

> Sprogø, it emerges, was used for the containment of women deemed ‘pathologically promiscuous’, ‘morally retarded’, ‘sexually frivolous’

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** At the Midsummer bonfire a neighbour tells Russell about the women's institution on Sprogø, founded by Christian Keller in 1923 and closed in the 1960s, where women deemed 'pathologically promiscuous' were confined. It prompts her month of enquiry into gender equality.
- **Why it matters:** It is the turning point of the June chapter, puncturing the image of an equal Denmark.
