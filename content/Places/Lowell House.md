# Lowell House

Georgian Revival undergraduate house of [[Harvard University]] in Cambridge, Massachusetts, with a blue-capped bell tower. In _Happy City_ it is the most coveted prize in Harvard's housing lottery, and a lesson in mispredicted happiness.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83qhr39rg196729003z2ccy)

> The most prestigious, Lowell House, with its grand redbrick facades, is a classic example of the Georgian Revival style.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Harvard freshmen are assigned to houses by lottery, and Lowell, with its redbrick facades and landmark bell tower, is the one they most want. Psychologist Elizabeth Dunn asked students to predict their happiness in each house and followed them for two years: those placed in the prized houses were less happy than they expected, and those in the despised ones happier.
- **Why it matters:** Lowell is the book's example of how people overrate architecture and location and underrate community when guessing where they will be happy. See also [[Mather House]].
