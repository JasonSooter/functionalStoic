# South Ormsby Hall

Country house in the Lincolnshire Wolds, [[England]]. In _The Spy and the Traitor_ it is Gordievsky's first refuge in England after his escape from the Soviet Union.

## The Spy and the Traitor — Ben Macintyre (2018)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19749410W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7xy5qq754n7fmtmgsqn2726)

> On Monday evening, Gordievsky found himself in South Ormsby Hall, a grand country house in the Lincolnshire Wolds, surrounded by servants, candlelight, splendid paneled rooms, and admiring people, eager to congratulate him.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** On the Monday evening after the escape, Gordievsky finds himself in this grand house, "surrounded by servants, candlelight, splendid paneled rooms, and admiring people." Exhausted, anxious and "wondering why he had been taken to this English palace in the middle of nowhere," he retires to a four-poster bed. He is later moved on to [[Fort Monckton]].
- **Why it matters:** The book uses the house for contrast: days earlier he was hidden in a car trunk at the Soviet border. Its remoteness also suits a secret guest.
