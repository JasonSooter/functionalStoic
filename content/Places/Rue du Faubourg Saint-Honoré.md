# Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré

The Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré is a long street in Paris's 8th arrondissement, home to grand houses and the Élysée Palace. In _Candide_ it is where a marchioness's card party strips Candide of his money and his diamonds.

## Candide — Voltaire (1759)
Source: [[Candide]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL100672W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m75kr6vrd5ytf8pn3qc3ds)

> Candide, who was naturally curious, let himself be taken to this lady's house, at the end of the Faubourg St. Honoré. The company was occupied in playing faro;

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The Périgord abbé brings Candide to the house of the Marchioness of Parolignac at the end of the Faubourg St. Honoré, where melancholy punters play faro. Candide loses fifty thousand francs, then sits through a supper of talk about books and tragedies. The Marchioness seduces him and takes his two great diamonds, and the abbé's forged letter from 'Cunegonde' follows.
- **Why it matters:** The centre of the Paris satire of polite society as a racket. Only the address is real; the house is invented.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The marchioness's house is fictional; the Faubourg St. Honoré is a real Paris street.
