# Palermo (Buenos Aires)

Large barrio in the north of [[Buenos Aires]], known for its parks and, lately, boutiques and bars. In _Bicycle Diaries_ David Byrne stays there and uses it to argue about gentrification and creativity.

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m76e9b6xk7qh7nf08r01xm26)

> Palermo, the district where we are now having a sandwich, used to be a quiet neighborhood with lots of pocket parks—which are still here, though it’s not so quiet anymore.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Byrne and musician Diego Frenkel eat at a sandwich shop across the plaza from Diego's old apartment while a World Cup match is on. Diego explains that the quiet neighbourhood of pocket parks has filled with boutiques, chic eateries and bars. Byrne compares it with New York and argues that when rising rents scatter artists from the centre, the mixing that feeds creativity is lost.
- **Why it matters:** It grounds the book's recurring argument about gentrification in the very district where Byrne is staying; the comparison with New York only works because Palermo has gone the same way.
