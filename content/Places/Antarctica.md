## These Extreme Travelers Fulfilled Their Craziest Dreams — Karen Emslie (2016)
Source: [Original](http://www.good.is/posts/find-yourself-with-extreme-travel) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjrq5wdazfsr2damk5gpxv)

> Five days later the ship—carrying crew, scientists, engineers, a few family members, and Philbert—set off. For the next 28 days, they navigated their way from base to base, picking up and dropping off researchers, delivering supplies and carrying out repairs.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Philbert's dream destination. After months of setbacks she is accepted aboard a Chilean Navy logistics ship and spends 28 days travelling between bases, seeing penguins, whales and icebergs.
- **Why it matters:** The goal of the second traveler's story; the setting where her dream is fulfilled.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> The southern oceans around Antarctica produce only about 3 percent of the world’s phytoplankton—far too little, it would seem, to support a complex ecosystem, and yet it does.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The southern oceans around Antarctica produce only about 3 percent of the world's phytoplankton, yet the pack ice supports perhaps fifteen million crab-eater seals as well as Weddell seals and emperor and Adélie penguins. Bryson calls the food chain "hopelessly top heavy" and says no one knows how it works.
- **Why it matters:** A puzzle that shows how little we understand ocean ecosystems.
