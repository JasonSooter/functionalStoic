# Florence Baptistery

The octagonal baptistery opposite Florence Cathedral, with a thirteenth-century mosaic cupola, in [[Florence]]; it appears in Carlo Rovelli's argument that the cupola gave Dante the shape of a three-sphere universe.

## There Are Places in the World Where Rules Are Less Important Than Kindness — Carlo Rovelli (2020)

Source: [[There Are Places in the World Where Rules Are Less Important Than Kindness]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21681470W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5b44hkd6by9mc6jvpky88hv)

> Dante left Florence in 1301, while the extraordinary mosaics in the cupola of the Baptistery were being completed.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In "Dante, Einstein and the Three-Sphere", Rovelli argues that the universe of the _Paradiso_ has the shape of a three-sphere, the same shape as Einstein's 1917 model. He suggests Dante took the image from the Baptistery: look up and you see a point of light (the lantern window at the apex of the cupola) ringed by the nine orders of angels, each labelled, "exactly as they appear in the _Paradiso_". An ant starting on the floor (the South Pole) and climbing any wall would end at that point of light (the North Pole).
- **Why it matters:** The building's geometry is the bridge in the argument from medieval imagination to modern cosmology. Without a real interior Dante knew, the claim would be only an analogy.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Rovelli presents the Baptistery as a likely inspiration ("I believe that Dante may have been inspired by one image in particular"), not as established fact.
