# Garo Hills

Hill country in western Meghalaya, north-east India. In 'Toomai of the Elephants' in _The Jungle Book_ it is the elephant-catching ground where Toomai sees the elephants dance.

## The Jungle Book — Rudyard Kipling (1894)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19870W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne2xsmr0gzb4ewke5m349f)

> After that he was taken off timber-hauling, and employed, with a few score other elephants who were trained to the business, in helping to catch wild elephants among the Garo hills.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In 'Toomai of the Elephants' Kala Nag works in the Garo hills drives, where wild elephants are herded into the stockade and Petersen Sahib tells Little Toomai he may enter all keddahs once he has seen the elephants dance. That night Kala Nag carries the boy through the Garo forest to a hilltop clearing where scores of wild elephants stamp until dawn; Petersen Sahib follows the tracks, confirms the dancing-place, and the boy is named Toomai of the Elephants.
- **Why it matters:** The hidden dance-ground in these hills is the story's climax, the thing no man has seen, and the elephant-catching camps anchor it in real north-east India.
