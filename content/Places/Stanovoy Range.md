# Stanovoy Range

Mountain range in the Russian Far East, on the divide between the Lena and Amur basins. In _Last of the Breed_ Joe Mack, reading a stolen map on its southern slope, realises he must winter in Siberia.

## Last of the Breed — Louis L'Amour (1986)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL51048W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6xdsegbfxdkn2q3jjby3ec6)

> He was, he decided, somewhere on a southern slope of what was called the Stanovoy Range. There was no chance he could escape Siberia now.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** After stealing a map of the Trans-Baikal from an engineer's shack on the railroad line, Joe Mack studies it in a hollow of birch and larch and places himself here. Marching northeast, he finds a cul-de-sac ringed by high mountains with an abandoned log house he guesses was a convict mine camp, kills a deer and smokes the meat for three days, then crosses a low saddle where a helicopter flies through the pass while he hides among granite slabs.
- **Why it matters:** This is where the escape plan changes: the range and the coming winter close the way to the Bering Strait, so he turns south to find a place to hide out until spring.
