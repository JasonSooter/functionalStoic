# Mark Twain Boyhood Home

Twain's childhood house in Hannibal, Missouri. It is the documented model for Aunt Polly's house and the whitewashed fence in Tom Sawyer.

## The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, Complete — Mark Twain (1876)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL53919W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k7hbaztebxv8q76vbw0ctd)

> Tom appeared on the sidewalk with a bucket of whitewash and a long-handled brush. He surveyed the fence, and all gladness left him and a deep melancholy settled down upon his spirit. Thirty yards of board fence nine feet high.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Aunt Polly's house on the village street is Tom's home base. On a bright Saturday he is set to whitewash thirty yards of board fence, and tricks Ben Rogers and the other boys into paying for the privilege of doing it for him. He later creeps back to the house at night from Jackson's Island to look in at the sitting-room window.
- **Why it matters:** The most famous scene in the book, and it fixes the domestic home base (kitchen, back rooms, outside stairway, cow-stable alley) from which Tom escapes.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Aunt Polly's house is fictional; reference works identify the Mark Twain Boyhood Home in Hannibal, with its 'Tom Sawyer fence', as the model.
