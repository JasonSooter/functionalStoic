# Poznań

City in western [[Poland]], German Posen during the occupation. In _The Son and Heir_ it is where the resettled Baltic Germans gather and Frans, in SS uniform, wins Wera back.

## The Son and Heir: A Memoir — Alexander Münninghoff (2020)

Source: [Original](https://www.letterenfonds.nl/en/books/heir-and-successor) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m85b09vm54x9tfrjzpeqjf3p)

> Members of the younger Baltic generation gathered daily at Café Schwan, and in no time, three-quarters of Riga’s handsome young men were marching around in SS uniforms.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Posen, a major railway junction in the annexed east, becomes home to most of the resettled Baltic Germans, among them Wera and her mother; Wera works for the chief dramaturg of the Grand Theatre, then is moved to the Deutsche Volksliste office, typing ancestry data that sorts the occupied population by "purity". Young Baltic Germans meet daily at Café Schwan, and many join the SS. Frans arrives in SS uniform with an interpreter's armband, signs the café's guestbook behind the bar and wins Wera back. The author is born in the city hospital near the bombed central station on 13 April 1944, between air raids; at the end of April the family's small house on Langemarckstrasse takes a direct hit, the only house on the street destroyed.
- **Why it matters:** The city links the book's love story to its politics: the reunion that leads to the author's birth happens inside the community that marched into the SS, and he is born in its bombardment.
