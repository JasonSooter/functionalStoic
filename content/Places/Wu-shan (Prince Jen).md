# Wu-shan (Prince Jen)

An enchanted mountain in Lloyd Alexander's fairy-tale China, pinned at the real Wu Mountains on the Yangtze in [[Chongqing]], whose name it shares. In the novel, General Li Kwang and his whole escort are turned to stone there, and the she-eagle Niang-niang nests on its tallest peak.

## The Remarkable Journey of Prince Jen — Lloyd Alexander (1991)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/319277/the-remarkable-journey-of-prince-jen-by-lloyd-alexander/9780142402252) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m887mn915dracg1eajv8ksv4)

> You are in Wu-shan, Mountain of Sorcerers.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Left waiting by the road, Li Kwang mends and tries the old saddle Master Wu chose as a gift. His mare bolts off the road toward high, bare grey peaks and into a cavern whose stone wall splits open on a hidden green land. There the Lady of Fearful Awakenings puts him to sleep, and he wakes turned to stone with all his troops; Master Wu appears and tells him where he is. The mountain comes back twice in later tales. Jen's kite carries the girl Fragrance of Orchid above the clouds to its tallest peak, where "Niang-niang, she-eagle of Mount Wu-shan" teaches her to fly and tells her that kingdoms and borders are human inventions. The painter Chen-cho recognises the ink stone among Jen's gifts as coming "only from one place: a grotto in Mount Wu-shan". At the climax Li Kwang tells Jen that "Master Wu found us trapped in Mount Wu-shan" and that the stone troop marched to Ch'angan to become flesh again.
- **Why it matters:** Losing the escort here strips Jen of his soldiers, so he continues with only his servant Mafoo and the flute girl Voyaging Moon, which turns a royal progress into the humbling journey the book is about. It is also the source of the magic that rescues him in the end: the stone troop and the ink stone that lets Chen-cho paint the tiger that defeats Natha.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The mountain is magical and its geography is not literal.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Best guess. The text gives only weak clues: high grey peaks that blaze red at sunset, a day's fast ride off the road, an enchanted country inside, and a summit above a sea of clouds. No review or reference work places it. The name matches Wushan ("Witch Mountain"), the real Wu Mountains of the Three Gorges, so the pin is at Wushan County. That is a naming echo, not geography: the real range lies far south of the book's other borrowed names (Ch'angan, the Lo, the T'ung Pass). Alternative: any mountain near Xi'an, or no real model at all.
