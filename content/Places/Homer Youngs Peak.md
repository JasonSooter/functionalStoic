# Homer Youngs Peak

Peak on the Continental Divide in the Beaverhead Mountains on the Montana–Idaho border. On its scree slopes Tim Ryback starts a rockslide and gashes his leg.

## The Ultimate Journey - Canada to Mexico Down the Continental Divide — Eric Ryback (1973)
Source: [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8bes4sanpkesawn9j4vewer)

> Only a few yards beyond the point from which I'd witnessed Tim's fall, the summit of Homer Young's Peak came into full view.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** From Lower Rock Island Lake the brothers climb the 3,000-foot scree slope of Homer Young's Peak, a "bleak eminence of brownish grey rock". Tim steps on a boulder that slides, taking him and half the rock field with it; he escapes with a deep gash in his calf, which Eric dresses before they camp on the summit. That night Tim speaks of feeling betrayed by the mountain, and Eric explains the bond between a hiker and nature.
- **Why it matters:** It is the chapter's title event ("Tim's Narrow Escape"); the injury slows them for days and forces the food-saving shortcut that follows.
