# The Chukchi shore (Last of the Breed)

An unnamed Chukchi fishing shore on the coast of north-eastern Siberia, where Joe Mack gets a kayak for the crossing to Alaska at the end of _Last of the Breed_; pinned at the [[Anadyr Estuary]] as a best guess.

## Last of the Breed — Louis L'Amour (1986)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL51048W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6ycpkv8z8xce2akt5bz43jj)

> Major Joseph Makatozi walked along the shore in the gray morning and looked at the gray seas rolling in to beat against the rocky shore.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** By a little cove with the piled roots of great trees, Joe Mack tells an old Chukchi mending nets that he has come "to see the place where once Olaf Swenson traded". The old man knew Swenson, remembers when "we Chukchis crossed the narrow seas to Alaska each year", and, hearing that Joe Mack's grandfather sailed with Swenson, agrees: "the kayak will be lying by those roots." Joe Mack means to cross that night, after going back up the mountain for the man hunting him.
- **Why it matters:** The goal of the whole escape, reached on the route by which, he tells the old man, his people first came to America.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The shore and the old man's village are never named. Olaf Swenson was a real Seattle fur trader on this coast.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Clues in the text: after the Penzhina he crosses "the Mayn" (probably the Main, a tributary of the Anadyr) in larch forest, is "nearing the sea again", and sees "the sea's reflection in the sky" with Alaska beyond the horizon; earlier he wondered how to cross "the Bering Strait or the Chukchi Sea", and a soldier's grandfather sold furs to an American trader "at Emma's Landing". No reference work found names the shore. Wikipedia places Swenson's first trading station on the north shore of the Anadyr estuary, and the estuary is the nearest coast after the Main, so the pin is there. Alternatives: Emma Harbour in Providence Bay (the book's "Emma's Landing"), or East Cape (Cape Dezhnev) on the Bering Strait, a much shorter crossing.
