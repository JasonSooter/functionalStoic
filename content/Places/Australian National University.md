# Australian National University

University in Canberra, [[Australia]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Bryson visits a geochemist here who keeps a rock that may hold the earliest trace of life.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8159kjr1rhxpmsh5ecax4fx)

> sits on a shelf in the office of a friendly isotope geochemist named Victoria Bennett in the Earth Sciences building of the Australian National University in Canberra.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In late 2001 Bryson visits the isotope geochemist Victoria Bennett in the Earth Sciences building of the Australian National University in Canberra. She hands him a striped rock of quartz and clinopyroxene from [[Akilia]] and explains that carbon isotopes and apatite suggest it once held life. The text adds that the university has long been a leading place for dating very old rocks.
- **Why it matters:** It opens the chapter on the origin of life with a scene: the dawn of life sitting on an office shelf.
