## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83dbm4rqvrmbfess839495v)

> Millions of years of isolation had allowed Hawaii to evolve 8,800 unique species of animals and plants.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In the 1890s Rothschild turned his collectors on Hawaii, perhaps the most temptingly vulnerable environment on Earth. Its birds were rare and easy to take: the greater koa finch would fly down if its song was imitated, and the last one was killed in 1896. At least nine species vanished in about a decade of intensive collecting.
- **Why it matters:** Isolation made the islands' life uniquely vulnerable, and collecting in the name of science hastened its end.
