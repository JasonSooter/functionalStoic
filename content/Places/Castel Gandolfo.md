# Castel Gandolfo

Hill town south-east of Rome, [[Italy]], the Pope's summer residence and historic seat of the Vatican Observatory, where Carlo Rovelli's essay on science and faith opens at a conference.

## There Are Places in the World Where Rules Are Less Important Than Kindness — Carlo Rovelli (2020)
Source: [[There Are Places in the World Where Rules Are Less Important Than Kindness]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21681470W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5b44m8gwhr3z0jnnm68mzyv)

> brought together in the beautiful setting of Castel Gandolfo, the summer residence of the Pope on the hills behind Rome

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** "Which Science is Closer to Faith?" opens at a small conference organized by the _Specola Vaticana_ on "Black Holes, Gravitational Waves and Singularities in Spacetime". Rovelli calls the group "a point of light, depth and rationality" amid growing obscurantism. He recalls an earlier visit when George Coyne directed the observatory, and meeting the current director, Guy Consolmagno. A priest-scientist friend from the Specola points him to Paul Dirac's 1968 article on Georges Lemaître, which drives the rest of the essay.
- **Why it matters:** An argument about how science and religion relate is set inside the Church's own scientific institution, at the Pope's residence. Set at a secular university, the essay would lose that framing.
