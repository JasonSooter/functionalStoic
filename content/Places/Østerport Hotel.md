# Østerport Hotel

Hotel by Østerport station in [[Copenhagen]], across the road from the Soviet embassy. In _The Spy and the Traitor_ its restaurant is where Gordievsky and MI6's Richard Bromhead first meet one to one.

## At a glance
| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Spy and the Traitor | Ben Macintyre | turning point | Gordievsky's choice for the first lunch with MI6: a restaurant opposite his own embassy, which makes Bromhead fear a trap. |

## The Spy and the Traitor — Ben Macintyre (2018)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19749410W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7p6zk0j5trj3en1x77ff72h)

> Instead he suggested they meet, in three days’ time, at the restaurant in the Østerport Hotel, directly across the main road from the Soviet embassy.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Gordievsky proposes the restaurant, so close to his embassy that Bromhead suspects a microphone or Soviet watchers. Bromhead walks through the cemetery behind the embassies, crosses Dag Hammarskjölds Allé and sits with his back to the window. Over the buffet they talk of religion, philosophy and music, and Gordievsky says he will file "a very neutral" report. Then the case goes quiet for eight months.
- **Why it matters:** It is the first real meeting of spy and handler, and the venue itself stages the question of the whole recruitment: is he a defector or a dangle?
- **Real vs. fictional:** The book names the hotel. ⚠️ The pin is today's Go Hotel Østerport; that it occupies the same building as the 1970s hotel was not confirmed.
