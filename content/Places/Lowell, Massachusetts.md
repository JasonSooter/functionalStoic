## Walkable City — Jeff Speck (2012)

Source: [[Walkable City]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL36883695M/Walkable_City) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbk2t0e2wnqb5p4cg9xb7e)

> What does this support look like? A great example is Lowell, Massachusetts, which is quickly transforming its downtown through a focus on new housing.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Speck, who grew up nearby, describes how Lowell's downtown housing units nearly doubled after 2000, with most new units market rate, through politics, permitting and pathfinding as explained by assistant city manager Adam Baacke. He later returns to Lowell for its parking garages and free evening curbside parking on restaurant row.
- **Why it matters:** Lowell is the book's recurring small-city success story for downtown housing and for parking policy. It shows a formerly avoided industrial city reviving.
