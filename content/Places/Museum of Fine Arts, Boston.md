> Given her constant search for clues to long-lost forests, it was odd that when she was approached the previous summer about a retrospective at the Museum of Fine Arts, she did not appreciate the opportunity it represented.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Nora helps with a retrospective of William Henry Teale's landscapes at the Museum of Fine Arts and stands in a virtual-reality chestnut forest full of birdsong, realising the scale of loss in the forests she studies.
- **Why it matters:** It is the emotional turning point for Nora, linking back to Teale's paintings of the same woods.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text says only "the Museum of Fine Arts"; Boston and Roxbury appear in the same chapter, so the pin is the MFA Boston.
