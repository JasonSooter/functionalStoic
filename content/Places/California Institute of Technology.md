# California Institute of Technology

Research university in Pasadena, California. In _Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!_ Feynman decides on a walk across campus to stay for his career, and in _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Clair Patterson dates the Earth in a sterile lab here.

## "Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!": Adventures of a Curious Character — Richard P. Feynman (1985)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL514629W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kfqp8dcqdq6bgtvqr6srms)

> I didn’t even make it across the campus to get to my office, when another guy came up—Matt Meselson, a biologist who had minored in physics.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Feynman moves from Cornell to Caltech, though his first year is spent in Brazil. After a smog attack he phones Cornell about going back, but the next day he is stopped on the way to his office first by news of Baade's discovery that the universe is older than thought, then by Matt Meselson with his density-gradient result on DNA. He decides he has to stay. Caltech is also where he spends a summer in the biology lab and where his drawings are later shown.
- **Why it matters:** The campus is where his career settles; Pasadena becomes the base for most of the book's later stories.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> For seven years, first at the University of Chicago and then at the California Institute of Technology (where he moved in 1952), he worked in a sterile lab, making very precise measurements of the lead/uranium ratios in carefully selected samples of old rock.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Clair Patterson moves here from Chicago in 1952 and works in a sterile lab, measuring lead/uranium ratios in old rock, because his samples kept coming up wildly contaminated with lead. Later, as he campaigns against leaded gasoline, lead-industry officials repeatedly press the school's trustees to shut him up or let him go.
- **Why it matters:** His sterile-lab work yields both the age of the Earth and the alarm about lead pollution.
