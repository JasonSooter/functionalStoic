# Forbes' Quarry

Quarry on the north face of the Rock of Gibraltar. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it yields a Neandertal skull in 1848 that nobody recognises.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8332se0mqz2tem3b981avx7)

> It had been found by workers blasting rock from a quarry in Gibraltar in 1848, so its preservation was a wonder, but unfortunately no one yet appreciated what it was.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** A well-preserved Neandertal skull was found by workers blasting rock from a quarry in Gibraltar in 1848, eight years before the Neander Valley find. No one appreciated what it was; it was sent to the Hunterian Museum in London and left undisturbed for over half a century.
- **Why it matters:** Minor: it explains why the first early humans are named after a German valley rather than Gibraltar.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The text says only "a quarry in Gibraltar"; reference works identify it as Forbes' Quarry, the site of the Gibraltar 1 skull.
