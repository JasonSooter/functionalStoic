# Schenectady, New York

City on the Mohawk River north-west of Albany, the old 'door' to the Mohawk country. In _Wedding of the Waters_ it is a stop the canal's backers scorned.

## Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation — Peter L. Bernstein (2005)
Source: [[Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3294009W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kvd6pdayvdm2dqhqkastkm)

> Poor Schenectady—S’coun-ho-ha! The Dutch had settled the town, the first of them exclaiming on his arrival in 1642 that this was “the finest land the eyes of man had ever rested upon.”

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** The name is explained as the 'door' to the Mohawk country. The 1810 commissioners reach Schenectady expecting bateaux that were never ordered and spend 4 July there, Clinton scorning it as a dull village 'destitute of books'. The book recounts its Dutch founding, the 1690 massacre and the 1819 fire, and in 1825 the bypassed town greets the Wedding of the Waters flotilla with a silent dinner.
- **Why it matters:** Schenectady stands for the towns the canal's route passed by and the resentment it caused.
