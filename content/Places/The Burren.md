# The Burren

Karst limestone region in north County Clare, known for its spring flowers. In _The Country Girls Trilogy_ it stands for the Irish homeland that Baba's husband Frank Durack keeps wanting to return to.

## The Country Girls Trilogy — Edna O'Brien (1986)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL69252W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76h3jec05r8mwv6pd7r6q)

> Said we’d build a house in the Burren, nightmare place, all limestone with a few gentians in the spring that people rave over.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Hickey mocks 'these eejits who come over to Burren to look at flowers'. In the Epilogue Baba recalls that Durack, 'mad for roots', wanted to build a house in the Burren, 'nightmare place'. At his fifty-fifth birthday he recites a poem, 'Corca Baiscinn', in honour of his native district; when Kate joins the refrain he throws a bronze sphinx at her.
- **Why it matters:** Minor as a setting, but it names the west of Ireland that the émigrés both romanticise and flee. The quarrel over it is one of Kate's last scenes alive.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Corca Baiscinn, the district in Durack's poem, is an old name for south-west Clare, south of the Burren.
