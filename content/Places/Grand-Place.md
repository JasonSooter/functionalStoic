# Grand-Place

Brussels' central market square, ringed by the Hôtel de Ville, the Maison du Roi and the guildhalls, in [[Brussels]]. It is Bill Bryson's base for killing time in Neither Here Nor There, the one beautiful set piece in a city he finds grey.

## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74125W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5a836gffkbm8jrczy0vfq6s)

> I settled instead for another circuit of the Grand’ Place. It is fetching, no doubt about it.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** With little else to do in Brussels, Bryson keeps circling the square, "the centerpiece of the city," and settles into De Gulden Boot, "the nicest café on the square," where a waiter once shortchanged him for wearing a Manneken-Pis tracksuit. He points out that the Maison du Roi "has never been a royal palace."
- **Why it matters:** The square is the exception that proves his rule about Brussels: one beautiful old set piece in a city of grey offices. Without it, and the restaurant lanes just behind it, the chapter would be all complaint.
