# Bletchley Park

Country estate in Buckinghamshire, England, home of Britain's wartime code-breakers. A review in The Nation calls it one birthplace of modern computing.

## How Everything Became Data — Ben Tarnoff (2023)
Source: [Original](https://www.thenation.com/article/society/how-data-happened/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k8y3v5zpad5880rtjb9c5j)

> Modern computing has many birthplaces, but one of them is Bletchley Park, the English country estate where, during World War II, the mathematician Alan Turing and his colleagues deciphered top-secret Nazi communications encrypted with the Enigma machine.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** The review treats Bletchley as a birthplace of modern computing where Turing's team built bombes and the Colossus computer to mechanize statistical analysis of Enigma settings. The authors are quoted: 'Bletchley mattered because it made data analysis industrial.'
