# Freemasons' Tavern

Former tavern in Covent Garden, [[London]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where thirteen gentlemen founded the Geological Society as a dining club in 1807.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80ghtz7w5ehdw1nt5qzhb89)

> In the winter of 1807, thirteen like-minded souls in London got together at the Freemasons Tavern at Long Acre, in Covent Garden, to form a dining club to be called the Geological Society.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The founders mean to meet monthly over Madeira and dinner, priced at fifteen shillings to keep out those whose qualifications were "merely cerebral." Bryson notes that the society soon grew far beyond a club and came to rival the Royal Society.
- **Why it matters:** It shows geology starting as a gentleman's hobby around a dinner table; the tavern is the social origin of a science.
