# Boulder City (Idaho)

Former mining camp on Boulder Creek above the Kootenai River in Idaho's far north. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is where Joe Rantz, aged ten, is turned out of the family cabin and lives alone in the schoolhouse.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m869xrz4qnn5p321h07see6a)

> Virtually all of the company’s three dozen employees and their families lived in Schnatterly’s mining camp, Boulder City.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** After the Spokane house burns, Harry Rantz takes the family to the Gold and Ruby mine's camp: thirty-five rough cabins on wooden sidewalks along Boulder Creek, a shingled schoolhouse on the hill and a wagon road down to a bridge over the Kootenai and the cookhouse on the Montana side. Nine-year-old Joe rides the steam shovel and races a go-cart down the switchbacks, but Thula, cut off from her violin, finds it dismal. When Joe is ten she makes Harry send him out of the house; he sleeps in the schoolhouse in return for keeping its fire going, and works for the camp cook for his meals. In November 1924 Harry has to rebuild a washed-out bridge on the road to Bonners Ferry to fetch a doctor for Rose's birth, and Thula leaves Idaho for good.
- **Why it matters:** Joe's exile here is the first of the abandonments that shape him, and the daily climbs between cookhouse and schoolhouse build the strength he later brings to the boat.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The camp is real but its site isn't mapped; the pin is on lower Boulder Creek near the Kootenai. ⚠️ The book puts it eighteen miles of mountain road from Bonners Ferry, which the pin does not confirm.
