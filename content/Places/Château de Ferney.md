# Château de Ferney

Voltaire's château at Ferney (now Ferney-Voltaire), in [[France]] just outside Geneva, where he lived from 1758 until shortly before his death. _Great Short Books_ names it as the place where Voltaire wrote _Candide_.

## Great Short Books — Kenneth C. Davis (2022)
Source: [[Great Short Books]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8f01rf2wmv1j7jzt5sbhefr)

> In 1758, he acquired a château near Geneva, where he would spend the rest of his life and where he wrote Candide, published in early 1759.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In Voltaire's biography Davis traces a life of exile and refuge: the Bastille in 1717, England, a lover's chalet, then Potsdam, Geneva and Frankfurt. In 1758 he acquired a château near Geneva, where he would spend his last decades campaigning against the treatment of French Protestants, and where, Davis says, he wrote _Candide_. He returned to Paris only in 1778.
- **Why it matters:** The château is where Davis places the writing of the book that begins in the [[Castle of Thunder-ten-Tronckh]]: a safe border retreat from which Voltaire could mock the powers of Europe.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real. Davis does not name the château; the 1758 purchase near Geneva where Voltaire spent the rest of his life is Ferney. ⚠️ Wikipedia (Candide) says Voltaire is believed to have written part of the book at Les Délices, his earlier house near Geneva, and at Schwetzingen in summer 1758, and the Ferney château was built 1758–66, so the book was likely written mostly before he lived here.
