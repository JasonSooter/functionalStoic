# Jericho

Oasis city near the Dead Sea in the West Bank. A setting in Colum McCann's _Apeirogon_: Bassam's walled home and orchard in Jericho: the book's final scene of night watering and the porch with Salwa.

## Apeirogon — Colum McCann (2020)

Source: [[Apeirogon]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/207433/apeirogon-a-novel-by-colum-mccann/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjr0f7tj0vkxrk3mdenskb)

> Bassam glances back to see an overturned bucket fallen from the porch. No matter. In the morning he will collect the fruit: even in winter the Jericho trees bloom.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In the book's closing cantos (counting down to 1) Bassam drives home late, texts Rami 'Home, brother', and talks with Salwa on the porch over the hookah as they swap the day's news. Then in the last pages he waters the orchard at night, tending the lemon, fig, clementine and other trees, a cigarette behind his ear. The book ends on 'The hills of Jericho are a bath of dark.'
- **Why it matters:** The walled house and garden in Jericho are where the book comes to rest on Bassam's ordinary evening routine, after the large-scale material. Set elsewhere, the closing image of a man watering trees in the dark would lose its anchor in his home and its place under the hills.
