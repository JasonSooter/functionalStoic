# Olekma River

Long tributary of the Lena in eastern Siberia, rising in Zabaykalsky Krai and flowing north into Yakutia. In _Last of the Breed_ it is the wide, guarded river Joe Mack must cross at night on a plank.

## Last of the Breed — Louis L'Amour (1986)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL51048W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6xdsd511hhpfcsvf8dvtkj6)

> To the east was the Olekma, a wide river. Yakov had said crossing might be dangerous, but cross he must.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Yakov warns him: "East is Olekma. Big river. Very dangerous for cross. Too many peoples, boats." Joe Mack watches a steam launch land about a dozen soldiers who camp where two streams enter the river, then crosses that night by pushing a plank through the icy water, drifting about a mile downstream past a barking farm dog and a flashlight. Over the next ridge he finds fifty or sixty workers and machines preparing a roadbed for a new railroad along another river, and is caught at gunpoint by an engineer.
- **Why it matters:** The crossing puts the main obstacle of his eastward run behind him and leads straight into the railroad camp, where a stolen map tells him he cannot leave Siberia before winter.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The text calls the railroad workers "the engineers on the BAM project"; it never says exactly where he crosses, so the pin is a mid-course point of the river.
