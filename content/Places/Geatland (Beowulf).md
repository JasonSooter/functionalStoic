# Geatland

Beowulf's homeland of the Geats, pinned at a best guess on Västergötland in southern Sweden. Hygelac's cliff-top hall, the dragon's barrow and Beowulf's own barrow at Hronesness are all on its coast.

## Beowulf — Seamus Heaney (1999)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1810107W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne605f1wm013r7f9vdb1pd)

> Hygelac the gold-giver, makes his home

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Sailing home from Denmark, Beowulf lands below familiar cliffs, and the harbour guard has the treasure carried up to Hygelac's hall 'on a secure cliff', where Beowulf tells the Heorot story and is given land, a hall and a throne. Fifty years later, as king, he fights a dragon roused from a barrow on a headland by the sea and dies of his wounds; the Geats burn him and raise his barrow at Hronesness, visible to ships.
- **Why it matters:** Geatland is Beowulf's home and the counterweight to Heorot; its coast holds the poem's last battle and its final monument.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Hygelac's hall, Earnaness and Hronesness are legendary and unlocated.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The poem says only that Hygelac's hall stands 'on a secure cliff' a short step from the harbour, a day's sail from the Danish coast, and that Beowulf's barrow stands on a headland 'on the coast' at Hronesness, visible to ships. The Geats are commonly identified with the Götar of Västergötland and Östergötland; Öland and Gotland have also been proposed. Pinned at Västergötland's representative point.
