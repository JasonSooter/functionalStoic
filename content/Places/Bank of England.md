# Bank of England

The UK's central bank on Threadneedle Street in the City of London. In _Around the World in Eighty Days_ a £55,000 robbery there sets Detective Fix on Fogg's trail.

## Around the World in Eighty Days — Jules Verne (1872)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1100007W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne3ba412bjc3nzpr5njhem)

> The affair which formed its subject, and which was town talk, had occurred three days before at the Bank of England. A package of banknotes, to the value of fifty-five thousand pounds, had been taken from the principal cashier’s table, that functionary being at the moment engaged in registering the receipt of three shillings and sixpence.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Described in Chapter III: 55,000 pounds in notes vanished from the principal cashier's table in the paying room, with no guards or gratings in sight. A well-dressed gentleman seen there fits the vague description that later makes Fix suspect Fogg.
- **Why it matters:** The robbery supplies Fix's pursuit and the false charge against Fogg.
