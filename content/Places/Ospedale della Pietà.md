## Range: Why Generalists Triumph in a Specialized World — David Epstein (2019)
Source: [[Range]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/550188/range-by-david-epstein/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjsjmwrmnq2htfak4byx9e)

> Someone, probably her mother, who was probably a prostitute, took baby Anna Maria to the doorstep of the Pietà on the waterfront of Venice’s St. Mark’s Basin, along a bustling promenade.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The Pietà was the largest of four ospedali, charitable institutions where foundlings were dropped into the scaffetta, a drawer in the outer wall. Girls grew up under quasi-monastic rules, learned to read and work, and learned many instruments for concerts in the adjoining church.
- **Why it matters:** It is the central scene of the chapter: an institution whose foundlings became Europe's most celebrated musicians, used to argue for a broad sampling period over narrow early specialization.
