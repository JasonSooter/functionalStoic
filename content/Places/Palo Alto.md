## Walkable City — Jeff Speck (2012)

Source: [[Walkable City]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL36883695M/Walkable_City) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbkyr6esk7w0ffve1q0er9)

> Back in Palo Alto, Alma Place, a nonprofit 107-unit single-room occupancy hotel, was granted a reduced parking requirement of 0.67 spaces per unit.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Speck uses Palo Alto as a progressive city that still burdens affordable housing: Alma Place, three blocks from a train station, was required to provide parking and barred from charging a \$100 monthly fee. He adds that a landscape-reserve experiment never converted to parking.
- **Why it matters:** It is the example of 'progressive' politics trapping pedestrians into subsidizing drivers, and of neighbors' spillover fears blocking parking reform.
