# Barbizon-Plaza Hotel

Hotel on Central Park South at Sixth Avenue in [[Manhattan]], now the Trump Parc apartments. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is Frida and Diego Rivera's base on both of their New York stays, in 1931 and 1933.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)

Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m737hcnmhvsc4cgad1fmn8qc)

> In less than two days, the Riveras had installed themselves in a two-bedroom suite on a high floor of the Barbizon-Plaza, and Diego was at work in the RCA Building.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** On their first New York stay (Chapter 9, from November 1931) the Riveras take an apartment here; Lucienne Bloch recalls that Frida could not stand the hotel because the elevator boys snubbed her as not rich. Back in March 1933 (Chapter 11) they take a two-bedroom suite on a high floor, and Frida spends her days reading, shopping, playing cadavre exquis and teasing reporters, once giving an interview from her bed.
- **Why it matters:** It is Frida's New York base, a place of little painting where her irritation with American class snobbery shows and the strain with Diego builds; after the Rockefeller Center firing the couple leave it for a cheaper flat at [[8 West Thirteenth Street]].
