## Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind — Yuval Noah Harari (2014)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17075811W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m74zpr316bke6b18m6ryb9)

> The journey of the first humans to Australia is one of the most important events in history, at least as important as Columbus’ journey to America or the Apollo 11 expedition to the moon.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** About 45,000 years ago Sapiens crossed the open sea from the Indonesian archipelago to Australia, a feat Harari counts as proof of the Cognitive Revolution. They met giants such as the diprotodon and the marsupial lion, and within a few thousand years 23 of the 24 large species were gone; the chapter weighs hunting, fire and climate as causes.
- **Why it matters:** It opens the 'Flood' chapter and is the book's first case of Homo sapiens as an ecological serial killer.
