# Günterstal

Southern district of [[Freiburg]], Germany, at the foot of the Black Forest. The Wikipedia history of Freiburg's trams treats it as the end of one of the oldest and most awkward lines, today's line 2.

Source: [[Trams in Freiburg Im Breisgau - Wikipedia]] · [Original](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Freiburg_im_Breisgau) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7h2cc9837qb65c3yeand9am)

> Since 1967, when the Lörrach tram network ceased its operations, the route to Günterstal was the southernmost tram route in Germany, until 2014 when Basel extended its network to Weil am Rhein.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the article:** Günterstal was the end of the original line D of 1901, a single-track route that needed passing loops and a trailer car; open summer cars carried holiday traffic. An extension to feed the Schauinslandbahn was planned after 1930, and a line on to Todtnau was once envisaged. Today line 2 ends here, and because the short terminus has no turning loop only bidirectional GT8Z trams can serve it.
- **Why it matters:** It is the network's most constrained old route and its link toward the Schauinsland and the Black Forest; the terminal's layout still dictates which trams can run on the line.
