# Trachis

Ancient town of the Malians in central Greece, just west of Thermopylae on the Asopus gorge. In _Gates of Fire_ the Persian camp fills its plain, and the Spartans' night raid on Xerxes's pavilion runs past its citadel.

## Gates of Fire — Steven Pressfield (1998)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/133742/gates-of-fire-by-steven-pressfield/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8a5e8fe8565sqgn26djny9f)

> Another half hour brought the squads to the edge of a wood directly above the citadel of Trachis. The channel of the Asopus thundered beneath the city walls.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Rooster reports that the king's pavilion stands "just beneath the cliffs at the top of the plain, right by the river", and proposes a raid. On the second night Dienekes's party climbs from Alpenoi over the rotten cliffs of Kallidromos in the rain, past Oita, to a deer thicket and the Persians' new mountain track. It skirts a felled wood where Thessalian cavalry sleep. From the wood above the citadel they see the Persian camp spread over the plain to the sea, then wade down the freezing Asopus past dams and sentries. They cut into the pavilion, set the women's quarters alight and fight into the great hall. They break out into the gale through the royal paddock and walk unchallenged through the waking camp. Alexandros, his hand severed, dies in the deer grove above the citadel at dawn.
- **Why it matters:** The raid is the Spartans' one strike at Xerxes himself, and the deaths it costs, Alexandros's above all, are the emotional peak before the last stand. The mountain track they find is the one the Immortals will use to turn the pass, so the flanking route the story hinges on runs through this ground.
