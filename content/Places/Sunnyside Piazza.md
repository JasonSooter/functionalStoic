# Sunnyside Piazza

Intersection repair at SE 33rd Avenue and SE Yamhill Street in the Sunnyside neighbourhood of [[Portland, Oregon]]. In _Happy City_ it is where a public-health researcher measured what painting a street does to a neighbourhood.

## At a glance
| Source | Author | Role | In one line |

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84ph8y25t69cbwcqcn3edsv)

> In 2001, after nine months of potlucks, workshops, and block parties, dozens of neighbors came together to paint a giant sunflower mural across the intersection of Southeast Thirty-third Avenue and Yamhill Street.

- **Role:** case study
- **In the book:** Chapter 13. Jan Semenza, shaped by the 1995 [[Chicago]] heat wave, and his wife Lisa Weasel rally their neighbours with City Repair's help. They paint a sunflower across the intersection, build a cob wall and raise an iron gazebo's roof by hand rather than by crane. Semenza's students survey residents here and at two other repairs, before and after, against other neighbourhoods.
- **Why it matters:** It turns [[Share-It-Square]]'s idea into evidence: after the repairs fewer people reported depression, they slept better and rated their health higher, and burglaries and assaults dropped nearby.
