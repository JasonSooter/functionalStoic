# Rockefeller Institute

Biomedical research institute (now Rockefeller University) on Manhattan's Upper East Side, [[New York City]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where Oswald Avery shows that DNA carries heredity.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8331ae18twxfnmpykfgyd9n)

> Finally, in 1944, after fifteen years of effort, a team at the Rockefeller Institute in Manhattan, led by a brilliant but diffident Canadian named Oswald Avery, succeeded with an exceedingly tricky experiment

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In 1944, after fifteen years of effort, Oswald Avery's team made an innocuous strain of bacteria permanently infectious by crossing it with alien DNA, showing DNA to be far more than a passive molecule. Alfred Mirsky, a colleague at the institute, did everything he could to discredit the work, and the worn-out Avery resigned and never set foot in a lab again.
- **Why it matters:** The proof that DNA carries heredity, told as a story of scientific rivalry rather than triumph.
