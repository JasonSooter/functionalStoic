# Euclid, Ohio

Suburb of Cleveland, [[Ohio]], on Lake Erie. In _Happy City_ it is where the 1926 Supreme Court zoning case began that gave American towns the power to separate land uses.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83qhmv0as0t3zekq1frh6tc)

> A local real estate developer took the village of Euclid, Ohio, to court to stop it from using zoning to block his industrial aspirations in 1926.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** A developer sues the village of Euclid to stop it zoning out his industrial plans; the fight goes to the U.S. Supreme Court, the village wins, and the federal government soon gives all municipalities the same power. Montgomery traces how this rule hardened into standard codes that cash-strapped towns now download ready-made.
- **Why it matters:** Euclid is the legal origin of the strict separation of uses that the chapter blames for locking sprawl in place.
