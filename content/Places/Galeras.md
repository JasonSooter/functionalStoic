# Galeras

Active volcano near Pasto in southern [[Colombia]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where a party of volcanologists was caught by an eruption in 1993.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Less than two years after the Unzen catastrophe another group of volcano watchers, led by Stanley Williams of the University of Arizona, descended into the rim of an active volcano called Galeras in Colombia.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Less than two years after the Unzen disaster, Stanley Williams of the University of Arizona leads a group of volcano watchers into the rim of Galeras; only two of the sixteen wear helmets. The 1993 eruption kills six scientists and three tourists and badly injures Williams, whose later book _Surviving Galeras_ disclaims any guilt.
- **Why it matters:** One of a string of deaths Bryson cites to show how little even experts can predict volcanoes.
