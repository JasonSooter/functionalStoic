# Ancient Agora of Athens

The civic square of ancient [[Athens]], below the Acropolis. In _Happy City_ it is the first example of a city built around a philosophy of the good life.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83dc3w9bfpgfde91qpg32mk)

> If you wandered into the city-state of Athens a little over twenty-four hundred years ago, you would invariably find your way to the agora, a broad plaza filled with market stalls and lined by the Athenian governing council’s meeting chambers, law courts, marbled temples, altars to gods, and statues of heroes.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Montgomery walks the reader through the agora as it was some twenty-four centuries ago: market stalls among the council chambers, law courts and temples, the place where Socrates questioned his fellow citizens about happiness and where he was later condemned. Nearby, citizens could speak on civic policy from the platform cut into the Pnyx Hill, just beneath the Acropolis.
- **Why it matters:** The agora is the book's starting point for the idea that urban form expresses a theory of happiness: its openness was an invitation to take part in the life of the polis.
