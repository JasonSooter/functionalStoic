# Table Bay

Natural bay at Cape Town, South Africa. In _Nathaniel's Nutmeg_ it is the watering stop where scurvy-stricken English fleets barter for meat before crossing the Indian Ocean.

## Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Giles Milton (1999)

Source: [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Reader's Companion]] · [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1970050W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m82efhnv377ywfpzmj6pe1mt)

> The usual port of call for ships rounding the Cape was Table Bay, a sheltered watering place first discovered by the Portuguese in 1503.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** On Lancaster's first voyage the scurvy-ridden ships put in at Table Bay, the usual port of call for ships rounding the Cape; the crew barter knives for cattle and sheep, hunt seals and penguins, and find fewer than two hundred men still sound, so the Merchant Royal is sent home. On the Company's first voyage in 1601 Lancaster returns there for seven weeks to barter for fresh meat, the crew again viewing the local people with contempt.
- **Why it matters:** It shows the resupply stop the long voyages depended on, and the toll the outward passage took.
