# Astakos

Small port on the Ionian coast of Akarnania in western Greece, facing Ithaka and Kephallinia. In _Gates of Fire_ it is the narrator's childhood home, and its sack sets him on the road to Sparta.

## Gates of Fire — Steven Pressfield (1998)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/133742/gates-of-fire-by-steven-pressfield/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m896hwg8zvbwenpf4x8d1t90)

> My childhood home, before fate set me upon the road which found its end at the Hot Gates, was originally in Astakos in Akarnania, north of the Peloponnese, where the mountains look west over the sea toward Kephallinia and, beyond the horizon, to Sikelia and Italia.

- **Role:** setting, turning point
- **In the book:** Xeones grows up on his father's farm outside the town, which has a small citizen militia, drill grounds and walls. Argive raiders and their Aetolian allies strike at dawn and sack the city. His parents are killed, and he and his cousin Diomache become refugees in the high snowbound mountains above the farms. "Astakos was a port, a poor one, but a western harbor nonetheless, which Argos had long coveted. Now she had it." There, with his hands ruined, he walks out to die under a pine and instead has a vision of Apollo that sends him to the bow.
- **Why it matters:** The fall of his city makes Xeones a stateless outsider. That explains why he can tell the Spartans' story to the Persian king as a witness rather than a citizen.
