## Iceland’s Water Cure - The New York Times — Dan Kois (2016)

Source: [Original](https://www.nytimes.com/2016/04/24/magazine/icelands-water-cure.html) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjr54fhgyn4bv2t9ey94yf)

> I reached the largest hot tub and sank to my chin. For one glorious moment, I felt my mind go blank: There was just my body, my big, stupid body in its stupid bathing suit, enveloped in warmth, the cold wind on my ears only heightening my delight.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** The article opens at the Vesturbaejar pool on a frigid February day in Reykjavik, where the author, hosted by folklorist Valdimar Hafstein, walks bare-chested across a freezing deck to the outdoor hot tubs. He sinks into the largest tub and feels his mind go blank. The scene introduces the argument that communal pools are central to Icelandic well-being.
- **Why it matters:** It is the author's first immersion and sets up the thesis about pools as social and contemplative space. A pool elsewhere in Iceland would serve similarly, but this is the neighbourhood pool the article starts from.
