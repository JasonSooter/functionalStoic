# Göbekli Tepe

Göbekli Tepe is a Neolithic hilltop site near Şanlıurfa in south-east Turkey. In _Sapiens_ its forager-built pillars suggest that religion came before farming.

## Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind — Yuval Noah Harari (2014)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17075811W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m75awcyetajsf5ted6vp6y)

> In 1995 archaeologists began to excavate a site in south-east Turkey called Göbekli Tepe.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Excavators found monumental structures from about 9500 BC built by hunter-gatherers, with pillars of up to seven tons and a fifty-ton half-chiselled pillar in a nearby quarry. Because einkorn wheat was domesticated in the Karaçadag Hills about thirty kilometres away, Harari suggests the temple may have driven wheat cultivation, with the village coming later.
- **Why it matters:** It overturns the image of foragers as simple and supports the idea that shared myths may have caused the Agricultural Revolution, rather than the reverse.
