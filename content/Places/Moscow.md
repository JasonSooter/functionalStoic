# Moscow

Capital of Russia. It appears in Giles Milton's _Nathaniel's Nutmeg_, where Richard Chancellor reaches Ivan the Terrible's court in 1553, in a James Clear article as the city where he hears Richard Branson speak, and in David Brooks's _How to Know a Person_ as the scene of his 1991 interview with Valentina Kosieva.

## Successful People Start Before They Feel Ready — James Clear (2012)
Source: [Original](http://jamesclear.com/successful-people-start-before-they-feel-ready?__s=opxsejwkn4yzqzgashdp) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fye4hqj0gg9etas71svd3j)

> Two weeks ago, I walked into a conference room in Moscow, Russia and sat down ten feet from Branson.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** Clear sits in a conference room in Moscow with about a hundred others as Branson tells the origin story of Virgin Airlines and advises 'just get on and do it'.
- **Why it matters:** The encounter opens the article; the place itself matters little beyond being where Clear heard Branson.

## Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Giles Milton (1999)

Source: [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Reader's Companion]] · [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1970050W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m82ef7tw67482vyy97s5c9gc)

> Dropping anchor in the White Sea close to present-day Archangel, he abandoned ship and trudged his way overland to Moscow.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** Richard Chancellor, who fares better than the frozen Willoughby, anchors in the White Sea near present-day Archangel and travels overland to Moscow. He finds the city "very rude", its houses of timber, but is won over by the splendour of Ivan the Terrible's court and banquet, and Ivan sends him home with a letter granting trading privileges to London's merchants.
- **Why it matters:** Chancellor's visit gives rise to the Muscovy Company, which the book presents as a forerunner of the East India Company; his survival is the counterpoint to Willoughby's fate.
- **Read so far:** about 28% (still reading).

## How to Know a Person — David Brooks (2023)
Source: [[How to Know a Person]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/652822/how-to-know-a-person-by-david-brooks/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8gkd58jd68x5rb2bfbn1tem)

> One of the greatest interviews of my life happened in Moscow. It was 1991. Tanks were in the streets. The whole city was in turmoil, and the Democratic Reform Movement was vying with the Soviet old guard.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In the chapter on asking the right questions, Brooks recalls meeting the ninety-four-year-old Valentina Kosieva in Moscow during the 1991 turmoil. She tells him of the 1905 pogroms, a near-execution around the 1917 revolution, her husband's arrest and exile to Siberia in 1937 and her son's killing by the Nazis in 1944, while he asks only "And then what happened?"
- **Why it matters:** It is his example that a simple, open question can draw out a whole life. The city in upheaval frames a woman on whom "every trauma that had been inflicted on the Russian people had been inflicted"; set anywhere else, the interview would lose that echo of history.
- **Read so far:** about 76% (still reading).
