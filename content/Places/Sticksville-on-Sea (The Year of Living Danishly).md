# Sticksville-on-Sea (The Year of Living Danishly)

Helen Russell's nickname for her unnamed beachside home village in Jutland, Denmark, which is the base for her year-long experiment in _The Year of Living Danishly_. The likeliest real place is Brejning on Vejle Fjord, site of Christian Keller's institutions.

## The Year of Living Danishly — Helen Russell (2015)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20000553W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5f0r1ar7p20ds2tcx42461r)

> Our quiet beachside village is now bustling with barbecuers, swimmers, canoeists and sailors fitting in a second shift of leisure after a not-so-hard day at the office.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Russell's home village through the year: in June the Midsummer bonfire burns on its beach, school-leavers celebrate there, and neighbours tell her about the red-brick institutions that make up most of the village. She writes of a quiet beachside village that fills up with barbecuers, swimmers and sailors after work.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's home base; the village's smallness and quiet shape her whole experiment.
- **Real vs. fictional:** "Sticksville-on-Sea" is Russell's nickname; the book never names the village.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Inferred. The text calls it "Our quiet beachside village" in Jutland, with "the 1920s red-brick institutions that make up most of the village", "set up by Christian Keller, the famous Danish doctor. The guy whose statue’s up there on the hill"; visitors fly in via Billund, and the Big City (Aarhus) is a three-hour round trip. No reference work names the village (Scan Magazine says only that she later moved to Vejle). Keller's Jutland institution was at Brejning on Vejle Fjord (now Kellers Park), so Brejning is the likeliest match; alternatives are Børkop nearby or another village on Vejle Fjord. The "open sea" feel of the text fits a fjord shore only loosely.
