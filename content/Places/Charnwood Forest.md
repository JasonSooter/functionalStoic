# Charnwood Forest

Rocky upland in north-west Leicestershire, in the English Midlands. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ a schoolboy finds a Precambrian fossil there that vindicates Reg Sprigg's Ediacaran discoveries.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Nine years later, in 1957, a schoolboy named John Mason, while walking through Charnwood Forest in the English Midlands, found a rock with a strange fossil in it

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The fossil resembled a modern sea pen and exactly matched specimens Sprigg had found in South Australia. Mason turned it in to a paleontologist at the University of Leicester, who identified it at once as Precambrian; it was named Charnia masoni.
- **Why it matters:** It is the find that made the establishment take Precambrian animal life seriously, after Sprigg's [[Ediacara Hills]] fossils had been brushed off.
