# Soviet Embassy in London

The Soviet (now Russian) embassy at 13 Kensington Palace Gardens in [[London]]. In _The Spy and the Traitor_ its top floor holds the KGB rezidentura where Gordievsky works as a double agent from 1982.

## The Spy and the Traitor — Ben Macintyre (2018)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19749410W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7rsbk6s9tb8f3zs99xqkhk1)

> a stocky man in a peaked leather cap slipped out of the Soviet embassy at Number 13, Kensington Palace Gardens, in London, and headed west toward Notting Hill Gate.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Soon after arriving in London, Gordievsky slips out of the embassy, shakes off any tail through a pub and calls the number MI6 gave him in Copenhagen. Inside, the rezidentura is portrayed as deeply paranoid: windows bricked up except in Guk's office, where speakers pump Russian music between the panes; notices warning DON'T SAY NAMES OR DATES OUT LOUD; secret talks held in a metal-lined basement room. Gordievsky meets his bosses Guk, Nikitenko and Igor Titov here.
- **Why it matters:** It is the enemy's house where the spy now spends his working days, and its atmosphere of suspicion is the backdrop to every risk he takes. The short drive from here to the MI6 safe flat in [[Bayswater]] shapes the whole London operation.
