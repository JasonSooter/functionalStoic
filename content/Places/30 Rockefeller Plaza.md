# 30 Rockefeller Plaza

Skyscraper at the centre of Rockefeller Center in [[Manhattan]], built as the RCA Building. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ its lobby is where Diego Rivera's _Man at the Crossroads_ mural is halted and covered in 1933, the crisis of the couple's New York stay.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)

Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m737hq1qwvpy2nxsnfcvyhdj)

> Rockefeller’s rental manager, followed by twelve uniformed security guards, stalked into the RCA Building and ordered the artist to stop working.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In Chapter 11 Rivera paints his mural facing the main entrance while Frida visits the scaffold two or three times a week. After his portrait of Lenin causes an uproar, on May 9, 1933 guards order him down, he is paid off and the mural is covered; protests and picketing follow, and the mural is later chipped off the wall.
- **Why it matters:** The firing is the central crisis of the New York chapter; it casts Frida as Diego's loyal defender and drives the couple's move downtown and their later quarrels.
