# Walsall

Industrial town on the edge of Birmingham in the West Midlands. Brompton's hinges were first cast here, by hand, at Deeley's Castings.

## The Brompton: Engineering for Change — Will Butler-Adams (2022)
Source: [[The Brompton - Engineering for Change]] · [Original](https://profilebooks.com/work/the-brompton/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8c394nn0fxsfmjhgzxdev90)

> The foundry which we used for the whiteheart malleable iron socket hinges in 2003, when they first came in, was Deeley’s Castings in Walsall, on the outskirts of Birmingham, and it seemed as though the only thing to have changed there since the days of the Peaky Blinders was the menswear fashions.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The author describes visiting the foundry: a furnace no bigger than a wine barrel, windows dark with smoke, a floor caked in casting sand, nobody wearing a visor or ear protection. Moulds were packed by hand and the shop made about eight hinges at a time. It depended on a few craftsmen and couldn't keep up, so Brompton moved to Swan Shephard (see [[Katowice]]). The foundry has closed; its site became a trading estate.
- **Why it matters:** It shows the risk of relying on a handful of artisans, and sets up the jump to an industrial foundry that made Brompton's hinge quality consistent. A nostalgic aside notes Deeley's name survives on replica Walsall FC shirts from 1983–84.
