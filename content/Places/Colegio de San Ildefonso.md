# Colegio de San Ildefonso

Former Jesuit college a few blocks from the Zócalo in [[Mexico City]], which housed the National Preparatory School. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is where the teenage Frida Kahlo runs with the Cachuchas and first watches Diego Rivera paint.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)
Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m72jsekmxteaa00ag2qezkrc)

> The fortress-like colonial structure of reddish-brown volcanic stone that houses the Preparatory School stands only a few blocks from the Zócalo, Mexico’s central plaza

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Frida enters the National Preparatory School in 1922; Herrera traces it to 1868, when the Jesuit College of San Ildefonso became part of the republic's free secular schooling. Its patios are the stage for the Cachuchas, the clique she joins, and for her romance with Alejandro Gómez Arias. In the Generalito, an assembly hall that had been a chapel, the Cachuchas set off a firecracker during Antonio Caso's lecture. In the Bolívar amphitheater, where Rivera is painting a mural in 1922, she steals from his lunch basket and soaps the stairs.
- **Why it matters:** Chapter 3 is built around this school: it moves Frida from Coyoacán into the political and artistic ferment of the capital, and it is where she first meets Rivera, the man who will shape the rest of her life.
