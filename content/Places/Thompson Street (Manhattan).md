## Range: Why Generalists Triumph in a Specialized World — David Epstein (2019)

Source: [[Range]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/550188/range-by-david-epstein/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjs21sf5ntw6z909prdghh)

> They sat her at a sidewalk table in the middle of a leafy block of Manhattan’s Greenwich Village, in front of a cleared chessboard.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In 2007 National Geographic TV seats Susan Polgar at a sidewalk table in Greenwich Village while a white truck bearing a chessboard diagram turns onto Thompson Street, past the deli. She glances at the diagram and recreates it perfectly, then fails to recreate a random arrangement on the truck's other side. The test reprises the De Groot and Chase and Simon chunking experiments.
- **Why it matters:** This street scene is the book's vivid demonstration that grandmaster memory is chunking of familiar patterns, not photographic recall. Set in a lab, it would lose the everyday setting of New Yorkers jaywalking past.
