# The Battery (Manhattan)

The Battery is the park and seawall at the southern tip of Manhattan in New York City. In _Moby-Dick_ it is where Ishmael opens his case that everyone is drawn to the sea, pointing at the crowds of water-gazers on the waterfront.

## Moby-Dick; or, The Whale — Herman Melville (1851)
Source: [[Exploration of Moby Dick]] · [[Moby Dick]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76ne5tvf1eyk163q8v1s8)

> Its extreme downtown is the battery, where that noble mole is washed by waves, and cooled by breezes, which a few hours previous were out of sight of land.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In Chapter 1 Ishmael describes the 'insular city of the Manhattoes, belted round by wharves', with the battery at its extreme downtown and 'crowds of water-gazers there'. He has the reader walk on a Sabbath afternoon from Corlears Hook to Coenties Slip and by Whitehall northward, past thousands of landsmen fixed in ocean reveries on the piers, spiles and rigging. He uses the scene to explain his own urge to go whaling.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's opening argument that water draws every mind, and the point Ishmael leaves from for New Bedford. Any port city would serve the logic, but the Manhattan crowd gives it its concrete, everyday form.
