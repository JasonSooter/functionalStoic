## The iPhone Thought Experiment — Wait But Why (2024)
Source: [Original](https://waitbutwhy.com/table/iphone-thought-experiment) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jvp2213y057gxr0zvxckge)

> I, for one, would be standing naked on a foresty and very crowded Manhattan island:

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** The author imagines himself on a primeval, forested Manhattan with two million starving people, hunting for fresh water, food and tools; the Hudson there is mostly salt water.
- **Why it matters:** Main concrete setting of the thought experiment; its character (crowded, salty river, no farming) drives the argument.

## How To Be A Time Lord: Time Management Techniques To Try — Dennis R. Mortensen (2017)
Source: [Original](https://blog.trello.com/time-management-techniques) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k5qa8atj2mr0efanr6w2tg)

> I walk the full length of Manhattan, which takes me short of three hours.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the article:** The author describes a weekly ritual of walking the entire length of Manhattan without phone or music, taking just under three hours. The walk is his technique for stretching time: he mulls over big challenges while taking in the changing scenery of the city.
- **Why it matters:** The walk through the city is the piece's main concrete technique and the setting where the author's thinking time happens.

## Abundance — Ezra Klein & Derek Thompson (2025)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL44034521W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m77mx8egq3hf3wvhctkq49)

> What they typically cannot do is follow Horace Greeley’s example and build a life in Manhattan, where the median home now sells for $1.1 million.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 1 opens with Horace Greeley, who urged others west but built his own life in New York. The authors say a young family today can't follow his example in Manhattan, where the median home sells for $1.1 million. They cite research showing New York once paid high wages even after living costs, and Michael Bloomberg calling the city "a luxury product".
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's lead example of a superstar city that has turned from a ladder of opportunity into a place you go to spend a fortune rather than make one.
