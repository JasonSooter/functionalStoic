# Seaside, Florida

Planned resort town on the Gulf Coast of [[Florida]], USA. In _Happy City_ it is the New Urbanist experiment that proved a new code could produce a town people loved, and the one critics love to hate.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84pgza3nqeqps7q0x3pt0mq)

> The resulting resort town of Seaside was the most influential piece of urbanism of its era—and among the most controversial.

- **Role:** case study
- **In the book:** Chapter 12, "Retrofitting Sprawl", section "Code Wars". Andrés Duany, Elizabeth Plater-Zyberk and developer Robert Davis tour Savannah and Charleston, then write their own code for Davis's land: narrow connected streets, alleys, small parks, porches, pitched roofs and a mix of uses. The result is an idyll of pastel cottages and picket fences, later the set of _The Truman Show_. Lots sold for as little as $15,000 in 1982; condominiums above town-centre shops later fetched $800,000.
- **Why it matters:** Seaside is Montgomery's proof that changing the code changes the city, and that people will pay a premium for places that work like traditional towns; its lack of socioeconomic diversity is the price of that popularity.
