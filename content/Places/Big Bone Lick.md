# Big Bone Lick

Salt-lick site in Boone County, [[Kentucky]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it yields the first bones of the "great American incognitum," the mastodon.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80ghwp5d9yak3f34qzmfwjj)

> The first of these bones had been discovered at a place called Big Bone Lick in Kentucky, but soon others were turning up all over.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Philadelphia naturalists assemble bones of a giant elephant-like creature, first called the great American incognitum and later identified, not quite correctly, as a mammoth. The first came from Big Bone Lick, and Americans seize on the beast to refute Buffon's claim that New World animals were degenerate.
- **Why it matters:** A brief but pivotal mention: the find turns fossil bones into national pride. Minor as a setting; the book does not take you there.
