# Bacharach

Small wine town in the Rhine Gorge, [[Rhineland-Palatinate]]. In _German: How to Speak and Write It_ it is the viewpoint of the book's frontispiece, a picture of the Rhine taken from the vineyard terraces above the town.

## German: How to Speak and Write It — Joseph Rosenberg (1962)
Source: [[German - How to Speak and Write It]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kn8qvmp0nfpexh4f1ktnk4s7)

> This picture is taken from the steep vineyard terraces above the little town of Bacharach, in the picturesque 80-mile gorge through which the river flows.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The frontispiece, captioned "The Rhine—chief waterway of Germany", introduces the river for its importance as a commercial waterway serving a vast industrial area and for its "far-famed romantic beauty", which give it "a special place in German life". The photograph looks down from the terraced vineyards above Bacharach into the 80-mile gorge.
- **Why it matters:** It is the first image of German life the course offers, pairing industry and romance in one view. Bacharach is incidental as a town but specific as the vantage point.
