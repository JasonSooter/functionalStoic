# The city (The Dry Heart)

The city is the unnamed Italian city where _The Dry Heart_ is set, pinned at [[Turin]] as a best guess (see [[Italy]]). Almost all of Natalia Ginzburg's novella happens here, from the narrator's boarding house to her marriage.

## The Dry Heart — Natalia Ginzburg (1947)

Source: [[The Dry Heart]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1449796W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ddmjq2gt2rexhy9fbt0tfw)

> We were leaning up against the wall of a bridge. It was dark and wagons were passing slowly along the street with paper lanterns swinging under the horses’ bellies, while birds whirred out of the tall rough grass beside the river.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Almost the whole novella happens here: the gloomy boarding house where the narrator lives as a young teacher, the bridge over the river where she tells Alberto she loves him and he doesn't answer in kind, the cafés, the park bench in the rain where she sits after shooting him, and the house "at the end of a quiet street, surrounded by a garden with trees" that she never feels is hers. Higher up there is "a large square overlooking the city", with trams ringing below and distant factory whistles, where Augusto talks to her about Francesca.
- **Why it matters:** The city's anonymity is part of the book's flatness and claustrophobia: a narrator with no name in a city with no name. Every important scene except the baby's death at [[Sanremo]] happens here.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The city is real in feel but never named. The narrator's home village, Maona, is a weekly bus ride away.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Best guess. The text gives a river with bridges, trams, boulevards, factories, a hill with a square overlooking the city, and lakes within easy reach for holidays. Rome is always somewhere else (Alberto goes "to Rome" on business). No critic or reference work found in a quick search names the city. Turin fits the clues (the Po and its bridges, the hill above the city, an industrial city with trams, the northern lakes not far off) and is where Ginzburg grew up and where the book was published (Einaudi). Milan is the main alternative.
