# Holmdel Horn Antenna

Bell Labs' horn antenna on Crawford Hill in Holmdel, [[New Jersey]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where Penzias and Wilson stumble on the hiss of the Big Bang.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8066qja1nm2vmb4jsw665mb)

> In 1965, they were trying to make use of a large communications antenna owned by Bell Laboratories at Holmdel, New Jersey, but they were troubled by a persistent background noise

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In chapter 1 Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson try to use the antenna but are plagued by a steady hiss that comes "from every point in the sky, day and night, through every season." For a year they test circuits, tape seams and sweep out bird droppings, not knowing that a Princeton team thirty miles away was looking for exactly this signal. It is the background radiation left by the Big Bang, and they win the 1978 Nobel Prize.
- **Why it matters:** Bryson uses it as the book's first great accidental discovery: the evidence for the Big Bang found by two people who did not know what they had found. It anchors the opening chapter on the origin of the universe.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The book says only "Bell Laboratories at Holmdel"; the pin is the horn antenna itself, which Wikipedia names as the instrument of the discovery (the main [[Bell Labs]] note is the Murray Hill lab).
