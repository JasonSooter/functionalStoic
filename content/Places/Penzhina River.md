# Penzhina River

River in north-eastern Siberia flowing into Penzhina Bay of the Sea of Okhotsk, in Kamchatka Krai, Russia. In _Last of the Breed_ its wide valley is open ground Joe Mack must get around on his way to the sea.

## Last of the Breed — Louis L'Amour (1986)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL51048W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6ycpj8a1rqfg4tas474gq34)

> Remembering the map, he thought the river was the Penzhina, and if so, it flowed down from the mountains before him and he would have it to cross.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** From a crest he looks over a valley with many small lakes, a road or trail and, six or seven miles away, a town and an airfield. With only tundra between, "there was simply no way he could cross without being seen", so he goes back down the mountain and works north through low hills, guessing from memory of the map that the river is the Penzhina.
- **Why it matters:** More waypoint than scene: it marks his turn north toward the Mayn and the sea, kept away from people to the end.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The town and airfield are unnamed, and the river's identity is Joe Mack's own guess.
