# Shark Bay

Bay on the remote north-west coast of [[Western Australia]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where Bryson stands over living stromatolites, survivors of Earth as it was 3.5 billion years ago.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Boardwalks have been built out into the bay so that visitors can stroll over the water to get a good look at the stromatolites, quietly respiring just beneath the surface.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In 1961 scientists found a community of living stromatolites at Shark Bay, though it took some years to grasp what they were. The bay has since become a tourist attraction with boardwalks out over the water. The stromatolites look like large cow-pats and hold an estimated three billion organisms per square yard; they survive because the water is too salty for the creatures that would graze them.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's one chance to see the planet's earliest life still at work, a "curiously giddying moment" for Bryson; any other site of living stromatolites could stand in, but this is where he went.
