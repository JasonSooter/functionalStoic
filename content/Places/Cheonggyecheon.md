## Walkable City — Jeff Speck (2012)
Source: [[Walkable City]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL36883695M/Walkable_City) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbjqbkwv1tdk79gf74h32f)

> The most celebrated, and properly so, has got to be the Cheonggyecheon (pronounced chung-yay-chun) Freeway in Seoul, where a traffic-choked elevated expressway was hauled down in the mid-2000s, daylighting the river that it had obscured for half a century.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Speck tells the story of the Seoul elevated freeway teardown: a grassroots effort that a mayoral candidate, Lee Myung-bak, adopted and began on inauguration day despite protests by street vendors. A sixteen-lane highway became a boulevard and a 3.6-mile river park, with restored ecology, lower heat and traffic, and property values up 300 percent.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's flagship case for reduced demand: removing a road that carried 168,000 cars a day made the traffic go away. Elsewhere it would just be one more teardown example.
