# Cheonggyecheon

Cheonggyecheon is a stream through central Seoul, South Korea, uncovered when an elevated expressway was removed. Jeff Speck and Charles Montgomery both hold it up as the model freeway teardown: Speck for vanishing traffic, Montgomery for bringing nature back into a dense city.

## Walkable City — Jeff Speck (2012)
Source: [[Walkable City]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL36883695M/Walkable_City) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbjqbkwv1tdk79gf74h32f)

> The most celebrated, and properly so, has got to be the Cheonggyecheon (pronounced chung-yay-chun) Freeway in Seoul, where a traffic-choked elevated expressway was hauled down in the mid-2000s, daylighting the river that it had obscured for half a century.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Speck tells the story of the Seoul elevated freeway teardown: a grassroots effort that a mayoral candidate, Lee Myung-bak, adopted and began on inauguration day despite protests by street vendors. A sixteen-lane highway became a boulevard and a 3.6-mile river park, with restored ecology, lower heat and traffic, and property values up 300 percent.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's flagship case for reduced demand: removing a road that carried 168,000 cars a day made the traffic go away. Elsewhere it would just be one more teardown example.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83qjdpbfb0r3bseq6v9zzx9)

> The modern example was set in 2005 by Lee Myung-bak, a daring mayor of Seoul, Korea, who demolished five miles of elevated downtown freeway to restore daylight to the ancient waterway that ran beneath it.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Mayor Lee Myung-bak tears down five miles of elevated freeway to uncover the old waterway, which becomes a ribbon of meadows and pools. Seven million people visit in its first summer, wildlife returns, and the corridor runs about 6.5°F cooler than the surrounding neighbourhoods.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's proof that biophilic retrofits are possible even in dense, expensive cities.
