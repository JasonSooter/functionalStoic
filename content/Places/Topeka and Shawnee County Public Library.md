## As budgets tighten, people are breaking out their library cards — Joe Hernandez (2026)
Source: [Original](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/05/nx-s1-5992908/libraries-and-cost-of-living) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6vfvghptt0r2x5axnwfv7gb)

> The Topeka and Shawnee County Public Library, where Izzy Rivera goes, logged an 8.1% increase in digital media checkouts last year, though physical circulation is down. There's also been a surge in requests for tech support, such as help with online job applications.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** The piece opens with Izzy Rivera, saving for law school, who leans on local libraries near her home in Topeka: physical books, Libby e-books, Kanopy films, a used-book sale where she bought 15 books for about $20, and a branch where she can spend time without being expected to buy anything. Later the library's own figures appear: digital checkouts up 8.1%, physical circulation down, and more requests for tech help with job applications.
- **Why it matters:** It is the article's human anchor for the claim that people use libraries more when money is tight; the national statistics hang on her story.
