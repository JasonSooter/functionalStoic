# Trinil

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83325fjd3xgancgjdkvewy9)

> found a section of ancient human cranium now known as the Trinil skullcap. Though only part of a skull, it showed that the owner had had distinctly nonhuman features but a much larger brain than any ape.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Eugène Dubois went to the Dutch East Indies in 1887 to find the earliest human remains, digging with fifty convicts lent by the Dutch authorities, first on Sumatra and then on Java. In 1891 his team, rarely joined by Dubois himself, found part of a cranium with nonhuman features but a much larger brain than any ape. He called it the missing link, and it became famous as "Java Man".
- **Why it matters:** It opens Bryson's chapter on human origins with the first deliberate hunt for ancient human bones.
