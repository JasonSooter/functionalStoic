# Red Cross Hospital (Mexico City)

The Red Cross Hospital that stood on San Jerónimo Street in central [[Mexico City]] in the 1920s. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is where Frida Kahlo is taken after her bus accident and nearly dies.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)

Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m72jsmt8jfq9mf4f0h1vs0k8)

> The ambulance came and took her to the Red Cross Hospital, which in those days was on San Jeronimo Street, a few blocks from where the accident took place.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** After the crash at [[Cuahutemotzín and 5 de Mayo (Frida)|Cuahutemotzín and 5 de Mayo]], Alejandro recalls, the ambulance takes Frida here; the doctors think she will die on the operating table. She is operated on for the first time and lies encased in plaster for a month, uncertain of survival, in a ward Herrera describes as grim; her sister Matilde visits daily, and Frida writes that death dances around her bed at night.
- **Why it matters:** It is where the long ordeal of her injuries begins, the first of the hospital beds that recur through the biography.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real; the building is not named. ⚠️ The pin is on San Jerónimo Street at the Claustro de Sor Juana, where a Chilango article places the old hospital.
