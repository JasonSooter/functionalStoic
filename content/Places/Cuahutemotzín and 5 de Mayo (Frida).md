# Cuahutemotzín and 5 de Mayo (Frida)

Street corner at the southern edge of central [[Mexico City]], where Calzada de Tlalpan begins. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is where a trolley hits Frida Kahlo's bus on 17 September 1925.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)

Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m72jsjrkt4whgsrz4s3cwmxt)

> When they reached the corner of Cuahutemotzín and 5 de Mayo and were about to turn onto Calzada de Tlalpan, a trolley from Xochimilco approached.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** On 17 September 1925, Frida and Alejandro Gómez Arias take the nearly full bus home to Coyoacán from the Zócalo. At this corner a trolley from Xochimilco keeps coming "as if it had no brakes" and smashes into it. A steel handrail impales Frida; Alejandro lays her in the display window of a billiard room until the ambulance takes her to the [[Red Cross Hospital (Mexico City)|Red Cross Hospital]]. Frida says the accident took place "on a corner in front of the San Juan market".
- **Why it matters:** This is the event the whole biography turns on: it ends Frida's carefree school years and sets up the pain, the long convalescence and the painting that follow.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The book gives the 1925 street names, Cuahutemotzín and 5 de Mayo, at the turn onto Calzada de Tlalpan, by the San Juan market. No reference work pins the corner exactly; a Chilango article on Frida's neighbourhoods says she described the crossing of Cuauhtemotzín and San Antonio Abad and thinks the crash was probably near Fray Servando and Tlaxcoaque, where Calzada de Tlalpan begins today. The pin is on Tlaxcoaque at that junction; the main alternative is a point a few blocks away on the same avenue.
