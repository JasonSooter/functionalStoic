# The Time Traveller's house (The Time Machine)

The Time Traveller's fictional house in Richmond, Surrey (now London), pinned at Richmond Hill. The story is told at his dinners there, and in 802,701 AD the White Sphinx stands on the same spot.

## The Time Machine — H. G. Wells (1895)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/55966/the-time-machine) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jy9snx2nscs8dxecctqcc7)

> The next Thursday I went again to Richmond—I suppose I was one of the Time Traveller’s most constant guests—and, arriving late, found four or five men already assembled in his drawing-room.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The narrator travels to Richmond each Thursday for the Time Traveller's dinners. The drawing-room, smoking-room and laboratory are the frame for the demonstration of the model machine and for the Time Traveller's dishevelled return and his telling of the whole story. The machine lands on a lawn among rhododendrons beside a huge white marble winged sphinx on a bronze pedestal. When he leaves it to climb the hill, the machine is dragged into the hollow pedestal. He hammers at the bronze panels, sleeps beside it, and is tethered to a circle of a few miles round it.
- **Why it matters:** Anchors the frame narrative in a real Victorian suburban location. The text also says this same hillside is where the machine lands in 802,701 AD, so the house and the future landscape are one spot.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The narrator "went again to Richmond" for the Thursday dinners, and in 802,701 AD the machine lands "on the hillside upon which this house now stands", above the Thames valley. No street is given; pinned at Richmond Hill.
