# Share-It-Square

Painted street intersection at SE Sherrett Street and SE 9th Avenue in Sellwood, [[Portland, Oregon]]. In _Happy City_ it is the neighbourhood piazza where City Repair began.

## At a glance
| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Happy City | Charles Montgomery | setting | Neighbours paint circles on an intersection, beat the city, and turn it into a village square. |

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84ph7bc63ncrnyw46ndvfgb)

> From then on, the intersection would be a piazza. They called it Share-It-Square.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 13, "Painting the Town". Mark Lakeman, back from [[Nahá|Naja]], sees the heart of a village buried under the asphalt at Sherrett and Ninth. He builds a teahouse of salvaged wood around a tree on his parents' corner; when the city orders it removed, thirty neighbours paint concentric circles across the intersection. The Office of Transportation threatens fines, but Lakeman wins a permit, and a book box, message board, produce stand and tea kiosk follow. Montgomery visits on the day of the annual repaint.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's clearest case of citizens retrofitting their own street grid, and the start of a movement copied across Portland, including [[Sunnyside Piazza]].
