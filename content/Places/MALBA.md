# MALBA

Museum of Latin American art in Palermo Chico, [[Buenos Aires]]. In _Bicycle Diaries_ David Byrne stops in for a show of contemporary works and thinks hard about art that pays people to do absurd tasks.

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m76e97zs3mb6zv5e36yzsgpq)

> While in Recoleta I stop by the new contemporary art museum, MALBA, where there is a show called Los Usos de la Imagen, with works mainly borrowed from a large Mexican art collection.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Byrne visits the show _Los Usos de la Imagen_, drawn mainly from a Mexican collection. He spends several paragraphs on works by Santiago Sierra and Francis Alÿs that pay people to perform pointless tasks, finding Sierra's disturbing and ambiguous, and asks how their meaning changes once they are sold in New York.
- **Why it matters:** The museum is the setting for one of the book's sustained arguments about art, money and exploitation; the place itself matters less than the show it hosts.
