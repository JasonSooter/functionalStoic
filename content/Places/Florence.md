## The Lost Towers of the Guelph-Ghibelline Wars — exurbe (2025)
Source: [Original](https://www.exurbe.com/the-lost-towers-of-the-guelph-ghibelline-wars/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5edehxzxbhqy96bassb5pdw)

> After many disasters, Florence’s solution was to BAN private buildings over a certain height, forcing everyone who had a tower to knock the top off down to regulation height, leaving these recognizable stone nubs all around the city.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Florence is where the author sees tower stubs in rough stone amid stucco buildings. After disasters caused by families burning enemies' homes from fireproof towers, the city banned private buildings over a height, so towers were cut down.
- **Why it matters:** The whole piece is about reading Florence's streets and skyline as relics of the Guelph-Ghibelline feuds.

## Renaissance Florence Was a Better Model for Innovation than Silicon Valley Is — Harvard Business Review (2016)

Source: [Original](https://hbr.org/2016/01/renaissance-florence-was-a-better-model-for-innovation-than-silicon-valley-is) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5j6jp0ffmexa51hnhkvvhh8)

> Those hoping to launch the world’s next great innovation hub would be better off looking to an older, even more remarkable genius cluster: Renaissance Florence. The Italian city-state produced an explosion of great art and brilliant ideas, the likes of which the world has not seen before or since.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The essay holds up Renaissance Florence as a model innovation hub and draws lessons from it: Medici patronage of Michelangelo, Leonardo's long apprenticeship, rivalry between Brunelleschi and Ghiberti, and the Opera del Duomo's rotating leadership. It also credits the Black Death's upheaval with enabling the city's flowering.
- **Why it matters:** The whole argument rests on Florence's character as a dense, competitive, patron-driven creative city.
