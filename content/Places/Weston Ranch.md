# Weston Ranch

Subdivision of tract homes in the south-west of [[Stockton, California|Stockton]], California. In _Happy City_ it is Montgomery's worked example of how distance and car dependence made the foreclosure crisis bite.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83dce46tawdxy65keynfn62)

> Imagine you lived, say, along the gentle curve of Erickson Circle in Weston Ranch, a collection of several hundred modest 2,500-square-foot homes just off I-5, south of Stockton.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Driving through, Montgomery counts two dozen FOR SALE signs and families loading moving trucks in five minutes. He tallies the distances from Erickson Circle: miles to the grocery store, gym, pool and mall, and a sixty-mile commute to San Francisco. When gas prices doubled in 2008, those distances pushed commuters into foreclosure; residents had a stronger tie to a distant metropolis than to their own city.
- **Why it matters:** Weston Ranch is the book's case that the dispersed city fails the resilience test, both financially and socially.
