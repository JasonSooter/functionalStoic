# New York Public Library Main Branch

The New York Public Library's main building on Fifth Avenue at 42nd Street, Manhattan. The Daily Stoic uses its famous founders' names to show how fame fades.

## At a glance

| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|

## The Daily Stoic — Ryan Holiday and Stephen Hanselman (2016)

Source: [[The Daily Stoic]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20046443W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5f0qkav52ghk8kssdkwz0s0)

> The library itself was designed by the firm of John Merven Carrère, one of the twentieth century’s most accomplished architects.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In the December 28 entry ('On Being Remembered'), the reader is walked to the library, designed by Carrère's firm and funded by Tilden, Astor and Lenox; its 1911 opening was attended by Taft, Dix and Gaynor. The text uses these names, mostly forgotten, to show the transience of fame.
- **Why it matters:** The building is the entry's main exhibit; its plaques and names carry the argument.

## Uncommon Type — Tom Hanks (2017)

Source: [[Uncommon Type]] · [Original](https://penguin.co.uk/books/1114786/uncommon-type/9781786091338.html) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k5vcwz2aqkkb8vrvzhd77v)

> The exact moment Sue discovered that the Main Library was closed on Mondays, a flash of lightning bleached the scraped sky of Mid-Manhattan.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Sue goes to rent a typewriter for her résumé, but the library is closed on Mondays. She breaks down in tears at the side door on Forty-Second Street, where Bob Roy spots her from a taxi.
- **Why it matters:** Her failure at the library door brings the rescue by Bob Roy and the story's turn.
