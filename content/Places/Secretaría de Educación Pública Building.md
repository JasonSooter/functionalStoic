# Secretaría de Educación Pública Building

Headquarters of Mexico's Ministry of Public Education in the historic centre of [[Mexico City]], whose courtyard corridors carry Diego Rivera's murals. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is where Frida calls Rivera down from his scaffold, and their courtship begins.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)

Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m72xapaf00px8skdreb6arsw)

> I was at work on one of the uppermost frescoes of the Ministry of Education building one day, when I heard a girl shouting up to me, “Diego, please come down from there! I have something important to discuss with you!”

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In Chapter 6, both Frida and Rivera recall the meeting: once she could walk again, she carried her paintings to the Ministry, where he was painting the frescoes in the corridors, and called him down from the scaffold to tell her whether they were any good. Herrera describes the ministry's "three stories of open hallways surrounding a huge courtyard" where Rivera painted his _Mexicanidad_ murals (1923–1928). In 1928 he painted Frida as a Communist militant in the _Insurrection_ panel of the _Ballad of the Proletarian Revolution_ on the third floor.
- **Why it matters:** This is where Frida, still recovering from the accident, puts herself in front of Rivera as a painter; the marriage and her career both follow from the scene. The building's murals also put her into his work for the first time.
