# Natural History Museum, London

Museum in South Kensington, London; in _Life on Air_ home of the fossil named Attenborosaurus, and in _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Richard Owen's creation.

## At a glance
| Source | Author | Role | In one line |

| A Short History of Nearly Everything | Bill Bryson | subject | Owen's creation, opened to all in 1880; his statue rules the main staircase while Darwin and Huxley sit in the coffee shop. |

## Life on Air — David Attenborough (2002)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL24547776M/Life_on_air) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5hy8mkm3qzg45gdef3gj32k)

> I asked Angela where this remarkable object could be seen. It was still on display, hanging on the wall of a gallery a few yards away from where we were talking.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In a gallery, Angela Milner congratulates him on Attenborosaurus; the cast still carries the old label because the redecoration budget was spent.
- **Why it matters:** A gentle anecdote; the museum is the site of the joke about his fossil fame.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Today his statue commands a masterly view from the staircase of the main hall in the Natural History Museum, while Darwin and T. H. Huxley are consigned somewhat obscurely to the museum coffee shop, where they stare gravely over people snacking on cups of tea and jam doughnuts.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Bryson credits Richard Owen with creating the museum, a "grand and beloved Gothic heap" opened in 1880, and with insisting it be open to everyone, including working people in the evenings, with public displays and labels. Owen's statue now looks down from the main staircase, while Darwin and Huxley are consigned to the coffee shop.
- **Why it matters:** It is the one generous legacy of a man the chapter otherwise paints as a schemer; the statues' placement is Bryson's joke about how history ranks the rivals.
