# Glacier River (Independent People)

The glacial river on the moors beyond Summerhouses in _Independent People_, where Bjartur is carried across on the back of a reindeer. Pinned at the Jökulsá á Dal as a best guess.

## Independent People — Halldór Laxness (1934)

Source: [[Independent People]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL757983W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5khcr7bt6ph2r5qvw52za5w)

> Here he was sitting neither more nor less than up to the waist in Glacier River, and that on no ordinary steed, but on the only steed that is considered suitable for the most renowned of adventures.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Searching the moors for a lost ewe, Bjartur tries to catch a bull reindeer on the western bank of Glacier River and is carried by it through the icy water to the eastern bank, the far pastures of another county. Cut off in a blizzard, he spends the night reciting ballads in a snowdrift and only gets home days later, to find Rosa dead.
- **Why it matters:** The crossing is the book's emblem of Bjartur's stubborn self-reliance, and it is the reason he is absent when his wife dies.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Fictional.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ "Glacier River" translates the common Icelandic river name Jökulsá. The text has it flowing north-east across the moors a day's walk from Summerhouses, with Glacierdale farms on the far side. If the valley is modelled on the Jökuldalsheiði heath, the river would be the Jökulsá á Dal, which runs below the heath through Jökuldalur; the pin is that river's article point, near its mouth. Alternative: the Jökulsá á Fjöllum, if the setting is further north.
