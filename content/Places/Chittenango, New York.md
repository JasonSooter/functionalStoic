# Chittenango, New York

Village in Madison County, east of Syracuse. In _Wedding of the Waters_ the canal's hydraulic cement is discovered in a barroom there.

## Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation — Peter L. Bernstein (2005)
Source: [[Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3294009W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kvdt5qs8g2r731rasjfbth)

> The encounter took place at Elisha Carey’s barroom in the middle of town, an unlikely locale for a scientific experiment but a good place for the impatient spectators to calm their nerves.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Canvass White and Benjamin Wright go to Chittenango, about twenty miles east of Syracuse, after contractors find limestone that will not slake. Dr Andrew Barto's mortar ball, left overnight in a pail in Elisha Carey's barroom, is found hard as rock the next morning, and White sets up a cement factory outside town.
- **Why it matters:** Native hydraulic cement freed the builders from costly English imports and made the locks and aqueducts possible.
