# Grimsel

Granite area and pass in the Bernese Alps, [[Switzerland|Switzerland]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ a woodcutter knows its glacier once carried boulders as far as Bern, long before scientists accept ice ages.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8331fpjrks0nwk3ncjpkkv1)

> The woodcutter matter-of-factly told him that the boulders had come from the Grimsel, a zone of granite some distance away.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In 1834 the naturalist Jean de Charpentier walked a country lane with a Swiss woodcutter who said, matter-of-factly, that the roadside boulders came from the Grimsel, carried by its glacier when it once reached as far as the town of Bern. Bryson contrasts this local knowledge with the scientific orthodoxy, which blamed floods or other agencies for such erratic boulders.
- **Why it matters:** It shows the glacial theory starting from plain observation, before Agassiz took it up and the experts resisted it.
