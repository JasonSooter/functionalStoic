## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> collected a gram of random soil from a beech forest near their lab in Bergen and carefully analyzed its bacterial content.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In the 1980s the Norwegian scientists Jostein Goksøyr and Vigdis Torsvik analysed a gram of soil from a beech forest near their Bergen lab and found 4,000 to 5,000 bacterial species, more than the roughly 4,000 types in Bergey's Manual. A second gram from a coastal site a few miles away held 4,000 to 5,000 different species.
- **Why it matters:** It is Bryson's sharpest illustration of how unknown microbial life is; the place is incidental, the ordinary soil is the point.
