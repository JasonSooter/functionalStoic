## Brave New World — Aldous Huxley (1932)
Source: [[Brave New World]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL64365W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kvanjvm8gjk7e1091dwpgn)

> He was being shown a bird’s-eye view of it at present, a bird’s-eye view from the platform of the Charing-T Tower.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Elated by his new fame, Bernard begins John's tour of civilised life on the platform of the Charing-T Tower, with the Station Master and Resident Meteorologist as guides. A rocket from Bombay lands, and John answers the speed boasts by quoting Ariel's girdle round the earth. The tower is also the landmark Henry and Lenina fly past and the measure of distance to the Savage's lighthouse.
- **Why it matters:** It is the hub of the World State's mobility and the start of the Savage's education in the new world, with Bernard at the height of his borrowed importance.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The tower is fictional.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text gives no street, but "Charing-T" is Charing Cross with the cross replaced by Ford's T, as with other Christian signs in the novel. Pinned at Charing Cross.
