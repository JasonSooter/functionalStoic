# Tsipa River

River in Buryatia, eastern Siberia, a tributary of the Vitim. In _Last of the Breed_ it is where the escaped pilot Joe Mack steals his first supplies and crosses on a log, some fifty miles from the prison camp.

## Last of the Breed — Louis L'Amour (1986)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL51048W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6xdsaekh30spcsy5145bbt8)

> Some fifty miles away, not far from where the Tsipa River flowed into the Kalar, Joe Mack was huddled in a thick grove of mixed Japanese stone pine and larch watching a shack built against a cliff.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** Days after his escape, Joe Mack watches a shack against a cliff where two miners live beside a mine portal. When one leaves for town and the other goes to work, he takes a sweatshirt, a dozen cans of fish and a hunting knife. Next morning he crosses a river on a log and hides under it from a passing outboard boat; the narrator adds: "It was the Tsipa, but this he did not know."
- **Why it matters:** This is the first theft of the manhunt, and the missing tins and knife later let the tracker Alekhin pick up his trail. It marks the turn from pure flight to living off the land.
- **Real vs. fictional:** ⚠️ The novel has the Tsipa flowing into the Kalar; in reality both are separate tributaries of the Vitim whose mouths lie close together, so the pin is at the Tsipa's mouth.
