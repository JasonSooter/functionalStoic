# Silicon Valley

Region of the San Francisco Peninsula and Santa Clara Valley in [[California]], the rich but drab foil to China's building in Dan Wang's Breakneck.

## Breakneck — Dan Wang (2025)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL42416011W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dcn9b7aw00ycv2w2cc18pg)

> Silicon Valley can be an amazingly drab place.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 1 opens on the peninsula south of San Francisco: natural beauty, but corporate parking lots and rug shops, homelessness and backup generators for an unreliable grid, which the author contrasts with the working infrastructure he sees on flights back to Asia.
- **Why it matters:** Frames the book's question of why the richest tech hub is surrounded by dysfunction while China builds.

## How Silicon Valley made me think small — Steli Efti

Source: [Original](http://blog.close.io/think-small) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fmrjhq2qagm3jnjkcpy1vx)

> Silicon Valley made me think smaller, but act bigger.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The author is asked at a startup event in Greece whether moving to Silicon Valley made him think bigger. He answers that it did the opposite, and the essay argues for small daily steps over grand visions. Other startup stories (Airbnb, Dropbox) are told as ones the Valley initially dismissed as small or crazy ideas.
- **Why it matters:** The Valley is the foil the essay turns on, the supposed home of big thinking that taught the author to think small. A generic startup hub would lose the irony of the talk's setting and the Airbnb 'came to the Valley' anecdote.

## Silicon Valley’s Unchecked Arrogance — Ross Baird (2016)

Source: [Original](https://medium.com/the-development-set/silicon-valley-s-unchecked-arrogance-d86cbb8db52) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5j7tzrtm1vqttztnyeyrt2y)

> But Silicon Valley’s view towards the rest of the world is often one of unchecked arrogance.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The essay critiques Silicon Valley's belief that the future is created there and its idea of a universal basic income for everyone else. It argues that startup investment is concentrated in a few metros and that a monocrop culture of well-off entrepreneurs solves narrow problems.
- **Why it matters:** The place is the target of the argument and its title.

## Abundance — Ezra Klein & Derek Thompson (2025)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL44034521W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m77kn8a1a5p1d1b8vfmc5q)

> New York leads the world in finance. San Francisco and Silicon Valley lead the world in technology.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In chapter 1 the authors argue that even generative AI, which could in theory be built anywhere, is concentrated in a few square miles along the California coast, where OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta sit near one another. They set this against New York, whose tech success is where "code serves finance".
- **Why it matters:** It supports the chapter's claim that innovation depends on people living near each other, which is why expensive, hard-to-build places matter so much.
