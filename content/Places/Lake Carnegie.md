# Lake Carnegie

Reservoir lake at Princeton, New Jersey, built for Princeton's rowers. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is the course of the July 1936 Olympic trials, where Washington wins the right to row in Berlin.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m878tpfmrdcbwrt2wh6rw7je)

> Lake Carnegie lay mirror smooth, reflecting a translucent blue sky.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** The crew arrives on July 1 and stays at the Princeton Inn; the boys are struck by the stone Gothic boathouse and by the lake itself, which Andrew Carnegie paid to have made by damming the Millstone River. They win their preliminary heat on July 4. Before the final, about ten thousand spectators gather; Washington starts badly when two oars wash out, settles at thirty-four, passes California and then Penn, and finishes a full length ahead. Moch is thrown into the lake.
- **Why it matters:** This race decides who goes to Berlin. The book also uses the lake's wealth to sharpen the contrast with the working-class boys who win on it.
