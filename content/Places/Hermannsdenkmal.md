# Hermannsdenkmal

Nineteenth-century colossal statue of Arminius (Hermann) on a hill in the Teutoburg Forest near Detmold. _Germany: Memories of a Nation_ uses it to show the rise and embarrassment of the Hermann cult.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)

Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m75ga4rs5mk4rj13tapya8)

> Today, over 100,000 visitors a year come to admire the colossal statue of Hermann that stands in the Teutoburg Forest, just outside the town of Detmold.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 7, 'Snow White vs Napoleon', explains the Teutoburg Forest as the dark woodland where in AD 9 Hermann's tribes destroyed a Roman army, a myth taken up by the Romantics and the Grimms. The nearly ninety-foot bronze statue near Detmold, begun after the Napoleonic Wars and finished in 1875, points its sword towards France. No official ceremonies marked the 2009 bimillennium.
- **Why it matters:** It is the chapter's physical symbol of how liberation-era patriotism was coarsened by later nationalism, and why Germans now handle that myth carefully.
