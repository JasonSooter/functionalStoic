# Judengasse (Frankfurt)

The former walled Jewish quarter of Frankfurt am Main, now remembered at the Museum Judengasse. In _Germany: Memories of a Nation_ it represents the confined world of German Jews before emancipation.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)

Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76y26fs2hvjmmqcxjrnys)

> And then there was the Frankfurt Ghetto, the major ghetto in Germany, where the

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 28, 'The new German Jews', describes the Judengasse as Germany's major ghetto, walled in with a curfew on Sundays and Christian holidays, built for a few hundred people but holding about 3,000 by the mid-eighteenth century. Goethe, born nearby, wrote of its confinement and dirt but also respected its people. Mayer Amschel Rothschild founded his bank here.
- **Why it matters:** It is the chapter's main example of formal ghettoisation before emancipation, and the starting point of the Rothschild story.
