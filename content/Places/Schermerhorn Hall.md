# Schermerhorn Hall

Building on the [[Columbia University]] campus in New York. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it houses Thomas Hunt Morgan's Fly Room, where chromosomes were tied to heredity.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83318nyz8679ed1199b6k6x)

> Working out of a small lab (which became known inevitably as the Fly Room) in Schermerhorn Hall at Columbia University in New York, Morgan and his team embarked on a program of meticulous breeding and crossbreeding involving millions of flies

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Thomas Hunt Morgan and his team worked out of the small Fly Room here, breeding millions of fruit flies, each caught with tweezers and examined under a jeweler's glass. After six years of failing to induce mutations they finally got one, and the work let them tie particular traits to particular chromosomes.
- **Why it matters:** Where chromosomes were shown to be at the heart of inheritance, the step between Mendel and DNA.
