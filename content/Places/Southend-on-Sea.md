# Southend-on-Sea

Seaside resort on the Thames Estuary in Essex. In _The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy_ the Infinite Improbability Drive turns deep space into a hallucinated version of its sea front.

## The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy — Douglas Adams (1979)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2163649W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne2aqnf3vz0b0530sfexpt)

> ‘Good God,’ said Arthur, ‘it looks just like the sea front at Southend.’

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In chapter 9, just after being hurled out of the Vogon airlock and picked up by the Heart of Gold at wild improbability, Ford and Arthur find themselves on a pavement that Arthur says looks just like the sea front at Southend. They debate whether they are mad, and the scene (sand, sea, buildings washing up and down, hot doughnuts for ten pence) warps as the Improbability Drive runs, until Southend splits into six segments and melts away. It is a hallucinated, drive-generated version of Southend.
- **Why it matters:** A very English seaside resort is the first reference point Arthur's mind reaches for in deep space, and the scene turns it into a surreal comic set piece.
