# Orca City (Gentle Ben)

Fictional salmon-fishing village on the coast of territorial Alaska, pinned here at a best guess of Kenai on Cook Inlet. It is the home of Mark Andersen and the brown bear Ben in Walt Morey's _Gentle Ben_.

| Source | Author | Role | In one line |

## Gentle Ben — Walt Morey (1965)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL9347710M/Gentle_Ben) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8d25wytj5sbse5cx89e824p)

> Below, on his right, lay the uneven roofs of homes and stores of the fishing village of Orca City. Its one mud street, black and drying under the warm sun, slashed straight through the center of town to the bay and the sea, which stretched away, flat and endless, to the distant horizon.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Thirteen-year-old Mark Andersen lives on a rise above town, with the bay below on one side and the tundra on the other. He secretly visits Ben, Fog Benson's chained brown bear, in a shed at the edge of town, and later leads him out to a creek valley and boulder field on the tundra, where his father Karl finds them asleep together. Karl agrees to buy Ben. Before the season opens the town swells from a few hundred people to thousands, the bay fills with 1,100 seiners, and seven canneries and the fish traps get ready. Mark hauls restaurant scraps up to Ben. The Andersens then leave the town dock on their seiner _Far North_ for the fishing grounds.
- **Why it matters:** The salmon town frames the story: Karl is a seiner fisherman, the season's opening sets the pace of the first half, and the tundra at the town's edge is where Mark and Ben are free together. Set elsewhere, the book would need the same mix of tundra, bears and canneries.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Fictional. The author's note says "There is no Orca City as such. But its description could fit any one of a dozen fishing villages along the coast." The book is set before statehood, in the Territory of Alaska.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Best guess. Clues in the text: "In the distance the Aleutian Range reared a row of white heads into the blue". The town's one mud street runs to a bay and a sea "flat and endless, to the distant horizon", with tundra and a glacier-fed salmon creek behind it. It has canneries, fish traps, cruise ships in summer, and Karl's eyes are "as cold as Columbia Glacier" (a Prince William Sound glacier, used only as a simile). Reference works (Wikipedia, the Oregon Encyclopedia on Morey) say only "Alaska". A sea horizon with the Aleutian Range in view fits the Cook Inlet coast, so the pin is on Kenai. The main alternative is Cordova, which the Columbia Glacier simile suggests but which has no view of the Aleutian Range. Other alternatives are Alaska Peninsula cannery towns.
