# Orca City (Gentle Ben)

Fictional salmon-fishing town on Prince William Sound in territorial Alaska, pinned at Cordova, which the book's clues point to. It is the home of Mark Andersen and the brown bear Ben in Walt Morey's _Gentle Ben_.

## Gentle Ben — Walt Morey (1965)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL9347710M/Gentle_Ben) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8d25wytj5sbse5cx89e824p)

> Below, on his right, lay the uneven roofs of homes and stores of the fishing village of Orca City. Its one mud street, black and drying under the warm sun, slashed straight through the center of town to the bay and the sea, which stretched away, flat and endless, to the distant horizon.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Thirteen-year-old Mark Andersen lives on a rise above town, with the bay below on one side and the tundra on the other. He secretly visits Ben, Fog Benson's chained brown bear, in a shed at the edge of town, and Karl agrees to buy him. In summer the town swells to thousands for the salmon run; the Andersens fish the Sound on their seiner _Far North_, delivering salmon to the Northern Fisheries dock and buying old bread for Ben in Butteville, Chenega, Latouche and Valdez. In autumn Orca City shrinks back to "some three or four hundred persons". Over the winter the men at the Club Bar plot against Ben, Fog Benson attacks him in his shed, and Mayor Henry Beckett gives the family two days' notice. Mark leads Ben down Main Street at dawn to the Northern Fisheries dock, where he is crated onto the _Hustler_ to be shipped away. Later Ed Wright flies from his floatplane field on a lake a mile out of town to rescue Karl from the wrecked _Far North_ near "the Needle", where Clearwater drowns. From then on the town is the supply base for the Windy Point trap.
- **Why it matters:** The salmon town frames the story. The season sets the pace of the first half, the tundra at the town's edge is where Mark and Ben are free together, and the town's fear of the bear drives the second half. "Mr. Jones doesn't understand Ben," Karl says. "Neither does Orca City."
- **Real vs. fictional:** Fictional. The author's note says "There is no Orca City as such. But its description could fit any one of a dozen fishing villages along the coast." The book is set before statehood, in the Territory of Alaska. Butteville, the old gold-rush cannery town a couple of hours from the seining grounds, and the Needle are also unplaced in the text.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Inferred. The Sound around Orca City is clearly Prince William Sound: the mail boat makes a "five-hundred-mile swing around the Sound", and the text names Valdez, Chenega, Latouche, Bligh Island, Constantine Harbor, [[Hinchinbrook Island (Alaska)|Hinchinbrook Island]] and the St. Elias Range. The town itself sits on "Orca City Bay" and "was built on a narrow ledge with the sea on one side and the mountain rising steeply on the other". Its floatplane field is on "a small lake" a mile out of town, and "The road goes only ten miles out of town". Karl's eyes are "as cold as Columbia Glacier". All of this fits Cordova, on Orca Inlet below the mountains, with Eyak Lake just outside town. Reference works (Wikipedia, the Oregon Encyclopedia on Morey) say only "Alaska". The main alternative is Valdez, but the text names Valdez as a separate town. One clue doesn't fit: the text twice has the Aleutian Range in view, which is far from the Sound. This was an earlier run's reason for a best guess at Kenai on Cook Inlet, which the rest of the book doesn't support.
