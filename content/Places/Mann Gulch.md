# Mann Gulch

Gulch in Montana's Gates of the Mountains, site of the deadly 1949 fire. Range uses it as the emblem of firefighters who would not drop their tools.

## Range: Why Generalists Triumph in a Specialized World — David Epstein (2019)

Source: [[Range]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/550188/range-by-david-epstein/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jvtq6rxyb4b272s68dtpvs)

> In Montana’s 1949 Mann Gulch fire, made famous in Norman Maclean’s Young Men and Fire, smokejumpers parachuted in expecting to face a “ten o’clock fire,” meaning they would have it contained by 10 a.m. the next morning.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Smokejumpers expecting a routine fire were chased uphill through dry grass when the fire jumped the gulch; foreman Wagner Dodge told them to drop their tools. Thirteen firefighters died.
- **Why it matters:** Karl Weick's emblematic example of people clinging to tools under pressure.
