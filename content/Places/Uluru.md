# Uluru

Sandstone monolith in the red centre of Australia, sacred to the Anangu. In _Bicycle Diaries_ David Byrne jogs around its base instead of climbing it.

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m76rjc32s9mq7etg2m6p0rff)

> A few days later I reach Uluru (aka Ayers Rock) and Kata Tjuta, another isolated rock formation in the middle of nowhere.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** After driving the Red Centre, Byrne reaches Uluru and Kata Tjuta, co-run by the Anangu, who ask visitors not to climb. A rope still lets many do so, but he jogs the three or four kilometres around the base, past caves whose paintings are layered one over another; he concludes that the act of making them matters more than the images. Nearby, by his motel, he later breaks down crying over an ant mound.
- **Why it matters:** It is the goal of the outback journey and the source of the chapter's reflection on making versus preserving.
