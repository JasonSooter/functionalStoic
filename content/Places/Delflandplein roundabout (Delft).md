# Delflandplein roundabout (Delft)

Large roundabout at the Delflandplein on the south side of Delft. In Dan Kois's New Yorker essay it is the boring masterpiece that shows how Dutch design makes cycling safe.

## How I Learned to Cycle Like a Dutchman – The New Yorker — Dan Kois (2019)
Source: [Original](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/personal-history/how-i-learned-to-cycle-like-a-dutchman) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k7cnwkh67z5am65pbazqcw)

> We biked out of the old city, past the train station and through the most magnificent traffic circle I’ve ever seen: a gigantic roundabout, at the Delflandplein, that capably handles bicycles, cars, express buses, and the tram to The Hague.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** On the last day before summer break, riding the girls to school, the family crosses this roundabout with right of way all around, cars waiting. Kois describes it as a masterpiece of boring design with zero reported crashes in 2014-2017, and the moment the family feels assimilated as fietsers.
- **Why it matters:** The roundabout is the essay's payoff: boring, well-designed infrastructure that makes cycling feel safe.
