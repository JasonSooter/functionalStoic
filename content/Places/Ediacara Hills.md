# Ediacara Hills

Low range in the Flinders Ranges, South Australia, with Precambrian fossils. Attenborough films the earliest animal imprints there in _Life on Air_, and in _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where Reg Sprigg first found them.

## Life on Air — David Attenborough (2002)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL24547776M/Life_on_air) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5hy8bz1jzcbk24cypytpdv7)

> In the first programme that dealt with the very earliest life forms, we filmed at an extraordinary site in the Ediacra Hills in South Australia.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** He films ancient sandstones with imprints of jellyfish-like creatures, and tells the local scientist that Charnia was named after Charnwood Forest.
- **Why it matters:** A key site for the earliest life in Life on Earth, connecting to his Leicestershire childhood.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> when he was sent to make a survey of abandoned mines in the Ediacaran Hills of the Flinders Range, an expanse of baking outback some three hundred miles north of Adelaide.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In 1946 Reg Sprigg, a young assistant government geologist for South Australia, was surveying abandoned mines here when, over lunch, he turned over a sandstone slab covered in fossils older than the Cambrian. _Nature_ rejected his paper and the 1948 geological congress in London was unimpressed, so he left geology for oil exploration; he later retired to an estate in the Flinders Range and made it a wildlife reserve. The find was vindicated only after a schoolboy's discovery in [[Charnwood Forest]].
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's case of a world-changing discovery ignored because it came from the wrong place and the wrong person; the specimens now sit in the [[South Australian Museum]].
