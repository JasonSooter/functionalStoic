# Surat

Port city on the River Tapti in Gujarat, India. In _Nathaniel's Nutmeg_ it is where William Hawkins lands in 1608 to open English trade with the Mughal empire.

## Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Giles Milton (1999)

Source: [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Reader's Companion]] · [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1970050W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8efcwsphd50v7tm562x0na5)

> The town of Surat lay some twenty miles up the River Tapti and was reached by way of a muddy estuary which was only navigable by the smallest of vessels.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The Hector anchors off the sandy bar and Hawkins rows upstream. By the castle he finds "a pleasant green, in the midst of which is a maypole". The Mughal governor Mukarrab Khan impounds and pockets his goods, the Portuguese seize his men, and a band of forty, egged on by a Jesuit, tries to storm his lodgings: "but I was always wary, having a strong house with good doores". In February 1609 he leaves for the emperor's court at [[Agra Fort|Agra]].
- **Why it matters:** It is England's difficult first foothold in India, and the Portuguese hostility here mirrors what the English meet in the Spice Islands.
