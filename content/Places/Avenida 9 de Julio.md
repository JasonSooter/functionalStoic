# Avenida 9 de Julio

Very wide avenue through central [[Buenos Aires]], with the Obelisco at its middle. In David Byrne's _Bicycle Diaries_ it opens his portrait of the city as "the Paris of the south".

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m76425hj27rntdsmjr52kx1m)

> Avenida 9 de Julio is the widest avenue in the world, so there you go Mr. Haussmann.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** In his opening sketch of Buenos Aires's wide avenues, cafés and nightlife, Byrne jokes that without the obelisk plunked in the middle you could land a 747 in the city centre. He then notes the flat streets suit cycling, yet hardly anyone rides.
- **Why it matters:** The avenue stands for the city's Parisian ambitions; it is scenery for the chapter rather than a scene.
