# Henry Ford Hospital

Hospital on West Grand Boulevard in [[Detroit]]. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is where Frida is taken after her 1932 miscarriage, and the title of the painting that launched her mature style.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)
Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m737hjc7j5d3zcg963qq940t)

> Frida was rushed in the ambulance to the Henry Ford Hospital. Lucienne and Diego followed in a taxi.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In Chapter 10, after her miscarriage on July 4, 1932, Frida is rushed here by ambulance and wheeled through a basement corridor of colored pipes. She stays thirteen days, bleeding and weeping, and asks for medical books so she can draw the lost child; she draws a self-portrait, then paints _Henry Ford Hospital_ on sheet metal, with the [[Ford River Rouge complex]] on its horizon.
- **Why it matters:** The miscarriage and hospital stay launch Frida's bloody, unflinching self-portraits in the retablo manner, the turn Herrera treats as the start of her original art.
