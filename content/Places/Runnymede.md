# Runnymede

Water-meadow on the Thames in [[Surrey]] where King John sealed Magna Carta in 1215. In Three Men in a Boat the narrator stops here and daydreams the whole scene of that June morning.

## Three Men in a Boat — Jerome K. Jerome (1889)

Source: [[Exploration of Three Men in a Boat]] · [[Three Men in a Boat]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1793164W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5gky0s4nsgt2t2h2ma45p5e)

> Slowly the heavy, bright-decked barges leave the shore of Runnymede.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Moored near Runnymede after breakfast, the narrator drifts into a long reverie of June 1215: armed barons and anxious townsfolk on the road, Cooper's Hill and the river, King John arriving and being rowed out by barge to the island to seal the Charter.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's most sustained historical set piece, a rare serious passage among the comic episodes. The daydream only works here, on the actual meadow, which is why the trip pauses at this reach.
