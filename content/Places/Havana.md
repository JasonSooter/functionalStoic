# Havana

Capital of [[Cuba]], on the island's north coast. In The Old Man and the Sea its glow on the horizon is what Santiago steers home by.

## The Old Man and the Sea — Ernest Hemingway (1952)

Source: [[The Old Man and the Sea]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL7265161M) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5gm04s7j863105wt7yz9cd4)

> That makes no difference, he thought. I can always come in on the glow from Havana.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** The village's marlin go by ice truck to the market in Havana. Far out at sea, Santiago reassures himself that he can always come in on the city's glow; its fading shows him the current is carrying him east, and its reflected light around ten at night tells him he is nearly home.
- **Why it matters:** Havana is never visited, but its lights are Santiago's only fixed point at sea, the line between being lost and getting home.
