# Brentford

Town in west London on the Thames. Brompton built its bikes here twice: in a railway arch from 1988, and from 1998 in larger premises at [[Kew Bridge]], the "Brentford factory" where much of the book's early action happens.

## The Brompton: Engineering for Change — Will Butler-Adams (2022)
Source: [[The Brompton - Engineering for Change]] · [Original](https://profilebooks.com/work/the-brompton/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8c38t2jzn6e5dzx0mm44vsx)

> Eventually he guaranteed an overdraft and became chairman, and at last Andrew was able to raise enough money to restart work in a railway arch in Brentford, within pedalling distance from his flat.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** After four years without production, investor Julian Vereker guaranteed an overdraft, and Andrew restarted work in a Brentford railway arch in 1988. Master brazer Abdul El-Saidi had his first day there, after answering an advertisement at the Ealing Job Centre. In 1998 Brompton moved to larger premises at Kew Bridge, Brentford, which the book calls "the Brentford factory": it describes the brazing department and its training academy there. In 2004, while tidying the stores of this "Aladdin's Cave", Will Butler-Adams found an abandoned titanium rear frame, which led to the titanium Superlight. The assembly line here is also the stage for the 2006–07 fight over a new pay system. In chapter 8 Will clears the cluttered mezzanine for offices, builds a second one in 2008, and by about 2012 there is room for only one assembly line, so a second shift runs, with mobile offices and a canteen in the yard and dispatch exiled to [[Unit 19 (Brentford)|Unit 19]]: "In Brentford, though, the space was gradually strangling us."
- **Why it matters:** Brentford is where Brompton became a real manufacturer, and where Will learned the business from Andrew after joining in 2002. The brazing skills built up in the arch and at Kew Bridge are what the book treats as the core of the bike, and the cramped site is what forced the move to [[Greenford]].
- **Real vs. fictional:** Both sites are real but unaddressed in the text; the pin is the town.
