# 716 Montgomery Street

Address in San Francisco's old artists' quarter, near [[Montgomery Street (San Francisco)|Montgomery Street]]'s north end. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is sculptor Ralph Stackpole's studio, Frida and Diego Rivera's home on their first stay in the United States.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)
Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m72xawrz3w1c5mtxqr5hdyh3)

> They arrived on November 10 and moved into Ralph Stackpole’s large studio at 716 Montgomery Street, in the old artists’ quarter.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In Chapter 9, "Gringolandia", Frida and Rivera arrive in San Francisco on November 10, 1930, Rivera holding mural commissions arranged by Stackpole. The painter Lucile Blanch and her husband live two flights below; the Riveras, with no phone, use theirs, and Lucile remembers Frida as too shy about her paintings to show them. Edward Weston records meeting Rivera in "Ralph's courtyard on Jessop Place" that December.
- **Why it matters:** It is the couple's base for their first months in the United States, the "Gringolandia" Frida both enjoys and mocks.
