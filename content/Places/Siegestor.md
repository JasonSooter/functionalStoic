# Siegestor

Triumphal arch on Ludwigstrasse in Munich, built in the 1840s for the Bavarian army. It opens _Germany: Memories of a Nation_ as the model of a monument that admits loss as well as victory.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)

Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m74yb36kjya5h5asprw399)

> The top register on this side of the arch is merely a blank expanse of stone. Underneath this uncompromisingly empty space are the words ‘Dem Sieg geweiht, vom Krieg zerstört, zum Frieden mahnend’

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The Introduction sets the Siegestor, built in the 1840s to celebrate Bavaria's valour in the Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars, beside London's Wellington Arch and the Arc de Triomphe. Its south side, damaged in the Second World War, was left as blank stone over the words 'Dem Sieg geweiht, vom Krieg zerstört, zum Frieden mahnend'. MacGregor adds that the Bavarian army had fought mostly alongside the French, against other Germans.
- **Why it matters:** It sets the theme of the whole book: German monuments that remember defeat and guilt as well as victory and point the past 'admonishingly' towards the future. The last chapter returns to it, calling the new Berlin Schloss as ambiguous as 'the Munich Siegestor with which this book began'.
