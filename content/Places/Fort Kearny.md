# Fort Kearny

Frontier army post on the Platte River in Nebraska. In _Around the World in Eighty Days_ Fogg leads its soldiers to rescue Passepartout from the Sioux, losing the train.

## Around the World in Eighty Days — Jules Verne (1872)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1100007W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne3x4wsykdcb09s2ym7wff)

> Fort Kearney station, where there was a garrison, was only two miles distant; but, that once passed, the Sioux would be masters of the train between Fort Kearney and the station beyond.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Fogg and Proctor arrange a duel at Plum Creek, but the train does not stop and they agree to fight in the rear car. A Sioux band attacks the train, and Passepartout crawls under the cars to uncouple the engine, bringing the train to a halt near Kearney station. Passepartout and two others are taken; Fogg, pressed by his duty, leads thirty soldiers from Fort Kearney south and rescues them, losing the train and about twenty hours.
- **Why it matters:** The decisive delay of the American leg: it costs Fogg the connection to New York, and it shows his sense of duty over his wager.
