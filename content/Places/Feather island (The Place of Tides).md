# Feather island (The Place of Tides)

Fjærøy, a tiny outer island of the [[Vega Archipelago]], Norway, where Anna keeps eider ducks on an island owned by someone else. It is the title place and setting of James Rebanks's memoir, which names it but whose name matches no mapped island.

## The Place of Tides — James Rebanks (2024)

Source: [[The Place of Tides]] · [Original](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/315745/the-place-of-tides-by-rebanks-james/9780241426937) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7h2cp3hkfcqxetp3jvfccam)

> Beneath a little hill in the middle of the island, tucked away from the worst Atlantic gales, was a white clapboard house and some scattered red barns. This seemed to be the final outpost before the ocean.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** On his first visit Rebanks is taken out by fishing boat past tide-marker poles to an island of a few acres, where he meets Anna on a seaweed-covered rock plateau and sees her tame eiders nesting in old hen huts, barns and cow byres. The helmsman says his people always called it "the place of tides"; the guide translates its name as "feather island". On the second journey, with Anna, Ingrid and Henrik, the boat passes the tidy neighbouring station of Flatholm ("This side is Flatholm … beyond is Fjærøy") and the book names it: "The main island in the sea estate of Fjærøyvær was called, simply, Fjærøy". Anna has run it for twelve years; it belongs to "an old man on Vega called Terje". The household airs the huts, dries seaweed on Crow Island, a tidal islet at the northern edge of the estate, and builds nests across the islets; Rebanks and Anna rebuild the stone nests on Ship Island, the rock in front of the house, and row to South Island, the biggest island of the _vær_. Anna's job includes keeping visitors away. Through spring the eiders arrive (the old records count 1,300 eiders in 1900 and 400 a century later; the nest total written on a barn beam has fallen below 200, and Anna struggles to get much over 120), and the household fights off an Arctic skua and three sea eagles that land on Ship Island, whose nests are "almost sacred". The down is dried and cleaned in the South Sea Barn, and Rebanks realises he is "in a place entirely run by women": "Despite the wild and hard nature of these islands, Fjærøy was a place of kindness and warmth: a nursery." Orcas pass through the bay. On an outing Anna takes him to the tern colony south of Fjærøy, a flat islet "swathed with green and yellow roseroot", her goodbye to the wild egg islands of her childhood; the engine fails on the way back and he rows home. Sleepless on the hill in the midnight sun, he breaks down and admits he is homesick. On the last day Anna writes the final diary entry (121 used nests, ninety-eight harvested), they make South Island's huts ready for winter, return "Terje's house" to the way he liked it and leave; its future as a duck station is in Terje's gift.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's title and setting; Anna's work, and Rebanks's search for a better way to live, happen here.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Best guess. The book names the island Fjærøy, the main island of the sea estate Fjærøyvær, reached by boat from [[Vega (Norway)|Vega]] past Flatholm; from its hill [[Måsøyvær (The Place of Tides)|Måsøyvær]] lies to the south, a little west of Vega, behind the island mountain of Søla "on the back of Vega", and Ingrid says her house on Vega can be seen from the island with binoculars. A review in _Resurgence_ also calls it Fjærøy ("feather island"). No mapped island near Vega carries the name (Nominatim finds none in Nordland), so it may be a pen name. Pinned at the archipelago's centre rather than at a specific island. Main alternative: Lånan, the archipelago's largest working eiderdown station (Hakai Magazine), but no source ties Anna to it.
