# Columbia, Missouri

University city in central Missouri. It is the town around William Stoner's whole life in _Stoner_, and where Zapier's founders bootstrapped their company.

## How Zapier Went From Zero to 600,000+ Users in Just Three Years — Groove (2015)
Source: [Original](https://www.groovehq.com/blog/zapier-interview-with-wade-foster) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5hy732x93ta6e01yw5yz9ws)

> We wanted to make a go of it and turn it into a business, but we were in Columbia, Missouri.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** Zapier's founders built the first prototype at a Startup Weekend in Columbia, then kept day jobs and worked nights and weekends there, because the city had little funding and businesses were bootstrapped. The article frames it as the opposite of Silicon Valley.
- **Why it matters:** Origin city and the article's example of a startup outside a tech hub.

## Stoner — John Williams (1965)
Source: [Original](https://www.nyrb.com/products/stoner) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8afpacmk9tczpmk8t1hv9px)

> The three of them—Stoner, Masters, and Finch—got in the habit of meeting on Friday afternoons at a small saloon in downtown Columbia, drinking large schooners of beer and talking late into the night.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Stoner reaches Columbia on foot in 1910 and asks the way to Ashland Gravel, the road to the farm of his mother's cousin Jim Foote, where he works for his board and studies in a windowless attic for five years. As a young instructor he drinks with David Masters and Gordon Finch in a downtown saloon, where Masters calls the University "an asylum" for the dispossessed. After the war Finch hosts a reception for returning veterans at Dean Claremont's white pre-Civil War house, "one of the large pre-Civil War homes that had once been common around Columbia", and there Stoner sees Edith Bostwick pouring tea. He courts her in the blue-velvet parlor of her aunt's house across town on winter nights, and they marry "in the large drawing room of Emma Darley." The newlyweds take "a vacant second-floor apartment in an old barnlike house five blocks from the University," which Edith scrubs "as if it were an enemy to be conquered"; their daughter Grace is born there. In 1924 Edith buys "an old two-storied house only a few blocks from the campus," which Stoner repaints, and he makes its paneled study "with a high north window" his own, until Edith takes it for her painting and moves him to "the glassed-in sun porch at the back of the house," driving him to work in his office at the University.
- **Why it matters:** The town is the narrow world Stoner never leaves; its rooms, the attic, the saloon, the dean's house, the aunt's parlor, the apartment and the house with the lost study, mark each step from farm boy to teacher to ill-matched husband. The friendships, the courtship and the slow failure of the marriage all happen here.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The city is real; the Stoners' apartment and house have no address in the text.
