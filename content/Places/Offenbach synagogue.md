# Offenbach synagogue

The former synagogue at Goethestrasse and Kaiserstrasse in Offenbach am Main near Frankfurt, and the newer synagogue across the street. In _Germany: Memories of a Nation_ they trace the fortunes of German Jews across the twentieth century.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)
Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76zr7cv2xkpv05k6m1fx1)

> Its site was at a powerfully symbolic address in the town centre: on the corner of Goethestrasse and Kaiserstrasse.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 28 follows the domed synagogue opened in 1916 at the corner of Goethestrasse and Kaiserstrasse, when the rabbi said 'we have arrived in paradise'. It was desecrated and burnt on Kristallnacht in 1938. After the war the small community of survivors declined to take it back and built a modest, hidden synagogue across the street in 1956, from a cardboard model now in the German Historical Museum; architect Alfred Jacoby, born in Offenbach, later opened it up to the city.
- **Why it matters:** The changing size and position of the buildings track Jewish life in Germany from assimilation through destruction to cautious renewal.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The 1916 synagogue survives as the Capitol theatre; the post-war synagogue stands across the street.
