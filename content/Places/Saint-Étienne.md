# Saint-Étienne

Industrial city in the Loire department of central France, ringed by hills. In Clifford L. Graves's essay on Velocio it is where Paul de Vivie opened his cycle shop, founded _Le Cycliste_ and is honoured each year.

## Velocio, Grand Seigneur — Clifford L. Graves
Source: [Original](https://cycling.ahands.org/bicycling/velocio.html) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7vbqsch4wx3cyt7dwhcep4e)

> In that year, he sold his silk business, moved to Saint-Etienne, opened a small shop, and started a magazine, Le Cycliste.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** The essay opens with cyclists from all over France converging on Saint-Étienne every year to honour Paul de Vivie, "Velocio". In 1887, aged 34, he sells his silk business, moves here, opens a small shop and starts _Le Cycliste_, discovering he can write. The mountainous country around the town makes him see the need for variable gears.
- **Why it matters:** The city is the base of everything the essay credits him with, from the magazine to his gearing ideas; its hills are the practical reason for the derailleur.
