## The Long, Sad History of American Attempts to Build High-Speed Rail, Part I — Brian Potter (2023)

Source: [Original](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/the-long-sad-history-of-american) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fmrhnhskg11qgkxzjr3den)

> A year after its creation, the office (under the newly-created Department of Transportation) signed a contract with the Pennsylvania Railroad to create a high-speed passenger rail route between New York and Washington DC.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The Metroliner demonstration ran on the existing New York–Washington track, which was upgraded but unsuitable for sustained high speed, so it never reached its 160 mph design speed. Later the 1976 Northeast Corridor Improvement Project spent \$1.75 billion trying to cut New York–Washington and New York–Boston times, ran over budget and missed its speed goals.
- **Why it matters:** The corridor is where the article's main US high-speed attempts (Metroliner, TurboTrain, NECIP) played out; its shared, aging track explains the failures. Elsewhere the story would lose its central worked example.
