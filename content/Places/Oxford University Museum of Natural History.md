# Oxford University Museum of Natural History

Natural history museum in central [[Oxford]], England. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it hosts the 1860 Huxley–Wilberforce clash over Darwin.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> The meeting was held at the Oxford Zoological Museum. More than a thousand people crowded into the chamber; hundreds more were turned away.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The 1860 meeting of the British Association, where Thomas Huxley and Samuel Wilberforce, Bishop of Oxford, faced off over Darwin, was held here. More than a thousand people crowded in, after a slumber-inducing two-hour talk by John William Draper; Huxley's retort about being descended from an ape set off uproar, and FitzRoy wandered the hall with a Bible shouting "The Book, the Book."
- **Why it matters:** The famous public showdown over evolution, which Bryson uses to show how Darwin's idea broke into public argument while Darwin himself stayed away.
