# Nā Wai ʻEhā

The four streams of central Maui (Waikapū, Wailuku, Waiheʻe and Waiehu). A subject in Nick Pachelli's “The Fall of the Sugar Goliath in Hawaii Makes Way for the Return of Kalo”: Maui region whose streams were diverted for sugar, now contested for kalo restoration.

## The Fall of the Sugar Goliath in Hawaii Makes Way for the Return of Kalo — Nick Pachelli (2016)

Source: [Original](http://www.good.is/posts/kalo-farming) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjr8zqqxvrez90mq2bgzyr)

> The plight of kalo on Maui can be traced back to 1862 and the establishment of the Wailuku Sugar Company, which subsequently diverted all the stream water in the Nā Wai ʻEhā region to its newly formed sugar plantations.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The region's four streams were diverted for sugar from 1862, drying lo'i and wiping out kalo. Farmers now petition the State to amend instream flow standards to release more water to the four streams.
- **Why it matters:** The water dispute here is the article's core argument about sugar's fall and kalo's return.
