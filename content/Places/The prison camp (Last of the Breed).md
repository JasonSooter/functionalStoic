# The prison camp (Last of the Breed)

The secret Soviet compound in the Trans-Baikal taiga where the American pilot Joe Makatozi is held and interrogated before his escape in _Last of the Breed_; pinned near the [[Tsipa River]] as a best guess.

## Last of the Breed — Louis L'Amour (1986)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL51048W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6xdsjtdbbr17hskdww4xeyd)

> About three hundred yards long, a hundred yards wide, three guard towers to a side, two men in each.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The opening chapters: Joe Mack steps into the long subarctic daylight and sizes up the compound, a ten-foot electrified fence with machine guns in every tower. Zamatev interrogates him, and he meets the captured British scientist Pennington. During a blackout he pole-vaults the fence with a building pipe and runs west, then east, into the taiga.
- **Why it matters:** The camp sets the whole manhunt in motion, and its remoteness, with roads circling it and the coldest land on earth around it, is why escape on foot seems hopeless to his captors.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Fictional; the text never gives its real location.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Clues in the text: Joe Mack's guess that he is "somewhere in the vicinity of Lake Baikal" and the narrator's "an area of the Trans-Baikal"; a basin some six or seven miles across with low mountains to the east; the Trans-Siberian "not far away"; a guard's mention of "Malovsky"; and the alert sent to police at Chita, Nerchinsk, Romanovka, Bagdarin and other towns. Most concrete is that, a few days after the escape, he is "some fifty miles away, not far from where the Tsipa River flowed into the Kalar." No reference work names a site, so the pin is at the Tsipa's mouth; the alternative is nearer Lake Baikal, as Joe Mack first supposed.
