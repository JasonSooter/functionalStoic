# Harvard College Observatory

Harvard's observatory on Garden Street in [[Cambridge, Massachusetts]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ its women "computers" find the yardstick for the universe.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Leavitt worked at the Harvard College Observatory as a computer, as they were known.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Henrietta Swan Leavitt works here as a "computer", studying photographic plates of stars. A fellow computer, Annie Jump Cannon, devises a stellar classification still in use, and Leavitt notices that Cepheid variables pulse with a regular rhythm, which lets their distance be worked out.
- **Why it matters:** Leavitt's standard candle is the tool Hubble needs to measure the universe, so this room of underpaid women underpins the cosmology chapter.
