# Burgess Shale

Fossil site in the Canadian Rockies above Field, British Columbia. Attenborough films its soft-bodied Cambrian fossils in _Life on Air_, and Bryson tells the story of its discovery and reinterpretation in _A Short History of Nearly Everything_.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m82rp7ze84b3wvyerp55fj0z)

> But what he is remembered for now is an astute but lucky find in British Columbia, high above the little town of Field, in the late summer of 1909.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Charles Walcott found a shale outcrop about the length of a city block, near a summit 8,000 feet up, packed with fossils from just after the Cambrian explosion; the popular tale of his wife's horse turning over a slab has no support in his diaries. He dug tens of thousands of specimens each summer from 1910 to 1925. A mudslide at the foot of a Cambrian cliff had preserved the creatures, and decades later Harry Whittington, Derek Briggs and Simon Conway Morris reread Walcott's drawers of fossils and found far stranger animals than he had described.
- **Why it matters:** It anchors the book's account of the Cambrian explosion and the fights over what it means; no other site preserves that moment so fully.
