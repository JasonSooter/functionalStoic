# Lake Malawi

Great Rift Valley lake along Malawi's eastern border. In _The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind_ it is the lake of Kamkwamba's father's trading trips and of William's love for his country.

## The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind — William Kamkwamba and Bryan Mealer (2009)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/314792/the-boy-who-harnessed-the-wind-by-william-kamkwamba-and-bryan-mealer/9780593866894) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8dps4dy4fqrxcpw2gzt3enj)

> Lake Malawi is one of the biggest in the world and nearly covers the entire eastern half of our country. It’s so vast it has waves like an ocean.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** As a young trader from [[Dowa]], the father buys dried fish, rice and used clothes at the lake. He rides the steamers _Ilala_ and _Chauncy Maples_ to Nkhotakota and to Mangochi, a bazaar town "on the southern tip of the lake, just near the mouth of the Shire River". There traders from four countries emptied their pockets in the boozing dens. William first sees the lake only at twenty, though he grew up two hours from its shores: "my heart was filled with a great love for my country."
- **Why it matters:** The lake belongs to the father's past more than to William's present. It is a backdrop that shows how close and yet how far the wider country is from a farm boy in Masitala.
