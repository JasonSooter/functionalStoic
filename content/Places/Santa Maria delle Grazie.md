# Santa Maria delle Grazie

Church and convent in [[Milan]], Italy, whose refectory holds Leonardo's _Last Supper_, which Bill Bryson finds disappointingly faded in Neither Here Nor There.

## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74125W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5bejn8aqd21ynt8zcjsdg0k)

> Afterward I walked a long way across the city to see Leonardo’s “Last Supper” in the refectory beside the church of Santa Maria delle Grazie.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 16. He pays "a load of money" and steps into a bare, dim hall. A railing keeps visitors about twenty-five feet away from a painting "so faded that it can barely be seen from five feet". He says that without the thousand reproductions you probably wouldn't recognise any of it.
- **Why it matters:** It is the chapter's best comic set-piece about a masterpiece that barely survives, and fits the book's running theme of famous sights that disappoint in person.
