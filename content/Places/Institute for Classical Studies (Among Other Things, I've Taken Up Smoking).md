# Institute for Classical Studies (Among Other Things, I've Taken Up Smoking)

Fictional classics institute in a Manhattan brownstone, most likely in the [[West Village]]. In Aoibheann Sweeney's novel it is where Miranda is sent to catalogue her father's Ovid library, and the main setting of the New York part.

## Among Other Things, I've Taken Up Smoking — Aoibheann Sweeney (2007)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguin.com.au/books/among-other-things-ive-taken-up-smoking-9781101202227)

> In the dark behind the sheets of rain I could make out the façade I had long since memorized in the photo of my father with Arthur Mitchell—the arched windows, the stout brownstone steps.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Miranda's father helped Arthur Mitchell found the institute, and she arrives by taxi from Port Authority on a rainy night to put its card catalogue on a database. She lives in a back garret, works in a library with arched diamond-paned windows and a ceiling painted with constellations, and shares the house with Walter and Robert, a couple living in Arthur's old apartment, and Nate, who tutors there. She fixes up the kitchen and, later, spends her first night with Nate in her room.
- **Why it matters:** The institute is her father's world without her father: finding his whiskey glasses in its kitchen, and the photo of him with Arthur on its steps, start the questions about his past that drive the second half. It is the hinge between the Maine island and the city.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The institute is invented; the novel never names its street.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Best guess. Clues: a quiet side street of brownstones with trees, a short taxi ride from Port Authority; she walks from it to Eighth Avenue, shops on Fourteenth Street, walks "through the West Village" along Eighth Street to the East Village, and on the way home crosses Christopher Street in "a neighborhood like the one the institute was in, tiny curved streets and quaint apartment houses". No source names a model. Chosen: the West Village. Alternative: western Chelsea, just north of Fourteenth Street.
- **Later in the book:** Back from Connecticut, Miranda follows a ringing phone into Walter and Robert's rooms and finds a photograph of her father, young and fresh from a swim. Robert, who was there when her father and Arthur met, tells her that Arthur left her father "that island of his" and that her father then "just disappeared, with that secretary or whatever": her mother. Flooded with loyalty and rage, she keeps the photograph and leaves the institute.
