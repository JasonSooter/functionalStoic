# Akilia

Small island in south-west [[Greenland]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ its 3.85-billion-year-old rocks carry the oldest claimed evidence of life.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8159mvk291g0h2h6h2tqp88)

> The rock came from Akilia Island in Greenland, where unusually ancient rocks were found in 1997.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The rock Bryson handles at the [[Australian National University]] comes from Akilia Island, where unusually ancient rocks were found in 1997. They are 3.85 billion years old, the oldest marine sediments ever found, and the oldest traces of life were excavated from the same deposit.
- **Why it matters:** It fixes the date for the first life on Earth that the chapter builds on.
