# Grand Canal (Venice)

Venice's main waterway, winding from the station to San Marco, in [[Venice]], Italy. In Death in Venice Aschenbach travels it in grief at leaving, then races back down it in joy within the hour.

## Death in Venice — Thomas Mann (1912)
Source: [[Death in Venice]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL16246083W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5c4pxvwzhtanzaz5pv5pns7)

> It was the familiar ride across the lagoon, past San Marco and up the Grand Canal.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** Aschenbach takes the vaporetto up the Grand Canal, past the Piazzetta and the Rialto arch, to the [[Venezia Santa Lucia railway station]], sorrowing at leaving forever. After the luggage mix-up he races back down it in the hotel's motor launch with his spirits raised.
- **Why it matters:** The canal carries the mood reversal from farewell to elation. As a corridor its role could be filled by another route, but the two journeys frame the turning point.
