# King's College London

University college on the Strand in [[London]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins produce the X-ray images behind the double helix.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> However senior or accomplished, they were not allowed into the college’s senior common room but instead had to take their meals in a more utilitarian chamber that even Watson conceded was “dingily pokey.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Wilkins and Franklin at King's were, at first, far ahead of Crick and Watson. Franklin had the best X-ray crystallography images of DNA in existence, yet women academics were barred from the senior common room and ate in a room even Watson called "dingily pokey", and she was pressed to share her results. In January 1953 Wilkins showed Watson her images, apparently without her consent, and that spurred the Cambridge pair on.
- **Why it matters:** Where the experimental evidence for DNA's structure came from, and Bryson's case that Franklin's part was sidelined.
