# Neue Wache

Neo-classical guardhouse by Schinkel on Unter den Linden, Berlin, now Germany's central memorial to the victims of war and dictatorship. In _Germany: Memories of a Nation_ it shows how Kollwitz's art became the nation's image of mourning.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)

Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76mkfvv9hphznee9r5b0f)

> On 14 November 1993, in the pouring rain, the President of Germany, Richard von Weizsäcker, came to the Neue Wache to rededicate it as the central National Memorial to the Victims of War and Dictatorship.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 22, 'The suffering witness', enters the austere neo-classical guardhouse on Unter den Linden, empty except for an enlarged Kollwitz Pietà beneath an open oculus. It had served as a Prussian memorial to the Napoleonic wars, the Weimar First World War memorial and the Soviet memorial to the victims of fascism and militarism before Kohl chose it as the national memorial and President Weizsäcker rededicated it on 14 November 1993. Critics asked whether a mother mourning her son could stand for the victims of the Holocaust.
- **Why it matters:** It frames the chapter's question of how one image of grief can stand for a century of German suffering and guilt.
