# Güstrow Cathedral

Gothic Protestant cathedral of Güstrow in Mecklenburg, north of Berlin, where Ernst Barlach's Hovering Angel hangs. In _Germany: Memories of a Nation_ it is Germany's answer to how to remember the First World War.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)
Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m770z2wnrhq9w4e44fwyka)

> In the Protestant cathedral in Güstrow, a small town just over 100 miles to the north of Berlin, is one answer.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 29, 'Barlach's Angel', centres on the bronze angel Barlach made in 1926 to hang above the font as a First World War memorial. The Nazis removed it in 1937 and it was melted down; a second cast went to Cologne, and a third was installed here in 1953 despite the Cold War. On 13 December 1981 Helmut Schmidt and Erich Honecker stood beneath it, and in 2014 the congregation left an empty space while the Angel was lent to London.
- **Why it matters:** It carries the book's closing argument about a shared, mourned past that could bridge even a divided Germany.
