# Fallingwater

Frank Lloyd Wright's house over a waterfall at Mill Run, Pennsylvania. Fast Company calls it the image that put Wright back in the public eye, and in Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is the Kaufmanns' house where Frida spends a flirtatious weekend in 1938.

## Frank Lloyd Wright Was A Great Architect–But He Was Even Better At Branding — Diana Budds (2017)

Source: [Original](https://www.fastcodesign.com/90129345/frank-lloyd-wright-was-a-great-architect-but-he-was-even-better-at-branding) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k7dhkzfernaxeq63kgan85)

> That house is in the imagination of the entire world, the idea of living floating above a waterfall.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** Fallingwater is a lavish 1930s house that put Wright back in public view. It made the cover of Time with his portrait. Bergdoll says the image of the house over the waterfall in nature is so powerful that it lives in the world's imagination.
- **Why it matters:** Marks Wright's 1930s return to fame.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)

Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m73hng3z1a873kgp2pxprzzp)

> When she retired, Fallingwater’s complicated double stairway served as the stage for the evening’s drama.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** During her 1938 New York stay (Chapter 14), Julien Levy takes Frida by train to visit his client Edgar Kaufmann Sr., who wants to be her patron. She flirts with Levy, her elderly host and his son; at bedtime Levy and Kaufmann each try to outwait the other, climbing and retreating on opposite sides of the double staircase, until Levy finds Frida waiting in his room.
- **Why it matters:** A comic episode of Frida's free life in the United States away from Diego, and the link to Kaufmann, who acquired one of her paintings.
