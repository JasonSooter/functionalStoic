# St Mary's Hospital, London

St Mary's Hospital in Paddington, London, is where Alexander Fleming had his laboratory, now the Alexander Fleming Laboratory Museum. _Abundance_ opens its chapter on deployment with his discovery of penicillin there.

## At a glance
| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|

## Abundance — Ezra Klein & Derek Thompson (2025)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL44034521W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m757e22z20r85ehz089wxq)

> IN THE FALL OF 1928, the Scottish scientist Alexander Fleming returned from a long holiday to his lab in London.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 5 opens in the fall of 1928, when Fleming comes back to his London lab and finds that a dish of staphylococcus left open to the air has been contaminated by a mold that killed the bacteria. He names the substance penicillin, but his work soon stalls, and the story moves to Oxford, Peoria and the wartime push to mass-produce the drug.
- **Why it matters:** It is the discovery scene the authors use to challenge the 'eureka myth': the breakthrough is only the start of the long work of deployment.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The book does not name the building; Wikipedia identifies Fleming's laboratory as the one at St Mary's Hospital, preserved as the Alexander Fleming Laboratory Museum.
