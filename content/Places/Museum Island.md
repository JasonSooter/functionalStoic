# Museum Island

Island in the Spree in central Berlin holding five major museums. In _Germany: Memories of a Nation_ it is Berlin's nineteenth-century dream of itself as a new Athens.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)

Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m776hjr30v8c1h1wfz7r0w)

> Frederick William IV in the 1840s to designate the island opposite the Schloss ‘a sanctuary for the arts’: the Museum Island, a Parnassus on the Spree

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In Chapter 30 MacGregor ends his walk at the island opposite the Schloss that Frederick William IV designated in the 1840s 'a sanctuary for the arts', with museums built as classical temples linked by colonnades. It sits with the Brandenburg Gate and Unter den Linden as a dream of Berlin as a city of learning and art.
- **Why it matters:** It expresses the German self-image as modern Greeks, and in the book's last pages it becomes half of a new dialogue of cultures with the rebuilt Schloss.
