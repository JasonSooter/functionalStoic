# Tangi Sharo Gorge (Full Tilt)

Gorge Dervla Murphy calls 'Tangi Sharo' on the Kabul–Jalalabad road; most likely the Tang-e Gharu gorge of the Kabul River. A swim and a nomad's kindness in _Full Tilt_.

## Full Tilt — Dervla Murphy (1965)

Source: [[Full Tilt - Ireland to India With a Bicycle]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL9584029M) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5j6mbjfp524jwbn7sxf2a6p)

> We passed through some tremendous scenery and saw a most awe-inspiring engineering feat – the road through the Tangi Sharo Gorge. Here the mountains are almost sheer walls of solid grey rock and the road pirouettes its way down thousands of feet to river level.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** She walks the unfinished gorge road, then bathes in the Kabul river, riding the current downstream. At midday a Kochi nomad pitches a goat-hair tent over her as she sleeps.
- **Why it matters:** The descent from the highlands toward the Indian plains, with the bathing episode and nomad kindness.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real place, named in the book by a name that is not on modern maps.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ No gorge called 'Tangi Sharo' appears in the standard references. The text puts it on the day's ride from Kabul towards Jalalabad, where 'the road pirouettes its way down thousands of feet to river level' beside the Kabul River. That matches the Tang-e Gharu gorge of the Kabul River east of Kabul, so the pin is there (inferred). Alternative: the gorges further down the river around Sarobi.
