## Animal Farm — George Orwell (1945)

Source: [[Animal Farm]] · [Original](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/56486/animal-farm)

> Mr. Jones, of the Manor Farm, had locked the hen-houses for the night, but was too drunk to remember to shut the pop-holes.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The whole story takes place here: old Major's speech in the big barn, the Rebellion that expels Jones, the renaming at the five-barred gate, the building and twice-destruction of the windmill on the knoll, Boxer's collapse at the quarry, and the final dinner in the farmhouse where the name reverts to Manor Farm. The farm has a farmhouse, yard, big barn painted with the Seven Commandments, orchard, small paddock, pool, spinney, hayfield, long pasture running to the main road, and a limestone quarry. Snowball picks the knoll as the site for the windmill; the quarrel over it splits the animals and leads to Snowball's expulsion. The mill is built, toppled by a gale, rebuilt and finally blown up by Frederick's men; the animals huddle on the knoll after the executions and look across the farm, and Boxer collapses here.
- **Why it matters:** The sole stage of the allegory; its ordinary English mixed-farm geography makes the corruption of the revolution feel local and domestic.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text names no county: the farm lies down a cart-track off the main road, Willingdon is the nearest town, and Foxwood and Pinchfield adjoin it. Reference works (Wikipedia on Wallington, Hertfordshire; Country Life) tie it to the Manor Farm in Wallington, where Orwell lived at The Stores from 1936; other claimants exist (Farmers Weekly reports a dispute). Pinned at Wallington village.
