# Pupella

Pupella is King Tranquo's seaside glen on the island of Tranque in the Arsacides, an old European name for the Solomon Islands in the southwestern Pacific. In _Moby-Dick_ Ishmael says he measured a whale skeleton kept in a palm temple there.

## Moby-Dick — Herman Melville (1851)

Source: [[Exploration of Moby Dick]] · [[Moby Dick]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m77cw0pw0g67j519f68d14)

> I was invited to spend part of the Arsacidean holidays with the lord of Tranque, at his retired palm villa at Pupella;

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In Chapter 102 Ishmael recalls visiting his 'royal friend Tranquo, king of Tranque' while on the trading-ship Dey of Algiers. At Pupella a stranded sperm whale's skeleton lies in a temple of palms, its skull an altar with a priest-kept flame for a spout. Ishmael wanders through the ribs with a ball of twine and measures the bones while the priests quarrel, and Chapter 103 gives the dimensions he had tattooed on his arm.
- **Why it matters:** It is Ishmael's claimed source for his knowledge of the whale's skeleton, and its green-vine temple is one of the book's great meditative images. The exotic island setting lets him handle a whole skeleton that no ship could carry.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text names Tranque as 'one of the Arsacides' and places Pupella near 'Bamboo-Town', the king's capital; Tranque, Tranquo and Pupella are invented. 'Arsacides' was an early European name for the Solomon Islands, and Wikipedia's Malaita article notes Cape Arsacides on Malaita and that Ishmael's Tranquo is fictional. Pinned at the Solomon Islands' representative point; Malaita, where the name survives, is the main alternative.
