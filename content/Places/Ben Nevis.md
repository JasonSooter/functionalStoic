# Ben Nevis

The highest mountain in the British Isles, above Fort William in the Scottish Highlands. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ C. T. R. Wilson's climbs there lead to the cloud chamber.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80ty2fb6k0gy9vvvpgxs5p4)

> IN 1911, A British scientist named C. T. R. Wilson was studying cloud formations by tramping regularly to the summit of Ben Nevis, a famously damp Scottish mountain, when it occurred to him that there must be an easier way to study clouds.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In 1911 C. T. R. Wilson is studying cloud formations by tramping regularly to the summit of Ben Nevis, a famously damp Scottish mountain, when it occurs to him that there must be an easier way. Back at the Cavendish he builds an artificial cloud chamber.
- **Why it matters:** The mountain is the comic prompt for the particle detector, the starting point of modern particle physics.
