# Phinney Ridge

Residential ridge in north [[Seattle]], just west of Green Lake. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is where Joe Rantz's father and stepmother scrape through the worst Depression winters in an unheated 1885 house.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86m5y1nfxf8wqsxmd8htbmj)

> Perched on the ridge, the house caught every wintry blast that blew down from the Arctic and into the city.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** After a first shed on the waterfront at the edge of Hooverville, Harry and Thula Rantz move into an old house on Phinney Ridge built in 1885, with one electrical outlet and a single woodstove they cannot afford to fuel. Thula cooks on an upturned electric iron and turns to soup kitchens and the Unemployed Citizens League's commissary, while her mother, living downhill near Green Lake, allows only a weekly Sunday bowl of Cream of Wheat.
- **Why it matters:** The ridge-top house shows what the family Joe was left behind by was going through, which complicates the story of his abandonment. It is backstory told in retrospect.
