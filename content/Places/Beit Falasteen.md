# Beit Falasteen

Mansion of the businessman Munib al-Masri on Mount Gerizim above Nablus, in the West Bank. A subject in Colum McCann's _Apeirogon_: Munib al-Masri's hilltop mansion above Nablus, built as a mosaic and metaphor of Palestine; sees Balata and Assira.

## Apeirogon — Colum McCann (2020)
Source: [[Apeirogon]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/207433/apeirogon-a-novel-by-colum-mccann/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjptpm689rrm6298wtj30k)

> Al-Masri—a chronic insomniac—built the mansion as a riddle, a mosaic, a metaphor for his country: he called it Beit Falasteen, the House of Palestine.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Munib al-Masri's mansion above Nablus on Mount Gerizim is described at length: Palladian design, a Hercules statue, a Roman-style amphitheater, an ancient monastery uncovered during excavation and preserved beneath it. It looks out toward Mount Ebal, the Balata refugee camp and Assira al-Shamaliya, and sits in Area A surrounded by Area C.
- **Why it matters:** The house is offered as a metaphor for Palestine as a jigsaw of beauty and excess, and links Nablus to the Ben Yehuda bombers' village in view. It is a digression, but a vivid one that shows Palestinian wealth, history and layers of ruin.
