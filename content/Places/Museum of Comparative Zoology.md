# Museum of Comparative Zoology

Zoology museum at [[Harvard University]] in Cambridge, Massachusetts. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Harvard builds it for Louis Agassiz when America gives him the esteem Europe did not.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8331hj2ahpmt1gp8nyzy1yn)

> In 1846, Agassiz traveled to America to give a series of lectures and there at last found the esteem he craved. Harvard gave him a professorship and built him a first-rate museum, the Museum of Comparative Zoology.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Agassiz went to America in 1846 to lecture and there at last found the esteem he craved: Harvard gave him a professorship and built him this museum. His ice-age ideas found a following in New England, and when he died in 1873 Harvard felt it had to appoint three professors to take his place.
- **Why it matters:** Marks where Agassiz's career, and with it the ice-age idea, finally found a home, just before his theories fell out of fashion.
