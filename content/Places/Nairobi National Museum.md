# Nairobi National Museum

Kenya's national museum in [[Nairobi]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Bryson meets its head of invertebrates, who calls the shortage of taxonomists the real biodiversity crisis.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> head of invertebrates at the Kenyan National Museum in Nairobi, whom I met briefly on a visit to the country in the autumn of 2002.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Bryson meets Koen Maes, the Belgian-born head of invertebrates at the museum, in autumn 2002. Maes tells him there are no specialized taxonomists in the whole of Africa and that his own contract is not being renewed for lack of money. He describes visiting a Kenyan cloud forest and, in half an hour of casual looking, finding four new millipede species and a new species of tree.
- **Why it matters:** It gives a human face to the chapter's argument that species go unrecorded because no one is paid to look.
