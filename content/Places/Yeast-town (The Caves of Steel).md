## The Caves of Steel — Isaac Asimov (1954)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL46401W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5hj3jmnfj5ppx4kb966rq29)

> What was called Yeast-town in popular speech was, to the Post Office, merely the boroughs of Newark, New Brunswick, and Trenton.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Yeast-town is the popular name for a broad strip of many-layered yeast farms across 'once Medieval New Jersey', fed with wood from the Alleghenies. In the New York Yeast laboratory there Baley questions the zymologist Clousarr, lectures him on colonisation and arrests him.
- **Why it matters:** The yeast vats are what keep twenty million people fed, which makes the City's fragility, and the Medievalists' grievance, concrete.
- **Real vs. fictional:** A future district made of real New Jersey towns.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Clues: the text names the boroughs of Newark, New Brunswick and Trenton, with Newark Center and Trenton Center as residential areas. Pinned at Newark, the first borough named. Alternatives: New Brunswick or Trenton along the same strip.
