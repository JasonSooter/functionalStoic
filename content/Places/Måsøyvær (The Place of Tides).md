# Måsøyvær (The Place of Tides)

Anna's family sea estate in the [[Vega Archipelago]], Norway, a little west of [[Vega (Norway)|Vega]]. In James Rebanks's memoir it is the island her family is named after, abandoned after the war, the duck station she spent fifteen years bringing back to life, and the place she was then cut out of.

## The Place of Tides — James Rebanks (2024)

Source: [[The Place of Tides]] · [Original](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/315745/the-place-of-tides-by-rebanks-james/9780241426937) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m81td4vcptbg8zet4zrwk6ba)

> Anna looked towards the south and pointed across the sea, a little to the west of Vega. ‘Måsøyvær,’ she said.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** On the hill of Fjærøy, Anna tells Rebanks she has run that island for only twelve years: "Måsøyvær before that … I am from Måsøyvær." Over rainy days she tells its history: her great-great-grandfather Harald bought the sea estate in 1852 and his children built it up to 1,500 nests; in the war German soldiers netted the tame eiders, and by 1945 Måsøy made only 400 nests; her parents left in 1946, two years before she was born. Her father kept going out to Måsøy, but escaped farm mink "would turn up on an island and kill every last eider", and the duck station "was slowly falling into disrepair". After seeing a stuffed eider in the new museum on Vega, Anna resolves to bring the ducks back, and the government has her released from her nursing-home job "to work on the abandoned duck station of Måsøyvær". Her first season fails: no ducks take to the nests she builds on the main island. The breakthrough comes on Flat Skerry, "just a plateau of rock a little above sea level" a short row from the main island, where she builds stone houses around the few wild nests and the birds accept her; it takes "five years of hard work, with no pay, to get a single duck to nest on the main island". After fifteen years there are sixty nests. After her father's death the siblings agree she will keep running the station, but some years later her brother Tore announces he will come each spring and make the nests himself: "And so, after fifteen years of work, bringing the duck station back to life, Anna never went to Måsøy again."
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's buried question, why Anna keeps ducks on someone else's island rather than her own, and its answer. Its decline and rebirth carry the story of the whole outer-island way of life.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Best guess. The text puts Måsøyvær "a little to the west of Vega", south of Fjærøy as seen from its hill, at one corner of "a giant triangle of ocean, a few miles long on each side" with Vega and Fjærøy; from Fjærøy's hill Anna "could see an island mountain of Søla, on the back of Vega, and she knew that behind that was Måsøy". No mapped island near Vega carries the name (Nominatim finds only Måsøya islands in Brønnøy, Rødøy and further north), so it may be a pen name. Pinned at the Vega Archipelago's representative point. Alternative: one of the outer duck islands west of Vega, such as Lånan, but no source names it.
