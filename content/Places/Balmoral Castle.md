# Balmoral Castle

Royal summer residence on the Scottish moors of Aberdeenshire, [[Scotland]]. In _The Spy and the Traitor_ it is where Margaret Thatcher gives the go-ahead for Gordievsky's exfiltration.

## The Spy and the Traitor — Ben Macintyre (2018)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19749410W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7xy5nw5kbddwaa4kp14fb6h)

> Balmoral Castle, the royal family’s summer residence since 1852, is a vast granite pile adorned with turrets and set on 50,000 acres of Scottish moors; on a gloomy and damp Scottish evening, it was quite hard to find.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** With Gordievsky's escape signal already flown in Moscow, Charles Powell flies to Aberdeen and drives to Balmoral on a damp evening, where courtiers stall him. A footman finally leads him to what looks like a "sort of garden shed", where Thatcher is "propped up in bed, surrounded by papers." It takes her only minutes to authorize Operation PIMLICO: "We have to honor our promises to our agent."
- **Why it matters:** It is the political turning point of the escape: the plan needed the prime minister's word, given far from Downing Street. The incongruity of a Cold War decision taken in a royal garden cabin is part of the scene's point.
