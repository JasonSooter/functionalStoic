# Cape of Good Hope

Rocky headland at the tip of the Cape Peninsula, South Africa. Mark Manson ends his book on its cliff edge.

## The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F\*ck — Mark Manson (2016)

Source: [[The Subtle Art of Not Giving a Fck]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17590212W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kvb862ch0sf6a8xb275v3q)

> I’m at South Africa’s Cape of Good Hope, once thought to be the southern tip of Africa and the southernmost point in the world.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Near the end of the book Manson climbs to the cliff edge at the Cape, once thought the southern tip of Africa, and inches to within a foot of the drop, sitting with his feet on a small rock while tourists take photos below. An Australian on the trail asks if he is okay, and he answers "Alive. Very alive."
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's closing scene, a deliberate confrontation with mortality that acts out the last chapter's thesis.

## Moby-Dick — Herman Melville (1851)

Source: [[Exploration of Moby Dick]] · [[Moby Dick]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m770b41nkmgnsjbp0sz78b)

> Cape of Good Hope, do they call ye? Rather Cape Tormentoso, as called of yore;

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** In Chapter 51 the Pequod turns eastward round the Cape into long, troubled seas and black weather. Ahab stands wordless through the gale, and Starbuck finds him asleep upright in his cabin chair, eyes on the compass, while the solitary spirit-spout still beckons. Chapter 54 opens by calling the waters round the Cape the four corners of a great highway where ships meet.
- **Why it matters:** The text dwells on it as a dark, tormented passage that marks the ship's turn into the Indian Ocean and shows Ahab's fixity. Renaming it 'Tormentoso' turns the place into a mood.
