# Crystal Palace Dinosaurs

Victorian concrete dinosaur models in Crystal Palace Park, Sydenham, south [[London]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ they are the world's first life-size dinosaurs, and the stage for Richard Owen's rivalry with Gideon Mantell.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80ghkrpwbqxjnr82r2fcxd7)

> In the district of Sydenham in south London, at a place called Crystal Palace Park, there stands a strange and forgotten sight

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Bryson opens his account of the dinosaur feud with the models: once among London's most popular attractions, "in effect" the world's first theme park, though the iguanodon wears its thumb on its nose and stands on four legs like an overgrown dog. He goes on to tell how, on New Year's Eve 1853, scientists dined inside the unfinished iguanodon with Owen at the head of the table and Mantell absent.
- **Why it matters:** The models are physical evidence of how much was guessed and how bitterly it was fought over; they let the book show early paleontology's errors on a public lawn.
