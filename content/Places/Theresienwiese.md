# Theresienwiese

Open fairground in Munich named after Princess Therese, where the Oktoberfest is held each year. In _Germany: Memories of a Nation_ the festival stands for regional, convivial German identity.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)

Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m75nad2z1qaxyz7872w9bv)

> On 12 October 1810, to celebrate his wedding, the first Oktoberfest was held in his capital city of Munich.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 10, 'One people, many sausages', tells how the first Oktoberfest was held in Munich on 12 October 1810 to celebrate Ludwig's wedding, and became the largest popular festival in the world. Food historian Peter Peter calls it a symbol of the new Germany, where traditional costume is worn without nationalist overtones.
- **Why it matters:** It is the chapter's emblem of beer and regional food as German identity; the festival, rather than the meadow, is what the text describes.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The text names the Oktoberfest but not the Theresienwiese, the meadow where it is held.
