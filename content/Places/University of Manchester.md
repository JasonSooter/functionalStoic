# University of Manchester

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80txqrbcws6qkgwg86bqe3p)

> he had moved on to Manchester University, and it was there, in fact, that he would do his most important work in determining the structure and nature of the atom.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** After McGill, Rutherford moves to Manchester University, where he does his most important work on the structure of the atom. In 1910, with Hans Geiger, he fires ionized helium atoms at gold foil and is astonished when some bounce back, concluding that the atom is mostly empty space with a tiny, dense nucleus.
- **Why it matters:** The nuclear atom, the base for everything later in the physics chapters, is worked out here. ⚠️ The text names the university for his Manchester years but doesn't name a lab for the 1910 experiment.
