# Old Goa

Former capital of Portuguese India, on the Mandovi River in Goa, India. In _Nathaniel's Nutmeg_ Ralph Fitch's party is jailed there, and Linschoten's Goa years supply the Dutch with their knowledge of the Indies.

## Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Giles Milton (1999)

Source: [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Reader's Companion]] · [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1970050W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m82effr4xe75xkdcpes6vw7z)

> No sooner had the party of Englishmen arrived in Hormuz than the town’s Portuguese authorities grew suspicious. Arrested and clapped in jail, they were eventually shipped to Goa to be dealt with by the Portuguese viceroy.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Arrested at Hormuz, Fitch's party is shipped to Goa to face the Portuguese viceroy; the English Jesuit Thomas Steven stands surety for them, and the party breaks up while Fitch slips away in disguise to continue his journey east. Jan Huyghen van Linschoten's years in Goa provide the stories and trade knowledge he later publishes.
- **Why it matters:** By jailing Fitch here the Portuguese unwittingly launch the research journey that marks England's entry into the spice race.
