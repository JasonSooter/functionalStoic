# Hohlenstein-Stadel

Hohlenstein-Stadel is a cave in the Swabian Jura of south-west Germany where the ivory lion-man figurine was found. _Sapiens_ uses that figurine as its first example of art and of belief in things that do not exist.

## Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind — Yuval Noah Harari (2014)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17075811W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m7791kjnv38r2v57sbxxpn)

> If we were to come across the artists of the Stadel Cave, we could learn their language and they ours.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The ivory lion-man figurine from the Stadel Cave, about 32,000 years old, is offered as one of the first indisputable examples of art and probably religion. Harari returns to it as the emblem of imagined realities: its sculptor may have believed in a lion-man guardian spirit, and Peugeot's lion logo is set beside it.
- **Why it matters:** It anchors the argument that the Cognitive Revolution let Sapiens invent fictions and myths, the basis of cooperation in large numbers.
