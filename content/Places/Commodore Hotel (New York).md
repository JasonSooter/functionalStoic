# Commodore Hotel (New York)

Hotel beside Grand Central Terminal in [[Manhattan]], rebuilt as the Grand Hyatt in 1980. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is where the Amateur Athletic Union's narrow vote in December 1935 ends the American campaign to boycott the Berlin Olympics.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86yeydz94fj8q6f1fgzm95h)

> A few days later, on December 8, at the Commodore Hotel in New York, the Amateur Athletic Union of the United States voted on a resolution to send a three-man committee to Germany to investigate claims of Nazi maltreatment of Jews there.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** On December 8, 1935, the AAU votes on sending a committee to Germany; counting fractional votes, the resolution fails 58.25 to 55.75, and the last serious American boycott effort is dead. Only weeks earlier, on November 21, ten thousand anti-Nazi demonstrators had marched down Eighth Avenue to Madison Square Park to demand a boycott. The book calls the result a victory for the young athletes, for Avery Brundage, and most of all for Hitler.
- **Why it matters:** The vote clears the way for the Washington crew to go to Berlin, and the book is clear-eyed about the cost: the boys' dream rests on the world agreeing to be deceived.
