# El Tintal Library

Public library in the Kennedy district of southwest [[Bogotá]], Colombia, built in a former garbage-processing plant. In _Happy City_ it is Charles Montgomery's proof that grand architecture in a poor barrio is a matter of equity.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84c7z90mdmk6633wp3rmxv1)

> In fact, it had spent most of its life as a garbage-processing plant before being retrofitted into a library by Daniel Bermúdez, one of the country’s most respected architects.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 10. From the pedicab Montgomery sees a white building with round windows and tilted skylights in a grassy park; the old dump-truck ramp is now its entrance. When he wonders aloud who would come here to read, the boy pedalling him answers: "My mother. And me." The book counts it among three monumental libraries Peñalosa built in poorer districts.
- **Why it matters:** The library turns the chapter's equity argument into a building: spectacular public architecture placed where people had the least, giving them books and a place to learn.
