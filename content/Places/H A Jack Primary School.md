# H. A. Jack Primary School

Government primary school in Highlands North, Johannesburg. In Born a Crime it is where eleven-year-old Trevor Noah first has to choose a race.

## At a glance

| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Born a Crime | Trevor Noah | turning point | At recess on his first day the school splits by colour, and Trevor asks to move to the black "B classes". |

## Born a Crime — Trevor Noah (2016)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17824318W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6yq44h0n3ss5bh5d45x7t80)

> At the end of grade six I left Maryvale to go to H. A. Jack Primary, a government school.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Chapter 4 ("Chameleon"). An aptitude test puts Trevor in the "A classes", which are almost all white. At recess the playground divides, "an ocean of black", and he is left standing in the middle. He realises the black kids are all in the B classes and asks the counsellor to switch, against her advice. He says choosing was how he realised he was black. The school returns in later chapters.
- **Why it matters:** this is the book's pivot from the sheltered mixed world of Maryvale to post-apartheid South Africa's de facto segregation. His choice of side shapes the rest of the memoir.
