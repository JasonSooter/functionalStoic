# The hanging valley (Last of the Breed)

A hidden forty-acre valley high in a Siberian river canyon where Joe Mack holes up in _Last of the Breed_; pinned in the [[Chersky Range]] as a best guess.

## Last of the Breed — Louis L'Amour (1986)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL51048W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6y2dkkk7jpe1ekkpgpgpdcv)

> Searching, he found a place where he could cross, and soon he was standing in a small hanging valley, its walls covered with stone pine and cedar, its basin half choked with aspen.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Crossing the stream that feeds a waterfall, Joe Mack finds a valley of "no more than forty acres" with two little meadows and a small pond, high above white water in a canyon at least a mile deep. He kills a goat, makes moccasins and a goatskin coat, rigs a bent-aspen trap on the cliff trail, and finds an old overhang walled with "a crude rock parapet of fitted stones" and a goat path out. He hides there for days while a plane searches, before going out to burn the soldiers' camp.
- **Why it matters:** The valley is the payoff of his plan to vanish in the canyons: a self-sufficient refuge where he re-equips himself as the hunters wait for him to show himself.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Unnamed; probably invented.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text hedges: "The canyon he had reached was either that of the Kolyma or the Indigirka, and he suspected the latter"; Alekhin later thinks the American is "somewhere near the head of the Indigirka or the Kolyma, and he believed the former" (the Indigirka). Joe Mack had been making for the Chersky Mountains, whose canyons Baronas described, and compares this one to the Snake and Salmon canyons of Idaho. No reference work places it, so the pin is at the Chersky Range's representative point; the alternative is a canyon on the upper Kolyma.
