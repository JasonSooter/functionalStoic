## Full Tilt — Dervla Murphy (1965)

Source: [[Full Tilt - Ireland to India With a Bicycle]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL9584029M) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5j6mnym00tcdx5ttjgx9vg8)

> Peshawar is like an English city with a few water-buffaloes and vultures and lizards thrown in. I’m installed in a dak bungalow, which is the cheapest accommodation for tourists in Pakistan and India.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** She spends several rainy days in a dak bungalow, buying second-hand clothes in the bazaar, visiting Irish priests and nuns, dining with a colonel's family and the Vice-Chancellor of the university, and watching the city gates shut at night. She weighs British colonial legacy against Afghan independence and debates purdah and Western influence with her hosts.
- **Why it matters:** Her first extended contact with the Indian subcontinent and its colonial legacy, prompting reflections that frame the rest of the book's Pakistan section.
