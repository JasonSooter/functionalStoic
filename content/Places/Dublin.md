## Ireland’s $27BN Mission to Save Dublin — The B1M (2026)
Source: [Original](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T_Jz9xzOoO4) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5c3nxywzz9faw9zvcm63p29)

> Dublin’s population has grown by nearly a third since the turn of the century but its travel infrastructure

- **Role:** subject
- **In the video:** The B1M calls Dublin the 11th most congested city in the world and says its transport has not kept pace with its population. It follows the plan for a metro, Metro North and now MetroLink, through its delays. Along the route, Albert College Park, about 15 minutes from the centre, must host an intervention shaft because the tunnel section between Collins Avenue and Griffith Park exceeds the EU's 1,000 m spacing for escape and ventilation points.
- **Why it matters:** The whole video is about the city's transport problem. Its specific places, such as [[St Stephen's Green]] and Albert College Park, show what building the metro will cost the city's green spaces.

## The Country Girls Trilogy — Edna O'Brien (1986)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL69252W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m75rgvjy4qpkvmz4e22qmd)

> We got in to Dublin just before six. It was still bright, and we carried our bags across the platform, stopping for a minute to let others pass by.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** At the end of The Country Girls the girls arrive by train and lodge with Joanna and Gustav, the Austrian landlords; Caithleen weighs sugar in Mr. Burns's grocery while Baba studies. The Lonely Girl is largely set here: a day waiting in vain for Mr. Gentleman at a sweetshop on the quays, a grocers' dance at Cleary's ballroom, a fortune-teller in Donnybrook, and the courtship with Eugene Gaillard. Kate and Baba finally leave on the ship Hibernia, watching 'the City of Dublin' recede 'the city I loved.'
- **Why it matters:** Dublin is where the girls try out adult life and where Kate meets Eugene; it is the bridge between the village and London. A different city would lose the Joyce references and the small, gossipy scale that lets the village reach her even here.
