# Vauxhall Gardens

Former pleasure garden south of the Thames in [[London]]. In _Happy City_ it is an 18th-century experiment in pay-per-visit public pleasure open to anyone with a shilling.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83dc6yev784b6njv4dfa838)

> In London, Jonathan Tyers, an erstwhile leather merchant, transformed the walled Vauxhall Gardens south of the Thames into a leafy, pay-per-visit wonderland of outdoor portraits in rococo style, hanging lanterns, open-air concerts, and spectacle.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The Prince of Wales visited, but so did anyone who could find the one-shilling admission. Tightrope walkers entertained crowds of thousands, fireworks went off, and mothers searched the garden's leafy corners for misbehaving daughters. Montgomery calls it egalitarian hedonism.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's Enlightenment example of happiness, understood as pleasure, being designed into a public place.
