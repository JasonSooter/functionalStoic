# Palais-Royal

Palace, gardens and arcades in central [[Paris]]. In _Happy City_ it is the pleasure ground whose café talk of a right to happiness fed the Revolution.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83dcavcvn5563pwtfjp81kx)

> The Palais-Royal became a public entertainment complex populated by bookshops, salons, and refreshment cafés.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Louis Philippe II, head of the House of Orléans and a follower of Rousseau's egalitarian ideals, threw open the complex's private gardens and arcades. It became a hub of bookshops, salons and cafés, of pleasure but also of philosophical and political ferment.
- **Why it matters:** Montgomery uses it to show public space where leisure and politics collide: the talk of happiness there contributed to a revolution that eventually cost its owner his head.
