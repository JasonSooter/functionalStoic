## Back To The Moderland? — Helen Russell (2025)

Source: [[Back to the Moderland]] · [Original](https://mshelenrussell.substack.com/p/back-to-the-moderland) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5e2xe7572ssqcraz91fnmbk)

> Even Billund - home of Lego, an airport, Legoland, a bakery, and Lego House - appeared in a new, ethereal light. I marvelled at the neatness. The efficiency.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** Billund appears in a new light on the return visit. The author marvels at its neatness and notes a two-storey slide beside the local bookshop that no one has defaced.
- **Why it matters:** The town stands for the order and trust of Danish life that she has been missing, so it shapes the piece's mood about what was lost.

## The Year of Living Danishly — Helen Russell (2015)

Source: [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5edhksgc0cqvrvttbyw56m3)

> The tiny town of Billund to the south of the peninsular had a population of just 6,100.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Billund is introduced as the remote Lego town the couple are moving to, mocked by friends as 'Bell End'. On arrival the author finds identikit bungalows and a silent, closed-feeling place; she later notes the Kirk Christiansens paid for its airport, church, school and library.
- **Why it matters:** It is the anchor of the book's premise (the Lego job) and the first culture-shock location, but the couple then choose to live elsewhere nearby, so it is partly a launch pad. Moving the job to Copenhagen would remove the rural-isolation theme.
