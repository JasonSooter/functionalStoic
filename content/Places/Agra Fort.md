# Agra Fort

Mughal fortress-palace on the Yamuna in Agra, India. In _Nathaniel's Nutmeg_ it is Emperor Jahangir's court, where William Hawkins becomes "Inglis Khan".

## Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Giles Milton (1999)

Source: [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Reader's Companion]] · [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1970050W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8efcyxywr59d8tmwpdj0aqw)

> Although the Taj Mahal had yet to be built, the city was nonetheless adorned with outlandish public monuments, none of which was more beautiful than Jehangir’s palace built inside the walls of Agra Fort.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** After riding up from [[Surat]] in 1609, Hawkins is offered a pension, four hundred horses and "the title of Inglis Khan", and agrees to stay "halfe a doozen yeeres" to "feather my nest". He sits in the railed enclosure at the daily durbar and joins the nightly drinking in the palace. When he jests that he would marry only a Christian, the emperor finds him an Armenian Christian bride. Gifts win him a licence for an English factory at Surat, but Mughal officials and the Portuguese get it "inexplicably countermanded", and Hawkins is left trying to mend fences amid the emperor's lion fights.
- **Why it matters:** It shows the English trying diplomacy at the Mughal court and getting little lasting for it, a contrast with the force the Dutch use in the Spice Islands.
