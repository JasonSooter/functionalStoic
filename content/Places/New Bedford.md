# New Bedford

New Bedford is a port city on the Acushnet River in southeastern Massachusetts, a leading whaling port of the nineteenth century. In _Moby-Dick_ it is Ishmael's first stop, where he lodges at the Spouter-Inn and meets Queequeg.

## Moby-Dick; or, The Whale — Herman Melville (1851)
Source: [[Exploration of Moby Dick]] · [[Moby Dick]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76pz9cqj1vc5y27wk8ce4)

> As most young candidates for the pains and penalties of whaling stop at this same New Bedford, thence to embark on their voyage

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Ishmael arrives on a Saturday night in December to find the Nantucket packet gone, and wanders the dark streets past the Crossed Harpoons and the Sword-Fish Inn to the Spouter-Inn, kept by Peter Coffin, where he must share a bed with the harpooneer Queequeg. The inn's smoky whale picture and whale-jaw bar, and the night and day there, carry Chapters 3 to 13, in which the two become friends. Chapter 6 describes the town by day, its cannibals and greenhorns and its fine houses paid for by whales. The two then board the schooner Moss and sail down the Acushnet past wharves piled with casks, and Queequeg saves a bumpkin swept overboard.
- **Why it matters:** It is the first land setting and the place where Ishmael and Queequeg's bond is formed. The text presents it as the busy port that has overtaken Nantucket, so it shows the trade's wealth before the voyage shows its cost.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The Spouter-Inn is invented; the text places it 'not far from the docks' on 'a sharp bleak corner', and it is folded into this note rather than pinned separately.
