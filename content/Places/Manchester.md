## Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation — Peter L. Bernstein (2005)
Source: [[Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3294009W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kve6rywar2zdvwtxvdzsjb)

> Under the banner of Corn Law repeal, two Manchester manufacturers, Richard Cobden and John Bright, formed the Manchester Anti-Corn Law Association in 1836, which two years later combined with similar groups to compose the Anti-Corn Law League.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** In the epilogue on the repeal of Britain's Corn Laws, two Manchester manufacturers, Richard Cobden and John Bright, form the Manchester Anti-Corn Law Association in 1836, which grows into the Anti-Corn Law League; a footnote adds that Manchester, a huge importer of raw cotton, is strongly pro-American.
- **Why it matters:** Manchester's free-trade campaign opened Britain to the grain the canal carried from the American West.
