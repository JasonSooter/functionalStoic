# Manchester

City in north-west England; see [[England]]. In _Wedding of the Waters_ it is the home of the Anti-Corn Law League, and in _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ John Dalton, father of atomic theory, teaches in a back-street school here.

## Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation — Peter L. Bernstein (2005)
Source: [[Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3294009W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kve6rywar2zdvwtxvdzsjb)

> Under the banner of Corn Law repeal, two Manchester manufacturers, Richard Cobden and John Bright, formed the Manchester Anti-Corn Law Association in 1836, which two years later combined with similar groups to compose the Anti-Corn Law League.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** In the epilogue on the repeal of Britain's Corn Laws, two Manchester manufacturers, Richard Cobden and John Bright, form the Manchester Anti-Corn Law Association in 1836, which grows into the Anti-Corn Law League; a footnote adds that Manchester, a huge importer of raw cotton, is strongly pro-American.
- **Why it matters:** Manchester's free-trade campaign opened Britain to the grain the canal carried from the American West.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80txnnvr77b09tbpja4d9g6)

> In 1826, the French chemist P .J. Pelletier traveled to Manchester to meet the atomic hero.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In 1826 the French chemist P. J. Pelletier travels to Manchester to meet John Dalton, the hero of atomic theory, expecting to find him at some grand institution. Instead he finds him teaching elementary arithmetic to boys in a small school on a back street. Dalton had moved to Manchester a decade after taking a job in Kendal and scarcely stirred from it for the rest of his life.
- **Why it matters:** A comic portrait of the modest home of atomic theory; the school itself isn't named.
