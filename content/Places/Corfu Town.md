# Corfu Town

Port and capital of the island of [[Corfu]]. In _My Family and Other Animals_ it is "the town" where the Durrells land, lodge at the Pension Suisse and come back for shopping, the cinema, Theodore's study and the procession of Saint Spiridion.

## My Family and Other Animals — Gerald Durrell (1956)
Source: [[My Family and Other Animals]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/289004/my-family-and-other-animals-by-gerald-durrell/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8fa99z6ccratnyngw3qxwsa)

> Around us the town rose steeply, tiers of multi-coloured houses piled haphazardly, green shutters folded back from their windows like the wings of a thousand moths.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The family leaves the customs shed and rides two horse-drawn cabs through the main thoroughfare, pursued by about two dozen dogs that Roger has provoked, to the Pension Suisse. The hotel stands "in the road leading to the local cemetery", and the endless ornate funerals passing under the balcony convince Mother there is an epidemic and that they must find a house in the country at once. In the main square's taxi rank they meet Spiro, who takes over their lives. Later the town is where Gerry takes tea in Theodore's study on Thursdays, where Mother and Margo go to the island's only cinema, and where the family is swept into the Saint Spiridion procession.
- **Why it matters:** The town is the hinge between England and the countryside: the funerals and the plumbing drive the family out to the villas, and Spiro, found here, becomes their guide. It recurs as the place the family goes "in" from the olive groves.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The book never names the town; it is the island's only town and port. The Pension Suisse has no street given, so it is folded into this note rather than pinned separately.
