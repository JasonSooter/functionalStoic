# Atari video game burial (Alamogordo)

Landfill in Alamogordo, New Mexico, where Atari buried unsold E.T. cartridges in 1983. A footnote in Range recalls the legend and the 2014 dig.

## Range: Why Generalists Triumph in a Specialized World — David Epstein (2019)
Source: [[Range]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/550188/range-by-david-epstein/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jvv4nn5rz5rry6cavz9w9p)

> Atari buried millions of copies in a landfill in New Mexico. In 2014, the site was excavated as part of a documentary.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** A footnote on the Atari E.T. game flop recounts the legend that Atari buried millions of unsold copies in a landfill in New Mexico. The site was excavated in 2014 for a documentary and did contain buried copies, but far fewer than millions.
- **Why it matters:** Minor: a footnote aside illustrating a legendary failure; not part of the book's main argument.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The footnote says only "a landfill in New Mexico"; the burial site is the Alamogordo landfill, excavated in 2014.
