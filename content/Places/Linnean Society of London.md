# Linnean Society of London

Learned society for natural history, based at Burlington House in [[London]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where Darwin's and Wallace's theory is first made public, to almost no reaction.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83311fksze03gbf695bgv1b)

> The venue they settled on was a meeting of the Linnaean Society, which at the time was struggling to find its way back into fashion as a seat of scientific eminence.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** To settle priority after Wallace's letter arrived, Lyell and Hooker arranged for Darwin's and Wallace's ideas to be presented together at a meeting of the Linnaean Society on July 1, 1858. It was one of seven presentations that evening, heard by about thirty people, and drew no discussion. Darwin was not there: he and his wife were burying their son.
- **Why it matters:** The unveiling of natural selection, and Bryson's best example of how little anyone sensed what they were witnessing.
