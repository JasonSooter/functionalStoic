# Sunda Strait

The Sunda Strait is the channel between Java and Sumatra in Indonesia, linking the Indian Ocean with the Java Sea. In _Moby-Dick_ the Pequod passes through it chasing a vast herd of sperm whales while being chased by Malay pirates.

## Moby-Dick; or, The Whale — Herman Melville (1851)
Source: [[Exploration of Moby Dick]] · [[Moby Dick]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m77b79sbh8x12jhh17wh00)

> By the straits of Sunda, chiefly, vessels bound to China from the west, emerge into the China seas.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In Chapter 87 the Pequod nears the straits, which Ishmael calls the central gateway to a walled empire of islands. A vast herd of sperm whales is sighted just as Malay proas appear astern, so the ship is pursuer and pursued at once as she passes Java Head and Cockatoo Point. Beyond the straits the boats chase the gallied herd, and Queequeg's boat is drawn into its calm centre, among nursing mothers and calves.
- **Why it matters:** It is the gateway from the Indian Ocean into the eastern seas on Ahab's way to Japan and the Pacific, and the setting of the book's great herd set-piece. Ahab reads the passage as the gate to his vengeance.
