# Tilaurakot (Kapilavastu)

Archaeological site in southern Nepal identified with Kapilavastu, the Buddha's childhood home. Mark Manson opens a chapter with the parable of the prince raised in its palace.

## The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F\*ck — Mark Manson (2016)

Source: [[The Subtle Art of Not Giving a Fck]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17590212W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kvb2nwczxettm7tm6x2mb5)

> About twenty-five hundred years ago, in the Himalayan foothills of present-day Nepal, there lived in a great palace a king who was going to have a son.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 2 opens "in the Himalayan foothills of present-day Nepal", with a king raising his son in a great palace behind high walls, shielded from all suffering. The prince sneaks out, sees sickness, age and death in a nearby village, and leaves for good; he later sits under a tree by a river for forty-nine days and becomes the Buddha.
- **Why it matters:** The walled palace illustrates the argument that a life without suffering feels empty. It is a parable setting, not a place explored.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The text names no site; tradition places the palace at Kapilavastu, which Nepal identifies with Tilaurakot (India claims Piprahwa).
