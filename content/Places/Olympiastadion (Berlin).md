# Olympiastadion (Berlin)

Olympic stadium on the western edge of [[Berlin]], by the Grunewald forest. In _The Boys in the Boat_ Hitler visits the old Deutsches Stadion on this site in 1933 and orders a vastly larger stadium for the 1936 Games.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m869xpa5rg2vg9qg6hbjqp4b)

> They had come to inspect the old Deutsches Stadion, built in 1916 for the ill-fated Olympic Games of that year.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In October 1933, the same week the Washington freshmen sign up for crew, Hitler steps out of his car on a rise west of Berlin with Werner March, Theodor Lewald and Wilhelm Frick. March explains that the adjoining racetrack prevents a major expansion of the 1916 stadium; Hitler declares that the racetrack must "disappear" and orders a stadium for at least a hundred thousand people and a massive sports complex. By the summer of 1935 the site is being transformed: west of the stadium the Maifeld has been levelled and a limestone bell tower is going up, its bell to bear "Ich rufe die Jugend der Welt!" between two swastikas. On August 1, 1936, the American team waits for hours on the Maifeld while the Hindenburg circles overhead; at exactly 4:00 p.m. Hitler enters the stadium, and the boys march in before 110,000 people, keep their flag up and their straw hats over their hearts, and watch the cauldron lit. Roger Morris calls it "the most impressive sight I have ever seen". The day after the final, the crew returns to the Reichssportfeld to receive their gold medals and laurel wreaths on the field, Moch on the top step with a potted oak sapling, as their names go up on the scoreboard and Joe weeps as the flag rises. The book looks ahead to the last days of the Third Reich, when Hitler Youth boys crouch among the limestone rubble below the tower, shooting at advancing Russians.
- **Why it matters:** The stadium is the stage of the Nazi spectacle the crew is drawn into, from Hitler's order to the opening ceremony, and finally the place where the boys receive their gold; the bell's summons to youth ends in boys dying at its foot.
