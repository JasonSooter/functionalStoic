# Syracuse

Ancient Greek city on the south-east coast of [[Sicily]], where Archimedes lived and died. It appears in Rovelli's essay on Archimedes, whose death in the Roman sack of the city begins a long eclipse of his science.

## There Are Places in the World Where Rules Are Less Important Than Kindness — Carlo Rovelli (2020)
Source: [[There Are Places in the World Where Rules Are Less Important Than Kindness]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21681470W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5b44npmdaydkkj7smd372xe)

> Archimedes was slaughtered by the Romans during the sacking of Syracuse, the last proud remnant of Magna Grecia to fall under the Roman yoke

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In "Some Think, O King Hiero, That the Grains of Sand Cannot be Counted", Rovelli sets Archimedes' _Sand Reckoner_ against _Ecclesiasticus_, which says the grains of sand cannot be counted. Archimedes' death in the sack of Syracuse begins a millennium in which his calculations "languished in a state of incomprehensibility". Rovelli adds an image from "near to Syracuse": the theatre at Taormina, above the sea and Etna, which staged Sophocles and Euripides and was then adapted by the Romans for gladiatorial combat.
- **Why it matters:** Syracuse's fall marks, for Rovelli, the "complete triumph" of resigned ignorance over Archimedes' confidence that the world can be measured. The essay then turns the tables, point by point.
