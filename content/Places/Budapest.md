## Living Danishly in Budapest? — Helen Russell (2024)
Source: [[Living Danishly in Budapest]] · [Original](https://mshelenrussell.substack.com/p/living-danishly-in-budapest) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5f0q1nryad4n0jh6gx1j27v)

> I’m writing this from a hotel in Budapest, looking out at a Danube that has burst her banks, reaching the highest water levels for a decade.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** Russell writes from a Budapest hotel on the morning she is to speak at PszichoFest ahead of her book's Hungarian launch. The Danube has burst its banks, and she reflects on the city's history, food scene and political climate.
- **Why it matters:** The city's flooded, unsettled state frames her question of whether 'living Danishly' travels. It is the setting of the whole post.

## Range: Why Generalists Triumph in a Specialized World — David Epstein (2019)
Source: [[Range]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/550188/range-by-david-epstein/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjryx5m08fsgb6nfnbsf7j)

> After eight months of study, Laszlo took her to a smoky chess club in Budapest and challenged grown men to play his four-year-old daughter, whose legs dangled from her chair.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Laszlo Polgar takes four-year-old Susan to a smoky Budapest chess club after eight months of training and challenges grown men to play her. She wins her first game and the man she beat storms off; she then wins the Budapest girls' championship. Budapest is also where Laszlo and Klara marry, move, and lobby the Ministry of Education over homeschooling.
- **Why it matters:** The club is where the Polgar experiment first shows results in public, the opening proof for the book's account of early specialization in a kind learning environment. The club is not named.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The club is unnamed.
