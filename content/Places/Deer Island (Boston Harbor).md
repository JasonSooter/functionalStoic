# Deer Island (Boston Harbor)

Former island, now a peninsula, in Boston Harbor, [[Boston]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ its military prison hosts a failed attempt in 1918 to infect volunteers with influenza.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> In an attempt to devise a vaccine, medical authorities conducted tests on volunteers at a military prison on Deer Island in Boston Harbor.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** During the 1918 pandemic, prisoners were promised pardons if they survived a battery of tests: infected lung tissue, aerosols, throat swabs and coughing sufferers. Of three hundred volunteers, sixty-two were chosen and none caught the flu, probably because the epidemic had already passed through; the only person who fell ill was the ward doctor, who died.
- **Why it matters:** Bryson uses it to show how little anyone understood the 1918 flu: even deliberate infection failed, while it killed the man running the test.
