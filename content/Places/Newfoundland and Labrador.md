# Newfoundland and Labrador

Canada's easternmost province, on the North Atlantic. _Not the End of the World_ uses its cod fishery as the classic stock collapse, and _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ a trilobite on Newfoundland as a puzzle for continental drift.

## Not the End of the World — Hannah Ritchie (2024)
Source: [[Not the End of the World]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL37518722W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5f0qzh536qb0605cvbjw4h9)

> Looking at the catch of North Atlantic cod from Newfoundland and Labrador in Eastern Canada, we can see that it started to ramp up from the 17th century.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Ritchie traces the catch of North Atlantic cod from Newfoundland and Labrador: rising from the 17th century, about 100,000 tonnes a year in the 18th and around 250,000 in the 20th, peaking in 1968 and then collapsing, with fisheries closed in the early 1990s.
- **Why it matters:** It is her example of a rich country's fish stock crashing. Province point, not the Grand Banks (approximate).

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80ty7et9jecnceca4x818nx)

> One species of trilobite that was well known in Europe was also found to have lived on Newfoundland

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In the chapter on continental drift, a trilobite species well known in Europe turns up on Newfoundland, but only on one side. No one can explain how it crossed two thousand miles of hostile ocean yet failed to get round the corner of a 200-mile-wide island.
- **Why it matters:** One of the fossil anomalies that the land-bridge theory couldn't explain and drift later could. The pin is the province point (approximate).
