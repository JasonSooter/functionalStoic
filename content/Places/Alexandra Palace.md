# Alexandra Palace

Victorian–Edwardian palace on a hill in north London, home of the first BBC television studios; Attenborough starts his BBC career there in _Life on Air_.

## Life on Air [revised & updated 2nd edition] — David Attenborough (2002)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL24547776M/Life_on_air) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5hx1hry7zgxx072f183w34j)

> Alexandra Palace was - and still is - a sprawling Edwardian building, built on the top of a hill surrounded by a park in the north-eastern suburbs of London.

- **Role:** setting / subject
- **In the book:** He visits Mary Adams in her office beneath the transmitter and is offered a BBC training course. He later does his first TV interview here (Gordon Pirie), works as assistant producer in the two primitive studios, and produces the Song Hunter folk series with Alan Lomax. The chapter describes the cameras, the cramped gallery and the Auntie/Alexandra Palace dynamic in detail.
- **Why it matters:** Birthplace of public television (1936) and the setting of Attenborough's first years in the BBC; the chapter is built around its improvised, primitive working conditions.
