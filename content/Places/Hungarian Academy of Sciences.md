# Hungarian Academy of Sciences

National academy of sciences in [[Budapest]], Hungary. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Milutin Milankovitch works out his ice-age cycles in its library while a prisoner of war.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> He spent most of the next four years under loose house arrest in Budapest, required only to report to the police once a week. The rest of his time was spent working in the library of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Arrested in 1914 as a Serbian army reservist, Milutin Milankovitch spent most of the next four years under loose house arrest in Budapest, reporting to the police once a week. The rest of the time he worked in the Academy's library on the tables of orbital cycles behind ice ages: "possibly the happiest prisoner of war in history."
- **Why it matters:** Where the calculations behind the Milankovitch cycles were done, a wry footnote to the ice-age story.
