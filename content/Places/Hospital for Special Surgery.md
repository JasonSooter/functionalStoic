# Hospital for Special Surgery

Orthopedic hospital in [[Manhattan]], in 1946 on East 42nd Street. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is where Frida has the spinal fusion of June 1946, the major operation of her later life.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)
Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m73vww450b6gy41jkbdh013a)

> Four vertebrae were fused with a piece of bone extracted from her pelvis and with a metal rod fifteen centimeters long.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In May 1946 Frida flies to New York with Cristina, whose hand she insists on holding before the anaesthetic, to be operated on by Dr. Wilson. The operation is performed in June. During her recovery she paints against doctors' orders, receives a last visit from Noguchi, and writes that she longs to flee "this terrifying city" for her beloved Coyoacán.
- **Why it matters:** The fusion was meant to end her back pain; the book traces how its outcome shaped her decline in the years that followed.
