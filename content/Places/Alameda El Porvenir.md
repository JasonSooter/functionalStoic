# Alameda El Porvenir

Raised pedestrian and cycle promenade through the poor southwestern barrios of [[Bogotá]], Colombia. In _Happy City_ it is Charles Montgomery's prime example of the "upside-down road", where walkers and cyclists get the centre and cars the rubbly edge.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84c7xngvnw815q1bfwt7mbv)

> The boulevard we found slicing through the middle of Paradise was just as surprising.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 10. Riding a pedicab through El Paraíso, Montgomery finds a wide runway of concrete and tile down the middle of the barrio, raised knee-high so cars cannot get on it, while cars bump along the rubble at the edge. He calls the whole arrangement a happiness intervention, and later cites the street as a place where the very old and very young can move safely.
- **Why it matters:** It is the clearest physical image of the chapter's equity argument: in a city where few own cars, the best road space was given to the majority who walk and cycle.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real. A photo caption in the book names it Alameda El Porvenir; the text calls the barrio El Paraíso ("Paradise").
