# Jaffa

Jaffa is an ancient port on the Mediterranean coast, now part of Tel Aviv-Yafo in Israel. In _Moby-Dick_ it is Joppa, where Jonah boards ship to flee God in Father Mapple's sermon.

## Moby-Dick — Herman Melville (1851)

Source: [[Exploration of Moby Dick]] · [[Moby Dick]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76vwkjghddtz0eh6yhmhz)

> Because Joppa, the modern Jaffa, shipmates, is on the most easterly coast of the Mediterranean, the Syrian; and Tarshish or Cadiz more than two thousand miles to the westward from that, just outside the Straits of Gibraltar.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In Chapter 9 Father Mapple tells how Jonah skulked about the wharves of Joppa and took ship for Tarshish, which he identifies as Cadiz, arguing that Jonah sought to flee as far from God as the ancient world allowed. In Chapter 82 the narrator claims that a whale skeleton long stood in a pagan temple at Joppa, said to be the monster Perseus slew, and notes that 'it was from Joppa that Jonah set sail'.
- **Why it matters:** A scriptural place used for argument, not a scene of the main story. It ties the sermon's theme of flight from God to the whaling fraternity's mythic past.
