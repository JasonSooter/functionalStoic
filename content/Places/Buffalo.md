# Buffalo

City at the east end of Lake Erie in western New York. In the Erie Canal article it wins the race with Black Rock to become the canal's western terminus.

## Erie Canal — wikipedia.org (2001)
Source: [Original](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_Canal) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k5sd0pmkw9cekt2d8r1p41)

> Buffalo won over Black Rock, and grew into a large city, eventually encompassing its former rival.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** Two villages, Black Rock on the Niagara River and Buffalo at the eastern tip of Lake Erie, competed to be the canal's western terminus. Buffalo widened and deepened Buffalo Creek to build a harbor and won, growing into a large city.
- **Why it matters:** The contest for the western terminus determined which town grew into a city.

## Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation — Peter L. Bernstein (2005)
Source: [[Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3294009W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kvd0are0d1t42ng60jkc1y)

> As the Seneca Chief pulled away from the docks, there were simultaneously a rattle of small arms fire, a blast from a thirty-two-pound cannon, the band playing fortissimo, and robust cheers from the crowds on the shores

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The canal commissioners reach Buffalo in 1810 to find thirty or forty houses; for years the western terminus is contested between Buffalo's shallow harbour and Black Rock's sheltered one and Peter Porter's portage, until Buffalo wins in 1822. On 26 October 1825 the Seneca Chief leaves the Buffalo docks to cannon fire relayed to Sandy Hook, and on its return Judge Wilkeson pours Atlantic water into Lake Erie. Joseph Dart's steam elevator later makes Buffalo the world's largest grain-transfer port.
- **Why it matters:** Buffalo is where the canal meets the Great Lakes; its victory over Black Rock decided which town would become a city.
