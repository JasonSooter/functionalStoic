## Breakneck — Dan Wang (2025)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL42416011W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dcne47qa1k4yjaqdmxkp14)

> I was much happier to live in Shanghai, where many streets have remained human-scaled rather than being built for cars. The French Concession, where I lived, remains leafy and full of cafés.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The author lived in the leafy, café-filled French Concession and cycled a fifteen-mile riverside path past the old World Expo site and preserved industrial buildings, in contrast with car-built Beijing and Shenzhen.
- **Why it matters:** The book's example of the engineering state getting a city right.

## Breakneck — Dan Wang (2025)

Source: [[Breakneck]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL42416011W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ebt57hpkm1rj65gksstkrt)

> Shanghai has many things superior to that of any American city: walkable and safe streets, vibrant street life, splendid food, an ease to go anywhere in the city or the country through mass transit.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Wang explains why he left Shanghai for Yale Law School after zero-Covid collapsed, weighing its urban virtues against censorship and the blocking of his personal website. He returns once at the end of 2024 and finds it strangely muted, with emptier restaurants and shopping districts lacking vitality.
- **Why it matters:** Shanghai stands for both the best of the engineering state's urbanism and the political cost of living under it; the 2024 revisit works as a barometer of 'Fortress China'.

## Around the World in Eighty Days — Jules Verne (1872)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1100007W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne3nhx17qxy9f3w0zccx6p)

> found himself at six o’clock not more than ten miles from the mouth of Shanghai River. Shanghai itself is situated at least twelve miles up the stream. At seven they were still three miles from Shanghai.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Pilot John Bunsby's pilot-boat Tankadere carries Fogg, Aouda and Fix 800 miles north through the Straits of Fo-Kien and a typhoon. On 10-11 November, three miles short of Shanghai, the American steamer is seen leaving, and Fogg has the cannon fired and the flag hoisted at half-mast in distress so it will pick them up.
- **Why it matters:** It is the improvised sea race that saves the wager after the Carnatic is lost.
