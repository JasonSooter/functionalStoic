## Walkable City — Jeff Speck (2012)
Source: [[Walkable City]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL36883695M/Walkable_City) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbkr3ksaxqsjk498ddzga7)

> Dallas presents an instructive example. In 1983, residents of fifteen Dallas-area cities voted to levy a 1 percent sales tax to create what has become the largest light-rail system in the United States.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Speck argues that Dallas's DART light rail failed to reduce driving because the city lacks neighborhood structure, parking is cheap, and stations sit by highways. Ridership dropped while the share driving to work rose, though $4 billion of development grew near stations.
- **Why it matters:** Dallas is the chapter's cautionary case that transit investment without walkable neighborhoods is an investment in real estate, not reduced traffic.

## How An 8-Year-Old Changed My Entire Approach To Design — Garrett Jacobs (2016)
Source: [Original](https://www.good.is/articles/a-lesson-in-participation) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjrghs3gtxvfba72hz261b)

> On presentation day in a new Dallas high school, Ron was asked about his favorite part of the design. His answer was that “we all built it together.”

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** The narrator drove the ReThink students to Dallas in a van to present their school design at a regional competition. In a new Dallas high school, the students presented to judges and other schools. Ron's answer there confirmed his theory of participation for the narrator.
- **Why it matters:** Dallas is where the narrator's lesson about collaborative design is confirmed; the text gives no specific character of the city, so it is a modest, journey-end setting.
