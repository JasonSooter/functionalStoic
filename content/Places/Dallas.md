## Walkable City — Jeff Speck (2012)

Source: [[Walkable City]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL36883695M/Walkable_City) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbkr3ksaxqsjk498ddzga7)

> Dallas presents an instructive example. In 1983, residents of fifteen Dallas-area cities voted to levy a 1 percent sales tax to create what has become the largest light-rail system in the United States.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Speck argues that Dallas's DART light rail failed to reduce driving because the city lacks neighborhood structure, parking is cheap, and stations sit by highways. Ridership dropped while the share driving to work rose, though \$4 billion of development grew near stations.
- **Why it matters:** Dallas is the chapter's cautionary case that transit investment without walkable neighborhoods is an investment in real estate, not reduced traffic.
