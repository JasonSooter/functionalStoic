# Haus der Kunst

Art museum on Prinzregentenstrasse at the edge of Munich's Englischer Garten, built in 1937 as the House of German Art. In _Germany: Memories of a Nation_ it is the stage for the Nazi split between 'German' and 'degenerate' art.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)
Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76sgbmhebpwsrka4gv89e)

> The Haus der Deutschen Kunst was a concept of Hitler’s and it was supposed to show the Aryan-ness and the ideals of German culture. So in July 1937 they opened the exhibition of great German art.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 24 explains through Chris Dercon, a former director, that the Haus der Deutschen Kunst was Hitler's idea, built to show 'Aryan' art. In July 1937 it opened the Great German Art exhibition, and a day later the Degenerate Art show opened a couple of hundred metres away in the Hofgarten, showing works by 112 artists, among them Chagall, Kandinsky and Klee, as examples of degeneracy. Far more people visited the degenerate show.
- **Why it matters:** The pairing staged the Nazi definition of what was German and what was not, and the irony of the crowds is the chapter's sharpest point.
