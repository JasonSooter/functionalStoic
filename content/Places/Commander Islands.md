# Commander Islands

Remote islands in the Bering Sea, east of Kamchatka in [[Russia]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ they are the last refuge of Steller's sea cow, found by shipwrecked Russians in 1741.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83dbffkxwtnqgq5b6rmpy9r)

> in 1741 a Russian expedition happened to be shipwrecked on the only place where the creatures still survived in any numbers, the remote and foggy Commander Islands in the Bering Sea.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** The expedition's naturalist, Georg Steller, studied the giant sea cow closely, down to the diameter of its whiskers, and kept a piece of its skin. Within twenty-seven years of its discovery the animal was extinct.
- **Why it matters:** The islands show how some species are known to science only through one chance encounter, and how fast that knowledge can become all that is left.
