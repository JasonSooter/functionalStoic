# Jesse Hall

Main administration building of the University of Missouri in Columbia, facing the old columns of the quadrangle. In _Stoner_ it is where Archer Sloane tells Stoner he will be a teacher, and its columns become his image of the life he has chosen.

## Stoner — John Williams (1965)

Source: [Original](https://www.nyrb.com/products/stoner) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8afp8qvf7f0cg32kk2pac2a)

> Sometimes he stood in the center of the quad, looking at the five huge columns in front of Jesse Hall that thrust upward into the night out of the cool grass; he had learned that these columns were the remains of the original main building of the University, destroyed many years ago by fire.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In the winter of his fourth year, Stoner climbs the wide stone steps into Jesse Hall and the "smooth marble stairs to Archer Sloane's second-floor office", a long narrow room lined with books. Sloane tells him he is going to be a teacher: "It's love, Mr. Stoner." He gives up the farm for graduate study. The columns in front, the remains of the burned main building, come to mean the way of life he has embraced, "as a temple represents a god." In the war years a student hangs a straw effigy of the Kaiser from one of them, and on Armistice Day a crowd sweeps Stoner through the hall's corridors past Sloane's open door, where the old man sits weeping. The senile Dean Claremont gets lost in the same corridors.
- **Why it matters:** The decision that shapes Stoner's whole life is made here, and the columns are the novel's emblem of the university as a refuge. The building is real, though Williams writes in his prefatory note that he took liberties with the University, "so that in effect it, too, is a fictional place."
