# Clinton Street (Brooklyn)

Brownstone street in [[Brooklyn]], New York; the book's scene is its corner with Pacific Street, near Atlantic Avenue. In _Happy City_ it is where a sleepless neighbour's rage at car horns turned into the livable-streets movement.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84ph4efcxp68q8jtb4wh185)

> They were honking because they were backed up at the light at Clinton and Pacific, not moving at all.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Chapter 13, "Anger and Action". Just before Christmas 2001, Aaron Naparstek is woken again by drivers leaning on their horns at the light at Clinton and Pacific, and throws eggs from his third-floor brownstone window. He then studies the traffic from that window, traces the jam to the signals at Atlantic Avenue, and gets the city to retime them; the honking eases. He goes on to found Streetsblog and Streetfilms. Montgomery talks with him on the stoop in 2010.
- **Why it matters:** The corner shows private anger becoming civic action, the chapter's argument that citizens can change their streets.
