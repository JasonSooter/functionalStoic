# Emerald Pool (Yellowstone)

Hot spring in the Black Sand Basin, part of the Upper Geyser Basin in [[Yellowstone National Park]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where the first heat-loving microbes were found, the start of the road to PCR.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> on my way out of the park, at a place called Emerald Pool, in the Upper Geyser Basin.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** Bryson visits Emerald Pool on his way out of the park because it is "a historic site": in 1965 Thomas and Louise Brock scooped scum from its rim and found microbes living in near-boiling water. The text follows that find to the heat-proof enzyme of _Thermophilus aquaticus_, Kary Mullis and the polymerase chain reaction, which won a Nobel Prize in 1993.
- **Why it matters:** It links the geology chapter to biology: life thriving in a hot spring changes what counts as habitable and, via PCR, transforms science.
