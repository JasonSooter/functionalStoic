# BMW Guggenheim Lab

Temporary urban-research pavilion by Atelier Bow-Wow, set up by the Guggenheim on a vacant lot in [[Manhattan]]'s East Village in 2011; the site is now First Park. In _Happy City_ it is Montgomery's base for experiments on how city spaces make people feel.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83qhyxsmtwa2cbs68m4xfk5)

> The museum commissioned Atelier Bow-Wow, a Japanese architectural firm, to design a temporary shelter in an empty lot in the East Village, then invited a team of collaborators from around the world to use it as a base for our experiments.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In 2011 Montgomery joins the lab's team to study comfort in New York. He describes a neighbourhood of tenement walk-ups and mid-rise condos cut by fast traffic arteries, where everything is within walking distance. With psychologist Colin Ellard, volunteers wear devices that log mood and arousal as they walk set routes from the lab; the lab's own bare-brick social-housing neighbour scores lowest.
- **Why it matters:** The lab's walks supply the book's own field evidence that small doses of nature lift city dwellers' moods (see [[M'Finda Kalunga Garden]] and [[Allen Street (Manhattan)|Allen Street]]).
- **Real vs. fictional:** The pavilion was temporary; the pin marks the lot it stood on.
