# Niagara Falls

## What if you funneled the Niagara Falls through a straw? — xkcd's What If? (2026)

Source: [Original](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/QZIEjvSlOMQ) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5bre12n6pghzvh9qedj2bqh)

> What would happen if one tried to funnel Niagara Falls through a straw?  The short answer is: the Earth would be destroyed.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The thought experiment pushes the falls' flow (at least 100,000 cubic feet per second) through a 7 mm straw; the water would have to move at around a quarter of the speed of light, and the jet would carry the power of a small star.
- **Why it matters:** The falls are the benchmark flow rate for the whole hypothetical. Any big river would work in principle, but Niagara is the familiar image of an unstoppable volume of water.

## The Hidden Engineering of Niagara Falls — Wesley Crump (2025)

Source: [Original](https://practical.engineering/blog/2025/10/21/the-hidden-engineering-of-niagara-falls) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5crere1bhzagw3p73086wrc)

> Niagara Falls is one of the most spectacular waterfalls in the world.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** A tour of the infrastructure around the falls: the [[Welland Canal]] for shipping, hydropower tunnels and plants, the [[International Control Dam]] that splits the flow, pumped storage, and the 1969 dewatering of the American Falls.
- **Why it matters:** The falls are the whole subject: a natural wonder that is also one of the most heavily engineered rivers in the world.

## Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation — Peter L. Bernstein (2005)

Source: [[Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3294009W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kvdkw1bp3jkp8amsskaff3)

> muttering a head of water; at length my eye lit on the falls of Niagara which instantly presented the idea that Lake Erie was that head of water.”

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** At James Geddes's dinner table in Geneva, Jesse Hawley spreads out a map, looks for a head of water for a canal, and his eye lights on the falls of Niagara: Lake Erie itself, held up behind the falls, could supply the water.
- **Why it matters:** The idea that Lake Erie could feed the canal by gravity is the conceptual breakthrough of the whole project.
