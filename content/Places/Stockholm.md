# Stockholm

Capital of Sweden, built on islands between Lake Mälaren and the Baltic (see also the [[Sweden]] travel note). It shows up in Rovelli's last meeting with Hawking, a video on its art-filled metro, an article on its street-tree pits and, in _The Son and Heir_, as the neutral spy capital of a wartime escape.

Not pinned yet: Djurgården, the island park where Bryson spends a Sunday morning in _Neither Here Nor There_ ("Djurgården is possibly the finest of them all") and comes away "admiring Stockholm all over again". No standard-source coordinate was found.

## There Are Places in the World Where Rules Are Less Important Than Kindness — Carlo Rovelli (2020)
Source: [[There Are Places in the World Where Rules Are Less Important Than Kindness]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21681470W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5bevjcnefav552gwj0dxkgq)

> The day after our meeting in Stockholm, where communication with him was so difficult and heartbreaking, Stephen gave a lecture in a huge theatre in the city.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** The essay "Thank You, Stephen". The last time Rovelli saw Hawking was in Stockholm, when "he could barely even move his eyes". The next day Hawking arrived onstage in a huge theatre full of young people and set a prerecorded lecture running with a movement of his eyes, joking about black holes and the meaning of life. "His last words were still an undaunted declaration of love for life."
- **Why it matters:** The Stockholm scene is the concrete evidence for the essay's claim that Hawking's greatest lesson was one of humanity. The city itself does little work; the theatre is not named.

## Every Metro System Should be this Beautiful — Not Just Bikes (2026)
Source: [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5c3pebmc41qf2g1p533w7p1)

> This is a metro station in Stockholm.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the video:** The video presents Stockholm's metro as a system of decorated stations that the city bills as the world's longest art exhibition. The host says about 90% of stations have some artwork, and he discusses cost, maintenance and the 18 new stations under construction, visiting [[T-Centralen]], [[Kungsträdgården metro station]], [[Hötorget metro station]] and [[Tensta metro station]] among others.
- **Why it matters:** The whole video is about this city's metro; it argues that Stockholm shows what every system could be.

## How Stockholm Is Sprouting Healthy Trees From Concrete — Peter Yeung (2025)
Source: [[How Stockholm Is Sprouting Healthy Trees From Concrete]] · [Original](https://reasonstobecheerful.world/stockholm-tree-pit-method/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ebrsxbyqa21xbz216525j7)

> When Stockholm’s Traffic Office conducted a general assessment of street traffic in the Swedish capital in 2001, it came to the shocking conclusion that two-thirds of all trees in the city center were dead or dying.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** A 2001 city assessment found two-thirds of the trees in central Stockholm dying, because paved streets, buried pipes and construction starved roots of air and water. The city's first tree officer, Björn Embrén, then developed the 'Stockholm tree pit' model, with building starting in 2003.
- **Why it matters:** The article is about Stockholm's street trees and the pit method invented there; the dense, paved, pipe-filled centre is exactly what makes the problem and its solution. The second half widens to general urban forestry.

## "Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!": Adventures of a Curious Character — Richard P. Feynman (1985)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL514629W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kfr1kdmhskyg6j8bb78psk)

> During the King’s Dinner I was sitting next to a princess who had gone to college in the United States.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** After winning the Nobel Prize, Feynman dreads the custom of backing away from the king and practises jumping up stairs backwards, then learns the rule has been dropped. At the King's Dinner he jokes with a princess about a handshaking machine for royalty, which falls flat, and hears from an organiser about the seating quarrel over the Russian ambassador; a Japanese ambassador then talks with him about diplomacy and Japan's rapid development.
- **Why it matters:** The ceremony is the book's set piece on Feynman's discomfort with honours and protocol. ⚠️ The text names only Sweden and the King's Dinner; the Nobel ceremony is held in Stockholm.

## The Son and Heir: A Memoir — Alexander Münninghoff (2020)
Source: [Original](https://www.letterenfonds.nl/en/books/heir-and-successor) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m85n9mrphgmse2vh1tehfagy)

> Despite its neutrality, the Swedish capital was swarming with spies, agents, and snitches, but Uncle Tom knew the city like the back of his hand and could shake followers and guide people to safe houses.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** The author's grandfather, the Old Boss, and Eduard Koning make three German-approved trips to Stockholm, nominally to study Swedish gas generators: in December 1942, in January 1944 (when Uncle Tom arranges a safe house for a day of talks with an official brought over from London), and in July 1944, flying via Berlin. On the last trip Tom takes Koning to a secret location, where a London agent brings him to a tiny airport outside the city and a plane to London; the next morning the Old Boss reports his companion's "disappearance" to the Stockholm police.
- **Why it matters:** Stockholm is the evidence for the book's claim that the Old Boss, who traded with the occupiers, also served the Dutch government in exile; without a neutral capital full of spies, his double game would have had nowhere to play out.
