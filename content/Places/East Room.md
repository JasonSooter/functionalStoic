# East Room

State room on the first floor of the White House in Washington, D.C. It turns up in How to Be an Antiracist (Ibram X. Kendi): White House room where Clinton announced the human genome map in 2000.

## How to Be an Antiracist — Ibram X. Kendi (2019)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL42715690M/How_to_Be_an_Antiracist) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fmsrjt9j118zef6yfpgvh3)

> “Today, the world is joining us here in the East Room to behold a map of even greater significance,” Clinton announced.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Clinton announces completion of the first survey of the human genome, saying humans are 99.9 percent the same, invoking Jefferson's map of the continent in the same room.
- **Why it matters:** The venue lends authority to the claim of genetic equality that the chapter on biology builds on.

## The Underground Art of the Insult - The New York Times — Anna Holmes (2015)
Source: [Original](https://www.nytimes.com/2015/05/17/magazine/the-underground-art-of-the-insult.html) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5g0edhq497kxj5k0wp6cvrv)

> Dressed in a brown fur coat, a sequined black dress and a large, glittery pendant, she strode through a dark red door into the East Room, waving regally at a line of well-wishers who stood to applaud her.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** At a 2014 White House event, Aretha Franklin enters the East Room and turns away from Patti LaBelle's outstretched hand; the moment goes viral and anchors the article's definition of 'shade'.
- **Why it matters:** The grand setting makes the small gesture newsworthy, the hook for the whole piece.
