# Canary Islands

Spanish archipelago in the Atlantic off north-west Africa. In The Old Man and the Sea Santiago dreams nightly of its harbours and of the African beaches he saw as a young sailor.

## The Old Man and the Sea — Ernest Hemingway (1952)
Source: [[The Old Man and the Sea]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL7265161M) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5gm07k78v2n7yxeree9w6q8)

> then he dreamed of the different harbours and roadsteads of the Canary Islands.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Santiago once sailed before the mast on a square-rigged ship to Africa and saw lions on the beaches. Now he only dreams of places: the white peaks of the Islands, the harbours and roadsteads of the Canary Islands, and the lions playing on the sand. The novella ends with him asleep, dreaming about the lions.
- **Why it matters:** The dream places carry Santiago's youth and peace through the book; the closing line returns to them, so they matter more than their brief mentions suggest.
