# Ch'angan (Prince Jen)

The royal capital of the fairy-tale Kingdom of T'ang in Lloyd Alexander's novel, named after the real T'ang capital Chang'an (today's [[Xi'an]]). Prince Jen's journey to the legendary kingdom of T'ien-kuo starts at its Celestial Palace.

## The Remarkable Journey of Prince Jen — Lloyd Alexander (1991)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/319277/the-remarkable-journey-of-prince-jen-by-lloyd-alexander/9780142402252) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m887mkkx95b1g8qtyttqrxk3)

> ONE MORNING, A RAGGED OLD MAN came hobbling to the Jade Gate of the Celestial Palace in Ch'angan.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The novel opens at the Jade Gate of the Celestial Palace, where the ragged Master Wu talks his way in to see the ailing King T'ai and tells him of T'ien-kuo, a kingdom "far north of here". Prince Jen volunteers to go, spends three days while Wu picks six humble gifts from the palace storerooms, and leaves with General Li Kwang's escort across the Great Square of Tranquil Harmony; the city's slums appal him and he vows to come back and help.
- **Why it matters:** The capital is the point of departure and the home Jen is told he can still slink back to; the whole journey is measured against it. As a stylised palace city it could be any imperial capital, so its real-world weight is mostly in the name.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The realm is invented, but the text calls it the "Kingdom of T'ang" and its capital "Ch'angan", the name of the real T'ang dynasty capital. The palace, gates and squares are fictional.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Inferred. The text names the city but gives no geography beyond T'ien-kuo lying "far north"; reviewers (Kirkus, Fantasy Literature) describe only an imaginary Chinese kingdom and name no real model. Pinned at Xi'an, the site of historical Chang'an, because the name and the "Kingdom of T'ang" point there. Alternative: treat it as purely fictional with no pin.
