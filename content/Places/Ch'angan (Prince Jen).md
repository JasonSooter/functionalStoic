# Ch'angan (Prince Jen)

The royal capital of the fairy-tale Kingdom of T'ang in Lloyd Alexander's novel, named after the real T'ang capital Chang'an (today's [[Xi'an]]). Prince Jen sets out from its Celestial Palace on his journey and comes back to win it from a usurper.

## The Remarkable Journey of Prince Jen — Lloyd Alexander (1991)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/319277/the-remarkable-journey-of-prince-jen-by-lloyd-alexander/9780142402252) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m887mkkx95b1g8qtyttqrxk3)

> ONE MORNING, A RAGGED OLD MAN came hobbling to the Jade Gate of the Celestial Palace in Ch'angan.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The novel opens at the Jade Gate of the Celestial Palace, where the ragged Master Wu talks his way in to see the ailing King T'ai and tells him of T'ien-kuo, a kingdom "far north of here". Prince Jen volunteers to go, spends three days while Wu picks six humble gifts from the palace storerooms, and leaves with General Li Kwang's escort across the Great Square of Tranquil Harmony; the city's slums appal him and he vows to come back and help. A year later, now king after his father's death, he rides back from Nang-pei and is seized at the Lotus Bridge on the outskirts by the rebel Natha, whose Yellow Scarves took the city when the old king died. Jen is marched through the Jade Gate to be beheaded in the square. Li Kwang's troop, turned to stone and now restored to flesh, arrives in time, and in the Happy Phoenix Gardens the painter Chen-cho's tiger drives Natha into a painting. In the Great Hall of Audience the six gifts are returned, and Jen rules with Voyaging Moon. Years later he sets out north again, but turns home "scarcely a full day from Ch'angan" when his old teacher Master Hu tells him T'ien-kuo is "any place you make it to be".
- **Why it matters:** The capital is both the start and the end of the journey, and the book's last turn makes it the real goal: the kingdom Jen sought is the one he left. As a stylised palace city it could be any imperial capital, so its real-world weight is mostly in the name.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The realm is invented, but the text calls it the "Kingdom of T'ang" and its capital "Ch'angan" (also "Ch'ang-an"), the name of the real T'ang dynasty capital. The palace, gates, Lotus Bridge, squares and gardens are fictional.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Inferred. The text names the city but gives no geography beyond T'ien-kuo lying "far north" and Nang-pei a hard ride or more to the north; reviewers (Kirkus, Fantasy Literature) describe only an imaginary Chinese kingdom and name no real model. Pinned at Xi'an, the site of historical Chang'an, because the name and the "Kingdom of T'ang" point there. Alternative: treat it as purely fictional with no pin.
