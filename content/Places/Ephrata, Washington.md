# Ephrata, Washington

Small town in the scablands of central Washington near the Grand Coulee. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is where Franklin D. Roosevelt speaks to twenty thousand people about the Grand Coulee Dam in August 1934.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86m5tj87r4pw2ebkqsrdk8j)

> Ephrata, a forlorn little town of 516 people, out in the desolate scablands, not far from the Columbia River and a fifty-mile-long dry canyon called the Grand Coulee.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** On August 4, 1934, roads across eastern Washington fill with cars converging on the town of 516 people. By midafternoon twenty thousand people, George Pocock and his family among them, stand behind a rope line as Roosevelt lays out what the dam will bring in return for 175 million public dollars: 1.2 million acres of desert reclaimed for farming, irrigation water and cheap power.
- **Why it matters:** The rally is the book's picture of the first real hope after the worst Depression years, the public mood against which the crew's rise plays out. The boys themselves are not there.
