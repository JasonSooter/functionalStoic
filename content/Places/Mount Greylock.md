## Dispatch from Herman Melville’s Farm — Cal Newport (2024)
Source: [[Dispatch From Herman Melville’s Farm]] · [Original](https://calnewport.com/dispatch-from-herman-melvilles-farm/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbgsytjb7qk6446mwwn0es)

> He pushed the table against a window offering a direct view of the hump-backed Mount Greylock in the distance:

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the article:** Melville positioned his writing table at a window facing Mount Greylock. The essay notes the legend that its whale-like shape inspired Moby Dick, rejects that since the novel was conceived earlier, but still calls the mountain an important source of poetic inspiration.
- **Why it matters:** The view is a recurring element of the writing setup, but the mountain is described only from the window. Moderate significance.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Legend of Moby Dick inspiration is debunked in the text itself.
