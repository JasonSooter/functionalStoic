# Eatonville, Florida

All-Black town just outside Orlando, Florida. In _How to Know a Person_, Zora Neale Hurston's hometown is David Brooks's main example of the place you come from and never quite leave.

## How to Know a Person — David Brooks (2023)
Source: [[How to Know a Person]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/652822/how-to-know-a-person-by-david-brooks/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8gxr69g52cydv1v176wnkrk)

> In those days the social center of town was the front porch of Joe Clarke’s store. The men would hang out there through the afternoons and evenings—boasting, trading gossip, exchanging passing opinions on the world.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In the chapter on how ancestors and origins show up in a life, Brooks follows Hurston from age three in Eatonville, an all-Black town with a Black mayor, marshal and council. Barred from Joe Clarke's store porch, young Zora dragged her feet past it to hear the men's "lying sessions" of boasts, gossip and folk tales. After years of wandering she came back on Franz Boas's urging to collect the town's folklore, and set her stories there.
- **Why it matters:** Quoting her biographer Valerie Boyd, Brooks says "essentially everything" Hurston would write and believe began in Eatonville. The town is the hinge of his argument that each of us has "a certain spot on this earth that is somehow sacred". It works only because Eatonville was a self-governing Black town, which gave her both individualism and community.
