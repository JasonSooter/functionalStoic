# Sakıp Sabancı Museum

Museum in the Sabancı family's former mansion on the Bosphorus at Emirgan, [[Istanbul]]. David Byrne is shown round it in _Bicycle Diaries_ and finds its Ottoman calligraphy more modern than the paintings downstairs.

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m76420rp32hbfswycgb6zsna)

> The house is a museum—in the Victorian sense. The ground floor is filled floor to ceiling with paintings, vases, period furniture, statues, and glass cases filled with silver objects.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** After a rainy drive along the Bosphorus, Byrne's hosts take him through a white gate to a giant mansion on the water, with a smaller modern house beside it. A butler shows him the collection of the businessman and philanthropist Sakıp Sabancı; upstairs the calligraphy (Qur'ans, edicts) strikes him as text as art.
- **Why it matters:** The mansion is one pole of his Istanbul, set directly against the Sulukule belly-dance house the same night.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The text describes the Sabancı mansion without naming it; it is now the Sakıp Sabancı Museum.
