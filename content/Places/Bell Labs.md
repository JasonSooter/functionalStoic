# Bell Labs

Bell Labs was AT&T's research arm, whose main laboratory was at Murray Hill, New Jersey. In _Abundance_ it is the model "idea factory" and the birthplace of the solar cell.

## Abundance — Ezra Klein & Derek Thompson (2025)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL44034521W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m753g5m423hmcbh3jd701w)

> Bell Labs, officially known as Bell Telephone Laboratories, was established in 1925 as the research and development arm of AT&T and Western Electric.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 4 sets Bell Labs beside DARPA as a midcentury idea factory: its engineers built the first transistor in 1947 and the first practical silicon solar cell in 1954, and Jon Gertner explains why its monopoly-backed funding worked. Chapter 5 returns to the 1954 solar cell, unveiled at a press conference with a miniature solar-powered Ferris wheel, as a technology America invented and then let other countries scale. Its Picturephone reappears at the 1964 World's Fair.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's prime example of a research institution worth reviving, and its solar cell anchors the argument that the US invents and then fails to deploy.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The book does not say where the lab was; Wikipedia's Bell Labs article places the transistor work at the Murray Hill site (600 Mountain Ave), which is the pin.
