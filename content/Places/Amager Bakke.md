# Amager Bakke

Waste-to-energy plant in the industrial harbour area of [[Copenhagen]], Denmark, with a ski slope on its roof. In _Happy City_ it opens Charles Montgomery's chapter on "hedonistic sustainability".

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84c84kzjw8jtxjdkn4j0nsw)

> Ingels proposed wrapping the giant structure in an exoskeleton whose winding roof would serve as an artificial ski slope the size of seven football fields (333,700 square feet).

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 11, "Everything Is Connected to Everything Else". In a 2010 competition for a new garbage-burning power plant, Bjarke Ingels Group proposed a winding roof that would be an artificial ski slope, so Copenhageners would not have to travel to Sweden for a mountain. The design mixes uses that are usually kept apart and turns an industrial district into a fun zone; BIG calls the idea "hedonistic sustainability".
- **Why it matters:** The plant is the chapter's opening metaphor: in a city everything is connected, and the green city and the happy city can be one design.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The book describes the 2010 design proposal; ⚠️ the finished building is not described in the text.
