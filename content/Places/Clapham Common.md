# Clapham Common

Common and surrounding district in south London, in [[London]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Henry Cavendish weighs the Earth in his house in Clapham, and Gideon Mantell is crippled crossing the common.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> His wealth and solitary inclinations allowed him to turn his house in Clapham into a large laboratory where he could range undisturbed through every corner of the physical sciences

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The rich, painfully shy Cavendish turns his house in Clapham into a laboratory. In late summer 1797 he assembles John Michell's apparatus there, with two 350-pound lead balls beside smaller spheres on torsion wires, and watches from an adjoining room through a telescope aimed through a peephole so as not to disturb it; nearly a year of measurements gives the mass of the Earth. Later, in 1841, Gideon Mantell falls from his carriage while crossing Clapham Common and is dragged over rough ground by the panicked horses, leaving him crippled.
- **Why it matters:** Cavendish's house is where the weighing of the Earth is finally done well, the climax of the chapter; the common is the scene of the accident that begins Mantell's long decline in the dinosaur chapter.
