# Schiehallion

Isolated mountain above Loch Tay in the central Scottish Highlands, in [[Scotland]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where Nevil Maskelyne's 1774 survey weighed the Earth.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> So for four months in the summer of 1774, Maskelyne lived in a tent in a remote Scottish glen and spent his days directing a team of surveyors, who took hundreds of measurements from every possible position.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Charles Mason finds the mountain they need, "just above Loch Tay," but declines the job, so Nevil Maskelyne spends the summer of 1774 camped in the glen while surveyors measure the mountain from every side, to see how far its mass pulls a plumb bob. Charles Hutton, turning the mass of figures into lines joining points of equal height, invents contour lines, and from the Schiehallion results calculates a mass for the Earth.
- **Why it matters:** The mountain experiment gives the first figure for the Earth's mass in the book, which uses its weak point (no one knew the mountain's actual density) to set up Cavendish's experiment. A mountain of known, regular shape was the whole requirement, so the setting is the method.
