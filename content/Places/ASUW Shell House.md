# ASUW Shell House

Former Navy seaplane hangar on the Montlake Cut at the [[University of Washington]], Seattle. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is the Washington crew's shell house, where Joe Rantz signs up for rowing in 1933 and George Pocock builds shells in the loft.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m869xd7ajkyrec823ta1rjg0)

> It was an old airplane hangar built by the U.S. Navy in 1918 to house seaplanes for the Naval Aviation Training Corps during the Great War.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** On a grey October day in 1933, Joe Rantz and Roger Morris walk down from campus to the point where the Montlake Cut enters Union Bay and join 175 boys crowding the ramp of the shell house, a hangar handed to the university in 1919. The freshmen gather on its ramp every afternoon to hear Coach Bolles, and on November 28, 1933, Bolles announces the first boat here, with Joe in it. At fall turnout on October 5, 1934, drought has left the floating dock "high, dry, and useless." In the winter of 1935 Ulbrickson summons Joe and four others to his office here and warns them they could lose their seats. In September 1935 George Pocock invites Joe up to his bright, varnish-scented loft, where an eight-oared shell lies on a long I beam; he shows him his tools and the cedar, flexing a thin sheet to show how wood under tension gives a shell life. Afterwards Joe returns often to sit with Pocock.
- **Why it matters:** It is where the crew's story begins and the base the boys return to every day; the cathedral-like hangar, and Pocock's loft above it, carry the book's sense of rowing as craft and devotion.
