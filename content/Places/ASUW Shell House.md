# ASUW Shell House

Former Navy seaplane hangar on the Montlake Cut at the [[University of Washington]], Seattle. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is the Washington crew's shell house, where Joe Rantz signs up for rowing in 1933 and George Pocock builds shells in the loft.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m869xd7ajkyrec823ta1rjg0)

> It was an old airplane hangar built by the U.S. Navy in 1918 to house seaplanes for the Naval Aviation Training Corps during the Great War.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** On a grey October day in 1933, Joe Rantz and Roger Morris walk down from campus to the point where the Montlake Cut enters Union Bay and join 175 boys crowding the ramp of the shell house, a hangar handed to the university in 1919. Inside, racing shells are stacked four high under banners while the freshmen are weighed and signed in, and Coach Al Ulbrickson watches from the floating dock as they leave at sunset. Afterwards the freshmen gather on its ramp every afternoon to hear Coach Bolles before practice, and George Pocock builds shells in the loft above the boats. On November 28, 1933, the last practice day of the fall term, Bolles announces the first and second freshman boats here, with Joe in the first. At fall turnout on October 5, 1934, a summer of drought has left the floating dock "high, dry, and useless," and Ulbrickson lays down his rules on the ramp.
- **Why it matters:** It is where the book's story of the crew begins and the base the boys return to every day; the cathedral-like hangar sets the tone of devotion that the book attaches to rowing.
