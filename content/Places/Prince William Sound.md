# Prince William Sound

Sound on the Gulf of Alaska coast, [[Alaska]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ its glaciers shrug off the strongest earthquake ever recorded in North America.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> In 1964, at Prince William Sound in Alaska, one of the largest glacial fields in North America was hit by the strongest earthquake ever recorded on the continent. It measured 9.2 on the Richter scale.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In 1964 the strongest earthquake ever recorded on the continent, magnitude 9.2, struck one of North America's largest glacial fields here. The land rose by as much as twenty feet along the fault and water sloshed out of pools in Texas, yet the glaciers just soaked it up and kept moving.
- **Why it matters:** Bryson's measure of the power of ice: nothing humans could do would stop an advancing ice sheet.
