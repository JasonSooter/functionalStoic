# Groningen

City in the north of the [[Netherlands]], famous for how much of its traffic goes by bike. It appears in a Not Just Bikes video on trams, and in _The Brompton_ as the home of the Dutch distributor whose buy-out Will Butler-Adams signed there.

## The Absolute Best Transportation for Cities (trams) — Not Just Bikes (2025)
Source: [Original](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bNTg9EX7MLw) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ce56jt29ejt6anc40081vt)

> Now compare that to the centre of Groningen.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the video:** The mostly pedestrianised centre has squares that had to stay open to buses, which turned them into roads with asphalt and kerbs. Groningen removed the buses, making the square better but the centre harder to reach by transit; the narrator argues trams would have avoided that trade-off.
- **Why it matters:** A counter-example that sharpens the case for trams.

## The Brompton: Engineering for Change — Will Butler-Adams (2022)
Source: [[The Brompton - Engineering for Change]] · [Original](https://profilebooks.com/work/the-brompton/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8cdhq99606jtnt2z38bs5j7)

> Two weeks later I walked into his office in Groningen. The documents were ready.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In chapter 11 Brompton decides to buy out its distributors, starting with Simon Koorn, the colourful Dutch distributor who had given Benelux Bromptons their own names. After what Will calls one of the most agonising and emotional meetings he has held, in Brussels, he walks into Koorn's Groningen office two weeks later; signing takes about thirty minutes and "there wasn't anything left to say". Chapter 12 returns to Groningen, Koorn's home town, as a city where most journeys are made by bike.
- **Why it matters:** The signing ends the era of independent distributors in Brompton's home markets, the shift the chapter is about.
