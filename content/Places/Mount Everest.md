# Mount Everest

Highest mountain on Earth, on the border of [[Nepal]] and Tibet. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it shows how badly the human body copes with altitude.

## At a glance
| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Short History of Nearly Everything | Bill Bryson | backdrop | Climbers' accounts of the Death Zone show how quickly altitude overwhelms the body. |

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> the climber Peter Habeler has written of conditions atop Everest, “every step at that altitude demands a colossal effort of will.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Bryson quotes the climber Peter Habeler on the colossal effort every step demands near the summit, and recounts Howard Somervell on the 1924 British expedition choking and coughing up the mucus lining of his larynx. He names the area above 25,000 feet the Death Zone.
- **Why it matters:** Part of the book's case that most of the planet is hostile to humans; Everest is the vivid extreme, though only briefly visited.
