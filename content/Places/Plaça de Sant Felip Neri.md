## Why Cities and Walking Go Hand in Hand — Sandy James (2016)

> This was a place where school children had been killed during the time of Franco-the space still held that grief, despite the fact that there was no marker depicting what had happened.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** A square tucked behind a cathedral, with soaring walls and families picnicking, is where school children were killed under Franco. Though unmarked, the space still carries the grief and people hurry through it.
- **Why it matters:** Shows how a place holds memory that walking lets you feel.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The essay does not name the square: a small space tucked behind a cathedral, with soaring walls, "where school children had been killed during the time of Franco". That matches Plaça de Sant Felip Neri, where a 1938 bombing killed children sheltering in the church; no other Barcelona square fits as well.
