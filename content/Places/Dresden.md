# Dresden

Capital of Saxony on the Elbe, a baroque court city largely destroyed in February 1945. In _Germany: Memories of a Nation_ it appears both as the home of Saxon porcelain and as the emblem of post-war ruin.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)

Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m766z7wyrpxvhg613xzfsk)

> In the centre of Dresden, for example, the destruction was almost total: two and a half square miles of one of the most beautiful cities in the world had simply vanished.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In Chapter 18 Dresden is where Böttger's experiments turned earth into 'white gold' and where Augustus the Strong's porcelain aviary and menagerie, including rhinoceroses after Dürer, can still be seen. Chapter 27, 'Beginning again', makes it the image of 1945: two and a half square miles of the city simply gone, with survivors short of food, fuel and housing, and a Trümmerfrau sculpture by Max Lachnit honouring the rubble women.
- **Why it matters:** It frames the book's contrast between courtly splendour and the scale of destruction from which the economic miracle began.
