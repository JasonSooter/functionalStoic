# East Bank

Newer part of [[Alexandra (Johannesburg)|Alexandra]] township, across the Jukskei River. In Born a Crime the low wall outside Bongani's house on Springbok Crescent is where Trevor Noah spends three years hustling.

## Born a Crime — Trevor Noah (2016)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17824318W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6z18ykjnqtx6gafbkkdtba0)

> After Twentieth Avenue you hit the Jukskei River, and on the far side of that, across the Roosevelt Street Bridge, was East Bank, the newest, nicest part of the hood.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapter 16 ("The Cheese Boys"). The government had cleared the squatters from East Bank and built two-bedroom houses with tiny yards, and families there sent their kids to schools like Sandringham. Bongani's parents live at the corner of Roosevelt Street and Springbok Crescent. Trevor and his friends sit on the low brick wall down the middle of the road every day, and sell CDs and DJ out of the garage. Noah writes that he "was about to spend the next three years of my life hanging out at that very spot."
- **Why it matters:** East Bank's relative comfort is what makes them "cheese boys", kids with a bit more than the rest. It is the everyday stage for the chapter's account of how hard it is to get out of the hood.
