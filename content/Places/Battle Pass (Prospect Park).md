# Battle Pass (Prospect Park)

Pass in today's Prospect Park, Brooklyn, fought over in the 1776 Battle of Long Island. Phil Klay uses the rout of Washington's citizen-soldiers there in his essay on the military.

## The Citizen-Soldier Moral Risk and the Modern Military — Phil Klay (2016)

Source: [Original](https://www.brookings.edu/research/essays/2016/the-citizen-soldier/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jvp4haemhvfs07jgf5ba6x)

> The American line stretched out across central Brooklyn, with British troops advancing from the south and the east. Though there was skirmishing during the day on August 26, the real fighting began the next morning when a column of Hessians marched up Battle Pass, in modern day Prospect Park.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** Washington's 19,000 citizen-soldiers face Hessian mercenaries in August 1776 and are routed; the Hessians mock them as amateurs. The defeat teaches Washington the need for a standing army.
- **Why it matters:** Anchors the essay's historical argument about distrust of professional soldiers; the place is a battlefield backdrop for the argument.
