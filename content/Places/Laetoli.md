# Laetoli

Fossil site in northern [[Tanzania]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it preserves the footprints of two australopithecines walking through volcanic ash.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> The prints had been made when two australopithecines had walked through muddy ash following a volcanic eruption.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Mary Leakey found footprints left by two australopithecines who walked through muddy ash after a volcanic eruption; the ash hardened and preserved their tracks for over twenty-three meters. The prints are firm evidence that they walked upright, and Bryson goes on to the museum diorama built on them.
- **Why it matters:** Hard evidence of early bipedalism, set against how much of the popular picture is imagined.
