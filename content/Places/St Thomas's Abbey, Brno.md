# St Thomas's Abbey, Brno

Augustinian abbey in Brno, [[Czech Republic]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where Gregor Mendel breeds his peas and works out the laws of inheritance.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Moreover, the monastery at Brno where he lived from 1843 was known as a learned institution. It had a library of twenty thousand books and a tradition of careful scientific investigation.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Mendel lived at the monastery from 1843; Bryson stresses it was a learned institution with a library of twenty thousand books, not the home of a naive kitchen-garden monk. With two assistants he bred and crossbred hybrids from thirty thousand pea plants over eight years, presented the results to the Natural History Society of Brno in 1865 to a politely unmoved audience, and was eventually made abbot.
- **Why it matters:** Site of the work that supplied the mechanism of inheritance Darwin's theory needed; the two ideas would not be joined for decades.
