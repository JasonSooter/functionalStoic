# Thermopylae

Narrow coastal pass and hot-springs site in central Greece, between the mountains and the sea. It is the "Hot Gates" that _Gates of Fire_ builds toward from its first pages.

## At a glance

| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gates of Fire | Steven Pressfield | setting | The pass the allies hold for three days, from the rebuilt Phokian Wall to the knoll of the last stand, where the dead are later buried. |

## Gates of Fire — Steven Pressfield (1998)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/133742/gates-of-fire-by-steven-pressfield/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m896htyeqztfjbmd36t83r8a)

> There is a double spring sacred to Persephone, called the Skyllian fountain, at the foot of the bluff beside the Middle Gate. Upon this site the Spartans established their camp, between the Phokian Wall and the hillock where the final tooth-and-nail struggle took place.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Xeones, the captured squire, first describes the place to the Great King: the "hot gates" named for the thermal springs, the spa with its bathhouses, the old Phokian Wall and the Skyllian fountain by the Middle Gate where the Spartans camped. The allies arrive on the sixth day out of Lakedaemon, through the village of Alpenoi, where locals have set up stalls for the army. Masons wreck the track toward Trachis and carve a message to Xerxes's Greek conscripts: "fight badly." Leonidas starts rebuilding the Phokian Wall by carrying the first boulder himself ("a wall of stone will not preserve Hellas, but a wall of men"). On the first day of battle, the Thespaians hold the "dance floor" of the Narrows between the mountain wall and the cliffs over the gulf, with marker stones (Lizard, Crown and Lion Stone) setting the lines. Xerxes watches from a throne on the ridge above. The Spartans rout the Median lancers, and farther back along the track Medes are shoved off the cliff into the sea. Dienekes loses an eye, and the allies give thanks at the spring behind the sallyport. At dusk the Immortals break against the Narrows. That night mudslides sluice the camp, and the Skyllian spring, until then sweet and cold, runs hot and sulphurous; beside it Xeo tells the wounded Dienekes about finding Diomache at [[Phaleron]]. On the second day the Thebans desert, and the enemy tears the stones from the face of the Wall and floods over it in a squall, until a thunderbolt strikes the cliff of Kallidromos and the allies drive them back. After the night raid on Xerxes's camp at [[Trachis]], the survivors rope down at sunrise and bury Alexandros in the Spartan precinct beside the West Gate. Most of the allies march out, burning the bathhouses. Leonidas, wounded in both legs, refuses the last Persian offer from the battlement, and the Spartans and Thespaians fight in the open before the Wall. When it is pulled down they fall back to the knoll where the last weapons are cached, and the Immortals shoot them down there. In the Persian historian's postscript, a Spartan memorial party, wives of the fallen among them, beaches at the mouth of the Spercheios the next summer and walks to the pass. Three bodies kept by the people of Trachis and an urn of ashes returned from Athens are buried in the Lakedaemonian mound "sited upon a knoll directly above the sea", under Simonides's two-line epitaph, while a veiled woman lingers alone at the grave.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's destination and title, and from the middle of the novel on its main stage. The whole narrative is a survivor's account of how the defenders came to stand and fight here, in a gap narrow enough for a few thousand to hold, so no other setting would work. The burial on the knoll and the epitaph close the novel here too.
