# Thermopylae

Narrow coastal pass and hot-springs site in central Greece, between the mountains and the sea. It is the "Hot Gates" that _Gates of Fire_ builds toward from its first pages.

## At a glance
| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gates of Fire | Steven Pressfield | subject | The pass and spa Xeones describes to Xerxes: Phokian Wall, Skyllian fountain, the Spartan camp. |

## Gates of Fire — Steven Pressfield (1998)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/133742/gates-of-fire-by-steven-pressfield/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m896htyeqztfjbmd36t83r8a)

> There is a double spring sacred to Persephone, called the Skyllian fountain, at the foot of the bluff beside the Middle Gate. Upon this site the Spartans established their camp, between the Phokian Wall and the hillock where the final tooth-and-nail struggle took place.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Xeones, the captured squire, explains the place to the Great King early in the novel. He describes the "hot gates" named for the thermal springs and the narrow defiles, the spa with its groves, pool houses and vendors' booths, and the old Phokian Wall, which the Spartans rebuilt. He also describes the Skyllian fountain by the Middle Gate where they camped, and the hillock of the last stand. Every chapter of his life story is told as "the road which found its end at the Hot Gates."
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's destination and title. The whole narrative is a survivor's account of how the defenders came to stand here, so no other setting would work.
