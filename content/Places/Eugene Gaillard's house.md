# Eugene Gaillard's house

The white country house of the film-maker Eugene Gaillard, in the mountains south of Dublin; pinned at Glencree in the Wicklow Mountains. In _The Country Girls Trilogy_ Kate lives there with Eugene through most of the second novel.

## The Country Girls Trilogy — Edna O'Brien (1986)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL69252W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76anc36y6fjam58c423sd)

> When we came around the corner of the drive, my first thought was that his house looked so happy and peaceful.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Kate first visits on a winter Sunday and meets Anna, the caretaker, who tells her of Eugene's absent wife, Laura. After escaping from home she takes the bus out and stays; her father, cousin Andy and the Ferret break in and attack Eugene in his study until Anna fires a shotgun into the ceiling. Kate and Eugene walk in the woods and by the lake, go to Mass at a new chapel miles off, endure stares in the village hotel, and quarrel over his friends Simon and Mary and his superior manner.
- **Why it matters:** This is the main setting of The Lonely Girl, the place where Kate's romance turns into a lopsided domestic life. Its isolation lets Eugene shape her, and makes her leaving a real escape.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The house is never placed by name. Clues: Kate takes a bus 'at the quays' and the drive is 'in fact only about an hour'; the poet Simon 'had cycled over from Glencree, nearby'; Simon jokes she is 'shining quietly behind a bushel of Wicklow bran'; the estate has a lake and a river 'that flowed out from the lake to the distant sea', woods and deer, and they drive 'down the mountain' to the Shelbourne. Reference works I checked say nothing about a model. Chosen: Glencree in the Wicklow Mountains, the one nearby place the text names. Alternatives: Lough Dan, Lough Tay or the Roundwood area, which also have lakes and rivers.
