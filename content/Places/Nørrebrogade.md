# Nørrebrogade

Main street of the Nørrebro district, [[Copenhagen]], Denmark. In the reading it is Copenhagen's showcase cycling street: where the green-wave pilot began, and where _Happy City_ sees street space handed from cars to bikes and buses.

## ‘It’s the best feeling’: how Copenhagen gave cyclists a green wave — Ajit Niranjan (2025)
Source: [[‘It’s the Best Feeling’ - How Copenhagen Gave Cyclists a Green Wave]] · [Original](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2025/sep/26/how-copenhagen-gave-cyclists-a-green-wave?CMP=Share_AndroidApp_Other) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5crem6mxh0z9fa86kcvrn96)

> “The idea is nice because usually the lights are set at the speed of the cars,” says Lars, a 33-year-old analyst cycling down Nørrebrogade, where the first pilot project began.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** The first green-wave pilot, traffic lights timed to cycling speed, began on Nørrebrogade; a commuter riding it wants the scheme expanded, and the city's mayor rides it daily to city hall.
- **Why it matters:** The street is the proving ground of the scheme and the article's anchor.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84c7q0y915pm89bqyt5x6jz)

> Tørslov’s answer is written right here on Nørrebrogade, the road that crosses Queen Louise’s Bridge.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 9, "Mobilicities II: Freedom". At rush hour Montgomery crosses Queen Louise's Bridge with city staffer Lasse Lindholm among hundreds of stylishly dressed cyclists. Before 2008 the street was clogged with cars, cyclists and bus passengers; traffic director Niels Tørslov's team gave it bus-only lanes, doubled the bike lanes, widened the sidewalks and diverted cars. By 2009 commuter car traffic had halved and thousands of new cyclists had joined.
- **Why it matters:** The street is the book's answer to the question of who has the right to the city's streets: Copenhagen gave the space to the people moving most efficiently, and the street got busier, not emptier.
