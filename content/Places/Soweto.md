# Soweto

Township in the south-west of Johannesburg, built under apartheid to house black workers. In Born a Crime it is Trevor Noah's grandmother's world and the place his mother escapes from.

## Born a Crime — Trevor Noah (2016)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17824318W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6yq3x3vg33fqzxvv4we758k)

> Soweto was designed to be bombed—that’s how forward-thinking the architects of apartheid were.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** in Chapter 2 Trevor describes Soweto as "a city unto itself" of nearly a million people, with only two roads in and out so the military could lock it down. He spends weeks at a time there with his grandmother during holidays, and remembers it as if he had lived there too. Chapter 1's "black church" is his grandmother's Methodist congregation there. Chapter 5 tells how his grandparents were forcibly moved to Soweto's Meadowlands from Sophiatown. It also tells how his mother came back from Transkei to Soweto and then fled again to the city, and how the township's gossip later pushed the family out to Eden Park.
- **Why it matters:** Soweto is the book's picture of apartheid as a designed prison, and of black life making a home inside it. It is the counterweight to the white suburbs and to his mother's escapes.
