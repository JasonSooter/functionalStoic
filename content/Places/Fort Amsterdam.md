# Fort Amsterdam

The Dutch fort at the southern tip of Manhattan, begun in the 1620s, around which New Amsterdam grew. In _Nathaniel's Nutmeg_ it is built as a copy of the Dutch fort on Neira in the Banda Islands.

## Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Giles Milton (1999)

Source: [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Reader's Companion]] · [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1970050W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8abf5mw00x0e6gjdwwqpxp4)

> A carbon copy of the impregnable Fort Belgica on Neira Island in the Bandas, it was shaped like a pentangle and stretched more than a thousand feet in circumference.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Engineer Cryn Fredericks arrives with "special instructions" for the fort's exact dimensions; it is moated, and "Beaver Street, Broad Street, Pearl Street and Whitehall Street in Lower Manhattan all follow engineer Fredericks’s original ground plan, as do Broadway, Park Row and Fourth Avenue." While it is being built Peter Minuit, first governor-general of New Netherland, arrives and buys the island "to the value of sixty guilders in trinkets". Only eight men had been left on Manhattan "to take possession" while the other settlers went to the Delaware, the Connecticut River and [[Hudson River|Fort Orange]].
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's bridge between its two worlds: the Dutch build their American outpost on the pattern of their nutmeg fortress in the [[Banda Islands]].
