# Walikale

Town in North Kivu, eastern DR Congo, near the region's tin and coltan mines. In Kevin Bales's excerpt on slavery in phone supply chains it is an isolated, besieged outpost.

## Your Phone Was Made By Slaves: A Primer on the Secret Economy — Kevin Bales (2016)

Source: [Original](http://blog.longreads.com/2016/03/08/your-phone-was-made-by-slaves-a-primer-on-the-secret-economy/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5j7tbzysdpc9evfv2gq54hd)

> Walikale is like Fort Apache, an isolated outpost surrounded by hostile forces, dense forests, and sheltering a handful of locals who scurried here through the bush to avoid capture after their villages were overrun by rebels.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** Bales reaches the jungle settlement by UN helicopter, hoping to get to a rebel-controlled coltan mine thirty miles away. He and his colleague sleep in a bullet-pocked school and are told by the army commander that a rebel squad waits to ambush them, so they must wait. The town stands for the lawless places where minerals for phones are taken.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book excerpt's central scene, where the supply chain behind cellphones becomes visible on the ground.
