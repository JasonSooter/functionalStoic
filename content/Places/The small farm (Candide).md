# The small farm (Candide)

The small farm on the Sea of Marmara (the Propontis) where Voltaire's company settles at the end of _Candide_, near the exile house of the Transylvanian prince Rákóczi. It is where the book ends, with the company working their garden.

## Candide — Voltaire (1759)
Source: [[Candide]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL100672W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m765nqakrw3kg8tbbv4y7d)

> they arrived at the house of the Transylvanian prince on the banks of the Propontis. The first objects which met their sight were Cunegonde and the old woman hanging towels out to dry.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Candide finds Cunegonde, now ugly, and the old woman hanging towels at the Transylvanian prince's house on the Propontis, ransoms them, and marries Cunegonde. The whole company settles on a small farm nearby, where they are bored and quarrel, watch exiled pashas pass, and are turned away by a dervish. An old Turk who lives on twenty acres with his children says their labour preserves them 'from three great evils—weariness, vice, and want', and Candide ends the book with 'let us cultivate our garden'.
- **Why it matters:** The setting of the book's conclusion and moral. A small farm, far from courts and philosophers, is the answer to the whole journey.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The farm is unnamed. Clues: Cunegonde "washes dishes on the banks of the Propontis" for "an ancient sovereign named Ragotsky", the Transylvanian prince; "There was a small farm in the neighbourhood"; Cacambo "took vegetables for sale to Constantinople". Wikipedia's _Candide_ article identifies the prince as Francis II Rákóczi, and its Rákóczi article says he lived in exile at Tekirdağ (Rodosto) on the Sea of Marmara, where his house is now the Rákóczi Museum. Pinned at Tekirdağ; the main alternative is somewhere closer to Constantinople on the Marmara shore, since the farm sends produce to the city.
