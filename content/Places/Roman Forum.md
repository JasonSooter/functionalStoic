# Roman Forum

Ancient civic square in [[Rome]], Italy, which Seneca, quoted in The Daily Stoic, names as the archetype of a chaotic place where you can still live at peace.

## The Daily Stoic — Ryan Holiday (2016)
Source: [[The Daily Stoic]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20046443W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5edgyrsx1hwwb3hetfwjzcw)

> Is there any place more full of confusion than the Forum? Yet even there you can live at peace, if needed.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Seneca's quoted letter points to the Forum as the archetype of a chaotic place, and says peace and the good life are available anywhere. Holiday's commentary then argues that the place to do your work is 'here', using a writer who traveled to exotic houses without finding inspiration.
- **Why it matters:** The Forum serves as Seneca's example of the worst possible working environment; the argument is that location does not matter. Elsewhere any noisy place would serve, but the Forum is Seneca's own pick.
