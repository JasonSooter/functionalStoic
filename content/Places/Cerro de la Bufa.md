# Cerro de la Bufa

Hill above the city of Zacatecas, site of the 1914 battle. In The Underdogs its slopes are the scene of the novel's military climax.

## The Underdogs: A Novel of the Mexican Revolution — Mariano Azuela (1915)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1209983W)

> Standing on the crest of the hill, they could easily sight one side of the Bufa peak.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Cervantes and Solis shelter on a hill during the battle for Zacatecas. Solis describes how Demetrio charged the trenches and took the machine guns, and they look at the slope below Bufa covered with dead, women stripping corpses, the burning town, and trains of fugitives at the station. Solis speaks the novel's epigraph about the beauty of the revolution.
- **Why it matters:** The capture of Zacatecas is the novel's military climax and the vantage of Solis's famous reflection on the Revolution.
