# Zócalo

Mexico City's main square, the Plaza de la Constitución, in the historic centre. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is the goal of the July 1954 protest march that is Frida's last public appearance.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)

Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m74ggts6vpg72zdw43k17af6)

> she wanted to express her feeling of solidarity with the crowd of more than ten thousand Mexicans who took to the streets, walking from Santo Domingo Plaza to the Zócalo to protest the ouster of Guatemala’s left-leaning President Jacobo Arbenz and the CIA’s imposition on that country of a reactionary regime headed by General Castillo Armas.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** On 2 July 1954, still convalescing from bronchopneumonia, Frida defies her doctors to join the Communist march. Diego pushes her wheelchair through the bumpy streets for four hours, and photographs show her holding a peace-dove banner in one hand, the other clenched in a fist, joining the crowd's cry against the "gringos". Because of the protest her pneumonia hangs on, and she dies eleven days later.
- **Why it matters:** It is her last public act, and the book presents it as a deliberate image of revolutionary fortitude that hastened her death.
