# Karl-Marx-Allee

Monumental socialist boulevard in former East [[Berlin]], lined with uniform apartment blocks. In David Byrne's _Bicycle Diaries_ it is his route out to the Stasi Museum and a lesson in architecture built on a superhuman scale.

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7641s0b4n8x1fe665vraggc)

> I bike out, appropriately enough, along the amazing Karl-Marx-Allee, a sort of Soviet-inspired version of the Champs Élysées or Avenida 9 de Julio in Buenos Aires or maybe New York’s Park Avenue.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** After reading _Stasiland_, Byrne heads for the Stasi Museum, out in the suburbs and hard to find. The boulevard is wider and grander than its Western models, its repeating apartment blocks a vision of an idealistic, utopian infinity; one side has forlorn ground-floor shops, the other cafés.
- **Why it matters:** The ride sets up the Stasi visit and frames East Berlin through its planning, one of the book's main lenses.
