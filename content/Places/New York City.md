## 30 Days, 9 Cities, 1 Question: Where Did American Prosperity Go? — Kyla Scanlon (2025)
Source: [[30 Days, 9 Cities, 1 Question - Where Did American Prosperity Go]] · [Original](https://kyla.substack.com/p/30-days-9-cities-1-question-where) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ce5e0fq0803z77naxg4e2r)

> Maybe that’s because it’s older, or because the architecture forces you to look up. History lives in the buildings here.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** The longest stop of the trip, with a documentary, a commercial, the Aspen Ideas conference and an NPR debate. She contrasts New York's attitude and its visible, maintained infrastructure with what she saw elsewhere.
- **Why it matters:** It is the essay's American counter-example to decline.

## Can Zohran Mamdani Really Freeze the Rent? — Rachel Holliday Smith (2025)
Source: [Original](https://www.thecity.nyc/2025/09/02/rent-freeze-mamdani-affordable-housing-stabilized-apartments/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5d21c0n7vs8cf9r1r2keq3w)

> But does the mayor of New York City actually have the power to stop rent increases in one of the most expensive cities in the world?

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** A THE CITY explainer on the mayoral candidate's promise to freeze rents on the roughly 996,600 rent-stabilized apartments: how the Rent Guidelines Board sets increases, how a mayor shapes it, and the freezes it granted under de Blasio.
- **Why it matters:** The whole city is the subject; no single neighbourhood or building does work in the piece.

## How Zohran Mamdani’s Big Goals Could Transform Life in New York City — RTBC Staff (2025)
Source: [Original](https://reasonstobecheerful.world/zohran-mamdani-goals-learn-more/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dr1q25k6n3eh6gq9t5cqrp)

> New York would become the biggest city in the U.S. — and perhaps the world — to eliminate bus fares entirely.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** After Mamdani won the 2025 Democratic mayoral primary, the piece lists his goals: fare-free buses, city-owned nonprofit supermarkets, free child care for ages six weeks to five, and 200,000 city-built affordable homes in 10 years.
- **Why it matters:** The city is the subject of the proposals; other cities appear as models.

## Where’s the Best Place in North America to Live an Edited Life? — David Friedlander (2015)
Source: [Original](https://lifeedited.com/wheres-the-best-place-in-north-america-to-live-an-edited-life/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5g0eg3y4ztarq523n0phfr4)

> It is one of the most walkable cities in the world. It has a peerless public transit system and an increasingly awesome network of bike lanes.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The author argues New York should be ideal for a small, simple life, being walkable, cultured and well served by transit, but its rents force long working hours that defeat the idea.
- **Why it matters:** The city is the post's test case for where an edited life is possible.

## The Caves of Steel — Isaac Asimov (1954)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL46401W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5hj3cp20f3ngddbecp57500)

> New York City spread over two thousand square miles and at the last census its population was well over twenty million.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The novel is set in a future New York that has become one enclosed City of over twenty million people, with moving strips, underground motorways, communal Section kitchens and Personals, yeast farms across what was New Jersey, and Spacers kept outside in their domes. Detective Elijah Baley and the robot R. Daneel investigate a Spacer's murder through its Sections.
- **Why it matters:** The City is the novel's real subject: the Spacer Fastolfe argues that a City like New York must spend every effort just getting water in and waste out, and its fragility drives the plot. Set anywhere else, the book's argument about crowding and the fear of open space would lose its anchor.
- **Real vs. fictional:** A real city imagined far in the future; the book's specific places are pinned separately as inferred or best-guess sub-places.
