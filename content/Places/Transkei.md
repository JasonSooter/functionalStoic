# Transkei

Former Xhosa homeland (Bantustan) in today's Eastern Cape. In Born a Crime it is where Trevor Noah's mother spends a hungry childhood after being sent away from Soweto.

## At a glance
| Source | Author | Role | In one line |

## Born a Crime — Trevor Noah (2016)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17824318W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6yq47faxhas1mxvpz0j0g1z)

> he packed her off and sent her to live with his sister in the Xhosa homeland, Transkei—he didn’t want her, either.

- **Role:** backdrop (his mother's childhood)
- **In the book:** Chapter 5 ("The Second Girl"). At nine, Trevor's mother is sent by her father to his sister in Transkei and does not see her family again for twelve years. She lives in a hut with fourteen cousins whose parents had gone to the cities, works like "one of the oxen", and at times eats dirt, mixing riverbank clay with water "to make a grayish kind of milk". Her village's mission school still teaches English. At twenty-one she is sent back to Soweto.
- **Why it matters:** her homeland childhood explains her hunger for escape and for English, which shapes how she raises Trevor. It is the book's picture of what the homelands policy did to black families.
- **Real vs. fictional:** real former homeland. The pin is a representative point; the book names no village.
