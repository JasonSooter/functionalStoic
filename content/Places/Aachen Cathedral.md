# Aachen Cathedral

Charlemagne's octagonal palace chapel and its treasury (Schatzkammer), in the centre of [[Aachen]], which is the high point of Bill Bryson's Aachen-and-Cologne chapter in Neither Here Nor There.

## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74125W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5b43hbvh9y3kz302taxrama)

> Next door was the octagonal cathedral, modeled on the church of San Vitale at Ravenna, and all that remains of a palace complex mostly destroyed during the Second World War.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 7 opens with Bryson taking the train from Liège because he "had always wanted to see Aachen Cathedral". In the cathedral close he first visits the Schatzkammer, "the finest assortment of reliquaries I ever expect to see", including the golden bust of Charlemagne and a sixteenth-century triptych of Pope Gregory's Mass. Then he goes into the cathedral itself: small and dark but exquisite, with its domed roof, striped marble and rich stained glass.
- **Why it matters:** It's the high point of the Aachen-and-Cologne chapter and the yardstick for what follows: a day later Cologne's far bigger cathedral, marooned on a concrete plaza, leaves him cold. Set elsewhere, the chapter would lose its small-beats-big contrast.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)
Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m75q4rek9apj3vtzxxf22m)

> Charlemagne was buried here in the year 814, and over the course of 600 years – from 936 to 1531 – thirty Holy Roman Emperors were crowned in this building.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 11, 'The battle for Charlemagne', opens in the octagonal chapel built on Roman and Byzantine models with columns brought from Rome. Charlemagne was buried there in 814 and thirty emperors were crowned there between 936 and 1531. French troops took its columns in 1794 and the city was annexed to France before passing to Prussia in 1815.
- **Why it matters:** It is the prize in the chapter's story of the French-German struggle over Charlemagne's inheritance.
