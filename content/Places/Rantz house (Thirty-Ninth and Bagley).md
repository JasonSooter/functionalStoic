# Rantz house (Thirty-Ninth and Bagley)

The house near the north end of Lake Union in [[Seattle]] where Joe Rantz's father and stepmother lived in the mid-1930s. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is where Joe's stepmother turns him away at the door.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86m5wdwt62tb7japygpdjz1)

> He moved his family to a small but respectable house at Thirty-Ninth and Bagley, not far from the bakery and not far from the north end of Lake Union where Joe rowed nearly every afternoon.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Once Harry Rantz finds work as a mechanic at the Golden Rule bakery, the family moves here. In the fall of 1934 Joe and Joyce knock on its yellow Dutch door, and Thula refuses him: "Make your own life, Joe. Stay out of ours." A year later, after Thula dies on October 25, 1935, Joe goes by the house on Bagley the next morning, follows a brick path around the back and finds his father and the four children at a picnic table on a soggy lawn, Harry mixing cherry Kool-Aid. They talk about Thula's life.
- **Why it matters:** The closed door confirms Joe's abandonment by his family; the morning on the back lawn after Thula's death begins the slow reopening, the wound the book lets the crew help heal.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real; the book gives only the intersection, so the pin is approximate.
