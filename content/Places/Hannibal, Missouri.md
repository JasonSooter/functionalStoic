# Hannibal, Missouri

Mississippi river town in northeast Missouri, Mark Twain's boyhood home. It is the documented model for St. Petersburg in The Adventures of Tom Sawyer.

## The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, Complete — Mark Twain (1876)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL53919W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k7habzf7ffe49ryj5y0ds1)

> A new-comer of any age or either sex was an impressive curiosity in the poor little shabby village of St. Petersburg.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** St. Petersburg is the village of the whole book. Tom fights the well-dressed new boy in its street, and its church, school-house, court-house, jail, tavern and graveyard host the main scenes: the graveyard murder, Muff Potter's trial, the search for the lost children. The town lies on the Missouri shore of the Mississippi, with a steam ferry and St. Louis downriver.
- **Why it matters:** It is the enclosed social world whose rules Tom flouts and whose gossip and attention he craves; every scene here takes its character from it.
- **Real vs. fictional:** St. Petersburg is fictional. Reference works identify Twain's boyhood town, Hannibal, Missouri, as its model; the text names no real town but puts it on the Missouri shore of the Mississippi with St. Louis downriver.
