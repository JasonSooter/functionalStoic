# Commercial Drive

Main street of East [[Vancouver]]'s Grandview-Woodland neighbourhood, laid out along an old streetcar line. In _Happy City_ it is the market street at the heart of Montgomery's own neighbourhood, his model of a happy middle density.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83qjbxsy2n9zsjt33srzny9)

> Five minutes away, down the hill, there is Commercial Drive, a market street of remarkable plenitude.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Montgomery describes his East Vancouver house on a 25-by-100-foot lot with small front yards, a boccie park four minutes away and Commercial Drive five minutes down the hill, with its shops, community centre and buses every six minutes. He argues that this streetcar-era geometry of small lots and a market street within a five-minute walk balances privacy, sociability and nature, and notes the area absorbed much of the city's growth between 1991 and 2006.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's "sweet spot": proof that moderate, well-connected density can be happy without towers.
