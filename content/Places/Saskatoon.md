# Saskatoon

City on the South Saskatchewan River in [[Saskatchewan]], Canada, frozen for almost half the year. In _Happy City_ it is where Robert Judge cycles through the winter and finds it transcendent.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m841xtny5y7ens78b8yp1prg)

> Sometimes, he said, he would pick up his three-year-old son from day care and put him on the backseat of his tandem bike and they would pedal home along the South Saskatchewan River.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Robert Judge lives in Saskatoon, where January averages around 1°F, and rides everywhere, proudest of his trips on studded tires to Superstore, a big-box grocery about three and a half miles from his inner-suburb home. He explains the pleasure with a story: riding home along the river with his son on the tandem, snow muffling the city and reflecting the dusk, until they feel part of winter itself.
- **Why it matters:** The harsh climate is the point: if self-propelled travel can feel this good here, the book argues, its satisfactions are not about comfort.
