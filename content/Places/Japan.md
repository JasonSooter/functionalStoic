# Japan

Japan is an island country in East Asia. In Melville's _Moby-Dick_ it is the nearest named land to the Japanese whaling ground where the Pequod's hunt reaches its climax.

## Moby-Dick — Herman Melville (1851)

Source: [[Exploration of Moby Dick]] · [[Moby Dick]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m75311049sk9mvbwmqtqdj)

> Penetrating further and further into the heart of the Japanese cruising ground, the Pequod was soon all astir in the fishery.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Chapters 113 to 125 take place on the whaling ground off Japan. The Pequod hunts with small success, meets the homeward-bound Bachelor full of oil, and Ahab smashes his quadrant under 'That unblinkingly vivid Japanese sun'. A typhoon brings the corpusants burning on the masts and Ahab's defiance of the fire, Starbuck stands at Ahab's door with a musket, and the lightning reverses the compasses. Starbuck reckons 'locked Japan' the nearest land, hundreds of leagues off.
- **Why it matters:** A dense run of turning-point chapters happens here, where Ahab's defiance and Starbuck's near-mutiny peak. The sea is off Japan rather than in it; Japan is the nearest named land.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The setting is the Japanese whaling ground at sea, which the text places hundreds of leagues from 'locked Japan'; it is filed under the existing Japan note because no reference gives the ground a position.
