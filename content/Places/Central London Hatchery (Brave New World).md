## Brave New World — Aldous Huxley (1932)
Source: [[Brave New World]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL64365W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kvabw30nspmgdfm2f53z8s)

> A squat grey building of only thirty-four stories. Over the main entrance the words, CENTRAL LONDON HATCHERY AND CONDITIONING CENTRE, and, in a shield, the World State’s motto, COMMUNITY, IDENTITY, STABILITY.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The opening chapters follow the Director and a group of new students through the Fertilizing Room, Bottling Room, Social Predestination Room, Embryo Store, the Infant Nurseries and the hypnopaedia dormitories, then out to the garden, where Mustapha Mond lectures on stability. The changing-room and lift scenes that introduce Lenina and Bernard are set here too, and later the Director tries to humiliate Bernard in the Fertilizing Room, only to be undone by Linda and John.
- **Why it matters:** The building is the World State's manufactured society made concrete: Bokanovsky's Process, caste predestination and conditioning are all shown here first. The book's satire depends on this factory of people.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The Centre is fictional; the novel twice calls it the Bloomsbury Centre.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text names the institution "CENTRAL LONDON HATCHERY AND CONDITIONING CENTRE" and the Fertilizing Room scene calls it "the Bloomsbury Centre", but gives no street. Pinned at the Bloomsbury district point. No reference work found that names a specific site.
