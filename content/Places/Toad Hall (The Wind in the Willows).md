# Toad Hall

Toad's riverside mansion in _The Wind in the Willows_, pinned at a best guess on Mapledurham House by the Thames. It is the home Toad squanders and the stoats and weasels seize, and the four friends retake in the book's last battle.

## The Wind in the Willows — Kenneth Grahame (1908)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL28570037W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne2gjxvn84zf4w6b5sd9ha)

> There’s Toad Hall,” said the Rat; “and that creek on the left, where the notice-board says, ‘Private. No landing allowed,’ leads to his boat-house, where we’ll leave the boat.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In Chapter II Rat rows Mole up to Toad's riverside mansion, with its boat-house, stables and old banqueting hall, and Toad talks them into the caravan trip. In Chapter VI Badger, Rat and Mole lock Toad in his bedroom to cure his motor-car mania, and he escapes by knotted sheets. At the end the stoats and weasels hold the Hall; the four friends get in by a secret passage from the river-bank to the butler's pantry, rout them at their banquet and hold their own feast there.
- **Why it matters:** Toad Hall anchors the comic plot: it is what Toad gambles away and what the final battle wins back.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Toad Hall is fictional.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text places it on the river's bank, with a boat-house, a lawn to the water and a secret passage from the riverbank. Wikipedia's Toad Hall article lists Fawley Court, Fowey Hall, Foxwarren Park, Hardwick House and Mapledurham House as proposed models; Countryfile adds Harleyford Manor and Cliveden. None is confirmed. Pinned at Mapledurham House; Hardwick House and Fawley Court are the closest alternatives.
