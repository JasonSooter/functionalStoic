# Vega (Norway)

Largest island of the [[Vega Archipelago]] off Helgeland, Norway, with a small town, church and harbour. In James Rebanks's memoir it is the last outpost of "civilization", the harbour from which boats leave for the outer islands, and the home Anna returns to at the end of the season.

## The Place of Tides — James Rebanks (2024)

Source: [[The Place of Tides]] · [Original](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/315745/the-place-of-tides-by-rebanks-james/9780241426937) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7h2cszt94d8564x9s4h1yt9)

> Vega was still civilization. Further out were places with no electric or shops or other amenities.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** Vega has a tiny town, a large wooden church with a plaque to those lost at sea, a fishing village and a harbour. The night before the voyage Rebanks sits in Anna's living room there while she tells him stories. Next morning the party loads her boxes through her family's sea barn, signed "MÅSØY BRYGGE", onto Henrik's boat; Rebanks finds the harbour quiet, some sea barns turned into holiday lets, like "a retirement village, or a museum". In Anna's family history Vega is where the outer-islanders were looked down on: in church "Anna's family and the other outer-islanders had to sit at the back and come in through a different door". Her parents settle inland on her mother's family land, Steinbakken, where Anna is born; she later cooks for thirty years in the nursing home, and a new museum on Vega about the duck stations, with its stuffed eider in a glass case, spurs her to revive [[Måsøyvær (The Place of Tides)|Måsøyvær]]. At the museum's World Heritage event the Queen of Norway leaves the agenda to talk with Anna and the duck women at the back of the room. At the end of the season "Vega glowed a luminous green" as they sail back in; walking up from the sea barns they meet a wedding procession (the bride is the daughter of Rita from the museum) and pass the old garage, once a café and shop, where Anna first heard of the Fjærøyvær job. The last chapters play out at Anna's white clapboard house at Steinbakken, below a spruce plantation next to her brother Tore's house: Rebanks mows the lawn, they drive through [[Gladstad]] to Henning's farm on the flatter part of the island to return his rifle, and the family gathers for a farewell meal. The book ends at her kitchen window.
- **Why it matters:** It is the threshold between the modern world and the outer islands, the place against which the remoteness of the duck island is measured, the home Anna's family was pushed into when the outer islands emptied, and where she comes "full circle" at the end.
