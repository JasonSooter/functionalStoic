# University of Tübingen

University in Tübingen, south-west [[Germany]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where Friedrich Miescher first finds DNA, in 1869.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83316pezrav36x94bmntjn3)

> It was discovered as far back as 1869 by Johann Friedrich Miescher, a Swiss scientist working at the University of Tübingen in Germany.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Johann Friedrich Miescher, a Swiss scientist working at Tübingen, found DNA in 1869 while examining the pus in surgical bandages under the microscope; he called it nuclein because it lived in cell nuclei. He later suggested in a letter that such molecules might carry heredity, but the idea attracted no attention.
- **Why it matters:** The starting point of the DNA story, and another of Bryson's insights that arrived long before anyone was ready for them.
