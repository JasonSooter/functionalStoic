# 2152 Leavenworth Street

House on Russian Hill in [[San Francisco]]. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is the home of Dr. Leo Eloesser, where Frida painted his portrait in 1931.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)
Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m72xaz1pz2kkp85t2sv59x0d)

> Frida Kahlo de Rivera painted it at my home at 2152 Leavenworth St., during the Riveras’ first visit to San Francisco.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In San Francisco Frida befriends Eloesser, a thoracic and bone surgeon who in December 1930 diagnoses her scoliosis and a missing vertebral disk, and whose medical advice she trusts above all others for the rest of her life. His weekly chamber-music evenings at his Leavenworth Street apartment draw musicians such as Isaac Stern and Pierre Monteux. In a 1968 note, quoted here, Eloesser says she painted _Portrait of Dr. Leo Eloesser_ at this address.
- **Why it matters:** The friendship begun here becomes one of the most important in Frida's life, and the portrait, an early work in a naïve provincial style, marks her move away from Rivera's influence.
