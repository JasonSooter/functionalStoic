# 42 Rue Fontaine

André and Jacqueline Breton's apartment in the 9th arrondissement of [[Paris]]. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is where Frida stays on arriving in Paris in January 1939, an unhappy start to the trip.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)
Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m73hnj77xctgbp4gzkpsmks6)

> Frida stayed first with André and Jacqueline Breton in their small apartment at 42 Rue Fontaine (the intersection of Surrealist and Trotskyite circles in Paris), but it was not a happy visit.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In Chapter 15 Frida arrives to find the promised exhibition unorganized: her paintings are stuck in customs and Breton has no gallery. She shares a cramped room with the Bretons' daughter Aube and blames the dirt and food there for the infection that follows, moving out at the end of January to the Hotel Regina.
- **Why it matters:** It begins her disillusionment with Breton and the Surrealists, which colors the whole Paris chapter.
