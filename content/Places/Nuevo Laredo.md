# Nuevo Laredo

Border city in Tamaulipas, [[Mexico]], across the Rio Grande from Laredo, Texas. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is where Frida crosses home over a flooded river on her rush to her dying mother in 1932.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)
Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m737hx9chd956afegc3350w9)

> Finally, they took a bus across the least damaged bridge to Nuevo Laredo, which was brimming with excitement—vendors hawking food, families saying passionate goodbyes, people standing around just enjoying the spectacle.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** At the end of Chapter 10, after a telegram about her mother's illness, Frida and Lucienne Bloch travel south by train. The Rio Grande is in flood, the track at Laredo, Texas is under water and they wait twelve hours at the border before crossing by bus; in Nuevo Laredo Frida buys cajeta, scooping it up with her fingers as she had as a girl.
- **Why it matters:** The crossing is Frida's first return to Mexico in years, a moment of homecoming on a desperate journey to her mother's deathbed.
