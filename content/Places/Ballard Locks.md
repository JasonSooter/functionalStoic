# Ballard Locks

Locks between Puget Sound and Seattle's Salmon Bay, in Ballard. In _The Boys in the Boat_ they are where Depression-era log rafts are lifted toward Lake Washington, and the hardest stage of twelve-year-old Roger Morris's fifteen-mile row home.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m869xk07rkypbh8s00ntyk60)

> then east through the Ballard Locks, where he wedged his small rowboat in among salmon trawlers and tugboats and rafts of logs

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** The opening panorama of Seattle in 1933 shows tugboats nosing log rafts into the locks in the old Scandinavian neighbourhood of Ballard. Later the book tells how Roger Morris, at twelve and with a toothache, rowed some fifteen miles from his family's cabin on [[Manzanita Bay]], through Agate Passage and across Puget Sound, then through the locks and Salmon Bay to the family home in Fremont.
- **Why it matters:** The row shows the strength and self-reliance that made Roger the only freshman who already knew how to row. The locks are a vivid stage of the trip, not a setting the book returns to.
