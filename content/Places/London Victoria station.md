# London Victoria station

London Victoria is a major railway terminus in Westminster, opened in 1860, serving lines to Brighton and the south coast. In _The Importance of Being Earnest_ its cloak-room is where the infant Jack was found in a hand-bag.

## The Importance of Being Earnest — Oscar Wilde (1895)
Source: [[The Importance of Being Earnest]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL27776452W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76b25723k3azy53er1287)

> In the cloak-room at Victoria Station. It was given to him in mistake for his own.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Jack tells Lady Bracknell that Thomas Cardew found him in a hand-bag in the cloak-room at Victoria Station, 'The Brighton line'; she replies 'The line is immaterial' and refuses to let Gwendolen 'marry into a cloak-room'. In Act Three Miss Prism confesses she left the hand-bag with the baby in the cloak-room at Victoria, the Brighton line, after putting her manuscript in the perambulator. Jack produces the bag and his birth is revealed.
- **Why it matters:** The cloak-room is the origin of Jack's foundling story and the hinge between Act One's refusal and Act Three's resolution. Lady Bracknell's line about persons 'whose origin was a Terminus' makes the station itself the joke.
