# Palm Springs

Desert resort city in California's Coachella Valley. In "My Right to Die" the family drives there to say goodbye to a dying father; in _The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind_ its wind farm is where William Kamkwamba's story comes full circle.

## My Right to Die — Kevin Drum (2016)
Source: [Original](https://medium.com/mother-jones/my-right-to-die-634643b0b104) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jy9cpy6zxrz07aws2krjya)

> A friend persuaded him to call both of them, and on January 30 we all drove out to Palm Springs to say our last goodbyes.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** The family drives to Palm Springs on January 30 to say goodbye to Harry, the author's father-in-law. After writing a note that no one assisted him, he goes into his bedroom, puts a bag over his head and opens a helium tank.
- **Why it matters:** It is the place of the personal story that frames the essay, and where the consequences of California's then-ban on assisted suicide play out.

## The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind — William Kamkwamba and Bryan Mealer (2009)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/314792/the-boy-who-harnessed-the-wind-by-william-kamkwamba-and-bryan-mealer/9780593866894) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8ebdnm41xh5nt44zq8vkqnc)

> Except I wasn’t in Malawi—I was in Palm Springs, California. And in that empty space between me and the hills, there suddenly appeared miles of windmills, more than six thousand of them, shooting from the earth like a giant forest of mechanical trees.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** On his first trip to America, after the electronics show in Las Vegas, William is taken to the wind farm outside Palm Springs. Each turbine towers "more than two hundred feet in the air", and the Windtec farm's head engineer, Chris Copeland, takes him inside one. The farm makes six hundred megawatts, "enough to power the entire country of Malawi" (ESCOM produces only 224). "I’d come full circle," he writes, and the narrative ends here: "If you want to make it, all you have to do is try."
- **Why it matters:** The turbines he first saw in a library book in [[Wimbe]] stand in front of him for real. Set anywhere else, the ending would lose its point: this is where his scrapyard windmill meets the industry it imitated.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real. The book names only "Palm Springs" and "the Windtec wind farm"; the turbines there belong to the San Gorgonio Pass wind farm, so the pin is on the town.
