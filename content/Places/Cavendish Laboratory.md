# Cavendish Laboratory

The University of Cambridge's physics laboratory, England. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where the electron, the particle detector and the neutron are found.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80txm1vmfz0vjw0b6kxw1xj)

> Back in the Cavendish Lab in Cambridge he built an artificial cloud chamber

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Rutherford fetches up here in 1895, when the Cavendish is about to become the hottest place in physics; the book lists the electron, the first particle detector, the neutron and later the structure of DNA among its discoveries. He leaves after three years for McGill and later returns as its head. In the atom-smashing chapter, C. T. R. Wilson builds an artificial cloud chamber here after his climbs of Ben Nevis, and so invents the particle detector.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's recurring home of subatomic physics, the place where the atom's parts are first seen.
