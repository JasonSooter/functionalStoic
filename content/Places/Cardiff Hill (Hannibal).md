# Cardiff Hill (Hannibal)

Hill above the river in Hannibal, Missouri. In Tom Sawyer it is the boys' treasure-hunting ground and the scene of Huck's night watch.

## The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, Complete — Mark Twain (1876)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL53919W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k7hd67kfk9s7pnsq5mcttf)

> They moved up the river street three blocks, then turned to the left up a crossstreet. They went straight ahead, then, until they came to the path that led up Cardiff Hill; this they took.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Tom and Huck dig for treasure at a dead-limb tree on the hill. Later Huck shadows Injun Joe and his partner up the path past the Welshman's house to the Widow Douglas's stile, and raises the alarm that saves her; the Widow's house on the hill hosts the closing party.
- **Why it matters:** The hill is the physical stage for the treasure hunt and for Huck's heroic night, linking the town, the haunted house and the widow's house.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Cardiff Hill is the book's name for the hill above the village; a hill of that name rises above Hannibal today (pinned from OpenStreetMap).
