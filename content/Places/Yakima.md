# Yakima

City in central [[Washington (state)|Washington]], USA. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is the town paralysed by ash from Mount St Helens, a small-scale preview of a Yellowstone eruption.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Ninety minutes after the blast, ash began to rain down on Yakima, Washington, a community of fifty thousand people about eighty miles away.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Ninety minutes after the [[Mount St Helens]] blast, ash begins falling on Yakima, about eighty miles away. Despite warnings the city has no volcano emergency plan and the emergency broadcast system stays off the air; five-eighths of an inch of ash turns day to night and paralyses it for three days.
- **Why it matters:** Bryson uses it as a measuring stick: if a thin ash fall can stop a city, the ash from a Yellowstone eruption would be catastrophic.
