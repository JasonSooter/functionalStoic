# Elkhorn Slough

Estuary and wildlife reserve at Moss Landing, Monterey County, [[California]]; in Jenny Odell's How to Do Nothing it is where the sight of the slough's birds brings the emotional climax of the final chapter.

## How to Do Nothing — Jenny Odell (2019)

Source: [[How to Do Nothing — Reader's Companion]] · [[How to Do Nothing]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20078135W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5edhpkq7m3re359qf4d3qtz)

> All of a sudden, as I rounded a turn, part of the slough came into view.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Odell drives from her cabin to the reserve after hearing a radio report of cyclones elsewhere. Rounding a turn she sees the slough and its huge flocks of birds, and starts crying. She realizes that her wish to preserve the place is also self-preservation, that her fate is linked to the places and beings around her.
- **Why it matters:** It is the emotional climax of the final chapter: the epiphany that attention to place becomes love and responsibility, and the reason she looks at her phone less. A different habitat would change the imagery but the slough's 'lack of progress' meaning and its biological productivity are part of the argument.
