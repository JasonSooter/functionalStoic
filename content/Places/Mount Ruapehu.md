# Mount Ruapehu

Mount Ruapehu is an active volcano and the highest peak of New Zealand's North Island, in Tongariro National Park. It is pinned for _The Hobbit_ because it is reported as the stand-in for the Lonely Mountain in Peter Jackson's films; it is not a place in the book.

## The Hobbit (Middle-Earth Universe) — J. R. R. Tolkien (1937)
Source: [[The Hobbit]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL27482W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m77e3e61qfzjf0fpvbmqe4)

> The Mountain lay dark and silent before them and ever higher above them.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In the book, the Lonely Mountain is the dwarves' lost kingdom and Smaug's lair. The company crosses the Desolation of the Dragon, camps by the spur called Ravenhill, finds the secret door, and Bilbo goes down alone to face the dragon; after Smaug's death the Battle of Five Armies is fought beneath it.
- **Why it matters:** The Mountain is the goal of the whole quest and the scene of its climax.
- **Real vs. fictional:** This is where Peter Jackson's Hobbit films were shot; it is not a place in the book.
- **Where is it?:** Wikipedia (Lonely Mountain) says Mount Ruapehu stood in for the Lonely Mountain in Jackson's films (citing The Telegraph, 2017). Sources differ: the film series article's location table lists Ruapehu's Turoa ski area for the Long Valley and the Wanaka–Mt Aspiring Road for the Lonely Mountain.
