## The Towns That Invent Their Own Money — Kaja Šeruga (2025)
Source: [Original](https://reasonstobecheerful.world/towns-invent-community-currencies/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ebpsc9a9wtygn7nakk2nse)

> In 1932 the small Austrian town of Wörgl was buckling under the weight of the Great Depression, with crumbling infrastructure, rising unemployment and ballooning debt.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** In 1932 Wörgl, hit by the Depression, issued its own depreciating 'labor certificates' usable for local purchases and taxes. Unemployment fell, tax revenue rose and the town funded road repair, a new bridge and a ski jump. After mayor Michael Unterguggenberger presented the scheme to 170 mayors in 1933 the central bank outlawed it.
- **Why it matters:** It is the opening historical case that frames the whole piece on community currencies; the article's argument rests on this example, though the piece then moves on to other places.
