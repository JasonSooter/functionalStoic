# Tring

Market town in [[Hertfordshire]], England. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is the home of Walter Rothschild, whose collectors sent millions of specimens back to his estate there.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83dbjr6sr13nvme78b21sdd)

> He lived his entire life in the nursery wing of his home at Tring, in Buckinghamshire, using the furniture of his childhood

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Lionel Walter Rothschild kept as many as four hundred collectors in the field at once. Their finds, particularly things that flew, were crated and sent back to his estate at Tring, where he and a battalion of assistants logged and analysed them, processing well over two million specimens and adding five thousand species to science.
- **Why it matters:** Tring is the base of Bryson's portrait of the collector as both student and destroyer of life: the people most interested in living things were often the ones who wiped them out.
- **Real vs. fictional:** ⚠️ Bryson places Tring in Buckinghamshire; the town is in Hertfordshire, close to the county border.
