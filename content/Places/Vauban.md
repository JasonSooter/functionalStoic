# Vauban

## Germany's "Green" City (with more bikes than cars!) — Not Just Bikes
Source: [Original](https://youtube.com/watch?v=6Vil5KC7Bl0&si=P6MFMbZXNvggqxGo) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5cre9r0k4tzewtgem0kjz93)

> One great example of this is Vauban, a suburb completed in the early 2000s, that was designed to be environmentally friendly from the start.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the video:** A long segment on Vauban: a former military base turned into co-operative housing, with filtered streets, play streets, very low car ownership and a tram line.
- **Why it matters:** The video's showcase for designing a sustainable suburb from the start.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m841xjht56s6a3gcveqjcsvp)

> Vauban, an experimental green neighborhood of about five thousand people, is built on a former military base, a 10-minute streetcar ride from the medieval heart of Freiburg.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** At dawn on a September morning the author joins five-year-old Leonard and his mother on his first bike ride to school, through a "rush hour" made up almost entirely of people walking. Cars crawl at five miles per hour, long-term street parking is banned, and car owners must buy a space in one of two garages at the edge of the village (Leonard's parents paid 20,000 euros), while car-free households buy a share of a leafy lot for about 3,700 euros instead.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's clearest case that moving parking away from homes fills streets with life: the farther away the parking, the livelier and safer the street.
