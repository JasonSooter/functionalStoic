# High Line

Elevated linear park built on a disused freight rail line on [[Manhattan]]'s West Side. In _Happy City_ it shows how dead infrastructure can become intimate urban nature.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83qjfs80rrz6x9wtvbn0px9)

> The High Line, the decommissioned elevated rail line converted into a nineteen-block linear park on Manhattan’s West Side, is most famous for the bird’s-eye glimpses it offers into offices, private living rooms, and down to the street from viewing platforms that turn evening traffic into rivers of light.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Beyond its famous views into offices and down onto the traffic, Montgomery dwells on the park's hundreds of plant species and on a day when he and strangers took off their shoes and splashed together in a shallow pond.
- **Why it matters:** It is a retrofit example: unused transport infrastructure turned into nature that invites contact, not just looking. See also [[Lincoln Center for the Performing Arts]] and [[Cheonggyecheon]].
