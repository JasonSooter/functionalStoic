# Poughkeepsie Bridge

The 1889 railroad bridge over the Hudson River at Poughkeepsie, New York, now the Walkway over the Hudson. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it marks the course of the Poughkeepsie Regatta, where Washington's freshmen become national champions in 1934.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86m5pam33dtm0vta02hmsnh)

> the Washington crews moved their shells into a dilapidated old boathouse on the western, Highland, side of the Hudson River, across from Poughkeepsie.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In June 1934 the Washington crews lodge at Florence Palmer's boardinghouse on the Highland bluff and keep their shells in a drafty boathouse on stilts, showering in foul river water. On race day the starting line lies a mile upriver of the "soaring, spidery" railroad bridge, and Joe's freshmen finish five lengths ahead of Syracuse to become national champions; the varsity loses to California. In June 1935 they are back in the same leaky boathouse, while Cal enjoys a new one on the Poughkeepsie side. Ulbrickson has demoted Joe's sophomores to the JV, and they win their race; but in the varsity race on a river of rollers, Cal beats Cornell by a third of a second and Washington finishes third. Bombs set off on the automobile bridge first signal a win for Cornell. In June 1936 the crew moves into Cornell's former house on the east bank, next door to California, where a cookhouse on the beach feeds them and the two crews pass each other in silence on the shared float. On race night, June 22, Washington, rowing far behind by design, is still nearly three lengths back as it passes under the black skeleton of the railroad bridge with a mile to go; Moch has steered along the lane line to dodge the swirl behind a bridge abutment; now he calls for ten hard strokes for Ulbrickson, ten for Pocock, and the boat sweeps past Columbia, Navy and California in the last half mile to win while Ulbrickson, on the observation train, has all but given up. On June 22, 1937, the same crew wins again by four lengths in a course record, the last race for Morris, Hunt and Joe.
- **Why it matters:** Poughkeepsie is the national stage each season aims for. The 1934 freshman win is the first proof that Joe's crew is exceptional; the 1935 varsity defeat puts Ulbrickson's job in doubt and keeps the Cal rivalry open on the road to Berlin. The 1936 win is the crew's first national title as the varsity and the proof that Moch's patience works, and 1937 closes their racing careers where they began.
