# Poughkeepsie Bridge

The 1889 railroad bridge over the Hudson River at Poughkeepsie, New York, now the Walkway over the Hudson. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it marks the course of the Poughkeepsie Regatta, where Washington's freshmen become national champions in 1934.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86m5pam33dtm0vta02hmsnh)

> the Washington crews moved their shells into a dilapidated old boathouse on the western, Highland, side of the Hudson River, across from Poughkeepsie.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In June 1934 the Washington crews lodge at Florence Palmer's boardinghouse on the Highland bluff and keep their shells in a drafty boathouse on stilts, showering in foul river water. At first the freshmen row badly on the Hudson's tide and current. On race day the starting line lies a mile upriver of the "soaring, spidery" railroad bridge, the river between the bridges is jammed with yachts, and Joe's freshmen finish five lengths ahead of Syracuse to become national champions. The varsity then loses to California by three-quarters of a length.
- **Why it matters:** Poughkeepsie is the national stage the whole season aims for; the freshman win is the first proof that Joe's crew is exceptional, and the varsity loss keeps the Cal rivalry alive.
