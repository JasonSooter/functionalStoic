# Flagstaff

City on the Colorado Plateau in [[Arizona]], home of Lowell Observatory. Its observatories are where tree-ring dating began in Ross Andersen's essay and where Pluto and its moon Charon were found in Bill Bryson's history of science.

## The vanishing groves — Ross Andersen (2013)
Source: [Original](https://aeon.co/essays/trees-of-deep-time-are-a-portal-to-the-past-and-the-future) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dqtaw466w0p0bx3ejfytnz)

> By 1901 AE Douglass was tiring of Lowell’s eccentricities, especially his obsession with Martian canals — an obsession that eventually led to a falling out between the two men. Douglass was also keen to test his theory of sunspots and trees, and so he began studying tree rings in old stands of ponderosa pine near his observatory in Flagstaff, Arizona.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** Around 1901 A. E. Douglass studied ponderosa pines near his observatory in Flagstaff. In 1904 he found thin growth in the years 1899 and 1902, the "Flagstaff Signature" that founded dendrochronology.
- **Why it matters:** Flagstaff is the birthplace of tree-ring dating in the essay, the first link between sunspots and tree rings.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8066s8p32t206dt9jtg65qv)

> it happened in Flagstaff, for it was there in 1930 that Pluto had been found in the first place.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In 1978 James Christy at the U.S. Naval Observatory in Flagstaff spots a blur beside Pluto in routine photographs: its moon, Charon. Bryson notes the "nice touch" that Pluto itself was found in Flagstaff, then tells the story of Percival Lowell, who endowed the observatory that bears his name, believed in Martian canals and hunted a "Planet X" beyond Neptune; in 1929 the Lowell Observatory directors hire Clyde Tombaugh, a young man from Kansas, who finds Pluto after a year of searching.
- **Why it matters:** Flagstaff is where the solar system's outer edge was mapped, twice, partly by luck. Bryson uses it to show how little was known of the outer solar system until very recently.
