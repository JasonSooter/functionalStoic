# Times Square

Square at Broadway and Seventh Avenue in Midtown Manhattan, [[New York City]], USA. In Another Brooklyn it is the place the girls dread most, where girls like them disappear.

## Another Brooklyn — Jacqueline Woodson (2016)

Source: [[Another Brooklyn]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dcn4vjgpakn91sce1kfmnh)

> But in Times Square that same year, brown girls were dying.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** The girls dread Times Square and the porn theatres of Eighth Avenue and Forty-Second Street, where girls like them are drawn in by pimps and drugs and found dead; the fear terrifies them even more than losing Angela.
- **Why it matters:** A place they never visit in the book but that shapes their dread.

## Walkable City — Jeff Speck (2012)

Source: [[Walkable City]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL36883695M/Walkable_City) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbjygewqcttnpcfpjk0mgn)

> Gehl has recently been consulting to the City of New York, which turned a stretch of Broadway in Times Square into a pedestrian park with great results.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Speck uses Times Square's Broadway pedestrian conversion, done with temporary bollards, potted trees and movable chairs, as the successful model of cheap, reversible pedestrianization in a very dense place. He contrasts it with failed 1960s-70s American pedestrian malls.
- **Why it matters:** It is the counterexample to failed Main Street closures; success depends on New York's density, not on expensive streetscape.

## Uncommon Type — Tom Hanks (2017)

Source: [[Uncommon Type]] · [Original](https://penguin.co.uk/books/1114786/uncommon-type/9781786091338.html) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k5vbv9rmdbdzg50jx9dkvr)

> Times Square was an exterior version of the station below—filthy, flooded, and overcharged with people.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** Two stories pass through it. In 'Who's Who?' (1978) Sue Gliebe comes up from a delayed subway into a rain-soaked Times Square, learns to keep moving past the drugs and porn of Forty-Second Street and heads east to the library. In 'Go See Costas' the newly arrived Assan eats a hot dog in a huge cafeteria where 'nine streets seemed to collide' and falls asleep in a cinema.
- **Why it matters:** The 1978 Times Square shows the city's hard side and leads to her breakdown.

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m772xbdjfze6vh75e6gq5y20)

> After the sun goes down I ride my bike through Times Square, which is dark except for police vehicles.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** During the blackout the great signs are shut down and become "just abstract shapes." The square is strangely crowded: tourists who don't know what to do, thousands of people hanging out or unable to get home, and an Irish bar on West Forty-fifth Street whose drinkers fill the whole street.
- **Why it matters:** The dark square is the centerpiece of Byrne's account of the blackout, which he uses to show the city's calm and cooperation in a crisis.
