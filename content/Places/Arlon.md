# Arlon

Town in the Luxembourg province of [[Belgium]], where Bill Bryson spends his first night in Europe, in 1972, in Neither Here Nor There.

## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74125W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5a7pz5vpr57409nxbqm933f)

> It took half an hour to reach Arlon, a dreary industrial town. Everything about it looked gray and dusty, even the people.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Still in the 1972 flashback. A travelling shoe salesman in a Citroën 2CV picks the hitchhiking Bryson up outside Luxembourg, drives like "an arcade game," and insists on taking him home to his two-room flat in Arlon: warm lager, one fried-egg plate each, both sides of a Gene Pitney album, and a wife glaring at all of it. He then drops Bryson at a decayed hotel in the centre, where Bryson sleeps eleven hours.
- **Why it matters:** It's the book's first full comic set piece and Bryson's first night on the continent. The point is the strangeness of being a stranger, not Arlon itself; the "dreary industrial town" works as a deadpan foil to his wide-eyed day in Luxembourg.
