# Massachusetts Institute of Technology

University in Cambridge, Massachusetts. In an Aeon essay on cosmology it is where Alan Guth vets the BICEP2 result in secret.

## Has cosmology run into a creative crisis? — Ross Andersen (2015)
Source: [Original](http://aeon.co/magazine/science/has-cosmology-run-into-a-creative-crisis) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5g0e68syj3yrt5tn5qv89xs)

> Guth told Kovac to take the back steps up to his office, to avoid being seen.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** Before the BICEP2 announcement, John Kovac takes a cab from Harvard to MIT and climbs the back stairs to Alan Guth's office; Guth questions him for over an hour and finds no fault in the data.
- **Why it matters:** The quiet check by inflation's founder is the essay's turning point before the result goes public.

## "Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!": Adventures of a Curious Character — Richard P. Feynman (1985)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL514629W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kfpwqn3bb4ztr61r9x7dbz)

> I often liked to play tricks on people when I was at MIT.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Feynman studies at MIT for four years after leaving Far Rockaway at seventeen. He pledges the Phi Beta Delta fraternity, whose members teach him to be sociable, survives its hazing, and plays pranks there: in "Who Stole the Door?" he takes a door off its hinges and hides it in the basement behind the oil tank, then confesses when the president polls the table and nobody believes him.
- **Why it matters:** MIT is the backdrop of his student years and the fraternity stories; he later contrasts its polished cyclotron with Princeton's messy, hands-on one.

## Co-Intelligence — Ethan Mollick (2024)
Source: [[Co-Intelligence]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL37565105W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76zqxhdzxyxqwbvvrq3dd)

> One of the earliest and most influential examples was ELIZA, a computer program created by Joseph Weizenbaum at MIT in 1966.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Joseph Weizenbaum creates ELIZA, the simulated psychotherapist that fooled users with simple pattern matching, at MIT in 1966. Later, MIT economists Shakked Noy and Whitney Zhang find that professionals using ChatGPT finish writing tasks 37 percent faster and better, though most do not edit the AI's output. Mollick also mentions working with the MIT Media Lab's AI group.
- **Why it matters:** A recurring source rather than a setting: it anchors both the history of chatbots and the book's evidence on AI and productivity.
