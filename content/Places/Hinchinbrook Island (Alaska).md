# Hinchinbrook Island (Alaska)

Large island guarding the entrance to [[Prince William Sound]], Alaska. In _Gentle Ben_ it is the brown-bear island where Ben is secretly put ashore, and where the hunters come for him.

## Gentle Ben — Walt Morey (1965)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL9347710M/Gentle_Ben) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8dcf1d48dn482d0twd6exsz)

> The following day Jones's beat-up red plane flew low over the seaward side of Hinchenbrook Island. He knew every bay, meadow, and stream on the island. He knew where the brownies hung out.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Shipped out of Orca City aboard Amie Nichols's _Hustler_, Ben swims ashore on "one of the largest brown-bear islands in the Sound", winters in a root-hole den two miles from the beach and comes out in spring to a meadow and a spawning stream below a shale mountain. Mud Hole Jones, a hunting guide, pays Amie $200 for the name: "Hinchenbrook. On the seaward side." Jones flies low over the island, lands on the beach, spots Ben by his torn ear, and writes to a client in the States. Earlier, cruising off Constantine Harbor (also on the island), Mark sees the pirate boat that has robbed the Windy Point trap.
- **Why it matters:** The island turns Ben from a town pet into a wild bear, and Jones's discovery sets up the hunt that the last chapters turn on. It also puts Ben back in the Andersens' path: the trap Karl watches the next summer is on the same kind of bear island, and the meadow Jones flies over is the one by the trap.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real; the text spells it "Hinchenbrook".
