# Sparta

City-state of ancient Greece in the Eurotas valley of Laconia, in the southern Peloponnese. It is the home of the warriors whose training and departure fill the first part of _Gates of Fire_.

## Gates of Fire — Steven Pressfield (1998)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/133742/gates-of-fire-by-steven-pressfield/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m896j3ysddfcy9hg0s2r6r47)

> I stood in the crowd before the Hellenion with Alexandros and the other boys of the training platoons, the women and children, the whole city drawn up beneath the acacias and cypresses, singing the hymn to Castor

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The text calls the city Lakedaemon. Dienekes teaches the boy Alexandros about fear on the slope below the statue of Phobos, beneath the temple of Athena. The two of them sit in the myrtle grove called the Twins, which Alexandros later returns to when he defies his mother and follows the army. Alexandros runs the Big Ring past Amyklai and across the slopes of Taygetos to beat his asthma. The whole city sees Leonidas's regiments march out along the Going-Away Street to Antirhion. Field training takes place at "the Oaks" in the Otona valley, which the text does not place more exactly. After Antirhion, the Deukalion mess on the Amyklaian Way puts Alexandros through its harrowing, and Dienekes faces down Polynikes beneath the oaks outside. At night Xeo is called to Dienekes's house, "two lanes off the Eventide Road, on the west end of the village of Pitana", where the lady Arete makes him swear to protect Rooster. Rooster dresses Xeo's whipping stripes at the Anvil, a helots' grove by the Eurotas. The Herakles regiment is drilling in a dry watercourse called the Corridor, north of the village of Limnai, when the old ephor brings word that Thermopylae is chosen. In the grove behind the Deukalion mess, the krypteia's night court tries Rooster until Arete breaks in with his infant son. Before the march, Arete meets Xeo at the county-day feast on Dienekes's farm, Daphneion, and offers him silver and his cousin Diomache if he will flee; he refuses. The Three Hundred muster on the plain beneath Athena of the Brazen House, and Leonidas speaks at the altar "in the long shadows cast by distant Parnon". Then the column marches out along the Aphetaid, the Going-Away Street, past the Forts, the hero shrines of Lelex and Amphiareus, the Running Course and Axiopoinos, while Agathe holds up Alexandros's infant son and Dienekes takes leave of Arete by the Hellenion. On the last morning at Thermopylae, Leonidas speaks to his men of the valley of the Eurotas, of Parnon and Taygetos and "the cluster of five unwalled villages" that make up Sparta, where visitors will find no monuments, only the memory of what they do that day.
- **Why it matters:** The novel is Xeones's portrait of Spartan training and character, seen by an outsider, and this is where it is formed. The march-out shows the city sending its men to a fight they will not survive, and the choosing scenes set up who goes to Thermopylae.
