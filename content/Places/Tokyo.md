# Tokyo

Capital of Japan; see [[Japan]]. Craig Mod writes about its redevelopment, vanishing sento and surviving shrines, and in _Life on Air_ it is the scene of a filming mishap at an orchestra's final concert.

## [RIDGELINE] Tokyo Notes - Public Commons — Craig Mod (2024)
Source: [[RIDGELINE Tokyo Notes - Public Commons]] · [Original](https://craigmod.com/ridgeline/190/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5f1x6mdrr023tpdtgarbcpr)

> I love this city. There is no place in the world I know better.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Mod reflects on Tokyo's change and nostalgia, lamenting luxury redevelopment and the decline of sento bathhouses, praising shrines as surviving commons, and mentions setting up a small writing studio in the centre.
- **Why it matters:** The city is the subject of the essay.

## Life on Air — David Attenborough (2002)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL24547776M/Life_on_air) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5hx2jr1t6p58pnpb5vc5ym5)

> The final concert was to take place in Tokyo.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** At the last concert Attenborough and Ken Higgins sit among the French horns with a muffled camera to get face-on shots of conductor Antal Dorati. The camera is switched on too early and grinds through a quiet passage, and the film nearly runs out before the final bars.
- **Why it matters:** The comic climax of the Japan film; the hall is the stage for the set piece, though little is said about Tokyo as a city.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m815907hhbxby4vwj7yjy489)

> Tokyo has already suffered one of the most devastating earthquakes in modern times. On September 1, 1923, just before noon, the city was hit by what is known as the Great Kanto quake

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Tokyo was devastated by the Great Kanto earthquake of 1 September 1923, which killed some 200,000 people and was more than ten times as powerful as Kobe's 1995 quake. The city sits where three plates meet and has been quiet for eighty years, so strain is building; with nearly thirty million people, the text puts the cost of a repeat at up to $7 trillion.
- **Why it matters:** It turns the history of earthquakes into a forecast: the next big one may fall on one of the world's largest cities.
