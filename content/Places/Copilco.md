# Copilco

Neighbourhood at the south end of [[Mexico City]], by the National Autonomous University. In _Happy City_ a scruffy plaza on its eight-lane Eje 10 Sur is the author's favourite example of street life thriving without any planner.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m841xh30jk4cc5z403vevwsd)

> For example, I once lived in Copilco, a messy collage of brick and rebar at the south end of Mexico City.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The author's favourite place in the neighbourhood was its ugliest: a small plaza on the edge of Eje Diez Sur, where _peseros_ spewed smoke under sagging power lines and billboards. A marble staircase down to the metro sent hundreds of people through every hour, and food stalls selling fresh juice and _tacos al pastor_ caught that flow, turning the plaza into a lively gathering place.
- **Why it matters:** It shows that conviviality need not be master-planned: sometimes it is enough to let street life grow around the city's natural flows.
