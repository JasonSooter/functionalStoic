# Oneida Carry

The short portage at Rome, New York, between the Mohawk River and Wood Creek, the key link on the old water route west. _Wedding of the Waters_ treats it as the seed of the canal.

## Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation — Peter L. Bernstein (2005)
Source: [[Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3294009W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kvd8ct5kbcseccff3vf1qt)

> The Indians called the short stretch of land separating the Mohawk River from Wood Creek De-o-wain-sta, or the Great Carrying Place; Europeans referred to it as the Oneida Carry or the Wood Creek Carry and built a short road between the two points in 1702.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The Indians called the strip of land between the Mohawk headwaters and Wood Creek De-o-wain-sta, the Great Carrying Place. The book dwells on its marshes, a 1730 'neck digged through' that it calls the first canal in the New World, and the twisting Wood Creek a 1791 traveller cursed; Elkanah Watson argued that a short cut here would open the longest water route in the world, and the Western Company built two miles of canal with two locks near Rome.
- **Why it matters:** The carry is where the idea of a cross-state waterway began, and the Western Company's cut here was the canal's direct ancestor.
