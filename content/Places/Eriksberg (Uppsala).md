## How I Learned to Understand the World — Hans Rosling (2019)

Source: [[How I Learned to Understand the World]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dqz4sb18857a14n6a0cpjd)

> Even then, in 1952, there was no deep drainage system in the Eriksberg area of Uppsala, where many factory workers, including my paternal grandparents, had their home.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In 1952, aged four, Rosling nearly drowned in the open drain in front of his grandparents' house while his mother was in hospital with tuberculosis. His grandfather, a brickworks man, had built the wooden house himself on a wooded plot.
- **Why it matters:** Earliest memory and the source of his sense of slum conditions (open drains) and of Sweden's rapid improvement.
