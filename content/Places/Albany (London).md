# Albany (London)

Albany is a block of bachelor apartments off Piccadilly in London, built in the 1770s. In _The Importance of Being Earnest_ it is the address of 'Ernest Worthing', Jack's invented town self.

## The Importance of Being Earnest — Oscar Wilde (1895)
Source: [[The Importance of Being Earnest]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL27776452W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m769jg6ga5qdmfmczyj2n0)

> ‘Mr. Ernest Worthing, B. 4, The Albany.’ I’ll keep this as a proof that your name is Ernest

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Algernon reads Jack's card, 'Mr. Ernest Worthing, B. 4, The Albany', and pockets it as proof. Jack admits he pretends to have a younger brother Ernest 'who lives in the Albany, and gets into the most dreadful scrapes'. Gwendolen says she has his town address at the Albany, and in Act Two Algernon sends the same card in to Cecily when he arrives as Ernest.
- **Why it matters:** The address anchors the false identity; nobody is ever seen there. It is a joke on the set of rich bachelors the Albany housed.
