# Bettsbridge

Fictional valley town in Edith Wharton's _Ethan Frome_, the bigger town beyond Starkfield, and likely modelled on the real [[Pittsfield]].

## Ethan Frome — Edith Wharton (1911)

Source: [[Ethan Frome]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL98501W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dr0f3m7eqzj5c234s66e1w)

> I had this from Harmon Gow, who had driven the stage from Bettsbridge to Starkfield in pre-trolley days and knew the chronicle of all the families on his line.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Bettsbridge is the larger valley town that connects Starkfield to the wider world: the stage once ran from there, Ethan's mother watched the Bettsbridge pike from the farm, and its paper, the Bettsbridge Eagle, is the only thing Ethan gets at the post-office. Zeena goes by train to Bettsbridge to stay with Aunt Martha Pierce and see a new doctor, and returns with the news that she has hired a girl, which triggers Mattie's dismissal. Ethan buys a box for Mattie there and knows of a deserted wife who opened a lunchroom in Bettsbridge.
- **Why it matters:** Bettsbridge is where Zeena's medical expeditions and the Pierce relations are based; it is the offstage source of the crisis that sends Ethan and Mattie down the hill. It also stands for the nearest 'bigger town' that Ethan never reaches.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Fictional; name resembles Berkshire/Pittsfield/Stockbridge (EBSCO). 'Bettsbridge Eagle' recalls the real Berkshire Eagle of Pittsfield (my observation, not in a source).
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ best-guess. Clues in the text: “who had driven the stage from Bettsbridge to Starkfield in pre-trolley days”; “a copy of the _Bettsbridge Eagle_”; “six months mending the Bettsbridge pike after the floods”; “During the winter months there was no stage between Starkfield and Bettsbridge”. Chosen: Pittsfield. Valley town with a weekly/daily 'Eagle', department 'Emporium', lunchroom, specialists visiting, a train connection and a stage/pike to the hill village, in the Berkshire setting. EBSCO says the name blends 'Berkshire', 'Pittsfield' and 'Stockbridge'. Alternatives: Great Barrington; Lee; Stockbridge; Lenox itself (as the nearby valley town)
