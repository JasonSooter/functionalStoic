# Grand Coulee, Washington

Town above the Columbia River beside the [[Grand Coulee Dam]], in central [[Washington (state)|Washington]]. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is the ramshackle boomtown where Joe Rantz lives in Shack Town and walks B Street on Saturday nights in the summer of 1935.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86yenwxrc45f5drqkp0xdjp)

> Just uphill from the main street in Grand Coulee lay B Street, a three-block stretch of dirt and gravel lined on either side with hastily contrived buildings housing every sort of distraction a young man could imagine—card rooms and bars and pool halls and brothels and fleabag hotels and dance emporiums.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Each night Joe hikes up the hill to Shack Town, where he has a cheap room in a long, rickety building for single men, with a hot plate and a banjo, and writes to Joyce. On payday nights B Street comes alive: brothels such as the Red Rooster and Gracie's, card rooms, and taxi dancers at the Grand Coulee Club and the Silver Dollar, while drunk men stagger home across the swaying suspension catwalk over the river. Joe, Johnny White and Chuck Day mostly obey Ulbrickson's no-drinking rule; Joe won't pay a dime for a dance when a dime buys a loaf of bread, and he has Joyce to think of.
- **Why it matters:** The rough boomtown is the book's portrait of Depression labor camps and a test of the boys' discipline; back in Seattle, "a mysterious place called B Street" becomes their running joke.
