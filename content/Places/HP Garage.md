# HP Garage

Garage at 367 Addison Avenue in [[Palo Alto]], California, where Hewlett-Packard began. A copywriting guide uses it as its example of a founding story.

## You're writing lots of content. It's not getting shared. Is it engaging? (Updated October 2020) — Copyhackers (2020)

Source: [Original](https://copyhackers.com/2016/02/how-to-write-engaging-content/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5j6k3abbbmqde6aarftzhqt)

> They started their company, Hewlett Packard, and built their first product in a garage in Palo Alto, CA, with a budget of only \$538. Today that garage is not only the birthplace of one of the largest computer corporations in the world – it’s also known as the birthplace of Silicon Valley.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The author uses Hewlett and Packard's garage as her example of storytelling in content. Two founders with a \$538 budget built their first product there, and HP still references the garage story across its websites and marketing.
- **Why it matters:** The garage is the concrete place anchoring the section's argument that narrative humanizes otherwise dry products.

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m772wvf2zvdrvqgdkvez8hmw)

> It’s no accident that the humble garage where Bill Hewlett and Dave Packard began their partnership in Palo Alto is an icon here.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Byrne does not visit the garage but treats it as Silicon Valley's shrine. Like Sun Studio in Memphis or Edison's Menlo Park, he writes, "this funky little garage is revered partly because it's nothing special. Its ordinariness is the point." HP's first product was an audio oscillator, "the tone heard round the world."
- **Why it matters:** It supports his argument that dot-com idealism, the belief that anyone can do it, descends from the hippie sentiments of the Bay Area.
