## How to Do Nothing — Jenny Odell (2019)

Source: [[How to Do Nothing — Reader's Companion]] · [[How to Do Nothing]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dqzmyf531mbn7fnmtpt36r)

> I did that partly because it was in the Rose Garden that I began brainstorming my talk.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Odell grounds the whole of Chapter 1 here: after the 2016 election she goes almost daily to sit and do nothing. She describes its terraced, branching paths and bowl shape in a hill, its 1930s WPA origins, the 1970s near-conversion to condos stopped by residents, the volunteers who maintain it, and the Mother of the Year plaques on the central promenade.
- **Why it matters:** It is her model of attention-holding architecture, public space and maintenance, and the place the book's argument is anchored in.

## How to Do Nothing — Jenny Odell (2019)

Source: [[How to Do Nothing — Reader's Companion]] · [[How to Do Nothing]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ebv7cdet5sgc4m5ag8xd0a)

> Even when I go to the Rose Garden, I’m not really alone.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** She argues the garden is a social place, full of diverse people, jays, ravens, towhees and others. There she read Braiding Sweetgrass while a song sparrow pecked nearby and felt 'species loneliness' lift.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's recurring retreat site, and here its role flips from solitude to ecology of encounter.
