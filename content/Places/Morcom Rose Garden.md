# Morcom Rose Garden

Municipal rose garden in [[Oakland]], California. It is the place Jenny Odell's How to Do Nothing is anchored in, where she sits and does nothing after the 2016 election.

## How to Do Nothing — Jenny Odell (2019)

Source: [[How to Do Nothing — Reader's Companion]] · [[How to Do Nothing]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20078135W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dqzmyf531mbn7fnmtpt36r)

> I did that partly because it was in the Rose Garden that I began brainstorming my talk.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Odell grounds the whole of Chapter 1 here: after the 2016 election she goes almost daily to sit and do nothing. She describes its terraced, branching paths and bowl shape in a hill, its 1930s WPA origins, the 1970s near-conversion to condos stopped by residents, the volunteers who maintain it, and the Mother of the Year plaques on the central promenade.
- **Why it matters:** It is her model of attention-holding architecture, public space and maintenance, and the place the book's argument is anchored in.

## How to Do Nothing — Jenny Odell (2019)

Source: [[How to Do Nothing — Reader's Companion]] · [[How to Do Nothing]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20078135W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ebv7cdet5sgc4m5ag8xd0a)

> Even when I go to the Rose Garden, I’m not really alone.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** She argues the garden is a social place, full of diverse people, jays, ravens, towhees and others. There she read Braiding Sweetgrass while a song sparrow pecked nearby and felt 'species loneliness' lift.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's recurring retreat site, and here its role flips from solitude to ecology of encounter.

## how to do nothing — Jenny Odell (2019)

Source: [Original](https://medium.com/@the_jennitaur/how-to-do-nothing-57e100f59bbb) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k7f461vnhmnts15rw0nh21)

> I’d like to start off by saying that this talk is grounded in a particular location, and that is the Morcom Amphitheatre of Roses in Oakland, California, otherwise known simply as “the rose garden.”

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** The talk is explicitly grounded here. After the 2016 election Odell went to the garden almost daily to sit and do nothing, wrote the talk there, and learned its birds (a scrub jay at a particular bench, ravens). She describes its branching paths, bowl shape, WPA origins in the 1930s, a 1970s threat to turn it into condos, volunteer gardeners and the Mother of the Year plaques.
- **Why it matters:** The garden embodies the argument: architecture of nothing, public space, maintenance and care.
