# New Workers' School (51 West Fourteenth Street)

Site on West 14th Street in [[Manhattan]] of a Lovestonite school in a since-razed building. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ it is where Diego Rivera paints his panels of American history after the Rockefeller Center mural is stopped.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)

Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m737hvh12mm86th6tth9zdx9)

> at 51 West Fourteenth Street that housed a Lovestonite organization called the New Workers’ School.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In Chapter 11, with no site offered to repaint the destroyed mural, Rivera paints a revolutionary history of the United States on twenty-one movable panels in a dilapidated building here, from July 15 to early December 1933. Assistants wait for him while he spends time with Louise Nevelson; public showings with lectures follow on December 8 to 10.
- **Why it matters:** The panels keep Rivera in New York for months more, the root of Frida's frustration at [[8 West Thirteenth Street]].
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real site; ⚠️ the pin is today's building at the address, not the 1933 one.
