## Like Water for Chocolate — Laura Esquivel (1989)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL953162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k8z0358ak0pdhh0tqwpd49)

> On Mama Elena’s ranch, sausage making was a real ritual.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The ranch is the novel's whole world: Tita is born on its kitchen table, grows up bound to Mama Elena's rule, cooks there for every wedding, funeral and revolution, and is separated from Pedro under its roof. The household goes to market in Piedras Negras, fetches the family doctor from Eagle Pass, and swims the Rio Grande nearby; at the end the ranch burns down and is buried under ash.
- **Why it matters:** It is the closed world of female authority, food and repression that drives the plot; its border location ties it to the Revolution.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The ranch is fictional and never named. The text places it near the Rio Grande, with Piedras Negras as its market town and Eagle Pass, Texas, across the river.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Inferred. Clues in the text: market trips to Piedras Negras, the family doctor in Eagle Pass reached by carriage, swimming across the Rio Grande nearby. No reference work names a real ranch; the pin is Piedras Negras itself. Alternatives: any ranch in the Coahuila borderland around Piedras Negras.
