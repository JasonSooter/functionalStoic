# Mid-Atlantic Ridge

The undersea mountain range running down the middle of the Atlantic Ocean. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ its central rift leads to the theory of seafloor spreading.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80typgzy8p798pyvg53xyb9)

> Down the middle of the mid-Atlantic ridge was a canyon—a rift—up to a dozen miles wide for its entire 12,000-mile length.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Surveys reveal a world-spanning submarine mountain range with a rift up to a dozen miles wide down the middle of the mid-Atlantic ridge. Core samples in 1960 show the ocean floor is young at the ridge and older away from it, which leads Harry Hess to seafloor spreading.
- **Why it matters:** It is the physical evidence that turns continental drift into plate tectonics.
