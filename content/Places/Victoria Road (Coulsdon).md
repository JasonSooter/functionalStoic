# Victoria Road (Coulsdon)

Residential street in Coulsdon, in the south London suburbs. In _The Spy and the Traitor_ it is where the MI5 officer Michael Bettaney lives and where MI6 watches him in disguise.

## At a glance
| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Spy and the Traitor | Ben Macintyre | turning point | Disguised as down-and-outs, Simon Brown and Veronica Price watch Bettaney leave Number 5 and know he is the KGB volunteer. |

## The Spy and the Traitor — Ben Macintyre (2018)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19749410W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7vbqcajjbd2gkgxwhqzcsrv)

> On the morning of July 4, a disheveled couple in tattered clothes could be seen lounging aimlessly at the end of Victoria Road, Coulsdon, in the South London suburbs.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In chapter 9 (1983), MI6's Simon Brown and Veronica Price, dressed as tramps, watch the end of the street. At 8:05 Bettaney comes out of Number 5, pauses at the gate and looks up and down the road, and Brown says: "That moment, I knew it was him." After Bettaney confesses, a search of 5 Victoria Road turns up the proof of his espionage.
- **Why it matters:** It is where the hunt for the would-be KGB spy "Koba", whose approaches threatened to expose Gordievsky, narrows to one man. The ordinary suburban street is the point: the traitor was a commuter.
