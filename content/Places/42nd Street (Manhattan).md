# 42nd Street

Crosstown street through Midtown [[Manhattan]]. In _Bicycle Diaries_ David Byrne proposes turning it into a pedestrian street.

## At a glance
| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m772xjhrqgr43zeyztwmvtzv)

> In my opinion, Forty-second Street could easily be a pedestrian street—well, it almost is now, with all the stalled traffic, picture taking, and jaywalking.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** After Enrique Peñalosa urges New Yorkers to imagine Broadway as a pedestrian street, Byrne offers his own candidate: Forty-second Street as an elongated plaza with theaters, restaurants, trees, central seating, outdoor cafés and free WiFi. He follows it with the argument that building more roads never relieves congestion.
- **Why it matters:** It is his specific, local application of the book's case for taking street space back from cars.
