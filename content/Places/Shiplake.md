# Shiplake

Village on the Thames between Henley and Reading, with wooded islands in the river below it. In Three Men in a Boat the friends camp on one of the islands and, coming back at midnight, cannot find which one.

## Three Men in a Boat — Jerome K. Jerome (1889)

Source: [[Exploration of Three Men in a Boat]] · [[Three Men in a Boat]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1793164W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5gkykty1taq4dmy8ns6dr3w)

> we decided to go back to one of the Shiplake islands, and put up there for the night.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** After Sonning they go back to one of the Shiplake islands for the night: Irish stew, Montmorency's fight with the kettle and George's banjo. Later George and the narrator walk back from Henley in the rain and, at a quarter to twelve, cannot tell which of the islands holds the boat, while the hotels at Shiplake and Henley are full.
- **Why it matters:** The lost-island night is one of the book's longest comic sequences; the cluster of near-identical islands is what makes it work.
