## Walkable City — Jeff Speck (2012)
Source: [[Walkable City]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL36883695M/Walkable_City) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbjethpjb79p8r4w3xbwhy)

> Yet here we are among so many other pedestrians—tourists and locals alike—making our way around Trastevere … on our toes, yes, but enjoying every minute of it.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Writing from Rome with a baby in a sling and a toddler in a stroller, Speck describes a city with missing sidewalks, no crosswalks, rutted pavements and bad drivers, yet full of walkers. Trastevere is where the family walks, as evidence that 'fabric' matters more than pedestrian facilities.
- **Why it matters:** Rome and Trastevere serve as the counter-example to Lowell: technically hostile but wonderfully walkable. The argument would work with any old European fabric, but Trastevere is the specific neighborhood the text puts the family in.
