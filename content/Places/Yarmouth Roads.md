# Yarmouth Roads

Anchorage off Great Yarmouth on the Norfolk coast. Crusoe's first ship founders in a storm there, in his first taste of shipwreck.

## At a glance

| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|

## Robinson Crusoe — Daniel Defoe (1719)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL45237W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne478b5emjhcsddhf36z2b)

> The sixth day of our being at sea we came into Yarmouth Roads; the wind having been contrary and the weather calm, we had made but little way since the storm.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Crusoe's ship anchors in Yarmouth Roads among Newcastle colliers waiting for a wind; on the eighth day a great storm rises, the masts are cut away, the ship springs a leak, and the crew are taken off by a boat from another ship. They land north of Yarmouth past Winterton Ness and walk to Yarmouth, where magistrates and merchants give them money, and the master calls Crusoe a Jonah.
- **Why it matters:** His first shipwreck is the first clear warning Crusoe ignores.
