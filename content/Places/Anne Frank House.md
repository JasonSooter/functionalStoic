# Anne Frank House

Canal-house museum on the Prinsengracht in [[Amsterdam]] where the Frank family hid during the war, and the emotional end of Bill Bryson's Amsterdam chapter in Neither Here Nor There.

## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74125W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5b43p434stmmwj5w5s51bd8)

> Afterward I strolled on to the Anne Frank House on Prinsengracht. It was packed with visitors, but moving nonetheless.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 8, on his last morning, after the Rijksmuseum (where "The Night Watch" had been removed after a knife attack). He describes the eight people hidden for three years "in a secret apartment above Mr. Frank's spice business" and the endless line of visitors filing past the bookcase that hid the entrance. He regrets that the museum gives "not even a passing mention" to the Dutch who helped, naming Miep Gies, who found food for eight people every day for three years.
- **Why it matters:** It's the most serious moment in a chapter otherwise about hotels, hash and graffiti. His point, that the house tells the horror but leaves out the helpers' courage, depends on this specific museum.
