# Cape Horn

Cape Horn is the southernmost headland of Tierra del Fuego in Chile, the stormy passage between the Atlantic and the Pacific. In _Moby-Dick_ it is where Ahab's monomania takes hold on the voyage home after losing his leg.

## Moby-Dick; or, The Whale — Herman Melville (1851)
Source: [[Exploration of Moby Dick]] · [[Moby Dick]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76y2m68kbeyx0yp7kjycp)

> for long months of days and weeks, Ahab and anguish lay stretched together in one hammock, rounding in mid winter that dreary, howling Patagonian Cape;

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In Chapter 41 Ishmael tells how, after Moby Dick took his leg, Ahab rounded the cape homeward in mid-winter, lashed in a hammock and raving in a strait-jacket. Ishmael places the birth of his final monomania there; in milder latitudes the madness seemed to pass but only went inward. In Chapter 44 doubling Cape Horn is named as the route the Pequod cannot make in time for the Season-on-the-Line.
- **Why it matters:** It is the told-in-retrospect origin of Ahab's obsession, so the novel's driving force is born here. The Cape's winter storms mirror the storm in his mind.
