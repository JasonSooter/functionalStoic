# Tahiti

Largest island of French Polynesia in the South Pacific. In The Simple Path to Wealth (JL Collins) it is where the young Collins meets people who live boldly on their own terms.

## At a glance

## The Simple Path to Wealth — JL Collins (2016)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20916711W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fzchc9s73xqpqegmaz6qy4)

> Tahiti is a collection of islands in the South Paci c, each seemingly more stunning than the last. On one of these we stayed for a while in a hut built over the crystal clear water.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In the chapter 'Tales from the South Pacific', Collins and his future wife quit their jobs and travel to Tahiti, staying in an over-water hut. The hotel's co-owner Muk tells how he and friends bought a pineapple plantation sight unseen, went broke, and were asked by the bank in Papeete to finish a half-built hotel; on the beach they meet a couple who sail the South Seas while co-owning a business in Los Angeles.
- **Why it matters:** The stories Collins hears here are his parable of using money to buy freedom, the book's closing message.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The book does not name the island; the pin is Tahiti itself.

## Moby-Dick; or, The Whale — Herman Melville (1851)
Source: [[Exploration of Moby Dick]] · [[Moby Dick]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m778ws979frcvdy26eg1x5)

> took one man with him, and setting the sail of his best whale-boat, steered straight before the wind for Tahiti, five hundred miles distant, to procure a reinforcement to his crew.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** In the Town-Ho's story (Chapter 54) the crew desert at a savage port and the captain sails a whale-boat for Tahiti, five hundred miles off, to find men. Steelkilt intercepts him at sea, makes him swear to beach his boat, reaches Tahiti first and ships with his men on two French vessels. In Chapter 58 Ishmael says that in the soul there lies 'one insular Tahiti, full of peace and joy', encompassed by the horrors of the half-known life.
- **Why it matters:** Tahiti resolves the Town-Ho plot by letting Steelkilt escape, and it gives the book one of its key images of the peaceful island set against the terrible sea.

## Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind — Yuval Noah Harari (2014)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17075811W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m766sxwzccgwnb9qcwfkfg)

> The Royal Society resolved to send an eminent astronomer, Charles Green, to Tahiti, and spared neither effort nor money.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** The Royal Society sent the astronomer Charles Green to Tahiti to observe the 1769 transit of Venus, with Banks, Solander and artists aboard Cook's ship. After the observation the expedition charted Pacific islands, visited Australia and New Zealand and returned in 1771.
- **Why it matters:** It opens the chapter on science and empire, an expedition that was scientific and military at once.
