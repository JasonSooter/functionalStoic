# Spandau Prison

Former Allied military prison in Spandau, western [[Berlin]], demolished after its last inmate, Rudolf Hess, died in 1987. David Byrne's _Bicycle Diaries_ dwells on how thoroughly it was made to vanish.

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7641q2qqk6ejjvj5txt5cs4)

> When Rudolf Hess died—the last Nazi prisoner held in Spandau Prison—reportedly by strangling himself with an electrical cord, the whole building in that western suburb of the city was said to have been dismantled, brick by brick.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In "Prisoner Number Seven" Byrne retells how the British, in whose sector it stood, carted the bricks off at night, ground them into powder and threw them into the sea, so the prison could not attract neo-Nazi sympathisers; only a sandlot remained. For twenty years Hess was the only prisoner, wandering the vast complex.
- **Why it matters:** It is one of Byrne's Berlin meditations on how the city erases or keeps the traces of its regimes.
