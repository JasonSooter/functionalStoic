# Sana'a

Highland capital of Yemen. In _Nathaniel's Nutmeg_ Middleton's captured men are marched here in midwinter 1610–11 to face the Pasha.

## Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Giles Milton (1999)

Source: [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Reader's Companion]] · [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1970050W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8efd1dd6m3932xbm6dqs055)

> At last they came to Sana’a, ‘a citie somewhat bigger than Bristol,’ where their fur gowns were confiscated and they were forced to march barefoot through the city like common criminals.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** The English prisoners from [[Mocha]] stumble into Taiz on Christmas Day 1610 as "the first few flakes of snow began to fall", then climb on through frost to Sana'a. Middleton is dragged before the Pasha and accuses the Aga of treachery. The Pasha then turns "kindly and avuncular", giving the men a large mansion, six cows and Middleton "a purse of 150 gold coins".
- **Why it matters:** A strange interlude far from the spice islands, it shows how the Company's men were at the mercy of Ottoman politics on the route east.
