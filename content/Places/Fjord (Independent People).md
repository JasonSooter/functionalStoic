# Fjord (Independent People)

The fictional trading town at the head of a fjord in _Independent People_, where Bjartur sells his wool and runs up debts with the merchant. Pinned at Akureyri as a best guess.

## Independent People — Halldór Laxness (1934)

Source: [[Independent People]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL757983W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5khcnk8xvjpt6k8k6jarx5c)

> The high heath in the east, over which lies the road down to the market town on the fjord, is considered a five hours’ crossing with pack-horses.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The crofters trade at Bruni's, the Danish merchant firm managed by Tulinius Jensen, five hours over the eastern heath with pack-horses. Bjartur takes his daughter Asta Sollilja there, and she sees the ocean for the first time from the edge of the heath. Over the years the town gains a co-operative society and a savings bank, the merchant goes bankrupt, and in the last part harbour workers strike at the breakwater, Bjartur leaves his son Gvendur among them, and the poor live in the hovels of Sandeyri, farther down the fjord.
- **Why it matters:** Fjord is where the crofters' independence meets credit, merchants and politics; every trip there tightens the economic trap the novel describes.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Fictional.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The town is invented and the text names no real one. It lies at the sea end of a fjord, east over a high heath from the valley, with a harbour and mail-steamers south to Reykjavik; the Bailiff trades in another town, Vik. A reference summary suggests Fjord may be modelled on Akureyri (and Vik on Húsavík), so the pin is Akureyri. If the valley is the Jökuldalsheiði heath in the east, as a travel guide suggests, an eastern fjord town such as Vopnafjörður or Seyðisfjörður would fit better.
