# Snow-White Villa

The Durrells' third and smallest house on [[Corfu]], identified as the villa Criseda at Perama, south of [[Corfu Town]]. It opens the third part of _My Family and Other Animals_, after Larry's guests have outgrown the [[Daffodil-Yellow Villa]].

## My Family and Other Animals — Gerald Durrell (1956)
Source: [[My Family and Other Animals]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/289004/my-family-and-other-animals-by-gerald-durrell/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8fmhk3vg7pbex5n9bj151qg)

> Perched on a hill-top among olive trees, the new villa, white as snow, had a broad veranda running along one side, which was hung with a thick pelmet of grape-vine.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** When Great-Aunt Hermione threatens to visit, Larry proposes they move "to a smaller villa" so there is no room for her, and Mother agrees. Spiro finds the new house: a white villa on a hilltop among olive trees, with a walled wild-flower garden, a magnolia, and a rutted drive down through olive groves, vineyards and orchards to the road. Gerry says it was here that he "got on really intimate terms with the mantids", and the garden wall's geckos, among them Geronimo under a stone in the zinnia bed beneath his window, become his new hunting ground.
- **Why it matters:** The third villa gives the book its last part and a smaller, quieter household after the crowded middle section. Its garden wall and hilltop are the stage for the mantid and gecko studies that open the chapter.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The book names no village. The Durrelliana "Corfu landscapes" page identifies it as Criseda in Perama, which "stands on a prominent rise overlooking Lake Halikiopoulou". ⚠️ The pin is Perama village, which it shares with the [[Strawberry-Pink Villa]], not the house itself.
