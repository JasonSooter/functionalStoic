# Nuremberg

City in Franconia, Bavaria, Germany. In _The Girl Who Drew Butterflies_ it is where Maria Merian runs a studio, teaches young women and publishes her first caterpillar book.

## The Girl Who Drew Butterflies — Joyce Sidman (2018)
Source: [[The Girl Who Drew Butterflies]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19731743W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kgrtfdavy9z9386eryghcd)

> Instead, along with new baby Dorothea and ten-year-old Johanna, she visited the gardens near Nuremberg Castle—gathering, watching, learning.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Maria and her husband Johann Graff move to his home town, where she helps run an engraving and publishing business, teaches a circle of young women and publishes her flower book and, in 1679, her first book on caterpillars. She keeps caterpillars in her kitchen and collects them in the gardens near Nuremberg Castle, wary of neighbours' disapproval.
- **Why it matters:** Nuremberg is the setting of her first books and growing research, and of the marriage she later leaves.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)
Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m75swbsaxczbsy1tjhyf3s)

> But eventually, around 1500, when the Hapsburgs consolidated their hold on the Empire, it settled in Nuremberg, making that rich and powerful Free City in a very particular sense Imperial

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 11 has the Imperial Crown settle in Nuremberg around 1500, making it a dream of German unity that Wagner celebrated and Hitler exploited, bringing the crown back from Vienna to the city of the rallies. Chapter 17 describes Dürer's birth and print business there, and Chapter 19 calls it 'more or less the centre of Europe', home of guild masterpieces such as the columbine cup and of Peter Henlein's supposed first watch.
- **Why it matters:** Nuremberg recurs as the book's model of an Imperial Free City whose trade and guilds made German craftsmanship, and whose symbols were later abused.
