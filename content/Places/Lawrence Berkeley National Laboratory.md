# Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory

US national laboratory in the hills above [[Berkeley, California|Berkeley]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Frank Asaro finds the iridium spike there that points to an asteroid.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80tyesrc63rbq6p4w2ygp4t)

> On June 21, 1978, at 1:45 p.m., we put a sample in the detector. It ran for 224 minutes and we could see we were getting interesting results, so we stopped it and had a look.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Luis Alvarez asks his colleague Frank Asaro here to analyse the Gubbio clay by neutron activation. On June 21, 1978 the sample goes in the detector and shows iridium more than three hundred times normal levels; over the following months Asaro and Helen Michel work up to thirty hours at a stretch, always with the same result.
- **Why it matters:** The iridium result is the hard evidence for an extraterrestrial impact at the end of the Cretaceous.
