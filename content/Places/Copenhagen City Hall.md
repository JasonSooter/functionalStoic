# Copenhagen City Hall

Town hall (Rådhus) in [[Copenhagen]], Denmark. Its bells ring in the Danish New Year in a newsletter on New Year's traditions, and in _The Spy and the Traitor_ an exhibition opening there is where MI6 first meets Oleg Gordievsky.

## The Ultimate Guide to Danish New Year's — Expat in Denmark (2024)
Source: [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5edgbwt49tcteeswhrd6g9m)

> When the clock strikes midnight, the town hall bells of the Danish capital will sound, marking the beginning of the new year.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The newsletter devotes a short section to the town hall bells that chime twelve times at midnight on New Year's Eve. Danes have quarrelled over which chime marks the new year, and since 1958 the clock and bells have been televised with a countdown, ending the debate.
- **Why it matters:** It is the national focal point of the Danish midnight ritual, but it is one item in a listicle of traditions; the piece would lose little if it were another landmark.

## The Spy and the Traitor — Ben Macintyre (2018)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19749410W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7p6zhev8p7bdegzq49n046s)

> Their first direct encounter took place at the Copenhagen town hall, a redbrick edifice called the Rådhus, at the opening of an art exhibition.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Knowing a Soviet delegation will attend an exhibition opening at the town hall, MI6's head of station Richard Bromhead greets the group, Gordievsky among them, without asking his name. The two fall into a halting conversation about art, and Bromhead reports to London that the target has been contacted.
- **Why it matters:** It is the first face-to-face contact between Gordievsky and MI6, a public, deniable meeting that starts the long courtship.
