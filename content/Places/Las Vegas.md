# Las Vegas

Las Vegas is a desert city in southern Nevada. It turns up in Walkable City (Jeff Speck) as an example of an unwalkable city, and in The Comfort Crisis (Michael Easter) as the home whose surrounding desert serves as his training ground.

## Walkable City — Jeff Speck (2012)

Source: [[Walkable City]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL36883695M/Walkable_City) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbnmk7nrjwe7hq99qfge6d)

> I remember the first time I visited Las Vegas, which, aside from the Strip and the old main street (Fremont Street) is a place where nobody walks.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Driving in a rented Mustang, Speck found the rental-car map of major roads was the whole city, with no fine-grained network.
- **Why it matters:** Anecdotal illustration of big-block unwalkability.

## The Comfort Crisis — Michael Easter (2021)

Source: [[The Comfort Crisis]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/634446/the-comfort-crisis-by-michael-easter/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fyd80j759x2z44jsy497g9)

> Finding discomfort in Las Vegas was easy. All I had to do was walk into the desert.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In chapter 7, over six months of preparation for the Alaska trek, Easter hikes the desert and canyons around Las Vegas in summer heat with a 40- to 60-pound pack and heavy winter boots, even loading up for chores and dog walks. He treats the desert as an escape from the built environment.
- **Why it matters:** The harsh desert on his doorstep lets him practise discomfort before the real test; the contrast with the city's comforts is the book's theme in miniature.
