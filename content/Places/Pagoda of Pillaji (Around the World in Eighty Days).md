# Pagoda of Pillaji

The fictional forest pagoda where Aouda is held before her suttee in _Around the World in Eighty Days_, pinned at a best guess in Bundelkhand. Fogg's party rescues her there.

## Around the World in Eighty Days — Jules Verne (1872)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1100007W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne3fjeh1ky1hsb02b3gz7t)

> “To the pagoda of Pillaji, two miles from here; she will pass the night there.”

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** At Kholby the railway turns out to be unfinished, so Fogg buys the elephant Kiouni and sets off for Allahabad. In the forest the party meets a Brahmin procession taking a drugged young widow, Aouda, to the pagoda of Pillaji for her suttee at dawn. They scout its guarded doors and begin to dig through its back wall; at dawn Passepartout, disguised as the dead rajah, rises from the pyre and carries her off.
- **Why it matters:** It is the setting of the book's main rescue, which brings Aouda into the party and, in the end, into Fogg's life.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Pillaji and the hamlet of Kholby are fictional names.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text puts the pagoda in a wood with a small stream, two miles from where the party sees the procession, after it crosses the main chain of the Vindhyas on the way from the railway gap at Kholby (fifteen miles beyond Rothal) to Allahabad, and the dead man is a rajah of Bundelcund. No reference work locates it. Pinned at Bundelkhand's representative point; a site nearer Allahabad, along the old Jabalpur–Allahabad line, would fit the distances as well.
