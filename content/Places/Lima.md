# Lima

Lima is the capital of Peru, on the country's Pacific coast. In _Moby-Dick_ it is Ishmael's example of the horror of whiteness and the city where he tells the Town-Ho's story at the Golden Inn.

## Moby-Dick — Herman Melville (1851)

Source: [[Exploration of Moby Dick]] · [[Moby Dick]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m7736806tss9sk8zwcavvb)

> it is not these things alone which make tearless Lima, the strangest, saddest city thou can’st see. For Lima has taken the white veil;

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In Chapter 42 Ishmael lists Lima's earthquakes, ruined spires and rainless skies and says it is the strangest, saddest city because it 'has taken the white veil', its ruins kept for ever new. Chapter 54 is framed as told 'at the Golden Inn' in Lima, on a saint's eve, to young dons Pedro and Sebastian, who interrupt with questions and jokes about Lima's corruption. At the end Ishmael asks for a copy of the Evangelists and swears on it that the story is true.
- **Why it matters:** It serves two arguments: whiteness as horror, and the truth of the Town-Ho tale, which Ishmael stakes with an oath there. The Golden Inn scene is only lightly sketched, but it is one of the few moments set after the voyage.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The Golden Inn is not identified with a real building and is folded into this note.
