# Park Avenue

Broad north–south avenue in [[Manhattan]] with a planted median. In _Happy City_ it is the monument to cars taking over the city, and the street New Yorkers briefly took back during the 2008 Summer Streets.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m841xvw8fpbkj4mctakecq5t)

> The idea had its greatest test on three sunny Saturdays in the summer of 2008, when New York City’s Department of Transportation erected barriers so that people on foot, bikes, and Rollerblades could claim several major thoroughfares, including Park Avenue.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** A footnote calls Park Avenue the monument to Motordom's transformation of central cities: it got its name in the 1850s because a long stretch really was a park, with lawns and a six-block brick promenade, and is now a highway-like thoroughfare. In the summer of 2008, Bogotá's Ciclovía idea came to New York as Summer Streets; thousands did yoga, samba and tai chi in the road, and residents set lawn chairs on the grass median "as though it were the park it hadn’t been for a hundred years."
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's proof that roads are malleable: the same avenue shows both what cars took and how quickly people reclaim it.
