# St. Louis

City on the Mississippi in eastern Missouri. In _Stoner_ it is the home of Edith Bostwick's banker parents, where Stoner asks for her hand.

## Stoner — John Williams (1965)

Source: [Original](https://www.nyrb.com/products/stoner) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8afpbx76wr7nk8r0kn5a25g)

> It was the largest and most elegant house that Stoner had ever been in.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Edith "lived with her parents in St. Louis", where her father is president of one of the smaller banks. Stoner takes the train down to meet them. Their house is high-ceilinged and dark, crowded with vases, silver on marble-topped tables and furniture so fragile he is afraid to move. After a stiff talk about his "prospects", Horace and Mrs. Bostwick consent; Edith insists "it must be soon," then avoids him all day. That night in the guest room he cannot sleep and, for the first time, questions what he is about to do, before catching the early train back to Columbia.
- **Why it matters:** The engagement scene foreshadows the cold marriage that dominates the middle of the novel, and the house's wealth and stiffness show the gulf between Edith's world and Stoner's farm.
