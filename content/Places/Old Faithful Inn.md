# Old Faithful Inn

Historic log hotel beside Old Faithful geyser in Yellowstone National Park. In Ryback's memoir it is a night of hot baths amid the crowds of the park's centennial summer.

## The Ultimate Journey - Canada to Mexico Down the Continental Divide — Eric Ryback (1973)
Source: [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8bescm60zw2569ms22zfbae)

> That evening we stayed at the Old Faithful Inn to repack all our equipment and supplies and to enjoy one of the greatest of civilization's inventions — a hot bath.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The brothers come out of the backcountry onto the Firehole River road and into the park's centennial-year crowds, which Eric satirizes at length. That evening they stay at the Old Faithful Inn to repack their gear and enjoy "one of the greatest of civilization's inventions — a hot bath." Next day Eric photographs the geysers with hundreds of onlookers while Tim watches the "aimless confusion" of the crowd.
- **Why it matters:** It is the centre of the book's critique of park commercialism, which Eric recalls later as "the travesty of Yellowstone".
