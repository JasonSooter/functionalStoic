# Metropolitan Museum of Art

Art museum on Fifth Avenue in Manhattan. It turns up in the reading as a test of taste: a Chris Creed blog post finds flawed art on its walls, and in Aoibheann Sweeney's novel Miranda fails to love its marble statues.

## A blog for people who make stuff for a living — Chris Creed (2015)
Source: [Original](http://chriscreedblog.com/art-doesnt-have-to-be-right/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fzbwc3z4nwpn9was13h3fw)

> We were walking around The Met this weekend and overheard a woman critiquing a piece of art.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** Walking through the Met, the author overhears a woman criticising a painting's perspective and proportions, and reflects that imperfect work hangs in the country's largest art museum.
- **Why it matters:** The museum's prestige makes the point: flawed art is still on its walls.

## Among Other Things, I've Taken Up Smoking — Aoibheann Sweeney (2007)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguin.com.au/books/among-other-things-ive-taken-up-smoking-9781101202227)

> The Metropolitan Museum of Art was set off from Fifth Avenue by a wide set of marble steps, fountains on one side and Central Park spread out like a garden behind it, as far as the eye could see.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Alone in the city, Miranda takes the subway to "the one museum my father had mentioned". She passes the Egyptian tomb and cases of bowls into a gallery of ancient marble statues, "impossibly ancient and perfect but somehow enervating", and feels it as "a test": "My father, I knew, would be disappointed that I did not like it more." She leaves for Central Park and thinks, "Maybe I just don't like cities."
- **Why it matters:** The Met stands in for her classicist father's world; failing to love it is Miranda's first quiet break from his tastes. Its grandeur, and the classical sculpture in particular, is what makes the scene work.
