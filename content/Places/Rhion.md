# Rhion

Port on the narrow strait at the mouth of the Gulf of Corinth, facing Antirhion on the northern shore. In _Gates of Fire_ Alexandros and Xeones are cast adrift in the strait, then watch Leonidas's army win the battle below Antirhion's walls.

## Gates of Fire — Steven Pressfield (1998)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/133742/gates-of-fire-by-steven-pressfield/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m896j2np5a9nf3e433wzfzp8)

> The twin ports of Rhion and Antirhion commanded the western approach to the Gulf of Corinth. This avenue threatened the Peloponnese and all of central Greece.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Leonidas marches to bring Antirhion into the alliance. Alexandros and Xeones trail the army through Selassia and Tegea and reach the bluff at Rhion after midnight on the third day, with Antirhion's lights visible across the strait. From an obscure launching beach called the Ovens they hire a "pirate" smack, which abandons them in the widest part of the gulf. They spend hours in the water talking about the people they love, then come ashore on a rock beach. The battle is fought on a dusty plain west of Antirhion, under the citadel walls and cut by a stream, the Akanathus. The Skiritai burn the harbour sheds and the smoke blows onto the enemy; the Antirhionians and their Syrakusan allies break. The boys watch from a bluff, then run down onto the field, where the squire Meriones dies asking to be buried "here", under the mound raised for the fallen. Afterwards Leonidas points across the channel from Antirhion to Rhion and says the Persians will find "enemies united and implacable" on both shores.
- **Why it matters:** It is the boys' first sight of real battle and Alexandros's first test, which the later chapters in Sparta keep returning to. Leonidas's speech over the strait states the book's case for a united Hellas.
