# Mediterranean Sea

Almost enclosed sea between Europe, North Africa and the Middle East. Not the End of the World uses it to show where Europe's beach plastic comes from.

## At a glance

## Not the End of the World — Hannah Ritchie (2024)
Source: [[Not the End of the World]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL37518722W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5f0qt987aq568p2yhj43cxw)

> It’s a closed basin, and almost all of its plastic has come from the countries around it.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Ritchie notes the Mediterranean is a closed basin whose plastic comes almost entirely from surrounding countries, so European coastlines are polluted by European rivers.
- **Why it matters:** It supports her point that Europe controls the cleanliness of its own shores. Representative point (approximate).

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Such an event occurred a little under six million years ago and provoked what is known to science as the Messinian Salinity Crisis. What happened was that continental movement closed the Strait of Gibraltar.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Bryson says the Mediterranean would dry out in about a thousand years if it were not replenished. A little under six million years ago continental movement closed the Strait of Gibraltar; the drying sea's water fell as fresh rain on other seas, diluting them enough to freeze over larger areas, and the extra ice may have pushed Earth into an ice age.
- **Why it matters:** It shows how a change in one sea can shift the whole climate; Bryson adds that it "may even have created us".
