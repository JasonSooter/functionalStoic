## How China is rewriting the book on human origins — Nature (2016)
Source: [Original](http://www.nature.com/news/how-china-is-rewriting-the-book-on-human-origins-1.20231) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jx736var2sksb2t79fbnyw)

> On the outskirts of Beijing, a small limestone mountain named Dragon Bone Hill rises above the surrounding sprawl. Along the northern side, a path leads up to some fenced-off caves that draw 150,000 visitors each year, from schoolchildren to grey-haired pensioners. It was here, in 1929, that researchers discovered a nearly complete ancient skull that they determined was roughly half a million years old.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Opens the article at the cave site where Peking Man was found, which launched the idea that humans first evolved in Asia; its significance later faded against African finds but still haunts Chinese researchers.
- **Why it matters:** Anchor of the whole narrative about China's role in human origins.
