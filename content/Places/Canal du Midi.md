# Canal du Midi

Canal across southern France from Toulouse to the Mediterranean, built 1666–1681. In _Wedding of the Waters_ it is the great European precedent for the Erie.

## Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation — Peter L. Bernstein (2005)

Source: [[Wedding of the Waters - The Erie Canal and the Making of a Great Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3294009W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kvdbgh2sj9r259j2f3vqmc)

> One of the most spectacular European canals was the Canal du Midi, built from 1666 to 1681 under the reign and with the blessings of King Louis XIV.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The book describes the canal built from 1666 to 1681 under Louis XIV, linking Bordeaux via Toulouse and Carcassonne to Sète: it climbs 620 feet through 103 locks fed by a huge reservoir, with puddled clay walls, and ruined its financier Riquet. Bernstein notes that the Erie's designers made the same choice of a wholly artificial waterway.
- **Why it matters:** It shows that the Erie belonged to a European tradition of summit canals rather than being invented from scratch.
