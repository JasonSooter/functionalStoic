# N Street Cohousing

Cohousing community of joined suburban backyards in Davis, [[California]]. A LifeEdited post profiles it as a way to retrofit the suburbs.

## What’s Right With This Picture? — David Friedlander (2015)
Source: [Original](http://lifeedited.com/whats-right-with-this-picture/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5gbgm3a7qgtbx8pab7ck94k)

> But if you look closely, right between N St and Lessley Pl, there’s a group of homes with irregular yards.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The post looks at an aerial view of a suburban block where the backyards of adjoining houses have been merged. The community began in 1986 when Kevin Wolf and Linda Cloud joined two houses, and more neighbours have joined since.
- **Why it matters:** The place is the article's example of turning existing suburbs into shared, smaller-footprint living.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m841x6kpqmnc282a4fme484c)

> It started in 1986, when Kevin Wolf and Linda Cloud, a pair of young environmental activists, bought neighboring homes on N Street on what was then the edge of the university town of Davis.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Wolf and Cloud tore down the fence between their two houses; as like-minded neighbours bought or rented next door, more fences came down, and in 2005 the couple financed a larger common house. Visiting on a Friday night in 2010, the author finds more than fifty people on two acres around a shared green with an orchard, chickens and gardens. Every lot is still privately owned, members pay $25 a month for the common house, and each household chooses how much to share.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's best example of a balance between privacy and conviviality: a "system of voluntary association" retrofitted into an ordinary suburban block.
