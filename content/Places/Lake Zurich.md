# Lake Zurich

Lake Zurich is a large lake in Switzerland whose shores yielded the prehistoric pile dwellings found in 1853–54 that started Europe's lake-dwelling craze. It is pinned for _The Hobbit_ as a best-guess anchor for Lake-town, which Tolkien modelled on such Alpine pile villages.

## The Hobbit (Middle-Earth Universe) — J. R. R. Tolkien (1937)
Source: [[The Hobbit]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL27482W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m775md51s47fkkbshcxmbd)

> A great bridge made of wood ran out to where on huge piles made of forest trees was built a busy wooden town, not a town of elves but of Men, who still dared to dwell here under the shadow of the distant dragon-mountain.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Bilbo and the dwarves reach Lake-town (Esgaroth) in barrels down the Forest River and are welcomed by the Master and the townsfolk, who sing of the return of the King under the Mountain. Later Smaug attacks the town, Bard shoots him, and the dragon falls on the town and destroys it.
- **Why it matters:** Lake-town is the human world between Mirkwood and the Mountain, where the dragon is killed; its pile-built design is one of Tolkien's most direct borrowings from real archaeology.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text describes a town "right out on the surface of the lake", built "on huge piles made of forest trees", with "The rotting piles of a greater town" visible in droughts. Wikipedia (Esgaroth) says Tolkien modelled it closely on the Neolithic pile dwellings found on Swiss lakes, probably via an artist's reconstruction such as Robert Munro's 1908 book (citing Hammond and Scull), and Deborah Sabo (Mythlore, 2007) notes the 1853–54 Lake Zurich finds that started the craze. No source names Lake Zurich as Tolkien's specific model; it is chosen as the best-known type site. Alternatives: other Alpine pile-dwelling lakes such as Lake Constance or Lake Neuchâtel.
