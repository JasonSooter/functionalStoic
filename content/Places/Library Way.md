# Library Way

Stretch of East 41st Street, Manhattan, leading to the New York Public Library, set with bronze plaques of literary quotations. The Daily Stoic opens an entry there.

## The Daily Stoic — Ryan Holiday and Stephen Hanselman (2016)

Source: [[The Daily Stoic]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20046443W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5f0qmytrpv10cnbw0eh51aa)

> Take a walk down Forty-first Street toward the beautiful New York City Public Library, with its majestic stone lions. On your way up “Library Way,” you’ll pass a gold placard laid into the cement

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The December 28 entry opens with the reader walking along Forty-first Street toward the library, passing gold plaques in the pavement with quotations from great writers, including Marcus Aurelius on how the famous and those who remember them are soon forgotten.
- **Why it matters:** It frames the entry with a real walk. The pin is the street's midpoint between Park and Fifth Avenues (approximate).
