# Shelbourne Hotel

Grand hotel on the north side of St Stephen's Green, Dublin. In _The Country Girls Trilogy_ it is where Kate leaves Eugene, handing a page boy a farewell note during a lunch.

## At a glance
| Source | Author | Role | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Country Girls Trilogy | Edna O'Brien | turning point | The Stephen's Green hotel where Kate slips a farewell note to a page boy and walks out on Eugene. |

## The Country Girls Trilogy — Edna O'Brien (1986)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL69252W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m75wj5fx1grk0a52w1hsfh)

> “The Shelbourne,” he said, and I looked out the window at the two crooked chalked signs on a limestone wall—UP THE IRA and SHUN CYCLING SLAVES, and memorized them, telling myself that I might never drive this way again; telling myself, yet not believing it.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Near the end of The Lonely Girl Eugene drives Kate in from the country to lunch at the Shelbourne with Simon and Mary. They get out at Stephen's Green; she slips into the cloakroom, gives a page boy the letter she has written and two shillings, and leaves, going back to Joanna's.
- **Why it matters:** This is where Kate makes her break with Eugene, the end of the second novel's love story. A grand public hotel suits a parting staged so that no scene can happen.
