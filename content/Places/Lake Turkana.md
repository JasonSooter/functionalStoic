# Lake Turkana

Desert lake in northern [[Kenya]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ its valley yields the Turkana boy, the most complete Homo erectus skeleton.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> The remote valley of Lake Turkana (formerly Lake Rudolf) in Kenya is now one of the world’s most productive sites for early human remains, but for a very long time no one had thought to look there.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** No one thought to look here until a diverted flight took Richard Leakey over the valley. Kamoya Kimeu then found a piece of hominid brow on a hill away from the lake, and digging produced a nearly complete skeleton of a boy who died 1.54 million years ago. Meave Leakey's Kenyanthropus platyops also comes from Lake Turkana.
- **Why it matters:** The Turkana boy gave the first near-complete Homo erectus skeleton, though Bryson notes experts still argue over what to call it.
