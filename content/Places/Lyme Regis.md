# Lyme Regis

Seaside town on the Dorset coast in [[England]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where the child Mary Anning found the first ichthyosaurus in the cliffs in 1812.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80gj0349emhe2yx9ar3zcey)

> In 1812, at Lyme Regis on the Dorset coast, an extraordinary child named Mary Anning

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Anning, aged eleven, twelve or thirteen depending on the account, finds a seventeen-foot fossil sea monster, now the ichthyosaurus, in the steep cliffs along the Channel. She spends the next thirty-five years gathering fossils to sell to visitors, also finding the first plesiosaurus and one of the best early pterodactyls, and Bryson praises the care with which she extracted them.
- **Why it matters:** The cliffs are the reason for the career: an eroding fossil coast let a poor girl make discoveries the gentleman scientists built on.
