## Not All Practice Makes Perfect — Anders Ericsson & Robert Pool (2016)
Source: [Original](http://nautil.us/issue/35/boundaries/not-all-practice-makes-perfect) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjqjjjy3q2y0zyg7y7a88v)

> Steve, an undergraduate at Carnegie Mellon University, where I was teaching at the time, had been hired to come in several times a week and work on a simple task: memorizing strings of numbers.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** Ericsson recounts the digit-memory experiment with undergraduate Steve Faloon, run in hour-long sessions while Ericsson was teaching at Carnegie Mellon. Steve broke through his short-term-memory ceiling on a Friday and went on to recall 82 digits over two years. Bill Chase, a Carnegie Mellon psychology professor, collaborated.
- **Why it matters:** It is the institutional home of the experiment on which the article's argument about practice rests; the text gives no more specific room or building.
