# Stanford University

University campus in [[California]], United States. It appears in the reading as Jenny Odell's workplace, where her students' overwork and fear illustrate the shrinking capacity for refusal.

## How to Do Nothing — Jenny Odell (2019)

Source: [[How to Do Nothing — Reader's Companion]] · [[How to Do Nothing]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20078135W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5e31ts3g96t19yk6nwqemnf)

> Back in 2013, students in my first art classes at Stanford were surprised that I didn’t know about “Stanford duck syndrome.”

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The author teaches at Stanford and describes students' overwork, the 'duck syndrome' and the 'Stanford Memes for Edgy Trees' page as signs of precarity and fear that erode capacity for refusal.
- **Why it matters:** The campus supplies a present-day case of the shrinking 'margin' for refusal; it is her workplace, so partly incidental to a general argument.

## Quiet — Susan Cain (2012)

Source: [[Quiet]] · [Original](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/55983/quiet-by-cain-susan/9780141970172) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5g8wt7vxr9sqxrry7evtqhb)

> When I caught up with Mike the following autumn, in his freshman year at Stanford, only a twenty-minute drive from Cupertino but a world away demographically, he seemed unsettled.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Mike Wei moves from the Cupertino 'cocoon' to Stanford, a twenty-minute drive away, and tells Cain over coffee outdoors that his quietness is now seen as weakness, even at his adviser's house.
- **Why it matters:** It marks the move from a sheltered culture into the Extrovert Ideal.

## The Billionaire Who’s Building a Davos of His Own - The New York Times — Alessandra Stanley (2016)

Source: [Original](https://www.nytimes.com/2016/04/17/business/the-billionaire-whos-building-a-davos-of-his-own.html) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjrm6a9k433ywdp1srgdqq)

> In a wood-paneled conference room at Stanford University, a score of scholars, many of them eminent and some from as far away as Johannesburg and Beijing, gathered last month to compare philosophical notions of hierarchy and equality.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** The article opens at a Berggruen Institute philosophy conference in a wood-paneled room at Stanford, where Berggruen speaks without interruption and gets chocolate mousse while others get fruit tarts. The conference ran from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. and a dinner was held at the Stanford Park Hotel.
- **Why it matters:** The opening scene frames Berggruen's influence. The setting is largely incidental, though the conference is the portrait's main observed event.

## Here Is The Powerful Letter The Stanford Victim Read Aloud To Her Attacker — BuzzFeed News (2016)

Source: [Original](https://www.buzzfeed.com/katiejmbaker/heres-the-powerful-letter-the-stanford-victim-read-to-her-ra) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jvr8tqxhsgpm3kkraks9w2)

> two Stanford University graduate students biking across campus spotted a freshman thrusting his body on top of an unconscious, half-naked woman behind a dumpster.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** The assault took place behind a dumpster after a campus frat party; two passing graduate students on bicycles intervened. The victim's statement returns repeatedly to this site and what was done to her there.
- **Why it matters:** Central site of the crime and the case; the statement also notes the world is 'bigger than Palo Alto and Stanford'.

## Meet the worst ants in the world — Annalee Newitz (2016)

Source: [Original](https://arstechnica.com/science/2016/08/meet-the-worst-ants-in-the-world/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jx9f90ees4h6hfzhyfkrs9)

> In biologist Deborah Gordon's lab at Stanford, there is a small group of researchers who observe Argentine ants in the wild. Which is to say, they go to a small, triangular copse of trees on campus bordered on all three sides by asphalt walkways.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** The author joins undergraduate Dylan MacArthur-Waltz on a July afternoon to map ant nests among the trees. He explains the winter shrinkage to two nests, the spring expansion to 20-30, and the queen cull.
- **Why it matters:** The article's main field scene, showing the ants' seasonal cycle first-hand.
