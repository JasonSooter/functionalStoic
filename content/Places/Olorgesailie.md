# Olorgesailie

Prehistoric site on the floor of the Great Rift Valley, about forty miles from [[Nairobi]] in [[Kenya]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Bryson visits its vast scatter of hand-axes, a tool "factory" that left no human bones.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Here, perhaps forty miles out of Nairobi, along the baking valley floor, is an ancient site called Olorgesailie, which once stood beside a large and pleasant lake.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Bryson visits in 2002 while in Kenya for CARE International. J. W. Gregory first noticed the ground littered with shaped stones in 1919, and the Leakeys later excavated about ten acres where Acheulean hand-axes were made in huge numbers from about 1.2 million to 200,000 years ago. His guide from the national museum explains that the stone came from mountains some ten kilometres away, and that the people left when the lake dried up.
- **Why it matters:** Olorgesailie is Bryson's on-the-ground illustration of how little is known about early humans: a site of enormous, purposeful labour with tools that seem curiously pointless and no bones of their makers.
