# Seamen's Bethel

The Seamen's Bethel is a chapel for sailors on Johnny Cake Hill in New Bedford, Massachusetts. It is the model for the Whaleman's Chapel in _Moby-Dick_, where Father Mapple preaches his sermon on Jonah.

## Moby-Dick — Herman Melville (1851)

Source: [[Exploration of Moby Dick]] · [[Moby Dick]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m76r5v99tkrj34fr8ykbws)

> In this same New Bedford there stands a Whaleman’s Chapel, and few are the moody fishermen, shortly bound for the Indian Ocean or Pacific, who fail to make a Sunday visit to the spot.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** On a stormy Sunday Ishmael visits the chapel, reads the black-bordered marble tablets to whalemen lost at sea, and sees Queequeg among the congregation. Father Mapple climbs a rope ladder into a pulpit built like a ship's bow and preaches on Jonah, who fled from God by ship from Joppa.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's first meditation on death in whaling, and the sermon on disobedience and the sea frames the voyage to come. The chapel's tablets make the danger concrete before the Pequod is even found.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Wikipedia's Moby-Dick and Seamen's Bethel articles say the Whaleman's Chapel is modelled on the Seamen's Bethel, which Melville attended before sailing on the Acushnet; the ship-bow pulpit was Melville's invention, and a replica was added to the chapel in 1961.
