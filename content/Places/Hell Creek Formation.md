# Hell Creek Formation

Late Cretaceous fossil beds in eastern [[Montana]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ a volunteer census there tested whether the dinosaurs were already dying out before the impact that ended them.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Using two hundred volunteers, he made a painstaking census of a well-defined, but also well-picked-over, area of the famous Hell Creek formation in Montana.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In the late 1980s Peter Sheehan of the Milwaukee Public Museum led two hundred volunteers over three years through a well-picked-over area of the formation, collecting every tooth, vertebra and chip of bone earlier diggers had missed. They more than tripled the global total of late-Cretaceous dinosaur fossils, and Sheehan concluded the dinosaurs were still numerous right up to the KT impact.
- **Why it matters:** It is the evidence Bryson uses against the idea that the dinosaurs were already fading out; the point depends on this being a famous, heavily searched site.
