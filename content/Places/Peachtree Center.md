# Peachtree Center

Office and retail complex linked by skyways in downtown [[Atlanta]]. In _Happy City_ its food court is where the author meets a commuter who walks about three hundred steps a day.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m841xnp7hzye52b19prfy84e)

> I was naive enough to ask that question of a fellow diner I met in the food court of the bunkerlike Peachtree Center in downtown Atlanta.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In the food court the author asks a fellow diner, Lucy, why people don't walk more. She drives about fifteen miles from Clayton County, pulls into a parking deck and follows a skyway to an elevator and her desk: about half an hour, maybe three hundred footsteps. Montgomery notes she had little alternative, since Clayton County had parked its whole bus fleet in 2010.
- **Why it matters:** The bunker-like complex is the book's picture of how dispersed design makes walking vanish from daily life.
