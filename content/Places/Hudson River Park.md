# Hudson River Park

Riverside park along the Hudson on Manhattan's West Side. A subject in “Humans of New York”: Riverside where a former jogger now picks up trash and finally sees the place she used to run past.

## Humans of New York — Humans of New York (2016)

Source: [Original](http://www.humansofnewyork.com/post/142356414341) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjrwtzd66aknz5jfj9hnq6)

> That first day they tried to send me to pick up trash along the Hudson. I said: ‘No, no, no. Anywhere but there.’ I used to jog along the Hudson; I was afraid some of my old friends would see me.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** A woman who lost her marriage and drinking-era life takes a trash-pickup job. She resists being sent to the Hudson because she used to jog there, is moved to Times Square, gets in trouble, and is told it is the Hudson or nowhere. After a couple of months there she says she now sees the greens, blues and yellows she never noticed while jogging.
- **Why it matters:** The Hudson stands for the life she was avoiding; working there rather than running past it changes how she sees the place and herself.

## Bicycle Diaries — David Byrne (2009)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14993423W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7641a4wwkr9eysmzxyyzdw9)

> The Hudson River Park’s bikeway on the west side of Manhattan is glorious—I use it to get uptown and downtown safely and quickly almost every day—but it’s right next to a highway, separated from the city as whole.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** In the introduction Byrne praises the bikeway as his near-daily route up and down Manhattan, but regrets that the highway beside it cuts the park off from the city, and wishes the road could be buried as Boston did with its freeway.
- **Why it matters:** The park is his home example of good cycling infrastructure that is still compromised by car-first planning.
