# Kalar River

Mountain river in Zabaykalsky Krai, eastern Siberia, flowing into the Vitim. In _Last of the Breed_ its valley is Joe Mack's first hideout, and he crosses it by canoe with the escapee Yakov.

## Last of the Breed — Louis L'Amour (1986)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL51048W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m6xdsbp5xaeesb1rgev8he1g)

> The stone pine was one of a considerable grove on a ridge overlooking the Kalar River.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Starving after about 150 miles, Joe Mack follows the valley where it narrows into a canyon and finds a shallow cave behind stone pine on a cliff. Over about five days he makes a sling and a bow, kills a blue fox and a mountain sheep and spears fish, until a motorboat of armed soldiers on the river drives him off. Later Yakov, a half-Lithuanian, half-Tungus escapee, leads him back to the Kalar ("The Kalar," he pointed) and paddles him across in a concealed canoe in under twenty minutes.
- **Why it matters:** The Kalar camp is where the hunted pilot turns into a hunter with Stone Age tools, the core of the novel; the crossing with Yakov gives him his first ally and the hint of a hidden community of exiles further east.
