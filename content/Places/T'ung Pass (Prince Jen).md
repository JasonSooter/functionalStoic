# T'ung Pass (Prince Jen)

The pass that leads Prince Jen's party out of a dry gorge into green woodland in Lloyd Alexander's fairy-tale China. It is pinned at the real Tong Pass (Tongguan) in [[Shaanxi]], the historic gateway east of Chang'an. It is the last landmark before the River Lo.

## The Remarkable Journey of Prince Jen — Lloyd Alexander (1991)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/319277/the-remarkable-journey-of-prince-jen-by-lloyd-alexander/9780142402252) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m887mrcsh6wkg1danckn7qqz)

> MASTER SHU, NEXT DAY, led the travelers through the T'ung Pass. Here, the rock-strewn course veered away. Jen found himself in the greenest woodlands and meadows he had ever seen.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** Master Shu takes the party off the long, roundabout road, down a cliff into the dry bed of a vanished river, where they abandon the horse and cart and cross boulder fields for weeks; there Jen and Voyaging Moon promise themselves to each other. The T'ung Pass ends the hardest stretch. Beyond it lie flowering woods full of birds, monkeys and gazelles, and then a merchants' road to the River Lo.
- **Why it matters:** The pass marks the change from ordeal to ease, just as Jen starts to dread reaching his goal and leaving Voyaging Moon. It is a threshold more than a setting.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The gorge and the woodland beyond are fictional.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Inferred. The text gives the pass's name and puts it "less than a week of easy travel" from the Lo. The real Tong Pass guarded the road from Chang'an east toward the Luo River and Luoyang, matching the book's sequence of borrowed names. No reference work confirms Alexander meant it. Alternative: a generic invented pass.
