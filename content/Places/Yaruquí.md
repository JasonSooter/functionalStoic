# Yaruquí

Parish town east of [[Quito]] in the Ecuadorian Andes. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is the start of the French Academy's 1735 meridian survey, a nine-and-a-half-year ordeal.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80ghh774e5k55w0pffv9vch)

> along a line reaching from Yarouqui, near Quito, to just beyond Cuenca

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** The 1735 expedition of Pierre Bouguer and Charles Marie de La Condamine sets out to measure a degree of meridian along a line from Yarouqui to just beyond Cuenca, choosing the Andes for the equator and the sightlines. In Quito a mob chases them out with stones, the expedition's doctor is murdered and the botanist goes mad; the survey drags on for nine and a half years, only for word to arrive that a second French team in Scandinavia has already settled the question.
- **Why it matters:** The mountains are both the reason for the site and the cause of the misery; Bryson uses the expedition as his great comic set piece about the price of early measurement.
