# Sarehole Mill

Sarehole Mill is an 18th-century watermill on the River Cole in Hall Green, Birmingham, near which Tolkien lived as a small boy. It is pinned for _The Hobbit_ as Tolkien's stated model for the Mill at Hobbiton.

## The Hobbit (Middle-Earth Universe) — J. R. R. Tolkien (1937)

Source: [[The Hobbit]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL27482W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m772vfrtwptr56p6rcpc6a)

> running as fast as his furry feet could carry him down the lane, past the great Mill, across The Water, and then on for a mile or more.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** On the morning after the unexpected party, Bilbo dashes out of Bag End without hat or pocket-handkerchief, down the lane, past the great Mill and across The Water, to reach the dwarves at Bywater just on the stroke of eleven. It is the last of the Shire he sees before the adventure starts.
- **Why it matters:** The Mill is only glimpsed in this book, but it is the clearest real-world anchor for Hobbiton: the rural Worcestershire edge of Birmingham that Tolkien knew as a child.
- **Real vs. fictional:** A real watermill, now a museum; Tolkien said it was his model for the Mill at Hobbiton.
- **Where is it?:** Wikipedia (Sarehole Mill) says Tolkien lived within 300 yards of the mill around the turn of the century, aged four to eight, when the area was rural Worcestershire, and that he said he used the mill as the Mill at Hobbiton (citing The Guardian, 28 December 1991). Wikipedia (The Shire) adds that Tolkien based the Shire's landscapes on Worcestershire and Warwickshire.
