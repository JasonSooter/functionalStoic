# Raqqa

City on the Euphrates in northern Syria. A setting in Jürgen Todenhöfer's “Into the heart of terror: behind Isis lines”: Isis-held base where the authors are confined and the masked driver's identity is suspected.

## Into the heart of terror: behind Isis lines — Jürgen Todenhöfer (2016)

Source: [Original](http://www.theguardian.com/world/2016/apr/04/behind-isis-lines-jurgen-todenhofer-journalist-syria-iraq-my-journey-into-the-heart-of-terror) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjrht076vzk8jphz60226a)

> Qatadah tells us we’re no longer allowed to go out and about in Raqqa. We have to stay in the apartment until we leave for Mosul.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** The authors reach Raqqa after a three-hour drive on Sunday 7 December 2014. They are confined to an apartment there while the masked driver and Qatadah refuse free reporting. The tense confrontations in which Frederic comes to suspect the driver is Jihadi John happen there.
- **Why it matters:** Raqqa is the first stop and the place where the visit turns from invitation to confinement. Elsewhere the argument would lose its sense of being held inside the Islamic State.
