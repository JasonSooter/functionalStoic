## The Old Man and the Sea — Ernest Hemingway (1952)

Source: [[The Old Man and the Sea]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL7265161M) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5gkzmfs2m3sr5bfardqe8q1)

> They sat on the Terrace and many of the fishermen made fun of the old man and he was not angry.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The fishermen sit on the Terrace and make fun of the old man, and he is not angry; the owner, Martin, sends him supper, and he and the boy drink beer and talk baseball there. After the voyage the boy fetches coffee from the Terrace, and a tourist party at the Terrace takes the marlin's skeleton for a shark's.
- **Why it matters:** The Terrace is the village's public face: it shows how the community sees Santiago before he sails and after he returns.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text names only 'the Terrace'. Wikipedia records that Hemingway frequented La Terraza restaurant in Cojímar; no reference work checked says it is the model. Chosen: La Terraza de Cojímar. Alternative: another harbour café in the same village.
