# Windy Point trap (Gentle Ben)

Fictional salmon fish trap and shore cabin on a brown-bear island in Prince William Sound, pinned on [[Hinchinbrook Island (Alaska)|Hinchinbrook Island]]. In _Gentle Ben_ it is where the Andersens spend their second summer, find Ben again and settle his fate.

## Gentle Ben — Walt Morey (1965)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL9347710M/Gentle_Ben) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8dcf34a7ak1tt5j5w825jkq)

> Far off to the left was the shape of the Windy Point trap and the shore cabin. A white seiner cruised several miles at sea. And far across that blue water, the St. Elias Range reared a necklace of white heads encircling the Sound.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In the first summer Fog Benson's pirates rob Six-Fathom Johnson's "Windy Point pile trap" an hour from Constantine Harbor, and Johnson asks Karl to buy it. After the _Far North_ is wrecked, Karl takes the job of trap watchman and the family moves into "a big two-room log cabin set back some hundred feet from the water", with the trap two hundred yards offshore. A mile down the beach is a spawning stream and "brown-bear meadow", where Ben has been living. Ben scatters Benson's gang when they raid the trap at night, then hangs around the cabin while Mud Hole Jones and his client, the lumber magnate Peter King, hunt him. King gets pinned under a rock below a shale mountain with a table-rock ledge, where Ben naps in the afternoons; Mark reaches him by a short cut and Ben rolls the rock away. King changes his mind and goes into partnership with Karl to buy the trap. The closing coda invites the reader to fly over the Windy Point trap and the meadow a mile beyond it.
- **Why it matters:** The climax, the rescue and the happy ending all happen here, and owning the trap is what lets the family stay on the Sound with Ben nearby. Set anywhere without brown bears, salmon traps and trap pirates, the second half of the book wouldn't work.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The trap, the cabin, the shale mountain and the meadow are fictional. The author's note says Morey worked as a fish trap inspector in Alaska.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Inferred. The trap is on "one of the biggest brown-bear islands in the Sound", a short float-plane hop from Orca City, with mountains behind the beach and the St. Elias Range across the water. The meadow and spawning stream a mile from the cabin are where Ben lives, and they are the meadow Jones flies over on "the seaward side of Hinchenbrook Island". In the first summer the trap is an hour from Constantine Harbor. The text never names the trap's island, and Karl doesn't suspect Ben is there because no boat could land a crate on that beach. Still, the shared meadow makes Hinchinbrook the likeliest island, and the pin is at its representative point. Reference works say nothing about the setting. The only real Windy Point in Alaska that OpenStreetMap lists near the area is a cape on the Kenai Peninsula, outside the Sound, so it doesn't fit.
