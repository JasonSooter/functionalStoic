## Patagonia’s Philosopher-King – The New Yorker — Nick Paumgarten (2016)

Source: [Original](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2016/09/19/patagonias-philosopher-king) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jy7wxhjeqgsm1adh716des)

> Our destination was the Bighorn River near Fort Smith, where it flows out of the Yellowtail Dam and north through the Crow Reservation, one of the biggest and poorest tribal territories in the country.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** The narrator and Chouinard drive ten and a half hours to the Bighorn, where Chouinard teaches a handful of Crow children tenkara fishing after a sage-burning ceremony. The text notes the river's trout abundance, the tribe's exclusion from the fishing economy, and Chouinard's views on guides and access laws.
- **Why it matters:** It is the article's closing scene, showing Chouinard's 'local level' activism and his belief that fishing leads people to care about water.
