# Hobbiton Movie Set

The Hobbiton Movie Set is the village of hobbit-holes built on the Alexander family's sheep farm near Matamata, New Zealand, now a tourist attraction. It is pinned for _The Hobbit_ because Peter Jackson's Hobbit films shot Hobbiton and Bag End there; it is not a place in the book.

## The Hobbit (Middle-Earth Universe) — J. R. R. Tolkien (1937)

Source: [[The Hobbit]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL27482W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m777qddbea4tthdfm580b5)

> It had a perfectly round door like a porthole, painted green, with a shiny yellow brass knob in the exact middle.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In the book, Bilbo's hobbit-hole has a perfectly round green door opening on a tunnel-like hall dug into The Hill. Gandalf marks the door, thirteen dwarves arrive for the unexpected party, and Bilbo is talked into the adventure; at the end he returns to find his home being auctioned.
- **Why it matters:** Bag End and Hobbiton frame the story as the comfortable home Bilbo leaves and returns to changed; the set is the best-known real place where that world was built.
- **Real vs. fictional:** This is where Peter Jackson's Hobbit films were shot; it is not a place in the book.
- **Where is it?:** Wikipedia (The Hobbit film series; Bag End; Hobbiton Movie Set) says the Hobbiton set on the Alexander farm near Matamata, first built for The Lord of the Rings, was renovated and re-used for filming The Hobbit.
