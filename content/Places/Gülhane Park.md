# Gülhane Park

Park below Topkapı Palace running down to a view of the Bosphorus, in [[Istanbul]], Turkey. It is where Bill Bryson ends Neither Here Nor There, with a Coke and a view of Asia.

## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74125W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5bekqjj6rca36bqfqdfh4v9)

> found myself presently at the entrance to a large and miraculously tranquil park called Gilhane, full of leafy shade, twittering birds, and happily ambling families.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** The final pages. The park's central avenue ends "in a sudden and arresting view of the Bosphorus". At an open-air taverna he orders a Coke and looks across to the white houses of Üsküdar and the ferries. "I had come to the end of my own road. That was Asia over there; this was as far as I could go in Europe." He is tempted to cross to Asia, but stays and watches the ferries, and the book ends.
- **Why it matters:** The book's last scene and the end of the trip; Bryson spells the park "Gilhane". Set anywhere else, the book would lose its closing image of Europe's edge.
