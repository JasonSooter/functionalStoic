# Mount Tambora

Volcano on the island of Sumbawa, [[Indonesia]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ its 1815 eruption opens the ice-age chapter and brings the year without summer.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8331e3ssxjaxxyxqxwavf7a)

> IN 1815 on the island of Sumbawa in Indonesia, a handsome and long-quiescent mountain named Tambora exploded spectacularly, killing a hundred thousand people with its blast and associated tsunamis.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In 1815 Tambora exploded, killing a hundred thousand people with its blast and tsunamis, the biggest eruption in ten thousand years. Its dust dimmed the sun: 1816 became the year without summer, crops failed, and a famine and typhoid epidemic followed in Ireland.
- **Why it matters:** Bryson's proof of how small a drop in temperature it takes to devastate the world, the opening of his argument about ice ages.
