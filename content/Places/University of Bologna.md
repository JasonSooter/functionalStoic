# University of Bologna

The university in [[Bologna]], founded in the eleventh century and among the oldest in the world, where Copernicus enrolled in 1497 and Carlo Rovelli studied in the seventies, as told in Rovelli's essay on Copernicus and Bologna.

## There Are Places in the World Where Rules Are Less Important Than Kindness — Carlo Rovelli (2020)
Source: [[There Are Places in the World Where Rules Are Less Important Than Kindness]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21681470W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5b44g5jk4caspgetwa30zhq)

> The university was occupied for several months by politically engaged students. I got involved with the friends of Radio Alice, the independent radio station that had become the voice of the student revolt.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The essay "Copernicus and Bologna" opens on 6 January 1497, when a young Pole paid nine _grossetti_ to enrol at the University of Bologna. Rovelli argues that Copernicus found two treasures in Italy: the books holding antiquity's knowledge (Ptolemy, Euclid) and a Renaissance spirit of revolt against received systems. He then says he found the same two things studying in Bologna in the seventies: Einstein and Dirac, assigned by his professor Guido Fano, and a generation that occupied the university and dreamed of remaking the world.
- **Why it matters:** The university is the essay's whole argument: a great university is a treasure-house of past knowledge and also a place where the courage to question it is nurtured. The parallel between Copernicus and Rovelli only works because both studied in the same place.
