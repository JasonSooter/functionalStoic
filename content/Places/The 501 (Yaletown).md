# The 501

Condominium tower at 501 Pacific Street on the edge of Yaletown, downtown [[Vancouver]]. In _Happy City_ it shows how a tower isolates its residents while the townhouses on its podium make neighbours of them.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83qjaksk852c64h67pk6qfh)

> Consider the experience of Rob McDowell, a diplomat who bought a condominium on the twenty-ninth floor of the 501, a hip, design-heavy tower in Vancouver’s Yale-town district.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Rob McDowell loves the view from his 29th-floor condo but feels isolated, sharing a hallway with 20 people and an elevator with nearly 300. He moves into one of the townhouses on the building's podium, which open onto a rooftop garden and volleyball court, and there he gets to know all his neighbours.
- **Why it matters:** The same building gives two opposite social lives, which makes the book's case that semi-private space and a manageable number of neighbours, not density itself, produce conviviality.
