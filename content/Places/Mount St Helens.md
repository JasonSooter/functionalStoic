# Mount St Helens

Volcano in the Cascade Range of [[Washington (state)|Washington]], USA. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ its 1980 eruption is the case study of a volcano everyone misread.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> On April 19 the northern flank of the mountain began to bulge conspicuously. Remarkably, no one in a position of responsibility saw that this strongly signaled a lateral blast.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** After two months of rumbling, with sightseers and helicopters around the summit, the north flank bulges from 19 April 1980, and nobody in charge reads it as a sign of a lateral blast; officials rely on models from Hawaii's gentler volcanoes, and only Jack Hyde warns of a catastrophic release. At 8:32 a.m. on 18 May the north side collapses in a 150-mph avalanche; 57 people die, including David Johnston, and the mountain loses 1,300 feet.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's proof that even well-watched volcanoes are poorly understood, setting up the Yellowstone chapter.
