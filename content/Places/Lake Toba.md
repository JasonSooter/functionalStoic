# Lake Toba

Crater lake in northern Sumatra, [[Indonesia]], left by a supervolcano. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is the last supervolcano eruption, which may have nearly wiped out humans.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m81592v0qcy1tdvrmpwhrkd9)

> The last supervolcano eruption on Earth was at Toba, in northern Sumatra, seventy-four thousand years ago.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Bryson names Toba as the site of the last supervolcano eruption on Earth, seventy-four thousand years ago; no one knows how big it was. Greenland ice cores show at least six years of volcanic winter afterwards, and the text says it may have reduced humans to a few thousand individuals.
- **Why it matters:** It is the precedent that makes a Yellowstone eruption more than a thought experiment.
