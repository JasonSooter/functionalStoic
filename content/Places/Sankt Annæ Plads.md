# Sankt Annæ Plads

Long tree-lined square in central [[Copenhagen]], near Amalienborg. In _The Spy and the Traitor_ it is where Gordievsky first passes KGB microfilm to MI6 in a brush contact.

## The Spy and the Traitor — Ben Macintyre (2018)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19749410W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7p6zv246pd8se04yqspwa4c)

> Sankt Annæ Plads is a long, tree-lined public square in the center of Copenhagen, not far from the Royal Palace.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** One spring day in 1977 Gordievsky passes a roll of microfilm to MI6's Geoffrey Guscott among the lunchtime crowds, covered by a "tourist" with a backpack, while PET makes sure no Danish surveillance is out. Guscott rushes to a nearby PET safe house to copy the film with a small device from MI6's technical department. Thirty-five minutes later the film goes back to Gordievsky at the other end of the square.
- **Why it matters:** It sets the pattern for the case's most productive method: getting documents out of the rezidentura and back before they are missed.
