# Seoni

Town and district in Madhya Pradesh, central India, the 'Seeonee' of Kipling's Mowgli stories. The wolf cave, the Council Rock and Messua's village of _The Jungle Book_ all lie in its hills.

## At a glance

## The Jungle Book — Rudyard Kipling (1894)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19870W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ne2q49z8s1zj5v2ksf0mx1)

> It was seven o’clock of a very warm evening in the Seeonee hills when Father Wolf woke up from his day’s rest, scratched himself, yawned, and spread out his paws one after the other to get rid of the sleepy feeling in their tips. Mother Wolf lay with her big gray nose dropped across her four tumbling, squealing cubs, and the moon shone into the mouth of the cave where they all lived.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** 'Mowgli's Brothers' opens at Father and Mother Wolf's cave in the Seeonee hills, where Shere Khan thrusts his head into the narrow mouth demanding the man-cub and Mother Wolf adopts Mowgli. At the Council Rock the Pack accepts him, with Baloo and Bagheera speaking for him; ten years later Mowgli drives Shere Khan off there with fire and leaves the jungle. In 'Tiger! Tiger!' the villagers of Messua's village pelt him with stones at its gate and he leaves the Man-Pack too.
- **Why it matters:** These hills are the home ground of the Seeonee Pack and the origin of the whole Mowgli story, the place of both his admission and his double exile.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Kipling took 'Seeonee' from the real town of Seoni (the Kipling Society notes he never visited it); the wolf cave, the Council Rock, the Cold Lairs and Messua's village are fictional.
