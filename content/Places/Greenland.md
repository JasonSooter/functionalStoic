# Greenland

Island and self-governing territory in the Kingdom of Denmark, between the North Atlantic and the Arctic. It is the whole subject of Helen Russell's open letter against buying it, and in _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ its ice cores reveal how violently Earth's climate has swung.

## Dear Donald, Re. Greenland — Helen Russell (2025)
Source: [[Bookshelf/Highlights & Notes/Articles/Dear Donald, Re. Greenland.md]] · [Original](https://mshelenrussell.substack.com/p/dear-donald-re-greenland) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5edfh64bdyc3sk9h5938jcw)

> Greenland isn’t an empty plot of land waiting for development: it’s a vast, ice-covered island.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The letter argues against the idea of buying Greenland, covering its self-rule since 2009, its colonial history under Denmark, its melting ice sheet and its 56,000 people.
- **Why it matters:** Greenland is the whole subject of the argument; the piece is about its status and sovereignty.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Thanks to ice cores from Greenland we have a detailed record of climate for something over a hundred thousand years, and what is found there is not comforting.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In the ice-age chapter, Greenland's ice cores give a detailed climate record of more than a hundred thousand years. They show that for most of its recent history Earth has lurched violently between warmth and brutal chill, including the thousand-year cold snap known as the Younger Dryas, rather than enjoying the stable climate civilization has known. Earlier, reports of the Greenland ice sheet help Agassiz's ice-age ideas win a following in America.
- **Why it matters:** The cores are Bryson's evidence that the climate is far less stable than we assume, the chapter's unsettling conclusion.
