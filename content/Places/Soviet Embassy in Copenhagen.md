# Soviet Embassy in Copenhagen

Soviet (now Russian) embassy on Kristianiagade in [[Copenhagen]]. In _The Spy and the Traitor_ it houses the KGB rezidentura where Gordievsky works and from which he sends his first signal to the West.

## The Spy and the Traitor — Ben Macintyre (2018)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19749410W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7p6zfthjjpet5ddhxr93wjj)

> The Soviet embassy consisted of three stucco villas on Kristianiagade in the northern part of the city, more like a grand gated hotel than a Soviet enclave, with immaculate sweeping gardens, a sports center, and a social club.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The Gordievskys arrive in January 1966; most of the embassy's staff are spies, and Yelena works in a listening post tapping the radio of PET, Danish security. After the 1968 invasion of Czechoslovakia he phones Yelena from the lobby and swears at the Soviet Union, knowing the line is bugged, a deliberate signal that goes unnoticed. He returns in 1972 as a political-intelligence officer.
- **Why it matters:** Copenhagen's embassy is where he works while falling for the West. Its closeness to the British and American embassies, back to back across a graveyard, shapes MI6's approach.
