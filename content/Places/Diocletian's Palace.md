# Diocletian's Palace

The Roman emperor's palace whose walls hold the old town of [[Split]], Croatia, which Bill Bryson praises in _Neither Here Nor There_ as a palace turned into a town by squatters.

## Neither Here Nor There — Bill Bryson (1991)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74125W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5beke5xm03fqhjj4qy442g6)

> I wandered along the quayside looking at the sailboats, then ventured into the sun-warmed lanes and courtyards that form the heart of Split.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 20 (Yugoslavia). He explains that this quarter, "roughly a quarter of a mile square, was the palace of Diocletian". After Rome fell, squatters built houses inside the walls: "corridors became streets. Courtyards and atriums evolved into public squares." Some lanes are so narrow you turn sideways to pass.
- **Why it matters:** The clearest affirmation of a place's character in the Yugoslav chapter, which otherwise runs on comic mishaps (a plank over a ditch, the wrong flat) around his lodgings.
