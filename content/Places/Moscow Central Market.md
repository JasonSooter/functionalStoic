# Moscow Central Market

Covered market in central [[Moscow]], just off the Garden Ring. In _The Spy and the Traitor_ its clock is the monthly meeting point where MI6 waits in case Gordievsky needs to make contact.

## The Spy and the Traitor — Ben Macintyre (2018)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19749410W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7rsbfda0bacbk8m9wh2arn0)

> At 11 a.m., on the third Saturday of every month, MI6 would send an officer to loiter under the clock in Moscow’s Central Market just off the Garden Ring Road, a bustling spot where a foreigner would not look out of place.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The market is the passive contact point of the plan for Gordievsky's return to Moscow: an officer in gray, carrying a Harrods bag, stands under the clock so that Gordievsky can see that MI6 is still looking out for him, or signal for a brush contact. Officers keep the vigil month after month, pretending to shop; one recalls buying "one winter tomato, probably the only one in Moscow". Gordievsky never comes. On Saturday, June 15, 1985, under KGB suspicion, Gordievsky himself takes up his post "beneath the clock" with a Safeway bag to signal that he needs to pass a message; he sees no Harrods bag, and the MI6 team never picks up the signal.
- **Why it matters:** A minor but telling setting: it shows how much patient, fruitless routine sat behind the case while Gordievsky was out of reach.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real. ⚠️ The pin is today's Central Market building; whether it occupies exactly the old market's site has not been checked.
