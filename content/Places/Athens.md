# Athens

Capital of Greece. In Jason's reading it is the finish line of Pheidippides' run, the city that shaped Plato, and the city the Persian army occupies and burns while Xerxes hears the story told in _Gates of Fire_.

## Financial Independence is Not a Marathon — MaggieBanks (2016)
Source: [Original](https://northernexpenditure.com/financial-independence-not-marathon-sprint/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jvp3xrfjtwfbf9jtzf08hq)

> He got there, proclaimed: “We were victorious!” And then he died.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the article:** Athens is the end of Pheidippides' run and the place where he dies; the author later says each financial step 'gets you closer to Athens', reusing it as the finish line of her analogy.
- **Why it matters:** Destination of the legendary run, used as a metaphor target.

## A Primer on Plato: His Life, Works, and Philosophy — Brett and Kate McKay (2016)
Source: [Original](https://www.artofmanliness.com/2016/07/10/a-primer-on-plato-his-life-works-and-philosophy/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jx6yhs4dj36skyv04jnx4e)

> Plato not only grew up witnessing the conflict between these two city-states, but also had a front row seat to the strife that roiled the citizens in his own backyard.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** Plato is born into plague-stricken, war-torn Athens, whose oligarchic coup, the Thirty Tyrants and the restored democracy that executes Socrates in 399 BC turn him from politics toward philosophy. Socrates and young followers talk in the agora and near the gymnasium.
- **Why it matters:** The city's turmoil is presented as the formative cause of Plato's rejection of democracy and his philosophy.

## Gates of Fire — Steven Pressfield (1998)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/133742/gates-of-fire-by-steven-pressfield/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m89v2p2kvx6h7c3twzbhnjwq)

> The advance force of Persian infantry and cavalry, fifty thousand strong, had entered Athens and taken possession of the city.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** In the frame story, Xerxes's council hears that the Athenians have abandoned the city and crossed the strait to Troezen and Salamis, where they huddle as refugees on the hillsides. A small band holding the Acropolis, trusting the oracle of the "wooden wall", is shot down by imperial archers. The Empire's fires now burn on the Acropolis, and the king plans to enter the city next day and raze its temples, with the smoke visible to the refugees across the strait. Xeones also mentions a recent embassy to Athens with Dienekes, during which he crossed the evacuating city at night to look for his cousin Diomache at [[Phaleron]]. Six weeks after Thermopylae the imperial army, drawn up inside the western walls, puts temples, houses, gymnasia and warehouses to the torch; the sight sends the recovering Xeones into a relapse, and he keeps asking whether the Phaleron sanctuary survived.
- **Why it matters:** The fall of Athens sets the stakes of the frame: the pass has been lost, and Xerxes is weighing whether to push on into the Peloponnese. It is reported, not seen. The book's action happens elsewhere, so the city is backdrop rather than setting.
