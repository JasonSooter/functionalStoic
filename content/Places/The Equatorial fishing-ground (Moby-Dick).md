# The Equatorial fishing-ground (Moby-Dick)

The Equatorial fishing-ground is the stretch of the equatorial Pacific where Ahab finally meets Moby Dick. In _Moby-Dick_ it is the site of the three-day chase and the sinking of the Pequod, pinned here at the real sinking site of the whaleship Essex.

## Moby-Dick — Herman Melville (1851)

Source: [[Exploration of Moby Dick]] · [[Moby Dick]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m754spt33ayrjkkaqvehaq)

> now, that he found himself hard by the very latitude and longitude where his tormenting wound had been inflicted

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In Chapter 44 Ahab's charts fix on the 'Season-on-the-Line', the time and equatorial waters where Moby Dick had been seen for years and where Ahab was maimed. Steering south-east from Japan toward the Equator, the ship passes rocky islets where seals wail like ghosts, and the first lookout is lost 'on the White Whale’s own peculiar ground'. After meeting the Rachel and the Delight, Ahab finds himself near the latitude and longitude of his old wound, and in Chapters 133 to 135 Moby Dick is chased for three days: the Parsee drowns, the whale rams and sinks the Pequod, and Ahab is dragged under by his own line. In the Epilogue Ishmael floats on Queequeg's coffin until the Rachel picks him up.
- **Why it matters:** It is the climax of the book and the end of every character but Ishmael. The text keeps it an unnamed stretch of tropical sea, so the place is defined by Ahab's wound rather than by any landmark.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text gives no position: the ship holds 'south-eastward by Ahab’s levelled steel' toward the Equator, 'sideways impelled by unvarying trade winds', to the 'Equatorial fishing-ground'; Chapter 44 says Ahab must 'arrive in the equatorial Pacific', and the chase is 'hard by the very latitude and longitude' of Ahab's wound. Wikipedia's Moby-Dick article says the ending is based on the sinking of the Nantucket whaleship Essex, which the novel itself recounts in Chapter 45; the Essex sank about 0°41′S 118°W. Pinned at the Essex site as a best guess; the text's route south-east from the Japanese ground could equally put it in the central equatorial Pacific further west.
