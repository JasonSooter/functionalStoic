# Piazza del Campo

Piazza del Campo is the main square of Siena, Italy. It turns up in Walkable City (Jeff Speck): An illustration for the edge-effect argument.

## Walkable City — Jeff Speck (2012)
Source: [[Walkable City]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL36883695M/Walkable_City) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbnvd08tq7d6qbbdqvg81p)

> He photographs Siena’s famous Piazza del Campo, where the main pavement is surrounded by chunky man-size bollards, each one having accumulated the appendage of a standing or sitting man.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Jan Gehl's photograph of the Campo illustrates the 'edge effect': people linger along the edges, tethered to bollards. Speck uses it to argue that porous, deep building fronts attract lingerers.
- **Why it matters:** An illustration for the edge-effect argument. It is a single paragraph, so its role is minor.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m841x870eazex0byt38pcf56)

> Take the Piazza del Campo, that glorious gathering place at the heart of Siena.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** The Campo radiates uphill from the Palazzo Pubblico to a semicircular promenade, with no cars, café-lined edges, chest-high bollards made for leaning, and a sloping brick floor where tourists sit watching the Torre del Mangia's shadow; families stroll by the Fonte Gaia after sunset. Jan Gehl studies it and suspects its geometry, not Italian temperament, is what draws people together and makes them linger.
- **Why it matters:** It is where Gehl's conviction that design shapes behaviour begins; he takes the lesson home to Copenhagen's Strøget.
