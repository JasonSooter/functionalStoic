# Mark Twain Cave

Limestone cave south of Hannibal, Missouri. It is the documented model for McDougal's Cave in Tom Sawyer.

## The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, Complete — Mark Twain (1876)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL53919W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k7he2ch4kfpj683wjxm1bx)

> The mouth of the cave was up the hillside—an opening shaped like a letter A. Its massive oaken door stood unbarred.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** McDougal's Cave lies three miles below town. Tom and Becky get lost in its passages during the Thatcher picnic, Injun Joe is found dead behind its sealed door, and Tom and Huck later recover the treasure from a chamber under a cross marked in candle-smoke.
- **Why it matters:** Introduced here as a vast labyrinth that no one fully knows, it is set up as the arena for the story's later climax, though that falls beyond this range.
- **Real vs. fictional:** McDougal's Cave is fictional; reference works identify it with the cave south of Hannibal now called Mark Twain Cave.
