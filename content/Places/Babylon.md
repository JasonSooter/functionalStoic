# Babylon

Ancient Mesopotamian capital on the Euphrates in present-day Iraq. A Farnam Street essay uses Hammurabi's Code, made there, to argue that builders should carry the risk of what they build.

## The Code of Hammurabi: The Best Rule To Manage Risk — Farnam Street (2017)

Source: [Original](https://www.farnamstreetblog.com/2017/11/hammurabis-code/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5k7bqccncm3ezx7d6fpjjyf)

> Almost 4,000 years ago, King Hammurabi of Babylon, Mesopotamia, laid out one of the first sets of laws.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Hammurabi ruled Babylon from 1792 BC for 43 years, expanding a modest kingdom by conquest and then settling down to rule. The piece examines his Code, especially its construction laws that tied builders' lives to the safety of the houses they built. It argues that this ancient system aligned incentives and margins of safety better than modern ones.
- **Why it matters:** The ancient city-state and its laws are the subject of the whole argument about incentives and risk.

## Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind — Yuval Noah Harari (2014)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17075811W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m75mess3s8m29k66zd839v)

> In 1776 BC Babylon was the world’s biggest city.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Babylon, the world's biggest city in 1776 BC, ruled an empire of over a million subjects under Hammurabi. His Code of about 300 judgements ranked superior people, commoners and slaves, and Harari sets it beside the American Declaration of Independence of 1776 AD as two rival imagined orders.
- **Why it matters:** It anchors the argument that cooperation among millions rests on shared myths, here a frankly hierarchical one.
