# Booneville (Stoner)

The village near William Stoner's family farm in central Missouri, most likely the real river town of Boonville. In _Stoner_ the bleak farm outside it is the life Stoner leaves for the University, and the place he returns to only to bury his parents.

## Stoner — John Williams (1965)

Source: [Original](https://www.nyrb.com/products/stoner) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8afp5rym84tsssw0ex76w7a)

> He was born in 1891 on a small farm in central Missouri near the village of Booneville, some forty miles from Columbia, the home of the University.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Stoner grows up doing chores on his parents' small, arid farm, in a crude square house whose "unpainted timbers sagged around the porch and doors." At the kitchen table his father proposes the new College of Agriculture; William "had never been farther from home than Booneville, fifteen miles away." In 1910 his parents drive him to Booneville in the mule-drawn wagon and he walks the rest of the way to Columbia. His parents drive overnight from the farm to his graduation in 1914, and on a visit that summer he finds he has nothing to say to them. Seventeen years after he left, word of his father's death sends him by bus back along the highway, now "wide and paved"; "Booneville had changed little," still looking "as if it were only a temporary arrangement." "They buried his father in a small plot on the outskirts of Booneville," and later his mother beside him; standing in that "bare, treeless little plot" he looks across the flat land toward the farm. Early in 1929 he sells the farm for "a little over two thousand dollars."
- **Why it matters:** The farm is the hard, silent life the novel measures Stoner against. Leaving it for books is the first choice of his life, and its land and his parents' endurance shape how he bears everything that follows; the funerals and the sale close that life for good.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The text spells the village "Booneville"; the real town is Boonville, Missouri.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text gives a village called Booneville in "central Missouri", "some forty miles from Columbia", fifteen miles from the farm, and says "The highway from Columbia to Kansas City, which cut through Booneville," was the road he first took to the University. The real Boonville, on the Missouri River west of Columbia and on that Columbia–Kansas City corridor, matches the name, region and road, though by road it is somewhat closer to Columbia than forty miles. No reference work found names the model. The alternative is that Williams invented the village outright. The farm itself is unplaced.
