# University of Strathclyde

University in central [[Glasgow]], Scotland, successor to Anderson's. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ the janitor James Croll teaches himself physics in its library and links orbits to ice ages.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8331k5pm83d14zhrxjzv5w5)

> He worked at a variety of jobs—as a carpenter, insurance salesman, keeper of a temperance hotel—before taking a position as a janitor at Anderson’s (now the University of Strathclyde) in Glasgow.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** James Croll, after work as a carpenter, insurance salesman and temperance-hotel keeper, became a janitor at Anderson's, now the University of Strathclyde. With his brother doing much of his work, he spent quiet evenings in the library teaching himself physics, mechanics and astronomy, and produced the papers on orbital variation and ice ages that won him recognition.
- **Why it matters:** The unlikely birthplace of the astronomical theory of ice ages, one of Bryson's favourite self-taught heroes.
