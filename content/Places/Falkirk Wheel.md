# Falkirk Wheel

Rotating boat lift near Falkirk, [[Scotland]], linking the [[Forth and Clyde Canal]] and the [[Union Canal (Scotland)|Union Canal]]. It is the subject of Wesley Crump's engineering article and a passing example in a sketch about how locks work.

## The Hidden Engineering Behind the Falkirk Wheel — Wesley Crump (2025)
Source: [[The Hidden Engineering Behind the Falkirk Wheel]] · [Original](https://practical.engineering/blog/2025/11/17/the-hidden-engineering-behind-the-falkirk-wheel) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5crd7ygahmgt8w9bgdrdgvv)

> The Falkirk Wheel opened for traffic in May 2002, and now, more than two decades later, it’s pretty clear that they nailed the idea.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** The article explains how the rotary lift replaced a flight of 11 locks, opened in May 2002 as the centrepiece of the Millennium Link, and keeps its gondolas balanced and level while it moves boats between the canals in minutes.
- **Why it matters:** It is the subject of the whole piece; without it the canals would still need the day-long lock staircase.

## Grandma's Rule: First___, Then___ — Sketchplanations (2025)
Source: [Original](https://sketchplanations.com/grandmas-rule) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dcmhs6vmavmywg6jte1rhb)

> For a single, remarkable lock, have a look at Falkirk Wheel lock in Scotland.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the article:** After a canal-boat trip on the [[Regent's Canal]], the author explains how locks work and lists examples; the Falkirk Wheel is described as the one and only rotating boat lift, replacing 11 locks with a two-minute rotation.
- **Why it matters:** A minor example in a list, alongside [[Caen Hill Locks]].
