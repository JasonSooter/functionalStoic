# Coney Island

Seaside amusement district at the southern end of [[Brooklyn]], New York City, remembered in Another Brooklyn as the family's Saturday outing with their father.

## Another Brooklyn — Jacqueline Woodson (2016)
Source: [[Another Brooklyn]] · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5dcmxecwsbrkjmk62cyrjw1)

> On Saturdays, my father took us to Coney Island, the three of us riding the double L train to the F train to the last stop.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** On Saturdays the father takes August and her brother by train to the end of the line; they watch the Wonder Wheel, the Parachute, the Cyclone and the ocean, fear the addicts and hawkers on the boardwalk, and can afford only a few rides and a hot dog.
- **Why it matters:** A recurring family outing that shows the family's poverty and the father's uncertainty, later remembered as long behind them.

## The Anxious Generation — Jonathan Haidt (2024)
Source: [[The Anxious Generation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL37575384W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kk29xqaspbfp58n40xkccy)

> These ultrasafe structures were entertaining when my kids were three or four, but by age 6 they wanted bigger thrills, which they found at Coney Island.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Haidt's children found New York City's ultrasafe playgrounds fun at three or four, but by six they wanted bigger thrills, which they found on the Coney Island rides; the drive there was full of talk about which scary ride each would try.
- **Why it matters:** A small example of the book's claim that children seek out risk and thrill.
