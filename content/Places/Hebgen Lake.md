# Hebgen Lake

Reservoir in south-west [[Montana]], just outside Yellowstone. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is the scene of the 1959 earthquake that buried campers under a fallen mountainside.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Hebgen Lake suffered a catastrophic quake. It was magnitude 7.5, not vast as earthquakes go, but so abrupt and wrenching that it collapsed an entire mountainside.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Just before midnight on 17 August 1959 a magnitude 7.5 earthquake strikes at Hebgen Lake and collapses an entire mountainside: some 80 million tons of rock fall and run up the opposite slope, burying part of the Rock Creek Campground. Twenty-eight campers die, nineteen of them never found; three brothers in one tent are spared while their parents in the next are swept away.
- **Why it matters:** It shows that the Yellowstone region's danger is not only the distant supervolcano but sudden quakes that strike without warning; the chapter's argument about unpredictability rests on it.
