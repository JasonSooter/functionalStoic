# Raymor-Playmor Ballroom

Former dance hall in Boston, two connected ballrooms. In _Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!_ the MIT student Feynman goes to its open Tuesday dances and ends up at a dance for the deaf.

## "Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!": Adventures of a Curious Character — Richard P. Feynman (1985)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL514629W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kfpy7mx1cexwwmccx7xvwp)

> I liked to go to a place called the Raymor and Playmore Ballroom—two ballrooms that were connected together—on Tuesday nights.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** As an MIT junior Feynman goes to the open Tuesday-night dances at "the Raymor and Playmore Ballroom—two ballrooms that were connected together", although his fraternity brothers disapprove. He dances with a hard-of-hearing girl and goes with her and a friend by taxi to a hotel where a dance for the deaf is being held, then to a cafeteria; he picks up a few signs and compares it to being in a foreign country.
- **Why it matters:** The ballroom shows his indifference to social rank, which the book sets against the fraternity's taste for dances with girls met "properly". Minor as a place: any open dance hall would serve.
