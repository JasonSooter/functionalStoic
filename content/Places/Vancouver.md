# Vancouver

City in British Columbia, Canada. It is Brian Scudamore's thinking-day city, and in _Happy City_ both the place where Charles Montgomery's quest begins and his main test of whether tower-built density makes people happy.

## Why Successful People Spend 10 Hours A Week Just Thinking — Brian Scudamore (2016)
Source: [Original](https://medium.com/@BrianScudamore/why-successful-people-spend-10-hours-a-week-just-thinking-79a5b16eb404) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jkzfjq9s4vzm0q7n0xtdz1)

> I often spend the day wandering around Vancouver.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** The author spends his Monday thinking day away from the office, wandering Vancouver. He moves between coffee shops, forest, bike rides, the beach and park benches whenever he feels stuck.
- **Why it matters:** The city is where his method plays out, but nothing specific to Vancouver is argued; any walkable city would do.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83dc29ba9ddp64pd30kc90g)

> The delegates met in a harborside convention center in Vancouver to figure out how to save the world’s exploding cities from disaster.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** At the World Urban Forum in 2006, mayors and officials meet in a harbourside convention centre to face a bleak forecast: cities producing most of the world's pollution and emissions while bearing most of the cost of climate change. The mood changes when Enrique Peñalosa takes the podium and tells the Bogotá story to loud cheers, which Montgomery says stayed with him through the years he spent on the book. The same year he buys a share of a fixer-upper on the city's working-class East Side and remortgages to enlarge it, sure it will make him happier. Later chapters return to the downtown peninsula, where more than 150 residential towers have risen since the late 1980s: planners kept view corridors to the mountains, low podiums and wide tower spacing, and traded height for public benefits, yet surveys find downtown residents less satisfied and tower dwellers lonelier.
- **Why it matters:** It is where the book's quest begins, and the city whose celebrated density is weighed most closely against the social life it delivers.
