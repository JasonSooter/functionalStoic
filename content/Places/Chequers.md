# Chequers

Country house of the British prime minister in Buckinghamshire. Attenborough plays tennis there with Anthony Eden in _Life on Air_, and in _The Spy and the Traitor_ it hosts the Thatcher–Gorbachev talks of 1984.

## Life on Air — David Attenborough (2002)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL24547776M/Life_on_air) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5hx2v6h5he141jhty9t3z3m)

> As we drew up in front of Chequers, Philip de Zulueta came out to greet me.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Attenborough is invited for tennis with Eden. His case is unpacked by a footman who removes his tennis clothes to iron them, so he plays in Philip de Zulueta's oversized borrowed kit, and then dines with the Prime Minister, where the Amboise/Arbois wine mistake is made.
- **Why it matters:** A full comic scene set at the Prime Minister's country house.

## The Spy and the Traitor — Ben Macintyre (2018)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19749410W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7vbqk76e2nrpqdyr5mbqzac)

> the visit was in essence an extended diplomatic démarche, as the Cold War adversaries cautiously sounded each other out in a series of meetings at Chequers, the prime minister’s country residence.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Mikhail Gorbachev's eight-day visit to Britain in December 1984 centres on meetings with Thatcher at Chequers. Gorbachev demands a nightly KGB memorandum forecasting the next day's talks; MI6 passes Geoffrey Howe's Foreign Office briefing to Gordievsky, who types it up in Russian, so that it reaches Gorbachev "verbatim". The visit is judged a resounding success.
- **Why it matters:** The house is where the book's argument plays out: a spy feeding both sides the same script helps two adversaries find common ground. The text dwells on the briefings more than on the house itself.
