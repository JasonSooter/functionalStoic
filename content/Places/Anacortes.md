# Anacortes

Port town on Fidalgo Island in [[Washington (state)|Washington]], facing Guemes Island across the Guemes Channel. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is where stroke Don Hume learns to row in a salvaged boat.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m878tds07hz4531p5v5mavrx)

> Walking the cobbled beach on the channel between Anacortes and Guemes Island one day, he came across an abandoned and dilapidated thirteen-foot clinker-built rowboat.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Hume comes from Anacortes, a gritty lumber and fish-canning port fifty miles north of Seattle. After the crash his father moves to Olympia for work and Don stays behind, working in a lumber mill. He finds an abandoned thirteen-foot clinker-built rowboat on the beach of the channel to Guemes Island, repairs it, and rows the channel and among the San Juan Islands; when the mill job ends he rows nearly a hundred miles in six days to join his parents in Olympia.
- **Why it matters:** It is the origin of the steady, untiring stroke that sets the rhythm of the Olympic boat.
