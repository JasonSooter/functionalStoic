# Neander Valley

Valley of the Düssel near Düsseldorf, [[Germany]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ quarrymen find bones here in 1856 that give the Neandertals their name.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8332re7wmy9swnc4sr1k3j8)

> So instead the name and credit for the discovery of the first early humans went to the Neander Valley in Germany

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In 1856 workmen at a quarry in a cliff face overlooking the Düssel River found curious-looking bones and passed them to the local schoolteacher, Johann Karl Fuhlrott, who saw that he had some new type of human. Many refused to accept the bones were ancient: the Bonn professor August Mayer insisted they belonged to a wounded Mongolian Cossack who had crawled into the cave to die in 1814.
- **Why it matters:** The find that named the first known early humans, and an example of how fiercely experts resisted ancient humans.
