# Cupertino

City in Silicon Valley, [[California]], Jenny Odell's hometown, which she describes in How to Do Nothing as placeless sprawl until attention revealed what was already there.

## How to Do Nothing — Jenny Odell (2019)
Source: [[How to Do Nothing — Reader's Companion]] · [[How to Do Nothing]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20078135W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5e31vp911n27b0yg0b66gt0)

> There’s a funny detail about Cupertino that I discovered as a teenager. Growing up there in the early 2000s, there wasn’t much to do except visit one shopping center after another in what I experienced as a mind-numbing sprawl with no obvious center.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The author describes her hometown as featureless sprawl of office parks and malls; Cupertino Crossroads sat at an intersection of two six-lane roads and replaced an old crossroads with a post office, general store and blacksmith, of which no sign remained. Vallco Fashion Park, a defunct mall, is the shared bond among those who grew up there. She says she later saw it 'all wrong'.
- **Why it matters:** It opens the chapter on attention: the place seemed placeless for lack of context, setting up the argument that attention reveals what is already there.

## Quiet — Susan Cain (2012)
Source: [[Quiet]] · [Original](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/55983/quiet-by-cain-susan/9780141970172) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5g8wpcf1n0fxc84qmchv6v4)

> At first blush, Cupertino seems like the embodiment of the American Dream.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Cain visits the Silicon Valley town to see how a heavily Asian-American community that prizes study over sociability treats introverts. Its library, high school and robotics culture frame the chapter.
- **Why it matters:** The town is the chapter's central case of a place partly shielded from the Extrovert Ideal.
