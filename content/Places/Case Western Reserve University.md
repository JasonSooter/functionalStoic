# Case Western Reserve University

University in [[Cleveland]], Ohio, formerly the Case School of Applied Science. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it is where Michelson and Morley searched for the ether in the 1880s.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80gjex2m0vwrrk9hfv0qxm1)

> There, in the 1880s, a physicist of early middle years named Albert Michelson, assisted by his friend the chemist Edward Morley, embarked on a series of experiments

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Bryson makes a "slight (but relevant!) detour" to Cleveland and the Case School of Applied Science. With an instrument funded by Alexander Graham Bell, Michelson and Morley try to measure the Earth's motion through the ether, and after years of exhausting work find that light's speed is the same in every direction and season.
- **Why it matters:** The null result is the crack that Einstein's relativity fills, which is why the chapter detours to Cleveland at all.
