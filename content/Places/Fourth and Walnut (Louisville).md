# Fourth and Walnut (Louisville)

Downtown street corner in [[Louisville]], United States, where Thomas Merton had his epiphany of loving the strangers around him, as recounted in Jenny Odell's How to Do Nothing.

## How to Do Nothing — Jenny Odell (2019)

Source: [[How to Do Nothing — Reader's Companion]] · [[How to Do Nothing]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20078135W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5e31n3hawq8qcv20zpmra8m)

> In Louisville, at the corner of Fourth and Walnut, in the center of the shopping district, I was suddenly overwhelmed with the realization that I loved all these people, that they were mine and I theirs

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Thomas Merton, accompanying a fellow clergyman on a trip to Louisville, has an epiphany at this downtown corner: he loves the strangers around him and wakes from the dream of separateness and renunciation. He afterwards disowns The Seven Storey Mountain and begins publishing on social issues.
- **Why it matters:** The corner is the hinge between contempt for the world and participation in it, which anchors the chapter's 'how, not whether' argument and leads to 'standing apart'. A crowded shopping district is the point: the revelation needs ordinary strangers.
