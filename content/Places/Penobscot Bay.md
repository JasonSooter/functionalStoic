# Penobscot Bay

Large bay on the coast of Maine. In _Nathaniel's Nutmeg_ it is Henry Hudson's first American landfall in 1609, where his crew loots a native village.

## Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Giles Milton (1999)

Source: [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg — Reader's Companion]] · [[Nathaniel's Nutmeg]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1970050W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8efcfx3122jfm71xa2d8h94)

> They dropped anchor in Penobscot Bay, some one hundred miles west of Nova Scotia.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** Sailing for the Dutch East India Company, Hudson turns the Half Moon west across the Atlantic after the ice blocks him in the north, and makes landfall here in July 1609. The first boats of local people come out "seeming glad of our coming", but the crew then row ashore with "two stone pieces or murderers", drive the "savages" from their houses and "took the spoil of them". Milton uses Robert Juet's journal to contrast Hudson's own restraint with his crew's distrust and violence.
- **Why it matters:** It is the first American stop on the voyage that leads to the Hudson River and [[Manhattan]], and it sets the pattern of violence that recurs upriver.
