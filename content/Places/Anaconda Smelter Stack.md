# Anaconda Smelter Stack

Tall brick stack, the surviving part of the Anaconda Copper smelter at Anaconda, Montana. From the ridges above, Ryback holds the smelter up as an image of land wrecked by industry.

## The Ultimate Journey - Canada to Mexico Down the Continental Divide — Eric Ryback (1973)
Source: [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8bervwqxdh6wdm254c1pkkb)

> And on the following day, we had looked out over the Anaconda Copper Smelter. That massive concrete beast, squatting atop mountains of black refuse and belching out clouds as large as those in the sky, controlled the lives of thousands of people and affected those of millions more.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** Eric routes the trek along the Divide in a wide arc around Butte rather than the Forest Service's easier trail. From the ridges he looks out over the Anaconda Copper Smelter, "that massive concrete beast" squatting on black refuse and belching clouds, controlling thousands of lives. The sight sharpens his longing to be back in the mountains, which they reach at Storm Lake.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's counterpoint to the wilderness, and Eric recalls it later when he passes the Climax molybdenum mine in Colorado.
