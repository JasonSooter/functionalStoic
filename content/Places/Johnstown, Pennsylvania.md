## Range: Why Generalists Triumph in a Specialized World — David Epstein (2019)
Source: [[Range]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/550188/range-by-david-epstein/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jkzny0npp41sspa6p4pdr1)

> Hesselbein adored Johnstown’s rich diversity, but it afforded some ugly lessons.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** Frances Hesselbein grew up among steel mills and coal mines in western Pennsylvania and stayed in Johnstown through her marriage, her husband's photography studio and the Human Relations Commission cases. There she took on Troop 17, chaired the United Way campaign and became Girl Scout council director before being called to New York in 1976.
- **Why it matters:** Johnstown is where Hesselbein's unplanned roles accumulated, and she later credits its diversity with teaching her the power of inclusion and exclusion. It is a small-town backdrop for the chapter's point that careers form by short-term matching.
