# East Hampton, New York

Town on the South Fork of Long Island, New York. A turning point in Neal Gabler's “The Secret Shame of Middle-Class Americans Living Paycheck to Paycheck”: Where the Gablers moved to cut costs, rented then bought a house; 'full-time like the poor people'.

## The Secret Shame of Middle-Class Americans Living Paycheck to Paycheck — Neal Gabler (2016)

Source: [Original](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2016/05/my-secret-shame/476415/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjqwzyacby4f7arqxqs1wk)

> Still, we moved to the tip of Long Island, in East Hampton, where we wouldn’t have to pay that exorbitant private-school tuition and where my wife could eventually quit her job as a film executive to be with the children, the loss of her income offset a little by not having to pay for child care.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the article:** The author and his wife move to East Hampton to avoid private-school tuition and child-care costs. They rent a house and later put together a down payment to buy it after a film-rights sale. The mortgage there is later part of the debt he describes.
- **Why it matters:** The move is a decision made to ease money pressure that instead begins the home-ownership debt; the contrast with the Hamptons image of wealth is the author's own point.
