# University of Missouri

University in Columbia, Missouri. It is the world of _Stoner_, where a farm boy becomes a lifelong English teacher, and a sketch of 1930s barriers for women scientists in a Brain Pickings piece on Barbara McClintock.

## An Illustrated Celebration of Trailblazing Women in Science — Maria Popova (2016)
Source: [Original](https://www.brainpickings.org/2016/07/28/women-in-science-rachel-ignotofsky/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jx89bmwqkwxdkqst5tk2g5)

> That was the attitude in the 1930s, anyway; when Barbara McClintock wore slacks at the University of Missouri, it was considered scandalous.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the article:** Quoting Ignotofsky, the piece recounts Barbara McClintock being seen as bold and unladylike at the University of Missouri. The faculty excluded her from meetings, offered little support, and would fire her if she married, so she left to find a better job.
- **Why it matters:** The university stands for institutional sexism in science and is the place that prompted McClintock's career-defining departure.

## Stoner — John Williams (1965)
Source: [Original](https://www.nyrb.com/products/stoner) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8afp77jc1vejgbcs9zdtj9b)

> The red brick buildings stretched upward from a broad field of green that was broken by stone walks and small patches of garden.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** "William Stoner entered the University of Missouri as a freshman in the year 1910"; eight years later he takes his doctorate and an instructorship there. Arriving on foot at dusk, he feels before the red brick buildings "a sudden sense of security and serenity he had never felt before." Enrolled in agriculture, he is undone in a sophomore survey when Archer Sloane reads Shakespeare's seventy-third sonnet aloud and asks what it means. He drifts through the library stacks, "inhaling the musty odor of leather, cloth, and drying page as if it were an exotic incense," graduates in 1914, and stays on to teach freshman composition.
- **Why it matters:** The university is the whole world of the novel, the refuge and the only life Stoner chooses. Williams dedicates the book to his colleagues in Missouri's English department and notes he took liberties with the place, "so that in effect it, too, is a fictional place."
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real university, deliberately fictionalized in its details by the author.
