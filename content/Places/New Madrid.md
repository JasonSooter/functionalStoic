# New Madrid

Town on the Mississippi in south-east [[Missouri]], USA. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ it names the great intraplate earthquakes of 1811–12.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m81591gjnpv37wynh6ct8de9)

> The most notorious such quakes ever to hit the United States were a series of three in New Madrid, Missouri, in the winter of 1811

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The most notorious earthquakes in US history strike New Madrid in the winter of 1811–12, starting just after midnight on 16 December. The land rolls in three-foot waves, fissures open, the air smells of sulphur, and chimneys fall in Cincinnati 400 miles away; Audubon is a witness.
- **Why it matters:** It makes Bryson's point that intraplate quakes are random: the next one "could be under Chicago or Paris or Kinshasa".
