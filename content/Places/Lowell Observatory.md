# Lowell Observatory

Astronomical observatory on Mars Hill above [[Flagstaff]], Arizona. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Vesto Slipher first sees the galaxies' red shift here, and no one notices.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Coincidentally, at about the time that Einstein was affixing a cosmological constant to his theory, at the Lowell Observatory in Arizona, an astronomer with the cheerily intergalactic name of Vesto Slipher (who was in fact from Indiana) was taking spectrographic readings of distant stars and discovering that they appeared to be moving away from us.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Around the time Einstein adds his cosmological constant, Vesto Slipher takes spectrographic readings of distant stars here and finds they appear to be moving away. No one much notices: thanks to Percival Lowell's obsession with Martian canals the observatory is, in the book's words, an outpost of astronomical endeavor, and Slipher knows nothing of relativity.
- **Why it matters:** It is the quiet first sighting of the expanding universe that Hubble later makes famous. A brief scene; the book doesn't linger there.
