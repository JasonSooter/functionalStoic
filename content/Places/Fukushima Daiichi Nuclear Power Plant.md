# Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant

Nuclear plant on [[Japan]]'s eastern coast, wrecked by the 2011 earthquake and tsunami. Hannah Ritchie uses the accident to put nuclear risk in perspective, and The B1M follows the decades-long job of taking the site apart.

## Not the End of the World — Hannah Ritchie (2024)
Source: [[Not the End of the World]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL37518722W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5e31hed9rgpzbzxyg9thhcz)

> In 2011, the Fukushima nuclear plant in Japan was hit by a tsunami after the country’s largest recorded earthquake. Remarkably, no one died directly from the incident.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** After the tsunami, the text says no one died directly, one possible cancer death was reported, and around 2,700 premature deaths are attributed to evacuation stress.
- **Why it matters:** Second of the two accidents used to put nuclear risk in perspective.

## The Race to Demolish Fukushima — The B1M (2026)
Source: [Original](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aBKNGIvw4XM) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m7kmfb45ey25fptzfh6d0b2g)

> On 11th March, 2011, the Fukushima Daiichi power plant was struck by the most powerful earthquake ever recorded in Japan’s history.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the video:** Fifteen years on, the video follows the clean-up of the wrecked plant, which the government aims to finish by 2051 while critics say it could take close to two centuries. Because the plant sits on the coast with groundwater flowing beneath it from the hills towards the sea, it could not simply be entombed like Chernobyl: at the peak about 540 m³ of water a day poured into the basements of units 1–4. The video covers the 1.5 km frozen-soil ice wall around the four reactor units, the structure built over unit four to remove its fuel, and trials to remove melted debris from unit two.
- **Why it matters:** The site's geography is the argument: its waterlogged coastal ground is what makes Fukushima a far longer and harder job than Chernobyl.
