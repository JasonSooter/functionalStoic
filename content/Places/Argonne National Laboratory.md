# Argonne National Laboratory

US national laboratory south-west of Chicago, [[Illinois]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ Clair Patterson measures the age of the Earth on its mass spectrograph.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> In the spring of 1953 he traveled to the Argonne National Laboratory in Illinois, where he was granted time on a late-model mass spectrograph, a machine capable of detecting and measuring the minute quantities of uranium and lead locked up in ancient crystals.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In spring 1953 Clair Patterson is given time on a late-model mass spectrograph here to measure the uranium and lead in ancient crystals. When the results come, he is so excited that he drives to his boyhood home in Iowa and has his mother check him into a hospital, thinking he is having a heart attack; soon afterwards he announces a definitive age for the Earth.
- **Why it matters:** This is where the age of the Earth is finally pinned down, the climax of the chapter.
