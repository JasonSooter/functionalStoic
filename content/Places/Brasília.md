# Brasília

Planned capital of [[Brazil]], founded in 1960. Both sources treat it as the archetype of the city built from scratch on a plan: a B1M video measures The Line against it, and _Happy City_ uses it to show what a plan legible from the air takes away from street life.

## Is The Line Really Dead? — The B1M (2025)
Source: [Original](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zFVYgZMEOFg) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5ce3h40v0g5xjeht52wrxvt)

> This is Brasília. Founded in 1960, it replaced Rio de Janeiro as Brazil’s capital.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the video:** The video uses Brasília, founded in 1960 to replace Rio de Janeiro as capital and laid out like an aeroplane, as the precedent for a city built from nothing in a remote place as a symbol of a nation's future.
- **Why it matters:** The comparison frames The Line as nation-building theatre as much as urbanism.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83qhvget69f0my4898f98fp)

> Early sketches of Brasilia resembled an airplane or a great bird with its wings outstretched. From above, the plan was exhilarating.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The 1950s capital separates functions along two axes, with the Plaza of the Three Powers at the "head", monumental avenues, and identical residential superblocks along the "wings", pedestrians kept apart from cars. Its first residents feel lost amid the sameness and empty space, miss the market streets of their old towns, and coin "brasilite" for the malaise; the city later spills beyond its plan into unplanned settlements.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's example of planners' oversimplification: a plan that reads beautifully on paper but strips out the messy public life that makes cities rewarding.
