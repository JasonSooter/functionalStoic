# The village (The Old Man and the Sea)

Unnamed fishing village on the north coast of Cuba near Havana where Santiago lives. The pin is a best guess at Cojímar, the village east of Havana where Hemingway kept his boat.

## The Old Man and the Sea — Ernest Hemingway (1952)

Source: [[The Old Man and the Sea]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL7265161M) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5gkzke8v71emq8hc21ekv4a)

> When the wind was in the east a smell came across the harbour from the shark factory;

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The skiffs are hauled up on the bank, marlin go to the fish house for the ice truck to Havana, and sharks are cut up at the shark factory across the cove. Santiago lives in a one-room shack of guano palm up the road, where he dreams of lions; he rows out of the harbour in the dark and sails back to the little patch of shingle below the rocks, then climbs to the shack to collapse.
- **Why it matters:** The village frames the whole novella: the fishermen who mock or pity Santiago, the boy who waits for him, and the tourists who see only a skeleton at the end.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text never names the village. Clues: the catch goes by ice truck 'to the market in Havana'; Santiago can 'always come in on the glow from Havana'; the boy walks on coral rocks; the boat lands on 'the little patch of shingle below the rocks'. Reference works checked (Wikipedia on Cojímar and on the novel) do not name the setting, but Wikipedia confirms Hemingway lived in Cojímar, kept his boat Pilar there and frequented La Terraza; travel writers commonly call it the model. Chosen: Cojímar. Alternative: another fishing village on the coast just east of Havana.
