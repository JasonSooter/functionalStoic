# Regattastrecke Berlin-Grünau

Rowing course on the Langer See (Dahme) at Grünau, in the south-east of [[Berlin]], venue of the 1936 Olympic regatta. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is rebuilt in limestone for the Games while the Nuremberg Laws drive Jews out of Berlin's rowing clubs.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86yewmj5bx5smmf7s0q96pk)

> Grünau was located on the west bank of the long, narrow Langer See, one of several lakes fed by the Dahme River, just where suburbs began to give way to open meadows and tracts of dark forest southeast of Berlin.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In 1935 masons and carpenters build a stately new boathouse, Haus West, beside the existing Haus Mitte and Haus Ost, with dressing rooms, hot showers, massage rooms and storage for ninety-seven shells, while wooden grandstands are planned out over the water. The book contrasts this with the seaplane hangar in Seattle and the rickety boathouses of Poughkeepsie. Beneath the surface, the Jewish Helvetia Rowing Club has been banned since 1933 and mixed clubs are threatened with dissolution under the new laws. In late July 1936 the Americans share a new shell house with the German crew, an American and a Nazi flag facing each other over the entrance, and row badly in the cold wind with colds. Ulbrickson loses an argument over the exposed outer lanes, and new lane rules give the sheltered lanes to Germany and Italy and lane six, out in the widest part of the Langer See, to the Americans. On August 12 they come from dead last to win their heat against Britain in 6:00.8, a new course, world and Olympic record. On the day of the final more than seventy-five thousand people pack the grounds, Hitler, Goebbels and Göring watch from the balcony of Haus West, and Germany wins the first five races. In wind and rain the Washington boat falls to last, then sweeps up alongside the north-side bleachers and, "in the span of a single second", crosses the line with Italy and Germany: 6:25.4, six-tenths of a second ahead of Italy. The next morning they return to re-row for Riefenstahl's cameras. In 2011 the author finds the grounds little changed and stands on the Haus West balcony, near where Hitler stood.
- **Why it matters:** Grünau is where the whole book is heading: first the polished face of the Nazi Games, with the persecution hidden behind it, then the course of the Olympic final the crew wins against the odds and the regime's favourites.
