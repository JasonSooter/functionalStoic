# Regattastrecke Berlin-Grünau

Rowing course on the Langer See (Dahme) at Grünau, in the south-east of [[Berlin]], venue of the 1936 Olympic regatta. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is rebuilt in limestone for the Games while the Nuremberg Laws drive Jews out of Berlin's rowing clubs.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86yewmj5bx5smmf7s0q96pk)

> Grünau was located on the west bank of the long, narrow Langer See, one of several lakes fed by the Dahme River, just where suburbs began to give way to open meadows and tracts of dark forest southeast of Berlin.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In 1935 masons and carpenters build a stately new boathouse, Haus West, beside the existing Haus Mitte and Haus Ost, with dressing rooms, hot showers, massage rooms and storage for ninety-seven shells, while wooden grandstands are planned out over the water. The book contrasts this with the seaplane hangar in Seattle and the rickety boathouses of Poughkeepsie. Beneath the surface, the Jewish Helvetia Rowing Club has been banned since 1933 and mixed clubs are threatened with dissolution under the new laws.
- **Why it matters:** Grünau is where the whole book is heading, the course of the Olympic final; here it first appears as the polished face of the Nazi Games, with the persecution hidden behind it.
