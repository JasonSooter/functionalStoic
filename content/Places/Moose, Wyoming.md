## Patagonia’s Philosopher-King – The New Yorker — Nick Paumgarten (2016)

Source: [Original](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2016/09/19/patagonias-philosopher-king) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jy7tcg78dg8h04p1apb2ac)

> The property is just north of the Jackson Hole airport, on the east side of the Snake River, up by the entrance to Grand Teton National Park.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the article:** The profile opens here: Chouinard sits alone in the dark listening to the Democratic convention, airing his despair about politics and climate, before leaving at dawn for Montana. The house, built in 1976 from beetle-kill lodgepole pine, is described as a relic amid encroaching vacation homes.
- **Why it matters:** It is the intimate frame for the portrait of Chouinard as a reclusive, disenchanted philosopher-king, and anchors the Jackson/northern Rockies setting.
