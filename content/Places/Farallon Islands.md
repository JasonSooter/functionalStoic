# Farallon Islands

Rocky islands about thirty miles off San Francisco, [[California]]. In _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ they mark where the US Navy dumped tens of thousands of drums of radioactive waste.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W)

> Since 1946, the United States had been ferrying fifty-five-gallon drums of radioactive gunk out to the Farallon Islands, some thirty miles off the California coast near San Francisco, where it simply threw them overboard.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** From 1946 the United States ferries fifty-five-gallon drums of radioactive waste out to the Farallon Islands and throws them overboard. When drums refuse to sink, Navy gunners shoot holes in them, letting plutonium, uranium and strontium escape; the text says almost fifty thousand drums went into the Farallons alone.
- **Why it matters:** Bryson's starkest example of how carelessly we treated a deep sea we knew almost nothing about.
