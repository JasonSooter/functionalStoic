## Walkable City — Jeff Speck (2012)
Source: [[Walkable City]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL36883695M/Walkable_City) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fbjp2f70req13ce2rpvyy8)

> Ironically, the city that resisted the greatest amount of federal highway spending was probably the nation’s capital. Most current residents are unaware that, at one time, the Washington area was slated to receive 450 miles of interstate highways, 38 of which would have plowed through the District itself.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Speck moved to Washington in 2003, went car-free, lost ten pounds, met his wife on a transit platform and built a house with a garden instead of a driveway near Logan Circle. Separately he recounts the twenty-two-year fight that blocked freeways under the Mall and along the Georgetown waterfront, so only ten miles were built and money went to Metrorail.
- **Why it matters:** Washington is both his personal example of a car-optional city and the highway-revolt success story. The text frames the saved neighborhoods and Metro as central to the city's resurgence.
