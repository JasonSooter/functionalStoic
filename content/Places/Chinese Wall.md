# Chinese Wall

Long limestone escarpment along the Continental Divide in Montana's Bob Marshall Wilderness. Ryback's party searches for it for two days in the snow and never sees it.

## The Ultimate Journey - Canada to Mexico Down the Continental Divide — Eric Ryback (1973)
Source: [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8berngz89bcgy6ybnx72gs2)

> We had battled for almost two entire days to see a huge and important granite formation which now seemed not to exist.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Eric had looked forward to the Chinese Wall, which the book describes as a huge granite formation 15 miles long and 1,500 feet high. In knee-deep snow and cloud they search for it three times over almost two days; it "seemed not to exist". With Tim near collapse, Eric gives up and they retreat into the valley of Rock Creek and down the Sun River valleys.
- **Why it matters:** It is the trek's first real disappointment, and the retreat into the valleys starts Tim's long spell of loneliness that the book calls "decivilization".
- **Real vs. fictional:** ⚠️ The book calls it granite; the real Chinese Wall is a limestone escarpment.
