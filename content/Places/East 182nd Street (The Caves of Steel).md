## The Caves of Steel — Isaac Asimov (1954)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL46401W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5hj3m19qmvhf59aqakw04gc)

> They were at East 182nd Street and in not more than two hundred yards they would be at the elevator banks that fed those steel and concrete layers of apartments that included his own.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Walking Daneel home, Baley is stopped by a crowd outside one of the retail departments on the ground levels. Inside, a customer's argument with robot clerks at a shoe counter is turning into a riot, which Baley and Daneel defuse.
- **Why it matters:** The first public sign of the anti-robot anger that drives the plot, set on Baley's home turf.
- **Real vs. fictional:** A real Bronx street name in a future, roofed-over City.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ Clues: 'East 182nd Street' in the 'Lower Bronx Section', a short walk from Baley's apartment. Pinned at a segment of today's East 182nd Street in the Bronx; the street runs for about a mile, so the exact point is approximate.
