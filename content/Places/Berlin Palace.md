# Berlin Palace

Royal palace of the Hohenzollerns at the end of Unter den Linden in Berlin, demolished in 1950 and rebuilt as the Humboldt Forum. In _Germany: Memories of a Nation_ it bookends the account of Berlin's layered memories.

## Germany: Memories of a Nation — Neil MacGregor (2014)
Source: [[Germany - Memories of a Nation]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20201162W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5m750q0kwp8g82rfx0ma948)

> Here stood the Stadtschloss, residence of the Hohenzollern kings and Kaisers, demolished by the GDR in the 1950s.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Chapter 1 looks east from the Gate down Unter den Linden to the Stadtschloss, built to show that Brandenburg had survived. The GDR demolished it in the 1950s, keeping only the balcony from which Liebknecht proclaimed a socialist republic on 9 November 1918, and from the mid-1970s the Palast der Republik stood on the site until it too was pulled down. In the final chapter a replica Schloss is rising to house the non-European collections.
- **Why it matters:** MacGregor treats it as the most contested site of memory in Berlin, where each regime erased the last one's symbol; its new role as a place of dialogue between cultures closes the book. The text gives the Palast der Republik's demolition as both 'by 2008' (Chapter 1) and 2006 (Chapter 30).
