# 69 Long Acre

Shop building on Long Acre in Covent Garden, [[London]], where Brompton opened its first London Junction store in 2013 on the site where Denis Johnson made Britain's first hobby-horse in 1819.

## The Brompton: Engineering for Change — Will Butler-Adams (2022)
Source: [[The Brompton - Engineering for Change]] · [Original](https://profilebooks.com/work/the-brompton/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m8cdhk1maqzqm16z9qvrmj6e)

> Finally a friendly estate agent gave us a tip about a last-minute opportunity, and Lorne and I rushed into central London to buy a bankrupt Belgian chocolatier from its liquidators, just because it was still the tenant of no. 69 Long Acre in Covent Garden and that was the only way of obtaining the lease.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Prestige landlords wanted fashion brands, not bike shops. Will and CFO Lorne Vary buy the failed chocolatier to get its lease, though the rent is about £20,000 over the board's budget. Looking back from across the road, they see a green plaque: Denis Johnson made and sold Britain's first bicycle, the hobby-horse, on this site in 1819. "That made our minds up."
- **Why it matters:** The flagship gave Brompton a central-London shop window, and the book says it made the later Greenwich Village store in New York easy to open.
