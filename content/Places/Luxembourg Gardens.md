# Luxembourg Gardens

Public park in the 6th arrondissement of [[Paris]], France. In _Happy City_ it is where mobility thinker Eric Britton shows Charles Montgomery that a shared bike is never more than a few minutes away.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84c7j1ds8yefp6m01h9ypp9)

> Instead we wandered north, up immaculate sidewalks and through the iron gates of the Luxembourg Gardens.

- **Role:** journey waypoint
- **In the book:** Chapter 9, "Mobilicities II: Freedom". Eric Britton, the economist whose early-1970s proposal led to Paris's Carte d'Orange transit pass, lives a few blocks from the gardens, where he secretly buried his late mother's ashes. He walks Montgomery from his apartment through the gates and across the park, and at its edge unlocks a Vélib' bike with his Navigo card to show that a "personal metro" is within minutes of any spot in the park.
- **Why it matters:** The garden walk opens the chapter's argument that freedom in a city comes from a menu of ways to move rather than from one machine; any well-served Paris street could stand in, but the park makes the point vivid.
