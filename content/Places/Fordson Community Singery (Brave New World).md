# Fordson Community Singery (Brave New World)

## Brave New World — Aldous Huxley (1932)

Source: [[Brave New World]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL64365W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kvah4va8t6mnmspbpvd3s7)

> Flood-lighted, its three hundred and twenty metres of white Carrara-surrogate gleamed with a snowy incandescence over Ludgate Hill;

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** On his Solidarity Service night Bernard flies east to the Singery, arriving late as Big Henry, its clock, strikes nine. In Room 3210 on the thirty-third floor twelve people drink soma, sing hymns and work up to the 'orgy-porgy', after which Bernard feels more isolated than before.
- **Why it matters:** The Singery replaces the church, and its rite is where Bernard's failure to fuse with the group is staged; it sets up his restlessness.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The Singery is fictional.
- **Where is it?:** ⚠️ The text says its "three hundred and twenty metres of white Carrara-surrogate gleamed with a snowy incandescence over Ludgate Hill", and Bernard flies eastwards to reach it. Pinned at Ludgate Hill; it stands where St Paul's would be, but the text does not say so.
