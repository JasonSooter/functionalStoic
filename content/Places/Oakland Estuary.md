# Oakland Estuary

Tidal channel between Oakland and Alameda on San Francisco Bay. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is the University of California's home water, where Washington's great rival Ky Ebright trains his crews.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)

Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86m5kzcdks61kz3k2a4gdwg)

> Six hundred and seventy miles to the south, on the Oakland Estuary—the University of California’s home water—Ky Ebright was facing remarkably similar problems.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** In early 1934 Ebright, with only one of his 1932 Olympic gold medal crew still rowing, watches his varsity turn in indifferent times on the estuary, and frets that its calm weather has left his crews unready for Lake Washington's whitecaps. In April 1935 the Washington crews come south to race here: a narrow, oil-slicked channel between Oakland and Alameda lined with warehouses, oil tanks and cranes. On race day, with the biggest crowd at the finish on the Fruitvale Avenue Bridge, the freshmen and the JV both win, and Ulbrickson's sophomore varsity beats Cal in a finish so close that the loudspeaker first announces "California by two feet" before the judges declare Washington the winner by six feet; the bridge crosses the course at an angle.
- **Why it matters:** The estuary is first the mirror image of Lake Washington, Cal's sheltered water against Washington's rough lake; then it is where Joe's sophomore crew sweeps California on its home water and becomes the Pacific Coast varsity champion.
