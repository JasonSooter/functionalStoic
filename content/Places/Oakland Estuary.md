# Oakland Estuary

Tidal channel between Oakland and Alameda on San Francisco Bay. In _The Boys in the Boat_ it is the University of California's home water, where Washington's great rival Ky Ebright trains his crews.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86m5kzcdks61kz3k2a4gdwg)

> Six hundred and seventy miles to the south, on the Oakland Estuary—the University of California’s home water—Ky Ebright was facing remarkably similar problems.

- **Role:** backdrop
- **In the book:** In early 1934 Ebright, with only one of his 1932 Olympic gold medal crew still rowing, watches his varsity turn in indifferent times on the estuary. Before the April regatta in Seattle he frets that the estuary's "typically but frustratingly calm and pleasant" weather has left his crews unready for Lake Washington's whitecaps. Later in 1934 the Washington boys ship a Pocock shell south, knowing they will soon race against it on the estuary.
- **Why it matters:** The estuary is the mirror image of Lake Washington: Cal's sheltered water against Washington's rough lake, a contrast the book uses to frame the rivalry. It is a backdrop rather than a scene.
