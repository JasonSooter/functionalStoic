# Nahá

Lacandon village in the rainforest of Chiapas, [[Mexico]], near the border with Guatemala (spelled "Naja" in the book). In _Happy City_ it is where Mark Lakeman found the village form he went home to rebuild in Portland.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m84ph5mn24djq7qez781df1w)

> Lakeman found the quality he was looking for in an unpaved village called Naja.

- **Role:** turning point
- **In the book:** Chapter 13. Travelling after architecture school, Mark Lakeman reaches Naja, where villagers cook on earthen hearths and build huts of rough mahogany. Where paths between homes and gardens merge, the ground flattens into gathering spaces where people meet in circles, children and adults together: "this earthen agora". The village leader, Chan K'in Viejo, tells him to go home and fix his own village.
- **Why it matters:** Naja is the source of the idea behind [[Share-It-Square]] and City Repair: a place that is constantly redesigned by the people who use it.
- **Real vs. fictional:** Real; the book spells it "Naja", the village is usually written Nahá.
