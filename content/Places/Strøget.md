# Strøget

String of pedestrian shopping streets through the old centre of [[Copenhagen]]. In _Happy City_ it is where Jan Gehl spent a year counting people after cars were banned in 1962, the experiment behind his people-first planning.

## Happy City — Charles Montgomery (2013)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21093281W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m841x979j0r80t68ffa15hg5)

> He spent a year on the Strøget, which changed before his eyes. People poured into the space that had been vacated by cars.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Back from studying Siena's Campo, Jan Gehl parks himself on the newly pedestrianized Strøget, sitting there every Tuesday and Saturday in sun, rain and slush to log who comes and what they do. Critics had insisted Danes would never sit in outdoor cafés in winter, but people poured into the space vacated by cars, in summer and in the darkest days of winter, and businesses thrived.
- **Why it matters:** It is the book's founding experiment for the idea that changing a street's form changes how people use it; Copenhagen's later network of car-free streets grew from it.
