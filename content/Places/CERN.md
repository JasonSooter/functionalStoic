# CERN

European laboratory for particle physics on the French–Swiss border near Geneva. In _Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!_ Feynman lectures there in his Nobel suit, and _A Short History of Nearly Everything_ uses it to show the scale of particle physics.

## "Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!": Adventures of a Curious Character — Richard P. Feynman (1985)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL514629W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kfr2jn7rbjvh66yhq16tma)

> Instead of coming home immediately, I went to CERN, the European center for nuclear research in Switzerland, to give a talk.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** "Instead of coming home immediately, I went to CERN" after the Nobel ceremony. He gives his talk in the suit he wore to the King's Dinner and jokes that he likes it; the physicists boo and Weisskopf tears off his coat, and the formality of Sweden is undone.
- **Why it matters:** A short coda showing his relief at returning to informal physics culture; minor as a place.

## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m80ty4fs6r88hpvp9c2n9wkq)

> CERN, the European Organization for Nuclear Research, is like a little city. Straddling the border of France and Switzerland, it employs three thousand people and occupies a site that is measured in square miles.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** Bryson describes CERN as like a little city straddling the border of France and Switzerland: three thousand employees, a site measured in square miles, a string of magnets that weigh more than the Eiffel Tower and an underground tunnel over sixteen miles around.
- **Why it matters:** It stands for the scale of modern particle physics, beside the abandoned Texas supercollider.
