# Dead Sea

Salt lake on the Israel–Jordan border, the lowest point on land; in _Life on Air_ its salt flats stand in for the dried-up Mediterranean in The First Eden.

## Life on Air \[revised & updated 2nd edition] — David Attenborough (2002)

Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL24547776M/Life_on_air) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5hy811rbkkn6m0gjakqfawf)

> We did, however, go to the Dead Sea and filmed the salt deposits that surround it, to give some impression of what that vast empty valley must have been like, while I tried to describe in words how the Mediterranean came into existence.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** For The First Eden, they film the salt deposits around the Dead Sea as a stand-in for the dried-up Mediterranean basin of six million years ago.
- **Why it matters:** A stand-in location for the series opening; the text does not dwell on the place itself.

## Apeirogon — Colum McCann (2020)

Source: [[Apeirogon]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/207433/apeirogon-a-novel-by-colum-mccann/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5jjq5ga01nr3p0vnnze1z6m)

> They launched onto the Dead Sea near the mouth of the Jordan. The sky was blue upon blue. The wind was laden with heat. Nothing else stirred. No fish, no birds.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The young Irish theologian Christopher Costigin and his Maltese sailor take their boat down the Jordan and out onto the Dead Sea in August 1835, taking soundings and searching for Sodom and Gomorrah; Costigin is ravaged by heat and salt and later dies in Jerusalem. Elsewhere the text describes its sinkholes, its shrinking, the Qumran scrolls found in cliffs nearby, and Smadar's photograph floating there.
- **Why it matters:** The Dead Sea anchors the book's water motif: a dying lake fed by a depleted river, and the site of both Costigin's folly and the scrolls found by a shepherd looking for a goat.
