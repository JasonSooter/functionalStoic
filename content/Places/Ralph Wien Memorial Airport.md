# Ralph Wien Memorial Airport

Airport of Kotzebue, a village on the Chukchi Sea in northwest Alaska, above the Arctic Circle. It is where The Comfort Crisis (Michael Easter) opens, on the tarmac before a 33-day Arctic trek.

## The Comfort Crisis — Michael Easter (2021)

Source: [[The Comfort Crisis]] · [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/634446/the-comfort-crisis-by-michael-easter/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5fycx65vbxptb1q0s51yt13)

> I’M STANDING ON a windy tarmac in Kotzebue, Alaska, a 3,000-person village 20 miles above the Arctic Circle on the Chukchi Sea.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** The book's opening scene: Easter, filmmaker Donnie Vincent, cinematographer William Altman and pilot Brian wait on the windy tarmac to fly bush planes into the Alaskan Arctic for a 33-day caribou hunt. Easter learns that Brian's colleague Mike recently crashed after misreading the weather.
- **Why it matters:** Kotzebue is the jumping-off point from the comfortable world into the backcountry, the contrast the whole book is built on; the airport stands for the last edge of modern comfort.
- **Real vs. fictional:** The text says only 'a windy tarmac in Kotzebue'; Ralph Wien Memorial Airport is Kotzebue's airport.
