# Dead Letter Office (Washington)

The US Post Office's office for undeliverable mail in Washington, D.C. In Melville's _Bartleby, the Scrivener_ it is the rumoured past workplace of Bartleby, the story's only clue to his despair.

## Bartleby, the Scrivener: A Story of Wall-Street — Herman Melville (1853)
Source: [[Bartleby, the Scrivener - A Story of Wall-Street]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL102732W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m5kfr8zs0c1n7862q7zs68js)

> When I think over this rumor, I cannot adequately express the emotions which seize me. Dead letters! does it not sound like dead men?

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** In the closing paragraphs the narrator passes on a report that Bartleby had been "a subordinate clerk in the Dead Letter Office at Washington", removed by a change in the administration. He imagines the work of handling letters that never arrive and sorting them for the flames. The office is never visited; it exists only as rumour.
- **Why it matters:** It gives the story its only hint of Bartleby's past and a possible source for his "pallid hopelessness".
