## A Short History of Nearly Everything — Bill Bryson (2003)
Source: [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL74128W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m83dbpq7d9xcwanf48bca6fy)

> until shortly before the last one died, forlorn and nameless, in a private Hobart zoo in 1936.

- **Role:** subject
- **In the book:** The Tasmanian tiger, a doglike marsupial with striped back, was hunted with bounties until shortly before the last one died, alone and unnamed, in a private zoo in Hobart in 1936. Visitors to the Tasmanian Museum today can see only photographs, because the last thylacine's body was thrown out with the weekly rubbish.
- **Why it matters:** Hobart shows that the urge to exterminate was not only American, and repeats the chapter's theme of carelessness even with the last specimen.
