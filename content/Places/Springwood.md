# Springwood

Franklin D. Roosevelt's family home at Hyde Park on the Hudson, New York, now a national historic site. In _The Boys in the Boat_ the Washington varsity rows upriver to visit it on the eve of the 1936 Poughkeepsie regatta.

## The Boys in the Boat — Daniel James Brown (2013)
Source: [Original](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310727/the-boys-in-the-boat-by-daniel-james-brown/) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m878tkggm0y13mrx9agqck0b)

> Walking through the adjoining nursery and up another path, they finally arrived on a wide gravel drive leading up to a large expanse of lawn on which stood Springwood, the Roosevelts’ stately three-story brick-and-masonry mansion, complete with a semicircular portico supported by white Grecian columns.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** On the Saturday evening before the June 1936 regatta the boys borrow the coaching launch, go up the Hudson past Krum Elbow to the Hyde Park landing and walk up through the woods to the mansion. Franklin Roosevelt Jr., who rows for Harvard, welcomes them, talks rowing and coaches, and shows them the president's library and the chair he broadcasts his fireside chats from. Johnny White records the visit in his diary.
- **Why it matters:** A quiet interlude before the biggest race of the season, it shows the boys' awe at a world of privilege far from their own.
