# Detroit Institute of Arts

Art museum on Woodward Avenue in [[Detroit]], home of Diego Rivera's _Detroit Industry_ murals. In Hayden Herrera's _Frida_ its Garden Court is Rivera's workplace and Frida's daily refuge in 1932–33, and the murals' unveiling sets off a public storm.

## Frida — Hayden Herrera (1983)

Source: [[Frida - A Biography of Frida Kahlo]] · [Original](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4114749W) · [Readwise highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m737hg584qprnnag963ghmhk)

> On May 23 the Arts Commission approved his sketches for two large panels on the north and south walls of the Detroit Institute of Arts’ glass-roofed Garden Court.

- **Role:** setting
- **In the book:** In Chapter 10 Rivera paints his industry murals in the glass-roofed Garden Court; Frida brings his lunch in a basket nearly every day, wanders the galleries with director Valentiner, and watches the August 31 eclipse from the roof. Chapter 11 opens with the murals' unveiling on March 13, 1933, attacked at once as sacrilegious, Communistic and obscene; Edsel Ford defends them, workers volunteer to guard them, and the Riveras leave Detroit a week later.
- **Why it matters:** It is the centre of the Detroit chapter, tying Frida's months of loss to Rivera's work, and the first of the mural fights that culminate at [[30 Rockefeller Plaza]].
