# About

![[Attachments/Jason Sooter Avatar.png|160]]

Hi, I'm Jason. I'm a solutions architect who spends a lot of time thinking about how systems fit together, whether they're data pipelines, bike networks, or cities. This site is where I work those ideas out in writing. It's half notebook, half blog.

## Work

I'm a Solutions Architect at [Twilio](https://www.twilio.com), on the go-to-market team covering Segment, Flex, and related products. Most of my days are spent with enterprise customers, largely in financial services, designing how their customer data should move: what gets collected, how it's governed, where it lands in the warehouse, and how it flows back out to the tools that act on it.

The work sits where architecture meets pragmatism. Regulated industries can't be sloppy with data, but they can't wait for a perfect design either. My favorite moment is when a messy whiteboard sketch becomes something a team can actually build and run.

I mostly reach for TypeScript and Node.js, Snowflake, AWS, and Terraform. I learn by building: I'd rather ship a rough prototype and iterate than read documentation for a week, and a lot of what I write here starts as one of those prototypes.

## What's here

The blog holds the longer pieces. My public Obsidian notes are the working drafts and connective tissue behind them. Some are polished, many aren't, and that's on purpose. You'll find writing on:

- **Software & solutions architecture**: data integration, customer data platforms, event pipelines, warehouses, and infrastructure as code
- **Cycling & bike infrastructure**: bikepacking, gear, and what makes a street feel safe to ride
- **Urban planning & public transit**: walkable cities and the systems that make them work
- **Travel**: mostly Europe, often by bike or train
- **Books**: what I'm reading, including a slow around-the-world challenge of one book per country
- **Moving to Freiburg**: relocating from the US to Germany, paperwork and all

## Outside work

I live in Freiburg im Breisgau, on the edge of the Black Forest, having moved here from Fort Collins, Colorado. Freiburg makes a pretty good case for most of what I write about urban design. I've been married for 20 years, and we have three kids.

Our household owns 15 bikes (three are still waiting in Colorado), which is either a transportation philosophy or a problem, depending on whom you ask. I ride gravel and loaded touring, have bikepacked through the Dolomites, and am slowly hiking the Westweg in stages. I read around 40 books a year, mostly literary fiction.
